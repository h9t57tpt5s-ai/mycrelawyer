## Reference examples (Texas and California — already completed to this standard; match this level of precision, citation density, and prose style)

**Texas — Landlord-Friendly**

Statutory Notice. Unless the parties have contracted for a shorter or longer notice period in a written lease or agreement, a landlord must give a tenant who defaults or holds over beyond the end of the rental term or renewal period at least three days' written notice to vacate before filing a forcible detainer suit. Beginning January 1, 2026, Texas Property Code § 24.005 distinguishes certain nonpayment actions by requiring a notice to pay rent or vacate when the tenant's right of possession is terminated solely for nonpayment of rent and the tenant was not delinquent before the month in which the notice was given. Section 24.005 also specifies approved notice-delivery methods, including mail, delivery inside the premises in a conspicuous place, hand delivery to a tenant age 16 or older, and approved electronic delivery where agreed in writing.

Self-Help / Commercial Lockout. Texas continues to permit commercial lockouts under Chapter 93 of the Texas Property Code. A commercial landlord may change the door locks of a tenant who is delinquent in paying at least part of the rent. However, the landlord may not intentionally exclude a commercial tenant except by judicial process unless the exclusion results from bona fide repairs, construction, an emergency, abandonment, or a lockout authorized by § 93.002. The landlord must post written notice on the tenant's front door identifying the person or company from which a new key may be obtained. Chapter 93 also provides a statutory reentry remedy if a tenant is unlawfully locked out.

2026 Update Note. Effective January 1, 2026, Chapter 24 of the Texas Property Code and Rule 510 underwent substantial revision through S.B. 38. The amendments address notice content, delivery methods, venue, computation of time, service requirements, summary disposition procedures, trial timing, and appellate procedures.

**California — Neutral**

Statutory Notice. Commercial notice requirements depend upon the basis for termination and the provisions of the lease. Before initiating an unlawful detainer action, landlords should verify the applicable commercial notice provisions, cure rights, and lease-specific requirements governing the default at issue.

Self-Help / Commercial Lockout. Self-help is not an available commercial eviction remedy in California. Commercial landlords generally must recover possession through unlawful detainer proceedings rather than lockout or other unilateral exclusion methods.

Timing. California continues to provide expedited unlawful detainer procedures. Effective January 1, 2026, Assembly Bill 1384 amended Code of Civil Procedure § 1170. In a commercial unlawful detainer action, when a defendant files a demurrer or motion to strike, the hearing generally must occur between five and seven court days after filing. In commercial tenancy cases, any continuance granted for good cause may not exceed ten court days after the first hearing date established by the court.

Damages. California Civil Code § 1951.2 governs damages following termination of a lease due to tenant breach. Upon termination, a landlord may recover unpaid rent earned before termination; rent damages accruing through the time of award; future-rent damages where authorized by the lease and statute; and other detriment proximately caused by the tenant's breach. Future-rent recovery remains subject to Civil Code § 1951.2(c), and mitigation principles continue to apply.

2026 Update Note. Effective January 1, 2026, AB 1384 amended California Code of Civil Procedure § 1170 and imposed stricter timing requirements for hearings on demurrers and motions to strike in commercial unlawful detainer proceedings.

- Attorney's fees provision.
- Detailed default and cure provisions.
- Reservation of remedies following reentry.
- Non-waiver language for acceptance of payments.
- Clarification of mitigation and reletting rights.
- Post-termination damage provisions.
