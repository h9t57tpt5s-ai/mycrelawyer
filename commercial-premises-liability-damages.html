How damages are usually measured

Claims against owners and operators for injuries on commercial property are valued like other injury claims (medical costs, lost earnings, pain and suffering), adjusted for whether the owner knew or should have known of the hazard, the injured person's own share of fault under the state's comparative-fault rule, and the state's rules on security and open-and-obvious conditions.

General background, not legal advice; the rules vary by state and by the documents in your deal.

What the calculator asks about

These are the facts it pulls from your description or documents for a premises liability claim against a commercial property:

  • State where the injury occurred
  • Medical specials incurred/anticipated
  • Lost wages claimed
  • Injury severity
  • Is this a slip-and-fall / hazardous-condition claim?
  • has actual or constructive notice of the hazard been proven?
  • does the hazard fit a self-service business's own operating method?
  • Is inadequate/negligent security (third-party criminal act) alleged?
  • were there prior similar criminal incidents on the property or in its immediate vicinity?
  • Is negligent maintenance / structural failure alleged (railing, stairs, elevator, etc.)?
