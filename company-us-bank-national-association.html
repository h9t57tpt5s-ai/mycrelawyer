In re F-Star Socorro, L.P. (Five Star Development): Court Conditionally Approves Disclosure Statement, Sending Commercial Property Debtors' $180M Exit Plan to a Creditor Vote

A Texas bankruptcy judge let Five Star Development's Commercial Property Debtors seek votes on a plan that would roll $180 million of bankruptcy loans into exit loans and pay allowed unsecured claims in full.

On November 13, 2026, the Houston bankruptcy court handling the F-Star Socorro cases conditionally approved the disclosure statement for the Commercial Property Debtors' Chapter 11 plan, so voting can begin, according to the ElevenFlo case summary. The plan, filed that day, would turn a $120 million senior mortgage DIP facility and a $60 million mezzanine DIP facility into exit loans without any cash changing hands. Ballots and objections are due December 7, and a combined hearing on final disclosure approval and confirmation is set for December 16, 2026.

$180 million in DIP loans converting to exit loans

Why it matters: The plan offers one route out of a lender dispute: replace the original lender's financing with new bankruptcy loans, then carry those loans forward as exit debt rather than refinance at emergence. Whether it is confirmed, and how the separate guaranty dispute tied to the lender's credit bid is resolved, will shape the outcome for creditors.

ZOM Holding Files Chapter 11 After $308.6M Arizona Judgment in Gray Development Dispute

ZOM Holding Inc. and two affiliates filed Chapter 11 in Florida after a $308.6 million Arizona judgment for Gray Development Group. The debtors plan an asset sale and want ZOM's appeal of the judgment to continue.

ZOM Holding Inc., ZOM Associates and ZH Development I, affiliates of Winter Park, Florida-based developer ZOM Living, filed Chapter 11 petitions on October 30, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Middle District of Florida, where the cases are jointly administered. The filing follows a $308.6 million judgment, plus interest, entered in May by a Maricopa County court after a unanimous jury verdict for Gray Development Group, which alleged ZOM took its proprietary project information and then pursued the Arizona projects on its own. ZOM is appealing, and the debtors have asked the bankruptcy court to let that appeal continue while they run a sale of substantially all of their assets, with non-debtor affiliate ZOM Holding LP as the proposed stalking horse and lender.

$308.6M judgment plus interest; about $541M in estimated unsecured claims across the debtors

Why it matters: The case shows how a joint venture that never closes can still produce damages measured by the profits of a whole project pipeline when the developer signed a non-circumvention agreement. It also shows a judgment debtor using Chapter 11 to keep its property-owning affiliate out of bankruptcy, keep its appeal alive and run a sale on its own timetable, which creditors and prospective partners should expect after a large verdict.

Lenox Mortgage IX LLC v. San Francisco Care Center, L.P.: Ninth Circuit BAP Limits Lender's Fee Recovery Under Old HUD Loan Documents After Assisted-Living Facility Sale

The Ninth Circuit BAP held old HUD loan documents barred lender Lenox Mortgage IX from recovering some of its $419,266.94 fee demand after a San Francisco assisted-living facility's bankruptcy sale.

On October 9, 2026, the Ninth Circuit Bankruptcy Appellate Panel ruled on a fee dispute that followed the bankruptcy sale of a San Francisco assisted-living and memory-care facility. Lender Lenox Mortgage IX LLC had demanded $419,266.94 in attorneys' fees on top of its payoff. The panel agreed with the bankruptcy court that the old HUD-era loan documents did not allow Lenox to recover fees from the earlier defaults and state-court litigation. It reversed as to the fees Lenox incurred in the bankruptcy case itself and the payoff fight, and it vacated the borrower's prevailing-party fee award for reconsideration.

$419,266.94 in disputed lender attorneys' fees on a $19.8 million payoff demand
Senior Living

Why it matters: The ruling is a warning to lenders holding older or acquired loan paper: whether fees can be added to the secured debt under Bankruptcy Code section 506(b) depends entirely on the exact wording of the documents. If the clause ties fee recovery to a court award or to foreclosure-sale proceeds, a lender that never got a court award and whose collateral was sold under section 363 instead of at foreclosure may recover nothing for years of accrued legal costs. California's reciprocal-fee statute can also turn a failed fee claim into a fee award against the lender.

In re Duplex at Sleepy Hollow, LLC: Ninth Circuit BAP Upholds Retroactive Stay Annulment That Validated Enterprise Bank's Nine Laguna Beach Foreclosures

The Ninth Circuit BAP upheld retroactive annulment of the automatic stay, validating Enterprise Bank's nine Laguna Beach foreclosure sales after bad-faith involuntary Chapter 11 petitions were filed minutes earlier.

On October 7, 2026, the Ninth Circuit Bankruptcy Appellate Panel affirmed a bankruptcy court order that retroactively annulled the automatic stay. The order validated nine nonjudicial foreclosure sales that Enterprise Bank and Trust held on October 15, 2025 for Laguna Beach properties owned by ten single-asset entities. Involuntary Chapter 11 petitions had been filed against the owners minutes before the sales began. A prospective bidder appealed, arguing all the sales should be re-noticed. The panel found no abuse of discretion, given the bankruptcy court's finding that the filings were made in bad faith.

Roughly $80 million first-lien debt on ten Laguna Beach properties

Why it matters: The ruling shows that a last-minute involuntary bankruptcy filed by related creditors to stop a foreclosure can be undone after the fact. That holds especially when the lender had no notice and already held stay relief from an earlier bankruptcy. It also tells foreclosure-sale bidders that sitting out an auction because they know of a bankruptcy filing, without telling the trustee, weakens any later claim that they were harmed.

Hotel lenders should note how quickly the cash collateral fight arrives, since room and event revenue is both the lender's collateral and the debtor's operating budget.

The Real Deal reported that the filing cites $33.7 million in judgments against Venetos personally, about $30.8 million of it held by three lenders: Silver Point Finance ($22.7 million, for breach of a promissory note), and Vista Bank ($3.1 million) and NexPoint Real Estate Finance ($5 million) on his personal guaranties.
