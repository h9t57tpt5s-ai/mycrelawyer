How damages are usually measured

Most land-use cases are about the approval rather than money: a court upholds or reverses a denial, or orders a permit issued. Damages are available only in narrower settings, such as a taking, a civil-rights claim, a vested-rights or estoppel claim after money was spent in reliance on an approval, or a statute that allows them, and the measure is usually tied to delay, lost use or wasted costs.

General background, not legal advice; the rules vary by state and by the documents in your deal.

What the calculator asks about

These are the facts it pulls from your description or documents for a zoning or land-use claim:

  • Was a variance or permit denied and appealed?
  • Is a rezoning being challenged as improper spot zoning?
  • Is an arbitrary or discriminatory zoning denial alleged?
  • Was a permit issued, money spent, then the code changed to kill the project?
  • Is there a long pattern of repeated, shifting requirements?
  • Was there a complete absence of notice or hearing?
  • Is there direct evidence of discriminatory intent?
  • Estimated lost project/development value
  • Is a development agreement alleged to have been breached?
