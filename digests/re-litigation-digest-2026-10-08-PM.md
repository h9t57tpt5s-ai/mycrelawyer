# Fifth Circuit Shuts the Door on Whitestone's Last Appeal in the Pillarstone REIT Fight

### A four-year partnership dispute between two Houston-area REITs reaches what may be its final appellate chapter, as the Fifth Circuit declines to rehear a ruling against Whitestone Uptown Tower, LLC

By CREdocket Legal News Desk — prepared as a legal news update for commercial real estate owners, operators, and in-house counsel.

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit has denied a petition for rehearing en banc filed by Whitestone Uptown Tower, LLC, closing out — absent a petition for certiorari — one of the last open fronts in a years-long partnership and bankruptcy dispute between Pillarstone Capital REIT and Whitestone REIT. The denial, entered October 5, 2026, follows the panel's September 4, 2026 affirmance of a district court ruling that had itself affirmed a bankruptcy-court decision adverse to Whitestone Uptown Tower's position. Pillarstone disclosed the sequence of events in its Form 10-Q filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission on October 7, 2026, covering the quarter ended June 30, 2025.

The litigation traces back to a shareholder rights plan — a "poison pill" — that Pillarstone Capital REIT adopted on December 27, 2021, with American Stock Transfer & Trust Company serving as rights agent. Whitestone REIT Operating Partnership, L.P. ("Whitestone OP"), which holds the sole limited partner interest in Pillarstone's operating partnership, took the position that adopting the rights plan breached the partnership's Amended and Restated Agreement of Limited Partnership and the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing that runs with it. Whitestone OP sued in the Delaware Court of Chancery, and on July 21, 2022 moved to preserve the status quo, seeking broad restrictions on Pillarstone's ability to buy properties, enforce the rights plan, or incur expenses outside the ordinary course. On January 25, 2024, the Chancery Court ruled in Whitestone's favor, finding that Pillarstone's adoption of the rights plan breached the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing and clearing the way for Whitestone to exercise a contractual redemption right.

Pillarstone, for its part, went on offense in Texas. On September 16, 2022, Pillarstone Capital REIT and Pillarstone Capital REIT Operating Partnership LP filed suit in the 189th Judicial District Court of Harris County, styled Pillarstone Capital REIT and Pillarstone Capital REIT Operating Partnership LP v. Whitestone TRS, Inc., Whitestone REIT, Whitestone REIT Operating Partnership, L.P., Cause No. 2022-59478, alleging that the Whitestone entities had breached the limited partnership agreement, the related management agreements, and their fiduciary duties by operating outside Whitestone OP's role as a passive limited partner.

The dispute then migrated into bankruptcy court. On March 4, 2024, Chapter 11 cases were filed for Pillarstone Capital REIT and its operating partnership, along with affiliated Whitestone-related property entities. Within that bankruptcy proceeding, Whitestone Uptown Tower, LLC pressed a claim that the bankruptcy court ultimately ruled against; a federal district court affirmed that ruling, and Whitestone Uptown Tower appealed to the Fifth Circuit on August 13, 2025. Pillarstone's SEC disclosures do not detail the substantive legal question the bankruptcy court decided, only the appellate history that followed — but that history is now essentially complete. The panel affirmed on September 4, 2026; Whitestone Uptown Tower sought rehearing en banc on September 18, 2026; and the full court denied that petition on October 5, 2026. Pillarstone's filing states that Whitestone Uptown Tower "is considering its available options," language that typically signals a possible petition for certiorari to the U.S. Supreme Court, though nothing in the record suggests one has been filed.

The appellate fight over this discrete bankruptcy-court ruling proceeded on a separate track from the broader monetary settlement the two REIT families reached in December 2025. Under a settlement approved pursuant to Bankruptcy Rule 9019, Pillarstone's operating partnership paid Whitestone approximately $33.4 million to resolve claims tied to a guaranty obligation connected to the Uptown Tower property, with Pillarstone Capital REIT itself recovering roughly $4.05 million and a further $2.5 million held in reserve. That settlement resolved the bulk of the parties' outstanding monetary claims; the appeal decided this month addressed a ruling that fell outside its scope.

For REIT general counsel and boards, the case is a reminder that a shareholder rights plan adopted to defend against what a board views as a hostile or opportunistic position can itself become the subject of years of derivative litigation — spanning state chancery court, state trial court, and the federal bankruptcy and appellate systems simultaneously — when the party on the other side of the plan also holds a governance or partnership stake in the company. It is also a reminder that a global settlement rarely closes every open issue between litigants who are tied together through multiple, overlapping legal instruments: a partnership agreement, a set of management agreements, and a bankruptcy estate can each spawn their own independent appellate track, on their own timeline, even after the parties believe they have put the larger dispute behind them.

**Practical takeaways:**

- A poison pill adopted against a counterparty that also holds a limited-partner or other governance stake can trigger breach-of-contract and fiduciary-duty claims under the partnership agreement itself, not just conventional corporate-law challenges to the rights plan.
- Multi-forum disputes between affiliated or formerly affiliated real estate entities — spanning Delaware chancery litigation, state trial court, and federal bankruptcy proceedings — often do not resolve on a single timeline; a global settlement in one forum may leave a distinct, fully-litigated issue alive in another.
- Boards and counsel should track the full appellate chain, bankruptcy court through district court through circuit court, independently of any settlement reached elsewhere in the dispute, since a settlement's release language may not reach every claim already on appeal.
- A denial of rehearing en banc does not necessarily end a case; parties retain the option to petition for certiorari, so counsel should continue to monitor docket activity even after an unfavorable circuit ruling becomes final at that level.
