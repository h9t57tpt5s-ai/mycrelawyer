# Stay Lifted in $45.5 Million Mechanics' Lien Fight Over Monarch Casino's Black Hawk Expansion

### A Colorado judge has cleared the way for PCL Construction's long-dormant lien foreclosure against Monarch Casino & Resort to move again, months after a sister lawsuit produced a $74.6 million verdict against the casino operator over the same expansion project.

Monarch Casino & Resort, Inc. (NASDAQ: MCRI) disclosed in its quarterly report filed October 30, 2026, that the judge presiding over a long-running mechanics' lien foreclosure action in Gilpin County, Colorado, lifted a litigation stay effective September 16, 2026, and set a case management conference for January 22, 2027. The order revives a nearly five-year-old dispute between the casino operator and its former general contractor, PCL Construction Services, Inc., over unpaid work on the completed expansion of the Monarch Casino Resort Spa in Black Hawk, Colorado.

The underlying fight traces back to the casino's multi-year expansion project, which added a hotel tower, gaming floor, and event space to the Black Hawk property roughly an hour west of Denver. PCL first sued Monarch Growth Inc. and its Colorado subsidiaries in Denver District Court in August 2019, alleging the company refused to pay for construction services rendered. Monarch answered with counterclaims alleging PCL breached the construction contract, duties of good faith and fair dealing, and express and implied warranties, and separately alleged PCL and its subcontractors made fraudulent or negligent misrepresentations the company relied on. That dispute, now known as the First Denver Lawsuit, proceeded to trial, and in February 2025 a Denver judge awarded PCL and four subcontractors $74.6 million in damages for Monarch's refusal to pay for completed work — one of the larger construction-payment verdicts against a casino operator in recent memory.

Separately, PCL filed a mechanics' lien foreclosure action in March 2021 in the District Court for Gilpin County — the county where the Black Hawk property sits — seeking to foreclose liens against the casino property itself rather than pursue a straight breach-of-contract judgment. That case, captioned PCL Construction Services, Inc. v. Monarch Growth Inc., et al., No. 2021CV30006, named Monarch and its Colorado subsidiaries as defendants, with PCL's own asserted lien amounting to roughly $45.5 million and additional subcontractor liens pushing the total claimed closer to $50 million. The Gilpin case had been stayed, likely pending resolution of the overlapping Denver litigation, until the September 2026 order. Monarch, for its part, filed a third action — the Second Denver Lawsuit, No. 2023CV30458 — against PCL in February 2023, continuing to press its own claims arising from the same expansion.

For commercial property owners and lenders, the case is a reminder that a mechanics' lien foreclosure action and a parallel breach-of-contract suit can run on separate, overlapping tracks for years, with a stay in one case quietly outlasting a judgment in the other. Monarch's 10-Q states the company "is currently unable to determine the probability of the outcome or reasonably estimate the loss or gain, if any" from the newly reactivated Gilpin lien case, even though a related judgment already running into the tens of millions of dollars has been entered against the company in a companion proceeding over the identical construction work. That disclosure underscores how a lien foreclosure against the real property itself remains a live threat to an owner's title and operations even after a money judgment has been entered in a parallel suit, since a lien claimant can pursue both remedies until one is formally resolved or released.

The case also illustrates the layered risk construction lien law creates for large hospitality and gaming developments. A general contractor's mechanics' lien attaches directly to the real property, giving the contractor leverage independent of whatever happens in a contract-damages lawsuit — and in Colorado, as in most states, a stayed lien case can be revived years later at a lien claimant's request, as happened here when "one of the lien claimants" moved to lift the stay.

**Practical takeaways:**
- Owners of large hospitality, gaming, and mixed-use developments should track mechanics' lien foreclosure actions separately from any parallel breach-of-contract litigation over the same project, since a stay in one case does not resolve exposure in the other.
- A money judgment in a companion contract case does not extinguish a contractor's or subcontractor's separate lien rights against the property itself; owners should budget for the possibility of both a damages award and a lien foreclosure arising from the same dispute.
- Expansion and renovation projects at operating hospitality properties carry lien exposure that can resurface years after substantial completion, particularly where litigation has been dormant under a stay rather than dismissed or settled.
