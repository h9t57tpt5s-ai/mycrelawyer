# Alexandria Real Estate Fights to Keep Its Option on a Manhattan Life-Science Expansion Parcel From New York City

### A $185.9 million pre-construction bet on an adjacent lot at Alexandria's East River Science Park megacampus has turned into a two-front legal fight with two New York City agencies

Alexandria Real Estate Equities, Inc., the nation's largest publicly traded owner of life-science laboratory space, disclosed in its third-quarter 2026 SEC filings that it remains locked in litigation with two New York City agencies over its right to incorporate an adjacent land parcel into its flagship Manhattan life-science campus — a dispute that has escalated from a 2024 breach-of-contract suit into a 2026 emergency bid to stop the city from terminating the underlying lease altogether.

**The underlying option.** ARE-East River Science Park, LLC, a subsidiary of Alexandria Real Estate Equities (NYSE: ARE), holds an option granted in 2006 to incorporate a parcel of land adjacent to and north of the Alexandria Center for Life Science – New York City campus (the "Option Parcel") into its existing ground lease, which would clear the way for an additional life-science building on the site. According to Alexandria's quarterly report filed October 26, 2026, the company's investment in pre-construction costs tied to the Option Parcel had reached $185.9 million as of September 30, 2026 — a substantial sunk cost riding on the option's continued validity.

**The federal suit.** On August 6, 2024, ARE sued the New York City Health + Hospitals Corporation ("H+H") and the New York City Economic Development Corporation ("EDC") in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, challenging the agencies' handling of the ground lease and option arrangements governing the parcel. ARE amended its complaint on January 24, 2025, adding claims for fraudulent inducement, breach of contract, breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, and a request for a declaratory judgment confirming the option remains valid.

**The default notice and the state-court injunction fight.** The dispute sharpened in 2026 when the city agencies issued a notice of default, asserting that a failure to cure the alleged defaults within a 30-day cure period would trigger escalating daily charges — starting at $1,000 a day and rising to $2,000 a day. Alexandria disputes the notice's underlying allegations and says it intends to defend against them vigorously. Rather than wait out the cure period in the federal case, ARE went to the Commercial Division of the New York State Supreme Court on May 12, 2026, filing a second action against H+H and EDC seeking a Yellowstone injunction — a New York-specific procedural device that tolls a commercial tenant's cure period and preserves the status quo — along with a preliminary injunction and temporary restraining order to stop the agencies from issuing a notice of termination while the parties' underlying rights are litigated.

**Why it matters for CRE owners and public-private ground leases.** The case is a timely study in the particular risks of long-horizon options and ground leases involving government counterparties. Alexandria's $185.9 million pre-construction exposure shows how far a sophisticated developer can commit capital toward a future building before the underlying right to build is finally resolved — and how quickly a government landlord's default notice can convert a contract dispute into an existential threat to a years-long capital plan. The Yellowstone injunction mechanism at the center of the state court fight is itself a tool every commercial tenant facing a landlord's default notice in New York should know: filed promptly, it can freeze cure-period deadlines and prevent a landlord from terminating the underlying lease or option while the merits are decided, buying the tenant time it would otherwise lose simply by litigating the contract claims on a slower track. For life-science and lab-space landlords and tenants specifically, the fight over this Manhattan megacampus parcel is also a reminder that option and expansion rights tied to a single, named campus can carry outsized stakes relative to their modest contractual footprint, since years of pre-construction spending can be riding on a single word in a decades-old ground lease.

**Practical takeaways:** Commercial tenants holding options or expansion rights under a ground lease with a government or institutional landlord should track default-notice cure periods closely and know, before a dispute arises, whether a Yellowstone injunction or similar device is available in their jurisdiction to freeze those deadlines while litigation proceeds. Owners contemplating significant pre-construction spending on an option-dependent expansion should weigh how much capital to commit before the underlying right is judicially confirmed, since a dispute over the option's validity can arise years after it was granted. And both sides of a long-term ground lease with layered option and development rights should build in clear, unambiguous default and cure mechanics up front — the kind of ambiguity that invites a fraudulent-inducement claim on one side and an emergency injunction fight on the other is exactly what years of litigation cost the most to resolve.
