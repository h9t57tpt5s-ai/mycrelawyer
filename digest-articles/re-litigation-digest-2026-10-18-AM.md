# Loan Buyer Sues to Foreclose on Stalled Penn Plaza Life-Sciences Site

### Quinlan Development Group says it is owed $65.9 million on a defaulted $54 million mortgage after buying the debt from Bank of China, putting a long-dormant Manhattan lab-and-office project on a path to a forced sale.

Quinlan Development Group has sued to foreclose on the leasehold interest behind a stalled life-sciences development near Penn Station, alleging the property's owner defaulted on a $54 million mortgage that has since ballooned to $65.9 million in claimed debt. The suit, filed October 13, 2026, in the Supreme Court of the State of New York, New York County, targets Maple 1 Farley Owner LLC, an affiliate of Cannon Hill Capital Partners that controls the leasehold at 320 West 31st Street, and names Cannon Hill itself as a defendant on a recourse guaranty. The case is a reminder that a secondary buyer of distressed commercial debt can move just as aggressively as an original lender once a stalled project's maturity date has passed.

**What happened**

According to the complaint and reporting on the filing, Cannon Hill Capital Partners planned to redevelop the site — previously home to part of Touro College's campus — into an 11-story building combining laboratory and office space. The project stalled before construction materialized, and the building has sat largely unchanged for well over a year. Quinlan Development Group acquired the underlying mortgage from Bank of China in September 2026 and, within weeks of taking over the loan, filed suit alleging the borrower had defaulted on the $54 million note.

The complaint puts the current amount owed at $65.9 million, a figure that reflects the original principal plus accrued interest, default interest, and additional charges that have accumulated since the maturity default. Quinlan is asking the court for permission to foreclose on Maple 1 Farley Owner LLC's leasehold interest and to schedule a foreclosure auction to satisfy the debt.

**The guaranty angle**

Beyond the foreclosure claim against the leasehold itself, Quinlan named Cannon Hill Capital Partners as a defendant because the development firm serves as a recourse guarantor on the mortgage. That structure matters: if a foreclosure sale of the leasehold does not generate enough proceeds to cover the full $65.9 million claimed, Quinlan has reserved the right to pursue Cannon Hill directly for any shortfall, rather than being limited to whatever the collateral itself brings at auction.

**Why it matters**

The case illustrates a pattern that has become increasingly common as distressed commercial mortgages change hands: a loan originated by a large bank, sold at a discount to a buyer with experience converting defaulted paper into ownership or settlement leverage, followed quickly by enforcement action. For borrowers and sponsors sitting on stalled or underperforming projects, a loan sale to an unfamiliar counterparty is often the clearest signal that the new holder intends to move toward foreclosure rather than negotiate an extension. The recourse guaranty also underscores why many sponsors' personal or corporate exposure on a commercial construction loan does not end merely because the project itself has stalled — a guarantor can remain on the hook for the full remaining balance regardless of what a foreclosure sale ultimately recovers.

**Practical takeaways:** Borrowers and sponsors on stalled construction or development loans should treat any sale of their loan to a new, especially unfamiliar, buyer as an early warning sign that enforcement is likely to follow quickly rather than a routine administrative change; guarantors should understand that a recourse guaranty survives a foreclosure sale and can expose them to any deficiency between sale proceeds and the full debt, including accrued default interest and fees; and development teams holding entitlements or partially built sites with no active construction should revisit loan covenants and maturity dates well before a default crystallizes, since the gap between an unpaid maturity date and a foreclosure filing can close faster once a loan has traded hands.
