How damages are usually measured

Defect claims are usually measured by the reasonable cost to repair, sometimes limited by the loss in the property's value. Who ends up paying depends on the contract chain: indemnity clauses (which many states' anti-indemnity statutes limit), which contractor's or subcontractor's work caused the defect, whether the general liability insurer covers it, and statutes of limitation and repose that run from substantial completion.

General background, not legal advice; the rules vary by state and by the documents in your deal.

What the calculator asks about

These are the facts it pulls from your description or documents for a commercial construction defect claim:

  • Project state
  • Is a defect alleged against the general contractor?
  • Estimated repair cost
  • Is this a catastrophic/structural/life-safety failure?
  • Is a design error alleged against the architect/engineer?
  • Are there multiple responsible parties with an indemnity clause?
  • does the indemnity clause purport to cover the indemnitee's OWN negligence (sole or concurrent), not just the indemnitor's own fault?
  • Is the defect traced to a single, identifiable subcontractor's workmanship (rather than shared among the GC and multiple trades)?
  • Has the owner/association already settled with and released the contractor(s) after an insurer already paid out for the defect?
  • Has a CGL insurer denied or disputed coverage?
