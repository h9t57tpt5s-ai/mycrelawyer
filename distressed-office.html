Distressed Office Litigation & the CMBS Maturity Wall

More than $100 billion of CMBS loans mature in 2026, over half of them expected to default, and office delinquency has hit a record above 12%. The workouts, foreclosures, and conversions this forces are the biggest single driver of CRE litigation this year.

The 2026 CMBS Maturity Wall

Roughly $930 billion in CRE debt matures in 2026. Hard CMBS maturities alone are pegged at $76.6 billion, with 39% concentrated in the fourth quarter — and office delinquency has climbed past 12%, a record.

The End of 'Extend and Pretend'

Years of loan extensions pushed the problem forward rather than solving it. Lenders are now losing patience — the office workout path is more likely than any other property type to end in a discounted payoff or foreclosure rather than a further extension.

Foreclosure Filings Rising

Trustees are moving. One 2026 suit sought foreclosure and a receiver over a $130 million New Jersey office park loan that matured January 1, 2026; another CMBS trust filed suit in the Southern District of New York in July 2026 over a separate defaulted office loan.

Receivership & Special Servicing Disputes

As lenders move faster to protect collateral value, borrowers are increasingly contesting the appointment of receivers and the terms under which special servicers are managing distressed loans.

Office-to-Residential Conversion Zoning Fights

Distressed office owners chasing conversion as an exit strategy are running into zoning and entitlement pushback in many markets, adding a land-use litigation layer on top of the underlying loan distress.
