First Care Therapy, LLC v. Congress Ave Boynton, LLC (Commercial Lease Default Damages Reversed)

A Florida appeals court affirmed a landlord's default judgment against a medical-office tenant and guarantor but reversed its $85,782.80 damages award for lack of a properly noticed trial.

Florida's Fourth District Court of Appeal affirmed a Palm Beach County circuit court's default judgment holding tenant First Care Therapy, LLC and guarantor Loodjhinssy Lafaille-Paul liable for breaching a commercial lease and guaranty on medical office space in Boynton Beach, but reversed the $85,782.80 damages award, finding the trial court awarded unliquidated damages without the properly noticed damages trial Florida law requires. The case returns to the circuit court for a new trial limited to damages.

$85,782.80 damages award reversed and remanded for a properly noticed damages trial
Nov 12, 2026 District Court of Appeal of Florida, Fourth District

Atwood v. Lamar Obie Co., LLC (Portland Wall-Sign Lease, Good Faith and Fair Dealing)

Oregon's Court of Appeals affirmed a $1,237,191.92 verdict against billboard company Lamar Obie Co. for breaching the implied duty of good faith by keeping an ODOT advertising permit from the Portland building owner who leased it the wall.

The Oregon Court of Appeals affirmed, on November 12, 2026, a $1,237,191.92 jury verdict for Portland building owner Jim A. Atwood against billboard company Lamar Obie Company, LLC for breach of the implied duty of good faith and fair dealing. The dispute arose after an ODOT advertising permit tied to Atwood's leased exterior wall sign ended up solely in the name of Lamar's predecessor and then Lamar itself, and Lamar surrendered the permit to the state for a relocation credit it could not use rather than transferring it to Atwood when the lease ended.

$1,237,191.92 jury verdict, affirmed on appeal
Nov 12, 2026 Court of Appeals of the State of Oregon
Zoning & Land Use Verified Update On Appeal

Bluetriton Brands, Inc. v. Washington Township Zoning Hearing Board and Weis Markets, Inc. (Pennsylvania Court Splits on Shopping Center Gas Station Fight)

Pennsylvania's Commonwealth Court reversed a trial court's site-plan ruling but remanded Bluetriton Brands' groundwater-contamination challenge to Weis Markets' proposed Bangor Plaza gas station, leaving the special exception unresolved.

The Commonwealth Court of Pennsylvania reversed a Northampton County trial court's holding that Weis Markets needed to submit a site plan before adding a fuel station to its Bangor Plaza supermarket, but remanded the case because the trial court never addressed all of objector Bluetriton Brands' arguments against the Zoning Hearing Board's grant of a special exception for the project. Bluetriton, which operates a spring-water extraction business on parcels adjoining and downstream of the site, presented hydrogeology and petroleum-storage expert testimony that the fuel station could contaminate the aquifer feeding its Deer Park brand's Greenwaltz Spring source.

No dollar figure stated; dispute over adding a fuel station to a 20.3-acre shopping center
Nov 10, 2026 Commonwealth Court of Pennsylvania
