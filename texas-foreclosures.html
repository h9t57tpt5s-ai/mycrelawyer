Texas Foreclosure Sales

Texas commercial properties posted for foreclosure sale.

Most Texas foreclosures never reach a court. The lender's trustee posts a notice with the county clerk at least 21 days before the sale, which is held on the first Tuesday of the month. CREdocket reads the notices that counties publish online and keeps only the properties the county appraisal district itself classifies as commercial, industrial or apartments, so houses and home loans are left out. A posted sale can still be postponed or called off. For loans in bond trusts, see CMBS Distress Watch; for lawsuits, State Court Watch.

A notice whose legal description cannot be read is matched only when the business it names as borrower is the parcel's owner on the appraisal roll. A notice is listed only when its parcel is classified commercial, industrial, apartment or commercial land, and notices carrying home-loan markers (FHA, VA, MERS, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and similar) are dropped. Owners and lenders are named only when they are businesses; individuals, trustees and law firms are never shown. Counties whose notices sit on sites that bar automated access (Dallas since February 2026, Tarrant, Denton, Travis, Montgomery, Galveston, Brazoria, Hays and others) are not read. A posted notice is not a completed sale. Not legal or investment advice.
