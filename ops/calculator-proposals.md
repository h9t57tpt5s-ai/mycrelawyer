# Case Value Calculator — proposed changes (NEEDS JEFF)

This file is written by the self-improvement routine per
ops/improvement-playbook.md: the valuation method itself (prompt, math,
rules in `supabase/functions/case-valuation-analyze/`) changes only with
Jeff's approval and can't be deployed from a cloud session. Everything
below is evidence and a proposal, not a change. Nothing here has been
applied to any code.

## 2026-10-15 — why v4.3 misses the final-outcome range 47% of the time

**Method:** replayed and inspected the 32 held-and-training backtest cases
in `case_valuation_project/backtest/results-v4.3/` that have a scorable
final outcome, using the already-published scoring in
`js/backtest-results-data.js` (`versions["v4.3"]`, generated by
`scripts/score_backtest.py`). No new API calls, no money spent — this is
all free replay of saved runs, as the playbook requires. 15 of 32 missed
(finalHits 17/32 = 53.1%, matching `ops/metrics/goals.json`). Of the 15
misses, 11 had the predicted range entirely **above** the actual outcome
(overshoot), 3 entirely **below** (undershoot), and 1
(`architectural-stainless-v-tm-partridge-creek-mall-2021`) was a complete
loss — actual $0 — against a predicted range that never includes zero.

### Finding 1 (the dominant pattern, 6+ of the 11 overshoots): a one-sided party figure gets priced as if it were the likely award, with no issue modeling the legal theory that actually capped recovery

In each of these cases a true, well-sourced dollar figure — a loan's face
amount, a punitive-damages prayer, a demand letter's full ask, a second
rent claim — gets attached to a `favorable` (0.6–0.75) or even `conceded`
(0.95–0.99) probability band and becomes (or dominates) the predicted
ceiling, while the actual outcome shows that figure was never the real
measure of damages once a specific defense, abandoned count, setoff, or
damages theory applied:

- `donegal-v-christie-scott-2020`: a $5,000,000 punitive-damages count
  priced `favorable` (0.6–0.75) sets the predicted ceiling at $5M. The
  outcome description says the tenant "abandoned all counts but
  conversion" — the punitive count was never decided on the merits at
  all. Actual net result: **-$43,304.91** (the landlord owed the tenant
  money back). Predicted range $3,013,337–$5,000,000. Off by ~80x.
- `can-iv-packard-square-v-schubiner-2021`: the loan's face amount,
  "nearly $54 million," priced `favorable` (0.6–0.75), sets the ceiling at
  $54M, with only two liens (~$916K, ~$331K) and two small offsetting
  issues subtracted. Actual judgment: **$13,992,936.05** — the guaranty
  claim amount after the theories and credits that actually governed,
  not the loan's original principal. Predicted range
  $31,750,192–$54,000,000, ~1.6x over at the low end.
- `wheeler-reit-khoshaba-2024`: the $25M note purchase and a $7M board
  investment (both true, sourced facts about capital raised) priced
  `favorable`/`even`, producing a $19.9M–$34.8M range. Actual: a
  **$7,125,000 settlement** — in a securities case, the capital a
  defendant raised is not the damages measure (typically price decline
  or restitution), and nothing in the priced issues modeled that
  distinction.
- `1700-market-v-common-grounds-2024`: two large unpaid-rent claims
  ($2,487,630.24 and $783,504.15, covering different and possibly
  overlapping periods) both priced `conceded` (0.95–0.99) and summed,
  producing a $3.12M–$3.24M range. The outcome: the court found the
  tenant "had been evicted (not abandoned)," which under the applicable
  rent-acceleration rule caps recovery to rent **through repossession**,
  not the full claimed period — actual **$223,000**, ~14x under the
  predicted floor. The model priced the claimed total with near-certainty
  without modeling how the evicted-vs-abandoned finding caps it.

**Common thread:** a `conceded`/`favorable`/`strong` band is being
assigned to the *fact that a party asked for or is owed a certain
amount*, not to the probability that *amount survives as damages under
the applicable legal theory*. A high-confidence band on an uncontested
fact (the loan was $54M; the tenant was billed $783,504.15; the complaint
sought $5M in punitives) is being read by the current prompt/math as a
high-confidence band on the *award*, which is a different question.

**Proposed change (NEEDS JEFF):** in the calculator's system prompt
(`supabase/functions/case-valuation-analyze/index.ts`), make explicit
that a `conceded`/`strong`/`favorable` probability band must attach to
the probability the stated figure is actually *awarded as damages under
the theory that governs*, not to whether the figure itself is
undisputed/sourced. Concretely: before pricing an issue derived from a
party's own demand, loan principal, or a damages count, the model should
check the record for (a) whether that count/theory was abandoned,
dismissed, or superseded by a different theory, and (b) whether any
accrual/offset/cap rule stated in the record (eviction vs. abandonment,
a payment already credited, a statutory cap) would reduce it, and lower
the band or add an explicit offsetting issue when so. This is a prompt
change, not a math change, and needs Jeff's review before any live
deploy.

### Finding 2 (eminent domain and other dueling-valuation disputes, 2 of 3 eminent-domain misses): when the record contains two experts' competing numbers but only one side's figure gets quoted into `figures`, the model falls back to one-sided pricing instead of the existing "competing figures" basis

v4.3 already has a `competing` basis (`issue_range()` in
`valuation-math.ts`/mirrored in `scripts/replay_backtest.py`): when an
issue's figures include genuinely competing numbers, the range runs from
the low figure to the high figure instead of summing. This works well
when the model actually extracts both sides' figures into one issue —
e.g. `hrt-enterprises-v-detroit-2025` (owner's $3–5M vs. the city's
$1.5–3.19M appraisals, all five figures on one issue, actual $1,976,820
lands inside) and `state-v-skyway-holdings-2021` (both the commissioners'
$74,429 and the owner's $508,548–$1M ask on one issue, actual $508,548
hits exactly).

But in the two eminent-domain misses below, only the property owner's own
number is ever quoted as a `figure` — the condemning authority's
appraisal never appears, so the issue is priced as `itemized`/`conceded`
using only the owner's figures, and the ceiling anchors to the owner's
own ask with no downward counterweight:

- `kks-properties-slingerlands-bypass-2020`: all figures on the dominant
  issue are KKS's own appraiser's numbers ($2,224,065 / $2,223,000 /
  $92,000-per-acre / $45,000-per-acre). The outcome says the court
  "credited the State's appraiser ... $52,339 per acre" — that number
  never appears anywhere in the predicted issues. Predicted range
  $1,128,559–$3,928,945 vs. actual **$744,500**.
- `elpa-builders-v-state-ny-2021`: figures are the state's advance
  payment ($302,460, not an appraisal) and the owner's own $2,000,000
  claim; no appraisal figure for either side's "before-taking value"
  appears. Predicted range $287,337–$2,000,000 vs. actual **$283,920**
  (the low end is close only because the advance payment happens to be
  close to the true award, not because the model modeled the valuation
  dispute).

**Proposed change (NEEDS JEFF):** in just-compensation / dueling-valuation
categories (eminent-domain here, but the same dynamic likely applies to
any "what is this property worth" dispute), instruct the model to
actively search the record for *both* sides' appraisal or valuation
figures before pricing the issue, and to flag explicitly ("opposing
valuation figure not found in record") when it can only find one side's
number, rather than silently pricing the issue as if only one appraisal
exists. Whether the gap is extraction failure or genuinely absent from
the input excerpt (the backtest excerpts strip "sentences stating the
trial court's award, its findings" per `cases.json`'s `excerptNote`,
which can also remove a competing figure the court's opinion was
summarizing) would need checking against the actual source opinions,
which this routine can't re-fetch from this environment (no live web
access besides WebSearch) — a local/Jeff session re-reading
`elpa-builders-v-state-ny-2021` and `kks-properties-slingerlands-bypass-2020`'s
source opinions against the stored excerpt would settle which it is.

### Finding 3 (smaller, mechanical): summing every issue's probability-weighted floor, including immaterial ones, can flip an exact hit into a miss by a tiny margin

Two near-misses are off by a trivial amount because a small secondary
issue's weighted contribution pushes the aggregate floor just past the
actual figure, even though the dominant issue alone would have hit
exactly:

- `lcra-st-louis-v-osher-2020`: the dominant issue already correctly
  spans both appraisals ($573,000–$4,930,000, `competing` basis) and
  actual is exactly the low figure, $573,000. But a second issue — a
  disputed $65,000 AT&T setoff, priced `even` (0.45–0.55) — adds
  0.45×$65,000 = $29,250 to the summed floor, moving it from $573,000 to
  $573,600 and excluding the actual-exact-match value by $600.
- `holcomb-dunbar-v-mad-hatter-2022`: predicted ceiling $130,000 vs.
  actual $133,900 ("the full amount claimed in the motion" — twenty
  months' rent plus late fees); the late fees were apparently never
  priced as their own figure, so the ceiling undershoots by $3,900 (3%).

**Proposed change (NEEDS JEFF):** not a change to the "no holistic
number" sum-of-issues rule itself (Jeff's instruction that the top line
must be the mechanical sum stands) — but consider whether a dominant
issue whose own figures already bracket the full claimed amount should
suppress double-counting from clearly subsidiary/ancillary issues (small
setoffs, late fees, costs) when those are a sub-component already
implicit in the dominant figure, versus a genuinely separate exposure.
This needs a math/prompt-design decision, not something to guess at from
two cases.

### Finding 4 (undershoots, 2 of 3): multi-claim or multi-count cases where not every winning theory gets its own priced issue

- `huntington-bank-v-limited-investment-group-2022`: outcome is judgment
  "in all four actions" (four consolidated note/guaranty cases); the
  predicted issues appear to price roughly one note's worth. Predicted
  ceiling $671,351.84 vs. actual **$1,374,413.88** (~2x under).
- `stark-v-mccarthy-corp-2021`: the court dismissed the opposing claim
  entirely and awarded Stark on his own counterclaim ($202,090.81
  completion cost less a credit); the predicted range is anchored mostly
  to the opposing (losing) claim's probability-weighted exposure, landing
  mostly negative ($-40,648 to $7,597) against an actual clean win of
  **$85,861.29** for the represented side.

**Proposed change (NEEDS JEFF):** when a record states a judgment "in all
N actions" or similar language signaling multiple consolidated claims,
instruct the model explicitly to enumerate and price each one rather than
the single largest; and when one side's claim is dismissed outright,
check that a comparably-weighted issue exists for the side that actually
won on its own theory, not just a negative-exposure issue for the side
that lost.

## 2026-10-17 — critical analysis for Jeff: liability is calibrated, damages are not

Free analysis of the 33 v4.3 cases with a final outcome (no API spend).
Issue-level outcomes were coded from each opinion into
`case_valuation_project/backtest/issue-coding-v4.3.json` (218 predicted
issues, 22 missing ones).

**Case level.** The model put the right side ahead in 27/33 (82%). Range
hits 17/33; even the widest honest envelope (opposing claims' full value to
the represented side's full ceiling) holds only 22/33, so 11 outcomes sit
outside every figure the model priced. Best guess: median ratio to actual
1.00 (centered) but only 7/33 within 25%. Where the winner was right, the
if-win ceiling ran a median 1.8x the award.

**Issue level (liability won or partial / won in full):** conceded 93% / 67%
(band 95-99%); strong 95% / 90% (80-90%); favorable 75% / 67% (60-75%);
even 39% / 23% (45-55%); unfavorable 33% / 17% (25-40%); weak 13% / 13%
(5-20%). The bands are calibrated on LIABILITY. Losses come from (1) partial
recoveries on claims won (conceded issues won in full only 67%), (2) the
opposing side's claims rated too high (opposing 'even' won 21%, 'favorable'
40%), (3) 22 issues never listed (Williams Alaska had none priced; Jack and
CVS turned on jury damages absent from the input).

**Recalibrating the bands alone barely helps:** replaying v4.3 with bands set
to the observed rates (opposing claims separately) moved final range hits
19/32 -> 20/32 and best guess within 25% 7/30 -> 9/30 (replay excludes fees,
hence 19 vs the published 17). Not adopted: too little gain to justify fitting
33 cases. The levers are the damages measure and issue completeness (v4.5
instructions, then a rule-based damages engine in code: interest to judgment,
collateral/fair-value credits, rent through repossession, guaranty caps,
partial-taking measure), scenario outputs scored for calibration, and asking
for missing deciding inputs instead of pricing without them.

**On a 95% target:** 95% of verdicts inside a narrow range is not a realistic
standard (tried and appealed cases are selected for being hard to predict;
verdicts vary on the same facts). 95% is attainable for a calibrated 95%
interval, for damages once liability is fixed, and is the right aim for
settlement acceptance measured against real settlements rather than
verdicts.

## 2026-10-17 — v4.5 candidate, first run (47 cases, $9.59)

Same cases as v4.3, run through the deployed function with promptVariant
v4.5 (results in case_valuation_project/backtest/results-v4.5/; 44 analyzed,
2 refused for a wrong-side classification, 1 unclassified). On the 37 cases
with a final outcome scored both ways: published range holds the final
outcome 17 -> 23 (46% -> 62%); right side ahead 27 -> 30; no estimate 6 -> 4;
best guess within 25% 7 -> 8; held-out cases 2/8 -> 4/8. Newly right:
1700 Market (eviction cut-off), KKS (State appraisal, partial-taking
measure), Williams Alaska, Elpa, LCRA v. Osher, Holcomb Dunbar. Newly wrong:

once ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is on the Mac) must report run-to-run spread first,
and fresh cases (offer-of-judgment and condemnation sourcing) are the real
out-of-sample test. Live stays on v4.4.
