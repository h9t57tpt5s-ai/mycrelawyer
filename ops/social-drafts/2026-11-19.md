## 1. SL Green's special servicer moves to foreclose a $399M loan on an Amazon-anchored Times Square tower

A division of SL Green, acting as special servicer of a $399 million CMBS loan, filed a
foreclosure suit in Manhattan Supreme Court over 1440 Broadway the week of November 9,
2026. StepStone has owned the building since a 2023 loan modification; the prior owners
(CIM Group and QSuper) bought it in 2017. The ownership allegedly defaulted on the
mortgage about two years ago — and the suit landed a day after Morningstar DBRS flagged
concerns about Amazon's 2029 lease expiration.

A reminder that a single-tenant lease-expiration risk can turn a loan-servicing concern
into a foreclosure filing well before the lease itself is actually up.

## 2. Residents ask a Pennsylvania judge to stop a $15 billion data center — no ruling yet

A Cumberland County judge heard arguments on November 13, 2026 on a challenge aimed at
stopping the PAX-1 data center development in Middlesex Township. Stop PAX-1, a group of
residents opposed to the $15 billion project, asked for a stay on the approved phases and
on future approvals; the developer's attorney moved to dismiss. The judge did not rule
after the arguments.

One of the largest dollar figures in any data-center zoning fight CREdocket has tracked —
worth watching for how a Pennsylvania court handles a stay request at this scale.

Full write-up: https://credocket.com/matters/live-434.html

## 3. Investors seek at least $1.9 billion to block a REIT's industrial portfolio deal

Sachem Capital Corp. disclosed in a quarterly report dated November 16, 2026 that a group
of investors claiming interests in IRG Master Holdings, LLC sued the company and certain
affiliates in Los Angeles County Superior Court on November 3, 2026, estimating
compensatory damages at not less than $1.9 billion and asking the court to block or undo
Sachem's pending property-contribution transaction with Industrial Realty Group Global,
LLC. Sachem said it had not been served as of the report date and intends to vigorously
defend itself.

A reminder that a pending portfolio contribution can draw a nine-figure-plus challenge
from investors in the counterparty entity, not just from the REIT's own shareholders.

## 4. A Las Vegas golf-course tax fight heads to the Nevada Supreme Court

Clark County commissioners voted on October 20, 2026 to appeal a district court judgment
over property taxes collected on the former Badlands Golf Course in Las Vegas. A Clark
County judge had granted summary judgment against the county in February, ruling that two
companies belonging to developer Yohan Lowie were entitled to nearly $12.6 million plus
daily interest of over $3,000; the county filed its notice of appeal to the Nevada Supreme
Court the week of October 29.

Daily interest of $3,000+ on an unpaid tax judgment is its own lesson in why a
jurisdiction's appeal clock matters as much as the underlying merits.

Full write-up: https://credocket.com/matters/live-425.html

## 5. Texas's new Business Court keeps a $45M affordable-housing construction suit — on a jurisdictional technicality

On November 17, 2026, the Business Court of Texas, Fourth Division, denied San Antonio
Housing Facility Corporation's motion to dismiss a declaratory-judgment suit by Cadence
McShane Construction Company LLC over the Horizon Pointe affordable-housing project. The
housing corporation argued the suit fell short of the court's $5 million jurisdictional
minimum, pointing to its own separate Bexar County suit seeking $4,999,999.99 — one cent
under the threshold. The court held Cadence's own petition controlled because the housing
corporation offered no evidence of the total value of the rights at issue.

A sharp illustration of how a single cent in a competing pleading's damages figure can
become the whole jurisdictional argument in Texas's new specialized business court.
