## 1. "Pretty damn egregious" — Freddie Mac v. Nord Group (Lake Jackson, TX)

A federal magistrate judge didn't hold back: it was "pretty damn egregious" that a
Nord Group affiliate kept collecting rent on Edgewater Apartments, a 228-unit complex
outside Houston — after Freddie Mac had already foreclosed on it — and then sued its
own lender instead of turning over the money.

Trade press reporting describes a broader pattern across Nord Group's multifamily
portfolio: lenders and investors accusing Nord entities of diverting rents, submitting
falsified insurance records, and letting buildings deteriorate, with filings describing
sewage backups, mold and vermin at some properties.

For lenders and special servicers sitting on 2021-2022 vintage multifamily loans: a
borrower that keeps controlling cash flow after foreclosure, and then goes on offense
against you, is a receivership-and-audit trigger, not just a collections problem.

## 2. A film studio sues a county over a data-center moratorium (DeKalb County, GA)

Shadowbox Studios — the Atlanta-area production company behind films including
Cocaine Bear — and an affiliate sued DeKalb County, Georgia, alleging its new
moratorium on data-center applications unlawfully blocks a project on their roughly
200-acre site, after planning staff had already told the company the use was
permitted there. The suit cites more than $30 million invested and alleges the
moratorium cut the property's value by as much as $200 million.

As moratoria on new data centers spread nationally, this is a live test of whether a
developer can turn a municipality's own staff representations into a takings claim
once a freeze lands on top of them.

## 3. A "Good Guy Guaranty" lesson from a SoHo retail landlord's loss

A SoHo landlord went after the individual guarantors of a Broome Street retail lease
for roughly $307,577.08 in unpaid rent — and lost, because the tenant had physically
vacated back in 2020, and New York's top guaranty case law says that's when a "good
guy" guarantor's liability ends, regardless of a separate lease clause tying it to a
later, formally defined "Surrender Date."

Good guy guaranties are everywhere in NYC retail leasing. This decision is a clean
reminder to reconcile any multi-layered "Surrender Date" language with the
vacatur-based release clause before the lease is signed — not after the tenant has
already moved out.

Full write-up: https://credocket.com/matters/live-311.html

## 4. An appeal just paused a $10.4M foreclosure auction — three days out

Rochester's century-old Hungerford Building was headed to a foreclosure auction on
October 29. Days before the sale, the owner's attorney filed an appeal challenging the
referee's report behind the judgment — and the sale is now stayed. The amount owed has
grown to $10,490,036.37 on an original 2022 loan of just $8.25 million, after default
interest, fees and a forbearance period that didn't hold.

A confirmed referee's report and a calendared auction date aren't self-executing in
New York. Lenders and servicers working out legacy mixed-use loans should build
appellate delay into the timeline, not just the payoff math.
