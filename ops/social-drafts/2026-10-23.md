## 1. A consultant's own contamination report wasn't enough to win summary judgment

A $12.2 million groundwater contamination judgment just evaporated on appeal. A Los
Angeles property owner had won summary adjudication against the current and prior
owners of a neighboring industrial site over a 1,4-dioxane plume -- leaning heavily on
the defendants' own 2020 consultant report, which found their site had contributed to
the contamination. California's Second Appellate District reversed across the board:
the consultant's causation findings didn't, by themselves, establish a breached duty of
care, and the damages measure used for the nuisance and trespass claims was the wrong
one for a continuing tort.

For owners of contaminated or formerly industrial sites facing a neighbor's claim: even
an unfavorable internal environmental report doesn't automatically hand the other side
summary judgment. Duty and damages are separate questions a causation finding alone
doesn't answer.

## 2. An appeals court vacated its own ruling three months later -- and reversed itself

Crocker Park, the mixed-use development in Westlake, Ohio, won its case at the Ohio
Court of Appeals in July 2026. Then, on reconsideration, the same panel vacated its own
decision and reversed course on reconsideration: Crocker Park had waived its right of
first refusal over a development parcel by waiting too long to exercise it, even though
no deadline was written into the controlling declaration. The panel left Crocker Park's
win on the City of Westlake's slander-of-title and abuse-of-process counterclaims
intact.

A reminder for anyone holding a right of first refusal or similar option without an
express deadline: courts can and do read in a reasonable-time requirement, and delay
can waive the right. And an appellate win isn't final until the reconsideration window
closes -- this panel revisited its own three-month-old ruling.
