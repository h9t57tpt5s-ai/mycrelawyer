## 1. D.C. collects $9.3 million from two landlords over algorithmic rent software

The District of Columbia's Attorney General announced consent judgments resolving its
claims that JBG Smith and Mid-America Apartment Communities (MAA) used RealPage's
revenue-management software to coordinate apartment rents — part of D.C.'s broader suit
against RealPage and a group of its largest landlord customers. JBG Smith will pay $8.1
million (tied to multiple District properties); MAA will pay $1.2 million (tied to one).
Neither company admitted wrongdoing, and both accepted multi-year restrictions on using
shared-data pricing software going forward.

For any multifamily owner still using a shared-data pricing tool: this is now a second
city (after similar RealPage settlements elsewhere) putting a real price tag on it, with
forward-looking restrictions attached, not just a one-time fine.

## 2. $310 million foreclosure suit hits a Chicago office tower that's 81% leased

A trustee for bondholders on the senior mortgage loan secured by 540 W. Madison Street —
a 1.15-million-square-foot, 81%-leased Chicago office tower — filed a $310 million
foreclosure suit in Cook County after the loan matured unpaid in September. The building
reportedly generates over $18 million in annual net cash flow against roughly $5.3
million in debt service: a maturity default, not a cash-flow default.

A reminder that occupancy and operating performance don't protect a borrower from a
foreclosure suit when a loan simply comes due and can't be refinanced or paid off.

## 3. $3.9 million punitive-damages award survives a fifth appeal

The Tennessee Court of Appeals affirmed an $8.36 million judgment — including $3.9
million in punitive damages — against general contractor The Weitz Company in a fraud
suit brought by its own drywall subcontractor over a Shelby County retirement-community
project. The court rejected Weitz's argument that the contract's waiver of consequential
damages barred a punitive award for intentional misrepresentation; this was the fifth
appeal in the litigation to reach the court.

A contractual damages waiver protects against ordinary breach claims — not against a
jury finding you lied to your own subcontractor.

## 4. A long-vacant Kansas site heads to sheriff's sale after a $46 million judgment

Metropolitan Commercial Bank won a final judgment of more than $46 million against Aryeh
Realty LLC over a defaulted 2019 loan on the long-vacant Mission Gateway site in Mission,
Kansas. After Aryeh missed an October 16 deadline to pay off the judgment, the bank asked
the court to proceed with a sheriff's sale of the property.

One of the larger single-asset land-development defaults tracked here this year — and a
site that's now been vacant long enough to outlast its original loan twice over.
