## 1. FERC blocks ComEd's bid to cancel the power deal behind a $20 billion data center

FERC rejected Commonwealth Edison's September notice seeking to cancel the transmission
security agreement behind PowerHouse Hillwood's roughly $20 billion, 1.8-gigawatt Joliet
Technology Center data center campus near Chicago. ComEd argued Hillwood hadn't properly
satisfied the agreement's initial credit-support term — which Hillwood had met with a $1
letter of credit ComEd says doesn't count. FERC's rejection leaves the underlying contract
dispute to a federal judge in the Northern District of Illinois.

A $20 billion campus riding on a fight over a $1 letter of credit is a reminder that in
data-center power deals, the fine print on credit support can matter as much as the
megawatts.

## 2. Wells Fargo moves to foreclose on a 288-acre former Kodak complex for $96.9 million

Wells Fargo, as CMBS trustee, has moved for summary judgment to foreclose on Rochester
Tech Park — a 288-acre former Eastman Kodak office-and-industrial complex outside
Rochester, New York — alleging the borrower and its guarantors owe roughly $96.9 million
in principal, interest and fees on a 2017 loan, plus about $2.356 million the lender
advanced to keep the property running.

A large single-asset legacy-industrial loan heading toward foreclosure, with the lender's
own advances to protect the collateral now part of the bill.

## 3. A deed in lieu wiped out the lender's own insurance claim, Sixth Circuit holds

The Sixth Circuit affirmed summary judgment against lender Sinmier, LLC in its suit
against the insurers of its defaulted borrower over storm and fire damage at a Sandusky,
Ohio resort — holding that Sinmier's own deed-in-lieu settlement with the borrower had
already extinguished the mortgagee insurable interest the suit depended on. The borrower's
insurer had separately settled the underlying property claim for $1,125,000, and Sinmier
had already recovered $627,375 from the borrower's owner under the foreclosure settlement.

A good reminder for lenders negotiating a deed in lieu: settling with your borrower can
settle away your own standing to chase the insurer next.

## 4. Fifth Circuit: apartment owner's hail-damage suit came years too late

The Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Third Coast Insurance Company against a
San Antonio apartment owner's breach-of-contract and Texas Insurance Code suit over a May
2020 hailstorm claim, holding the 2024 suit was filed after both the policy's own
contractual limitations period and Texas's statutory limitations period had run. The
insurer's damage estimates — about $37,058 and later $61,840 — both fell below the
policy's $359,856.60 deductible either way.

Beyond the merits, this is a clean example of how a late-filed claim can lose on the
clock alone, regardless of who was right about the damage estimate.

## 5. Georgia court upholds a county's industrial permit over a neighboring developer's fight

The Georgia Court of Appeals affirmed Bartow County's grant of a conditional use permit
letting Rice Commercial Investments expand an industrial mulch-processing and
recovered-materials operation next to developer Norhaven Partners' planned 97-unit
townhome community. The court held the county's quasi-judicial decision was supported by
the record and rejected Norhaven's procedural challenges to the permitting process.

A reminder that a neighboring landowner's procedural objections to a county's zoning
process face a steep climb once a quasi-judicial decision is on the record.
