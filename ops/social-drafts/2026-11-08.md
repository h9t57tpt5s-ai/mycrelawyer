## 1. Fractional ownership doesn't buy a tax discount, Colorado's appeals court rules

Colorado's Court of Appeals affirmed Eagle County's roughly $122 million property tax
assessment of the 54 fractionally owned condominium units at the Ritz-Carlton, Bachelor
Gulch, rejecting Marriott subsidiary Ritz-Carlton Development Co.'s argument that
dividing the units into 648 slices of 21 days' use a year entitled it to a valuation
discount versus conventional whole-ownership comparables.

Worth flagging for anyone who owns or finances a branded fractional or private-residence-
club program: assessors now have a published result supporting valuation at or near
whole-ownership comparables, with no automatic fractional-structure discount.

## 2. A CMBS special servicer sues its own building's co-owner to stop next-door demolition

Torchlight, the special servicer that foreclosed on the JW Marriott Chicago on behalf of
CMBS bondholders, sued developer Prime Group in Cook County, alleging unpermitted
demolition and office-to-residential conversion work in the same historic LaSalle Street
building cut the hotel's phone and internet service during a sold-out night. Torchlight
wants the conversion work halted until the hotel's consent is secured.

A preview of the consent and operational-disruption disputes likely to multiply as more
office-to-residential conversions proceed next to live hotel and retail operations in the
same building.

## 3. A credit union inherited this $236M Chicago office loan through a merger — now it's foreclosing

CommunityAmerica Credit Union sued to foreclose a loan on the Leo Burnett Building at 35
West Wacker Drive, now totaling $236 million with interest and fees on an originally
roughly $220 million balance. CommunityAmerica picked up the loan through its merger with
Unify Financial Credit Union; the borrowing entity is an affiliate of investor Shaya
Prager's Opal Holdings, which defaulted.

Notably, the personal guarantor isn't named as a defendant while an involuntary Chapter 7
petition against him remains contested — a reminder that a pending personal bankruptcy
can delay collection against a guarantor even as the lender moves directly against the
collateral and the borrowing LLC.

## 4. Years of paying an incentive doesn't stop a city from later walking away, West Virginia rules

West Virginia's Intermediate Court of Appeals affirmed dismissal of a shopping center
owner's suit demanding the City of Logan resume a 57% business-tax rebate under a 1997
annexation incentive deal — even though the City had kept making the payments for years
before stopping in 2021. The court held the dissolved development authority's statutory
immunity defeated the claim, and years of voluntary compliance didn't estop the City from
later challenging the deal.

A caution for any CRE owner relying on a municipal incentive agreement: a government
counterparty's ongoing performance isn't itself a guarantee the deal will hold up if the
city later decides it wasn't authorized to begin with.

Full write-up: https://credocket.com/matters/live-377.html

## 5. Clearing zoning doesn't guarantee the permit, Illinois appeals court confirms

An Illinois appeals court reinstated Chicago's denial of a large recycling facility permit
for an RMG affiliate on the Southeast Side, reversing a hearing officer's 2023 ruling that
had cleared the project. The decision confirms the city's health department retains
substantive discretion to deny a permit on compliance-history and public-health grounds
even after a facility clears zoning.

A real setback for RMG's years-long push to relocate metal-recycling operations to this
site, and a reminder that zoning approval is necessary but not sufficient for an
industrial use that still needs its operating permit.

Full write-up: https://credocket.com/matters/live-378.html

## 6. A $308.6M jury verdict over a joint venture that was never signed — now funding a Chapter 11 appeal

ZOM Holding, parent of multifamily developer ZOM Living, filed Chapter 11 after an Arizona
jury found it froze former would-be joint-venture partner Gray Development Group out of a
Phoenix multifamily and commercial pipeline, awarding roughly $308.6 million even though
no joint-venture agreement was ever signed. ZOM is using the Chapter 11 filing to fund an
appeal of the judgment while shielding its active development platform.

A reminder that non-circumvention and good-faith obligations can be enforced against a
developer with real money behind them even without a signed JV agreement — and that
Chapter 11 is increasingly a tool to buy appeal time against a judgment, not just a
response to one.
