## 1. A $250M office loan in Jersey City goes to special servicing — occupancy already down to 64%

A $250 million CMBS loan on 101 Hudson Street, a 42-story, 1.4 million-square-foot
office tower in Jersey City, went to special servicing after missing its October 2026
maturity, Commercial Observer reported on Nov. 19, citing a Morningstar Credit alert.
Occupancy has slipped to 64% after an anchor tenant's 2018 move-out, and Merrill Lynch —
still the largest tenant at nearly 30% of the space — has a lease expiring in five
months. The building traded for $346 million in October 2022.

A lease expiring in five months and a loan already in special servicing is the kind of
double deadline that tends to force a decision one way or the other.

## 2. A data center developer must answer a noise class action after a New Jersey judge refuses to dismiss it

A New Jersey Superior Court judge (Cumberland County) denied DataOne's motion to dismiss
a class action over noise from the 300-megawatt data center it's developing in Vineland,
N.J. for the Nebius Group, WHYY reported in an article updated Nov. 19. The suit alleges
DataOne failed to soundproof the engines and cooling systems; plaintiffs' counsel says
roughly 1,000 residents within 1.5 miles could be class-eligible. The defense called it
temporary construction noise and "a mere annoyance" — that argument didn't win the
motion, and DataOne must now file an answer.

Another reminder that the legal risk around data-center buildouts isn't only zoning
approval — it's what happens to neighbors once the cooling systems are running.

## 3. Nevada's Supreme Court orders a fence and QR-code gate system torn off a Las Vegas Strip parking lot

Nevada's Supreme Court affirmed a preliminary injunction requiring Siegel entities to
remove a chain-link fence and QR-code-gated access system they installed on a Strip
parking lot — bought subject to a Parking Easement Agreement benefiting the neighboring
Silver City strip mall and its patrons. The district court granted the injunction back
on July 10, 2024; the Supreme Court's order of affirmance came Nov. 20, 2026. The court
held that buying a lot "fully aware" of crime and homelessness concerns doesn't let the
buyer unilaterally gate around an easement it granted away.

A parking easement is still an easement, even with a fence and a QR code bolted onto it.

## 4. A South Carolina city offers $0 to condemn a landowner's driveway consent rights for a Target project

The City of Easley, S.C. filed a condemnation action on Sept. 11, 2026 offering $0 to
acquire SDBUCKS, LLC's right to approve changes to a shared driveway that a developer
needs for a Target development's access road, according to CASD Council Watch, a local
newsletter. SDBUCKS rejected the offer, demanded a jury trial, and on Nov. 12 filed its
own suit arguing the taking serves a private commercial development rather than a public
use and that the city skipped required notice and waiting-period steps. The city says
the finished road will be dedicated to the public.

Whether a driveway consent right taken for a private developer's access road counts as
"public use" is exactly the kind of eminent-domain fight CREdocket exists to track.
