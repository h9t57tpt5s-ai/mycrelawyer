Panorama Corporate Center Receivership: Arapahoe County Judge Appoints Trigild IVL at Request of Wells Fargo, Trustee of a $133 Million Loan

An Arapahoe County judge on Nov. 16 appointed Trigild IVL receiver for Panorama Corporate Center, a six-building, 780,000-square-foot office complex in Centennial, at the request of Wells Fargo, trustee of a $133 million loan.

BusinessDen reported that Arapahoe County District Court Judge Joseph Whitfield on Monday, Nov. 16, 2026, appointed Trigild IVL to oversee Panorama Corporate Center, a six-building, 780,000-square-foot office complex in Centennial, Colorado. Wells Fargo, the trustee of a $133 million loan issued in January 2016 to Denver-based Sagard Real Estate, requested the appointment. BusinessDen described the complex as having defaulted on its loan.

$133 million loan (principal later reduced to $74.5 million, according to Wells Fargo)
Office

According to BusinessDen, Sagard Real Estate paid $190.6 million for the complex in January 2016, and Wells Fargo says the loan's principal was reduced to $74.5 million later that year.

Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. v. Tech Park Owner LLC (Rochester Tech Park $96.9M Foreclosure)

Wells Fargo seeks summary judgment in its foreclosure on Rochester Tech Park, a 288-acre office and industrial complex in Gates, alleging the owner and guarantors owe $96.9 million in principal.

Wells Fargo Bank, N.A., which provided just over $110 million in loans on Rochester Tech Park in 2017, has asked a Monroe County court for summary judgment in its 18-month-old foreclosure against Tech Park Owner LLC, the Rochester Business Journal reported on October 22, 2026. The lender alleges the owner and two guarantors defaulted and owe $96.9 million in principal plus interest, and it also details $2.356 million it paid Rochester Gas & Electric in 2024 to restore and keep power at the property. Justice Daniel J. Doyle is scheduled to hear arguments on January 12, 2027.

$96.9 million in principal alleged owed, plus interest and fees
Mixed-Use

Why it matters: The lender's emergency utility payments show how a borrower's unpaid operating bills can be added to what a lender claims. A receiver has run the property for more than a year, and close to two dozen tenants remain.

Oct 22, 2026 Supreme Court of the State of New York, Monroe County

Dundon Capital Partners' 2100 Ross Avenue Dallas Office Tower Posted for Foreclosure on $98M Loan

2100 Ross Avenue, a 33-story downtown Dallas office tower owned by Dundon Capital Partners, was posted for an October 6, 2026 foreclosure auction over a $98 million Wells Fargo loan the owner allegedly defaulted on.

2100 Ross Avenue, a 33-story, 840,000-square-foot office tower in downtown Dallas's Arts District, was listed for an October 6, 2026 foreclosure auction, The Real Deal reported, citing Roddy's Foreclosure Listing Service. The owner, Dallas billionaire Thomas Dundon's Dundon Capital Partners, allegedly defaulted on a $98 million loan Wells Fargo made in 2016. Pacific Elm Properties manages the building. The Real Deal noted that the owner could still reach a deal with the lender before the sale date.

$98 million loan alleged in default
Office

Why it matters: A foreclosure posting makes a loan default public and fixes a sale date, which leaves the owner and lender a few weeks to negotiate before the auction. The tower's history also shows how one office building can go through foreclosure twice in about 15 years under different owners.

Wells Fargo Foreclosure Auction of 100 Summer Street (Rockpoint, Boston)

Rockpoint was the sole bidder at a $260 million foreclosure auction of 100 Summer St., the Boston tower it bought for $806 million in 2019 with a $470 million Wells Fargo loan.

Wells Fargo foreclosed in September 2026 on 100 Summer St., a 32-story, 1.1-million-square-foot downtown Boston office tower owned by Rockpoint, which bought it for $806 million in October 2019 with a $470 million Wells Fargo mortgage, the Boston Globe and Bisnow reported. The foreclosure auction, first set for Oct. 20, was rescheduled. Rockpoint was the sole bidder at the $260 million opening bid, Bisnow reported on Oct. 27.

$470M mortgage; $806M 2019 purchase price; $260M winning auction bid
Office

U.S. Bank & Wells Fargo v. Wilmorite (Eastview Mall $210M Foreclosure and Eminent Domain Fight)

U.S. Bank and Wells Fargo, foreclosing on Eastview Mall's $210 million mortgage, call the Town of Victor's plan to take mall parcels by eminent domain a 'debt evasion scheme'; the town says the public needs the mall.

U.S. Bank National Association and Wells Fargo Bank, which hold the $210 million mortgage on Eastview Mall in Victor, N.Y., filed a foreclosure action in state Supreme Court in New York County on September 8, 2026, the Rochester Business Journal reported. In a September 25 letter to the town, their counsel called the town's plan to take the mortgaged portions of the mall by eminent domain and sell them to a Wilmorite-owned entity a 'debt evasion scheme.' Town Supervisor Jack Marren says a healthy mall is in the public interest, and he and Wilmorite say special servicer Rialto Capital Advisors has held back funds needed to run it.

$210 million mortgage in default
Retail

Why it matters: The dispute tests whether a town can use eminent domain to acquire a defaulted mall and pass it to an affiliate of the current owner while lenders pursue foreclosure. The lenders argue eminent domain 'is not an appropriate mechanism' for giving a borrower relief from its secured creditors.

Sep 8, 2026 Supreme Court of the State of New York, New York County

In re Palmer Center Foreclosure — Wells Fargo Tower & FirstBank Building (Colorado Springs Public Trustee Foreclosure)

Foreclosure notices were filed over a roughly $59 million unpaid loan balance on Colorado Springs' two tallest buildings, the Wells Fargo Tower and FirstBank Building at Palmer Center.

El Paso County Public Trustee's Office records show a foreclosure notice filed against RPalmer LLC, Overland Palmer LLC and Palmer Center Owner LLC, Delaware companies controlled by real estate firms with principal offices in California and Canada, The Gazette reported on August 25, 2026. The owners have an unpaid principal balance of about $59 million on a $74 million loan now held by a trustee for Bank of America, and a foreclosure auction was scheduled for October 21. The filings cover the Wells Fargo Tower and FirstBank Building at Palmer Center, the city's two tallest buildings, and several business-suite properties; the adjacent Antlers hotel is not in foreclosure.

~$59M unpaid principal balance on an original $74M loan

Why it matters: The filing puts the two most prominent office buildings in downtown Colorado Springs into Colorado's non-judicial public trustee foreclosure process. The Gazette reported that it was unclear what led to the default, while a longtime local developer it quoted tied the foreclosures to wider office-market conditions.

Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. v. Workspace Property Trust Affiliates (Multistate CMBS Foreclosure on $1.28B Suburban Office Portfolio)

Wells Fargo, as trustee for a CMBS trust, filed foreclosure suits in multiple states in August 2026 over a $1.28B loan backing Workspace Property Trust's four-state office and industrial portfolio, Bisnow reported.

Wells Fargo Bank, representing the CMBS trust that holds a $1.28 billion loan to Workspace Property Trust, filed foreclosure lawsuits in multiple states in August 2026, Bisnow reported. A suit in Hennepin County, Minnesota puts the unpaid principal at about $1.23 billion plus interest, special servicer fees and attorney expenses. The 2018 loan, backed by a portfolio now of 143 office and industrial properties in Arizona, Florida, Minnesota and Pennsylvania, went to special servicing in May 2023, and Trigild was appointed receiver for the whole portfolio after a September 2025 Arizona suit. Workspace Property Trust declined to comment.

$1.23B unpaid principal, plus accrued interest, special servicer fees, and attorneys' fees, on a $1.28B original CMBS loan

Why it matters: The loan is one of the largest CMBS loans in special servicing, and the filings move it from a receivership into foreclosure in several states at once. Morningstar Credit values the collateral at $1.24 billion, about the size of the unpaid balance and down from $1.63 billion at issuance.

Aug 24, 2026 Hennepin County District Court, Minnesota, and other state courts; earlier action in Maricopa County, Arizona

Centre Square Receivership Sale Order (1500 Market Street, Philadelphia)

A federal judge approved and directed the $70 million receivership sale of Centre Square, Philadelphia's largest office complex, to affiliates of Dean Adler and PMC Property Group, Bisnow reported.

On Aug. 17, 2026, U.S. District Judge Nitza I. Quiñones Alejandro approved and directed the $70 million sale of Centre Square, the 2.2-million-square-foot two-tower office complex at 1500 Market St., to affiliates of Dean Adler and PMC Property Group ahead of an Oct. 16 deadline, Bisnow reported, citing the Philadelphia Business Journal. The buyers had tried to get out of the deal despite a $5 million nonrefundable deposit, and rival bidder Universal Group Co. has appealed. Wells Fargo Bank v. 250 Livingston Owner LLC, Clipper Realty Inc. and Clipper Realty L.P. (CMBS Foreclosure)

Wells Fargo, as trustee for a CMBS trust, sued to foreclose the $125 million loan on Clipper Realty's 250 Livingston Street in Brooklyn after city agencies vacated; a court appointed a temporary receiver in April 2026.

Wells Fargo Bank, National Association, as trustee for GS Mortgage Securities Trust 2019-GC40, sued 250 Livingston Owner LLC, Clipper Realty Inc. and Clipper Realty L.P. in Kings County Supreme Court on March 25, 2026 over defaults on the $125 million mortgage loan secured by 250 Livingston Street in Brooklyn, according to Clipper's Form 10-Q filed November 4, 2026. The court granted the lender's request for a temporary receiver on April 29, 2026. Under a consent and cooperation agreement effective June 4, 2026, the parties agreed to jointly market the loan for sale, after which the lender may foreclose or take a deed in lieu; Clipper reported that as of November 4 the lender had done neither.

$125 million CMBS loan in default
Office

Why it matters: The filing traces how the loss of a single government tenant led to a CMBS default and a court-appointed receiver within months. It also shows that a consent agreement giving the borrower a marketing period and a right to bid for its own loan can delay, but not remove, the lender's foreclosure rights.

Apr 29, 2026 Supreme Court of the State of New York, Kings County

Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. v. 181 West Madison Property LLC ($240M Loan Foreclosure on Chicago Loop Office Tower)

Wells Fargo sued in federal court to foreclose on a $240 million loan on 181 West Madison, a 50-story Loop office tower; the court appointed a receiver after the borrower did not respond.

Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. sued 181 West Madison Property LLC in federal court in Chicago on March 27, 2026 to foreclose on the $240 million loan on the 50-story Loop office tower, and on April 3 moved to have Hilco Real Estate's Matthew Mason appointed receiver. The Real Deal reported that the loan's special servicer, Situs, is behind the suit, and that the default followed anchor tenant Northern Trust's shrinking its space from 400,000 to 225,000 square feet. The borrower did not respond or appear, and the court appointed Mason receiver on April 24 and entered a default against the borrower on June 14.

$240 million loan; more than $223 million due, per the receivership motion as reported

Why it matters: A single large tenant shrinking its lease can cut an office building's income enough to stop debt payments, even when the building looks stable. Here the borrower did not contest the receivership, and a receiver took control within a month of the motion. That shows how quickly an owner can lose control of operations once a lender files.

Mar 27, 2026 U.S. District Court, Northern District of Illinois (Eastern Division)
