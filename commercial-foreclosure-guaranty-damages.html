How damages are usually measured

A lender's recovery starts with the unpaid balance, interest and default interest, and protective advances for taxes and insurance, less what the collateral brings. Whether the lender can pursue a deficiency, and whether a fair-value offset applies, depends on the state and on how the foreclosure was conducted. Guaranty claims turn on the guaranty's terms, including the carve-out triggers that make a nonrecourse loan recourse. A borrower's counterclaim for lender misconduct is a separate exposure with its own measure of damages.

General background, not legal advice; the rules vary by state and by the documents in your deal.

What the calculator asks about

These are the facts it pulls from your description or documents for a commercial loan, foreclosure or guaranty claim:

  • Property state
  • Outstanding loan balance
  • Has a foreclosure action been filed?
  • Foreclosure method used, if known
  • Does the borrower dispute the default itself?
  • Lender protective advances
  • Foreclosure sale proceeds, if known
  • Court-determined or appraised fair market value at foreclosure, if any
  • Has a receivership motion been filed?
  • Is a guaranty carve-out trigger event alleged?
