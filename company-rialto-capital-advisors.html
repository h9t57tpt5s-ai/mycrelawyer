Rialto Capital Takes 580 Market Street, a San Francisco Financial District Office Building, in Foreclosure

Special servicer Rialto Capital took over 580 Market Street, a 35,000-square-foot Financial District office building, for $5 million after owner Paragon Company's $15.7 million loan matured unpaid.

Special servicer Rialto Capital took control of 580 Market Street, a century-old, 35,000-square-foot office building next to San Francisco's Montgomery BART station, through foreclosure, The Real Deal reported, citing the San Francisco Business Times. Owner Paragon Company's $15.7 million loan matured in June 2025, and a notice of default was served in October 2025. A trustee's deed recorded in July 2026 showed $17.5 million in unpaid debt, and Rialto paid $5 million for the property.

$17.5M unpaid debt; Rialto paid $5M
Office

Why it matters: The $5 million price, against a March appraisal of $9.9 million and a 2015 purchase price of $24.2 million, gives owners and lenders a current reference point for small Financial District office buildings. It also shows that a lender that takes back an office building may plan to hold and lease it for years rather than sell right away.

U.S. Bank & Wells Fargo v. Wilmorite (Eastview Mall $210M Foreclosure and Eminent Domain Fight)

U.S. Bank and Wells Fargo, foreclosing on Eastview Mall's $210 million mortgage, call the Town of Victor's plan to take mall parcels by eminent domain a 'debt evasion scheme'; the town says the public needs the mall.

U.S. Bank National Association and Wells Fargo Bank, which hold the $210 million mortgage on Eastview Mall in Victor, N.Y., filed a foreclosure action in state Supreme Court in New York County on September 8, 2026, the Rochester Business Journal reported. In a September 25 letter to the town, their counsel called the town's plan to take the mortgaged portions of the mall by eminent domain and sell them to a Wilmorite-owned entity a 'debt evasion scheme.' Town Supervisor Jack Marren says a healthy mall is in the public interest, and he and Wilmorite say special servicer Rialto Capital Advisors has held back funds needed to run it.

The lenders argue eminent domain 'is not an appropriate mechanism' for giving a borrower relief from its secured creditors.

Rialto Capital Advisors v. WC MRP Calumet Center, LLC ($45.6M Calumet City Retail Foreclosure)

Special servicer Rialto Capital has sued to foreclose on a Calumet City, Illinois shopping center held by an entity backed by Nate Paul, seeking $45.6 million after a 2025 reinstatement deal broke down, The Real Deal reported.

Rialto Capital, acting as special servicer for a CMBS trust, filed a $45.6 million foreclosure suit in Cook County against WC MRP Calumet Center, LLC, an entity backed by Austin developer Nate Paul's World Class Holdings. The property is a shopping center of more than 159,000 square feet at 410-500 River Oaks West Road in Calumet City, Illinois, and it is one of six retail centers securing a $38.3 million loan, The Real Deal reported. The lawsuit alleges the borrower paid the first $500,000 quarterly installment under a 2025 reinstatement agreement but did not make the December payment or those due in 2026.

$45.6M

Why it matters: A reinstatement agreement protects a borrower only while the payments keep coming; here the suit alleges one missed installment led to a default notice and then acceleration of the debt. Owners whose property is one of several securing a single cross-collateralized loan should watch the whole pool, because the lender can enforce against each property in turn.

Aug 10, 2026 Circuit Court of Cook County, Illinois

Rialto Capital Advisors v. Jenel ($17.7M Loop Retail Foreclosure, 36 South State Street)

Rialto Capital Advisors, as special servicer, sued in Cook County to foreclose on a nearly 19,000-square-foot Loop retail property at 36 South State Street after a maturity default, claiming $17.7 million.

Rialto Capital Advisors, acting as special servicer for a securitized mortgage loan trust, filed a foreclosure complaint in Cook County on March 31, 2026 against New York landlord Jenel over the nearly 19,000-square-foot retail property at 36 South State Street. The complaint alleges the loan, a $16.8 million balance Jenel took on when it bought the property from Thor Equities for $26 million in 2018, went unpaid when it matured last June; Rialto claims $17.7 million including default interest and liquidation fees. The Real Deal reported that a hearing was set for Sept. 28.

$17.7M claimed (default interest and fees on ~$16.8M principal)

Why it matters: A fully leased retail building that covers its debt payments can still end up in foreclosure if its value falls and the owner cannot pay off the loan when it comes due. Owners with loans maturing on downtown retail should start refinancing talks early and plan for a lower appraisal, not just today's rent.

Mar 31, 2026 Circuit Court of Cook County, Illinois
