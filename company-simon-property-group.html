Southridge Mall Foreclosure: Milwaukee County Court Approves Sale of Simon-Affiliated Mall to Receiver-Linked Buyer Assuming $101.9M Loan

A Milwaukee County judge approved the sale of Southridge Mall from a Simon affiliate to a receiver-linked buyer assuming the $101.9 million loan and paying $7 million in cash, ending a CMBS foreclosure.

On October 6, 2026, Milwaukee County Circuit Judge William Pocan entered an order approving the sale of Southridge Mall in Greendale, Wisconsin, ending a foreclosure that began in December 2020, the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel reported. The mall, excluding its separately owned department store anchors, is being sold by a Simon Property Group affiliate to Southridge Mall Member LLC, a newly formed buyer tied to court-appointed receiver Spinoso Management Group. The buyer will assume the mall's $101.9 million loan and pay $7 million in cash, according to court documents cited by the paper.

$101.9 million loan assumption plus $7 million cash
Retail

Why it matters: The case shows how long a contested regional-mall foreclosure can take: almost six years from complaint to an approved sale, even after the borrower agreed to a foreclosure judgment in 2021. The structure is also notable. A receiver's affiliate buys the asset by assuming the securitized debt instead of the lender taking title or holding an open auction, which gives CMBS holders and special servicers a model for exiting long-stalled retail loans.

Oct 6, 2026 Wisconsin Circuit Court, Milwaukee County
Zoning & Land Use Verified Update Ruling Issued

The Retail Property Trust v. Nassau County Dept. of Assessment (Roosevelt Field $4.8M Reporting-Fine Appeal)

The Second Circuit upheld Nassau County's $4.8 million fine on Roosevelt Field's Simon-affiliated owner for skipping two years of income and expense filings, holding businesses can invoke the Excessive Fines Clause but this fine was not excessive.

The Second Circuit on August 6, 2026 affirmed summary judgment for Nassau County in a challenge by The Retail Property Trust, which owns the Roosevelt Field mall in Garden City, to a $4.8 million fine for not filing Annual Statements of Income and Expense for 2013 and 2014. Writing for the panel, Judge Richard J. Sullivan held that the Eighth Amendment's Excessive Fines Clause protects business entities but that the fine, a fraction of one percent of the property's value, was not grossly disproportional. Judge Richard C. Wesley concurred, writing that the court did not need to decide whether the clause covers businesses.

$4.8M cumulative fine for two years of non-compliance

Why it matters: The ruling makes it Second Circuit precedent that trusts and corporations can invoke the Excessive Fines Clause, while upholding a penalty scaled to a commercial property's market value. Nassau County owners who fail to file income and expense statements face fines of 0.25% to 0.75% of market value, and the court held that an Article 78 proceeding is adequate process to contest one.

Aug 6, 2026 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit

Herb Simon v. David Simon's Family (SFG Co. Restructuring Suit)

Herb Simon is suing the family of his late nephew David Simon, alleging David dissolved private real estate company SFG Co. and moved its assets to a new entity without equity holders' consent; Eli Simon calls the suit meritless.

Herb Simon and several affiliated entities sued 17 defendants in Indiana state court in late July 2026, alleging his late nephew David Simon used his control of SFG Manager LLC to dissolve SFG Co., a private real estate company formed in 1995 to hold property interests kept outside Simon Property Group when it went public. The complaint says David moved SFG Co.'s assets into a new entity, SFG HoldCo, less than two weeks before his death in March 2026, under an operating agreement that removed preferred distribution rights. The plaintiffs seek to unwind the restructuring and recover compensatory and punitive damages. Eli Simon, David's son, who heads SFG Manager and succeeded his father as Simon Property Group's CEO, called it a 'meritless complaint.'

Undisclosed; plaintiffs seek to unwind the restructuring plus compensatory and punitive damages

Why it matters: Members of a closely held real estate LLC are protected only as far as its consent provisions are honored; here the plaintiffs say a unanimous-consent requirement was bypassed. Families and co-investors holding property through private LLCs should check who can amend or dissolve the entity and what notice members are owed.

Jul 31, 2026 Indiana state court

Greenwood Park Mall Shooting Negligent-Security Suit Against Simon Property Group (Indiana Supreme Court)

The Indiana Supreme Court heard argument Jan. 15, 2026 on whether Simon Property Group and its security contractor can be sued over the 2022 Greenwood Park Mall shooting.

The Indiana Supreme Court heard oral argument on January 15, 2026 in a negligence suit brought by a woman wounded in the attack and her family against Greenwood Park Mall owner Simon Property Group, L.P. and security contractor Universal Protection Service, LLC over the July 17, 2022 food-court shooting that killed three people, the Indiana Capital Chronicle reported. The defendants argue the suit should be dismissed because such an attack could not be foreseen. A Marion County trial court and the Indiana Court of Appeals both refused to dismiss the case.

Unspecified compensatory damages sought; underlying premises-liability and negligence claims

Why it matters: The court's answer will shape whether Indiana negligent-security claims against mall owners and their security contractors over a mass shooting can get past a motion to dismiss and into discovery. Related wrongful-death suits are on hold until it rules.

Jan 15, 2026 Supreme Court of Indiana

Oak Land and Development LLC v. Simon Property Group Inc. (Penn Square Mall Antitrust Tying Suit)

Developers of Oklahoma City's OAK project sued Simon Property Group, alleging it tied lease deals elsewhere to steering tenants to Penn Square Mall; Simon calls the suit meritless and has moved to dismiss.

The owners and developers of OAK, a mixed-use project across the street from Penn Square Mall in Oklahoma City, sued Simon Property Group on December 2, 2025 in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Oklahoma, News 9 reported. They allege Simon used threats involving leases at its properties in other cities to steer tenants to Penn Square or away from OAK, an illegal tying arrangement under the Sherman Act and Oklahoma law. Simon has called the suit "meritless and fictional," and on June 2, 2026 it moved to dismiss the second amended complaint; the motion was fully briefed in August 2026.

Unspecified damages sought over alleged antitrust tying; jury demanded

Why it matters: The case tests whether a landlord's leverage across its national portfolio can support an antitrust tying claim in a single local leasing market. Simon argues that offering package deals on properties in other locations is ordinary competition, not an antitrust violation.

Dec 2, 2025 U.S. District Court, Western District of Oklahoma
