Industrial & Logistics Litigation & Emerging Issues

Industrial real estate whipsawed in 2026 — from a well-documented oversupply into one of the tightest leasing markets in years — and the disputes now forming are as much about that reversal as about the earlier glut.

Trending Issues

What's driving disputes right now

Grounded in current market and legal reporting, not speculation — each item links to its source.

From Oversupply to Tightening, Fast

Warehouse vacancy that built up through 2025 reversed quickly in 2026 — leasing volume rose roughly 49% year-over-year in some markets — creating friction over renewal options and expansion rights signed during the soft market at pricing that no longer reflects current conditions.

Tariff-Driven Lease Renegotiation

Tenants front-loading inventory ahead of tariff deadlines are demanding shorter terms and more flexibility just as landlords are regaining pricing power — a direct collision course on renewal and expansion terms.

Tariffs

Obsolescence of Older Stock

Remaining industrial vacancy is concentrated in older facilities that no longer meet modern logistics and automation specifications — creating valuation and permitted-use disputes distinct from the broader market's recovery.

Power Competition With Data Centers

Industrial and logistics developers are increasingly competing with data center demand for the same grid capacity and land near substations — a new source of siting and interconnection friction for both asset classes.
