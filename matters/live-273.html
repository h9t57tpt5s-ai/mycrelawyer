Stop 65, LLC v. Beach Park Railway, LLC: Ohio Appeals Court Upholds Shopping-Center Eviction of Museum Tenant Over Unapproved Sublease

An Ohio appeals court upheld a shopping-center owner's eviction of a museum tenant, holding that letting a motorcycle-repair operation occupy space was an unauthorized sublease and no 30-day notice was needed.

On October 5, 2026, Ohio's Ninth District Court of Appeals affirmed an Avon Lake Municipal Court judgment evicting Beach Park Railway, LLC, a state-funded museum operator, from shopping-center space owned by Stop 65, LLC. The court held that letting an outside motorcycle-repair operation occupy dedicated space in the premises was an unauthorized sublease, not a mere license, and that a commercial landlord needed no separate 30-day termination notice before its statutory three-day notice to vacate.

Why it matters

The decision gives commercial landlords a clear test for when a tenant's informal side arrangement becomes a sublease that breaches an anti-subletting clause, and it confirms that Ohio's residential 30-day termination rule does not apply to commercial leases. It also shows how a non-disturbance agreement with a public funder can add notice-of-default conditions that complicate a tenant's own damages counterclaims.

The premises are Suite A at 33501 Lake Road in Avon Lake, Ohio, a roughly 15,000-square-foot space in a shopping center. Beach Park Railway, LLC used the space to operate a museum it established with state grant money, and in 2019 it signed a non-disturbance and estoppel agreement with the Ohio Facilities Commission and the then-owner protecting the Commission's interest. The tenant originally occupied as a subtenant of TBP Avon Lake, LLC, paying $583 a month. After TBP sold the property and leased it back, Stop 65, LLC acquired the shopping center, evicted TBP in a separate Lorain County case decided in March 2024, and became the museum operator's direct landlord.
