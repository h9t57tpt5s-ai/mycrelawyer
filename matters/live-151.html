Wal-Mart Stores East, LP v. Johnson (Independent-Contractor Shock Injury Premises Liability Appeal)

Florida's Second District Court of Appeal affirmed a jury verdict against Wal-Mart Stores East, LP in favor of Joseph Johnson, an independent contractor's technician who was shocked and fell from a ladder while installing an automatic door at a Walmart store after touching a concealed, defectively wired junction box in a Walmart-controlled electrical room. The court rejected Walmart's argument that it owed no duty to a contractor's employee injured while performing the contracted work.

Why it matters

Clarifies that Florida's independent-contractor no-duty rule protects owners only from claims tied to hazards inherent in the work actually contracted for, not from separate, undisclosed dangers the owner controls and knows or should know about, particularly in restricted-access areas only the owner can inspect.

Original source ↗Read the official opinion ↗

Florida's Second District Court of Appeal has affirmed a jury verdict, reported at $6.8 million, against Wal-Mart Stores East, LP in a premises liability suit brought by an independent contractor's technician who was shocked and fell from a ladder while installing an automatic door at a Walmart store. The decision, issued June 24, 2026 in Docket No. 2D2025-0567, rejects Walmart's argument that it owed no duty of care to a contractor's employee injured while performing the very work the contractor was retained to do, and clarifies where Florida law draws the line between hazards inherent in a contracted job and hazards the property owner itself creates or conceals.
