Florida's Second DCA affirmed a jury verdict against Wal-Mart Stores East for a door contractor's technician shocked by an ungrounded junction box in a locked room at a Hillsborough County store.

Florida's Second District Court of Appeal on June 24, 2026 affirmed a final judgment entered on a jury verdict against Wal-Mart Stores East, LP in a negligence suit by a service technician for D.H. Pace Door Company, who was shocked and fell from a ladder in July 2020 while connecting a new automatic door at a Hillsborough County store. The court held that Walmart could not rely on the independent contractor defense because the evidence supported that it had constructive knowledge of an ungrounded junction box in a locked area only its staff could open, a hazard not inherent in the door work.

Why it matters

The opinion applies the exception to Florida's independent contractor defense for concealed dangers that the owner knows or should know about and that are not inherent in the contracted work. It treats the owner's exclusive control of a restricted area, and the length of time a defect existed, as support for constructive knowledge.

Full write-up

Florida's Second District Court of Appeal affirmed a final judgment entered on a jury verdict against Wal-Mart Stores East, LP on June 24, 2026 (No. 2D2025-0567), the Tampa Free Press reported. The appeal came from the Hillsborough County Circuit Court, where Judge Christopher C. Nash presided. Judge Sleet wrote for a unanimous panel that included Chief Judge Lucas and Judge Rothstein-Youakim.

According to the opinion, the plaintiff, a service technician for D.H. Pace Door Company, went to a Walmart store in July 2020 to replace a manual door with an automatic one in the order pick-up area. The new door had to be connected to an existing metal junction box in a restricted room that a store manager opened with a key card. The technician could not find the right breaker because about 200 breakers in the breaker room were unlabeled, so he decided to check the wires with a voltmeter. When he touched the loosened metal cover of the box, he was shocked, fell from his ladder to the concrete floor and injured his head. He then found the wires uncapped and the grounding wire unsecured. Experts for both sides agreed the box did not comply with the National Electrical Code, which Florida has adopted as part of its building code.

Walmart argued that a property owner owes no duty to an independent contractor's employee injured while doing the work he was hired to do, and that neither exception to that rule applied. The court disagreed. It held that the evidence supported constructive knowledge: Walmart built the store and installed its junction boxes, the box sat in a locked area only Walmart personnel could enter, and the condition had existed for an extended period. Because the hidden danger was not inherent in installing a door, the court held, the trial court properly denied Walmart's motions for summary judgment and directed verdict.
