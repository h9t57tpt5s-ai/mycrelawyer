The Mission LLC v. State of California and City of Santa Barbara

A federal judge dismissed, with leave to amend, The Mission LLC's suit claiming SB 158 singles out its 270-unit builder's-remedy project behind the Santa Barbara Mission; it filed an amended complaint July 10.

On June 2, 2026, U.S. District Judge Hernán D. Vera dismissed The Mission LLC's suit against the State of California and the City of Santa Barbara, finding it unripe because the project application is still under review and that the company had not overcome the state's sovereign immunity, the Santa Barbara Independent reported. The company alleges SB 158, a budget trailer bill, illegally singles out its 270-unit builder's-remedy project behind the Santa Barbara Mission. The judge said the law raises "serious constitutional questions" and allowed an amended complaint, which the company filed on July 10, 2026; the defendants have moved to dismiss again.

Why it matters

The ruling leaves open a constitutional challenge to a state law the developer says targets a single project, while holding that the challenge must wait for a concrete dispute. The case continues on the amended complaint.

Full write-up

The Mission LLC has proposed a 270-unit, eight-story housing development behind the Santa Barbara Mission, with 54 below-market units and three stories of underground parking, using California's "builder's remedy" provision to bypass zoning, the Santa Barbara Independent reported. The paper noted concerns about the site including flood zones, wetlands, a historic graveyard, known archaeological resources, an underground creek, landslide potential and traffic on a narrow exit road from a very high fire-risk area.

In October 2025, state legislators passed SB 158, a budget trailer bill that removes builder's-remedy projects from an environmental-review exemption when they meet a set of criteria: a city of 85,000 to 95,000 people in a county of 440,000 to 455,000, a property of four acres or more, FEMA-identified floodways, state-identified wetlands and adjacency to a state historic landmark. The Independent reported that The Mission LLC's project appears to be the only one the law affects. The company sued the state and the city in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California on October 24, 2025, alleging the law illegally singles out its project and that the city lacks a compliant housing element.

After a March 5, 2026 hearing on the motions to dismiss, Judge Vera ruled on June 2, 2026 that the case was not ripe because the project application remains under review, calling the complaint "woefully short" on specifics, and that the company had not met the requirements for an exception to the state's sovereign immunity, according to the Independent. He expressed no view on the merits but said SB 158 raises "serious constitutional questions," called the ripeness defects "eminently curable," and gave the company until June 22 to amend. The company did not respond to the Independent's request for comment.

The CourtListener docket shows an amended complaint filed July 10, 2026, a renewed motion to dismiss filed July 24, 2026, and further entries on that motion in October and on November 2, 2026.
