Soros Fund Management v. Eretz Group (224 West 57th Street Foreclosure & Tenant-Takeover)

September 1, 2026
Added to tracker

Court or forum

Amount / scale
$158.7M foreclosure judgment (on a $145M original mortgage)
Parties
Plaintiff (Lender): Soros Fund ManagementLender (Soros affiliate): QPTF LLCDefendant (Borrower/Owner): The Eretz Group

Soros Fund Management, which purchased a defaulted $145 million mortgage on 224 West 57th Street from original lender Aareal Capital Corp., took title to the Midtown Manhattan office building at a September 1, 2026 foreclosure auction after obtaining a $158.7 million judgment against owner Eretz Group. The building is occupied primarily by Open Society Foundations, George Soros's own philanthropic organization, meaning the property's anchor tenant effectively became its owner.

Why it matters

Illustrates how distressed-debt acquisition lets a well-capitalized party, here a landlord's own tenant, gain control of a building through a discounted mortgage purchase rather than a conventional sale, and shows that repeated maturity extensions on legacy office loans frequently defer rather than resolve an underlying default risk.

Original source ↗NYSCEF Index No. 850134/2024 ↗

George Soros's family office has completed one of the more unusual foreclosure outcomes in recent Manhattan commercial real estate history: after acquiring a defaulted mortgage on its own landlord's building, Soros Fund Management took title to 224 West 57th Street at a September 1, 2026 foreclosure auction. The ten-story, roughly 107,000-square-foot Midtown office building, a former auto showroom on the block known as Billionaires Row, is occupied primarily by Open Society Foundations, the philanthropic organization founded and funded by George Soros, leaving the building's own anchor tenant as its new owner.
