Shareholder Class Action v. Franklin BSP Realty Trust, Inc.: Investors Sue Mortgage REIT Over Dividend Statements

On February 26, 2026, a shareholder filed a putative securities class action in the Eastern District of New York against Franklin BSP Realty Trust, Inc. (NYSE: FBRT) and three of its officers. The complaint alleges they overstated the company's prospects and its ability to maintain a $0.355 quarterly dividend between November 5, 2024 and February 11, 2026, when the commercial mortgage REIT reported fourth-quarter 2025 results and reset the dividend to $0.20. The company says the suit is without merit; a related derivative suit filed in May 2026 has been stayed.

Why it matters

The case tests whether management's repeated statements of confidence in reaching dividend coverage can support securities-fraud claims once a REIT cuts its dividend. No court has ruled on the merits.

Full write-up

The complaint, filed February 26, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of New York, asserts claims under Sections 10(b) and 20(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 on behalf of investors who bought FBRT securities between November 5, 2024 and February 11, 2026. It names the company and three officers. It alleges that on earnings calls from late 2024 through October 2025, the defendants said they were confident in the company's earnings power and in a path to covering the $0.355 quarterly dividend while distributable earnings fell short of it, and that they recklessly overstated the company's prospects and ability to maintain the dividend.

According to the complaint, the company announced a leadership change on February 10, 2026, and on February 11 reported fourth-quarter and full-year 2025 results and said its board had reset the quarterly dividend to $0.20. The complaint alleges the stock fell $1.44, or 14.18 percent, to $8.71 on February 12, 2026.

In its quarterly report for the period ended June 30, 2026, Franklin BSP Realty Trust said it believes the class action is without merit, intends to defend itself vigorously, and does not expect it to have a material impact on its financial condition. The report also disclosed a related shareholder derivative complaint filed May 8, 2026 in the same court, alleging the board breached its fiduciary duties by allowing the challenged statements; the company says that suit is also without merit and has been stayed. The class action is assigned to District Judge Gary R. Brown, and a motion to appoint a lead plaintiff was filed in 2026; the docket shows no ruling on the merits.
