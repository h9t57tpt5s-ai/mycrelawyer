Orchard BJK Company, LLC v. Coolbaugh Township Board of Supervisors (Challenge to Wetland Buffer Amendments Dismissed as Unripe)

Property type

Not a monetary claim; developer sought a declaratory judgment voiding stormwater buffer ordinance amendments as unconstitutional and invalid special legislation

Pennsylvania's Commonwealth Court held developers' challenge to Coolbaugh Township's wider wetland buffers was premature because their waiver request for a Lot 2 warehouse was still pending.

On October 15, 2026, the Commonwealth Court of Pennsylvania affirmed the dismissal of a suit by Orchard BJK Company, LLC and Pocono Mountains Industries, Inc. challenging Coolbaugh Township's August 2023 stormwater ordinance amendments, which raised minimum wetland buffers from 50 to 150 feet and from 100 to 150 feet. The developers sued while their own petition asking the township's Board of Supervisors to waive the new buffers for a planned warehouse on Lot 2 was still pending before the Board. The court held the claims were not ripe because the Board could still grant the waiver.

Why it matters

The ruling shows that a Pennsylvania court may treat a challenge to a land-use ordinance as premature while the ordinance's own waiver process remains available and an application under it is pending before the same body. It also shows that a local government's preliminary objections asserting no actual controversy are enough to preserve a ripeness defense.

Full write-up

Orchard BJK and Pocono Mountains Industries own commercially and industrially zoned properties in Coolbaugh Township, Monroe County. In April 2023 the township's Board of Supervisors approved their subdivision of one tract into a 35.5-acre Lot 1 and a 73.16-acre Lot 2 and conditionally approved a land development plan for an approximately 333,000-square-foot warehouse on Lot 1 under the ordinances then in effect.

On August 2, 2023, the Board amended the township's Stormwater Management and Earth Disturbance Ordinance, increasing the minimum inner wetland buffer from 50 to 150 feet and the minimum buffer between buildings and wetlands from 100 to 150 feet. The amendments left in place a provision allowing the Board, on application, to modify or waive stormwater requirements development by development. On April 29, 2024, the developers filed a preliminary land development plan for an approximately 186,000-square-foot warehouse on Lot 2 and a petition asking the Board to apply the pre-amendment buffers.

On May 3, 2024, with both still pending, they sued the Board in the Monroe County Court of Common Pleas, alleging the amendments violated their constitutional right to enjoy their property and were invalid special legislation, and seeking a declaration that the amendments were void. They alleged the amendments effectively prevented warehouses on Lot 2, reduced the value of their other properties, and were designed to restrict industrial development because of local opposition to warehouses. The Board filed preliminary objections, and on June 27, 2025, the court dismissed the complaint as unripe. Citizens for Pennsylvania's Future, the Tobyhanna Creek/Tunkhannock Creek Watershed Association and the Tobyhanna Conservation Association are also named as appellees.

In an unreported memorandum opinion by Judge Lori A. Dumas, joined by Judge Christine Fizzano Cannon and Senior Judge Bonnie Brigance Leadbetter, the Commonwealth Court affirmed. It rejected the developers' argument that the trial court raised ripeness on its own, pointing to the Board's preliminary objections, which argued there was no actual controversy until the Board ruled on the waiver request. Applying the lenient ripeness standard for declaratory judgment actions, the court still held the suit premature: whether the buffers would block development of Lot 2 or other properties "remains purely theoretical" while the developers may still obtain relief through the waiver process they had invoked four days before suing.
