KKR Real Estate Finance Trust sought a $24.2 million judgment and a public sale of Philadelphia's Bourse after hotel conversion work stopped amid the owners' partnership split; the case was dropped Oct. 12.

KKR Real Estate Finance Trust, through plaintiff KREF Capital LLC, sued LAK Bourse Owner, LLC and LAK Bourse Master HTC Landlord, LLC in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania on July 28, 2026, seeking a $24.2 million judgment plus interest and fees and a public auction of the Bourse, The Philadelphia Inquirer reported. The lender alleged the owners defaulted when the development team abandoned its hotel-conversion plans and stopped work amid a split between Dean Adler and Keystone Development + Investment. The lender voluntarily dismissed the case on October 12, 2026, according to the court docket.

Why it matters

The lender moved for a forced sale while the sponsors' control fight was still before the Delaware court. Its notice of dismissal gives no reason, and the sources reviewed do not report how the loan default or the Delaware dispute was resolved.

Full write-up

In June 2024, KKR Real Estate Finance Trust lent Lubert-Adler Real Estate Funds and Keystone Development + Investment $83.7 million to buy Philadelphia's 131-year-old Bourse building and convert part of it into a hotel, and to buy 400 Market St., which was converted into apartments as planned, The Philadelphia Inquirer reported. The loan required the hotel project to be near completion by the end of May 2026. KKR says 400 Market is not part of its lawsuit.

On July 28, 2026, KREF Capital LLC sued LAK Bourse Owner, LLC and LAK Bourse Master HTC Landlord, LLC in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania. According to the Inquirer, the complaint says the development team "abandoned its plans" for the hotel and stopped conversion work earlier in 2026, defaulting the mortgage with more than $24 million in principal outstanding, and that the owners missed mortgage payments starting in June. It sought a $24.2 million judgment plus interest and fees and a public auction of the building. At least five contractors had filed more than $2 million in liens for unpaid work, the paper reported.

The suit followed a breakup between real estate investor Dean Adler and Keystone. Adler decided to scrap the hotel and event-space plan and, according to the Philadelphia Business Journal, planned to work with PMC Property Group on a multifamily conversion. Keystone sued Adler in Delaware Chancery Court for breach of contract, alleging he failed to provide promised funding, and Adler countersued, accusing Keystone of unauthorized spending and "gross negligence." In a March ruling reported by Law360, a judge rejected Adler's attempt to oust Keystone and kept Keystone in control of the project, the Inquirer said.

Keystone declined to comment on the lawsuit but said it remained "confident" it would prevail against Adler in Delaware. Adler told the Inquirer he believed the Keystone case was near resolution, that KKR's potential foreclosure would follow, and that the Bourse would be revitalized as 400 Market was. On October 12, 2026, KREF Capital filed a notice voluntarily dismissing the federal case under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 41(a)(1)(A)(i); the notice gives no reason.
