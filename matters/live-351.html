PMC Property Group, Inc. and three affiliated property entities sued Apogee Enterprises, Inc. in the U.S. District Court for the District of Minnesota on Oct. 27, 2026, seeking to hold it responsible for allegedly defective glass at three Philadelphia residential towers, USGlass magazine reported. The complaint asserts unjust enrichment and seeks declarations that subsidiaries Wausau Group and Viracon are Apogee's alter egos, so that judgments against them could be enforced against Apogee. Why it matters

The filing moves PMC's parent-company theory to a new federal court after a jurisdictional dismissal in Pennsylvania. Whether the alter-ego claims succeed will decide whether Apogee itself can be reached for any judgment against its subsidiaries over the glass breakage.

Full write-up

PMC Property Group, Inc. filed a new federal lawsuit against Apogee Enterprises, Inc. on Oct. 27, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the District of Minnesota, USGlass magazine reported. Three affiliated property entities joined as plaintiffs, according to the CourtListener docket (No. 0:26-cv-04642). The complaint alleges that Apogee controlled its subsidiaries, Wausau Group and Viracon, and directed their responses to complaints about spontaneous glass breakage at Franklin Tower, Riverwalk Tower I and Riverwalk Tower II in Philadelphia. It follows an August ruling by a Pennsylvania federal judge that dismissed several claims in PMC's earlier suit, including the claims against Apogee for lack of personal jurisdiction.

PMC alleges that Wausau supplied defective window systems and that Viracon made at least some of the insulating glass units used in them. It claims nickel sulfide inclusions in the glass caused windows to break spontaneously at all three properties, and that the inclusions could not have been found at delivery or installation because they cannot be seen with the naked eye. PMC says breakage has increased since 2024, leading to inspections, violations, penalties and costs for inspecting, cleaning and replacing glass.

The Minnesota complaint has three counts: unjust enrichment and two requests for declarations that Wausau and Viracon are alter egos of Apogee. PMC asks the court to pierce the corporate veil so that judgments against the subsidiaries can be enforced against Apogee. As evidence of control, it cites a November 2019 email exchange in which Wausau employees indicated that Apogee dictated contract language on glass treatment, an October 2020 exchange in which a Wausau employee allegedly sought Apogee's approval for a warranty credit involving Viracon, and a January 2021 request by an Apogee procurement director to speed deliveries because the orders mattered to fourth-quarter results. PMC also says its warranty requests were forwarded to Apogee personnel.

PMC seeks actual, incidental and consequential damages, interest, attorneys' fees and the alter-ego declarations. The complaint does not state a total damages figure, but PMC says it paid Wausau about $50 million for the window systems at the three properties. USGlass did not report a response from Apogee.
