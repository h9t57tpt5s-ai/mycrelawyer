Alexandria's subsidiary is suing NYC Health + Hospitals and the EDC over its option on a Manhattan campus parcel, with $185.9 million invested, and separately lost a bid for a Yellowstone injunction over a lease default notice.

Alexandria Real Estate Equities disclosed in its quarterly report filed October 26, 2026 that its subsidiary ARE-East River Science Park, LLC is in two lawsuits with New York City Health + Hospitals Corporation and the New York City Economic Development Corporation. In federal court, it seeks a declaration that its 2006 option to add an adjacent parcel to the ground lease for the Alexandria Center for Life Science campus remains valid; its fraud and implied-covenant claims were dismissed in March 2026, and the agencies counterclaimed. In a separate state-court action filed May 12, 2026 over a default notice on the existing towers, the court denied a Yellowstone injunction, and the subsidiary's appeal is pending.

Why it matters

Alexandria says its $185.9 million investment in pre-construction costs for the option parcel is at stake, with potential losses ranging from zero to that amount depending on the outcome. The state case also shows the limits of a Yellowstone injunction: the court declined to toll the cure period on the agencies' default notice.

Full write-up

According to Alexandria's Form 10-Q for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, its subsidiary ARE-East River Science Park, LLC holds an option, granted in 2006, to add a parcel adjacent to and north of the Alexandria Center for Life Science in New York City to its existing ground lease, which would allow an additional life-science building. Its investment in pre-construction costs for the parcel was $185.9 million as of September 30, 2026.

On August 6, 2024, the subsidiary sued New York City Health + Hospitals Corporation and the New York City Economic Development Corporation in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York over the ground lease and option. It amended its complaint on January 24, 2025 to assert fraudulent inducement, breach of contract, breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, and a claim for a declaration that the option remains valid. On March 27, 2026 the court dismissed the fraud and implied-covenant claims; Alexandria says it intends to appeal, and the declaratory claim remains pending. On April 10, 2026 the agencies answered and counterclaimed, seeking among other things a declaration that the option expired, entitlement to a $5.0 million security deposit, and damages of at least $3.8 million.

Separately, on April 20, 2026 the EDC delivered a notice alleging that the subsidiary had defaulted on obligations to provide sublease, tax and financial information for the existing towers on the campus, with daily charges of $1,000, rising to $2,000, if not cured within 30 days. Alexandria disputes the allegations. On May 12, 2026 the subsidiary sued in the Commercial Division of New York State Supreme Court for a Yellowstone injunction, a preliminary injunction and a temporary restraining order to toll the cure period and bar a notice of termination. The court denied the restraining order on May 13 and the Yellowstone and preliminary injunctions on June 15, 2026. The subsidiary appealed, and on September 10, 2026 the Appellate Division, First Department denied interim relief pending the appeal, which remains pending.

On July 2, 2026 the subsidiary amended its state complaint to add claims for breach of contract, breach of the implied covenant, tortious interference and unfair competition, alleging the agencies misused confidential subtenant information and diverted a subtenant to a competing City-sponsored project. The agencies moved to dismiss on August 6, 2026, and oral argument is scheduled for January 15, 2027, according to the filing.
