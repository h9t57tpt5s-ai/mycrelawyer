1440 Broadway Foreclosure: SL Green Special Servicer Sues Over $399M CMBS Loan on Amazon-Anchored Times Square Tower

An SL Green special servicing unit has sued to foreclose on 1440 Broadway, the 745,000-square-foot Times Square office building owned by StepStone and anchored by Amazon, over a $399 million CMBS loan, The Real Deal reported, citing Crain's.

A division of SL Green, acting as special servicer of a $399 million CMBS loan, filed a foreclosure suit in Manhattan Supreme Court over 1440 Broadway the week of November 9, 2026, The Real Deal reported, citing Crain's. StepStone has owned the building since a 2023 loan modification; CIM Group and QSuper, which bought it in 2017, were the previous owners. The ownership allegedly defaulted on the mortgage about two years ago, and the suit came a day after Morningstar DBRS raised concerns about Amazon's 2029 lease expiration.

Why it matters

The suit shows that a building can be mostly leased to a top-credit occupier and still land in foreclosure when the loan matures before the capital markets will refinance it. Amazon occupies roughly three quarters of the tower, yet the loan went back to special servicing on a maturity default with weak debt service coverage, and the tenant's lease runs out in 2029. Owners with single-asset CMBS debt and a dominant tenant should read it as a warning about rollover concentration and about how long a servicer's patience lasts.

Full write-up

The special servicer on 1440 Broadway, a 745,000-square-foot office building near Times Square, has gone to court to foreclose. The Real Deal reported on November 12, 2026, citing Crain's, that a division of SL Green, the special servicer of the debt, filed the suit in Manhattan Supreme Court that week. According to the report, the ownership allegedly defaulted on the mortgage about two years ago.

QSuper, the Australian pension investor that is now a division of Australian Retirement Trust, bought the building with CIM Group in 2017. JPMorgan Chase and Oaktree Capital Management originated $399 million of financing in March 2021, and the debt was securitized into commercial mortgage-backed securities. By 2023 the loan was in special servicing and the owners were preparing to hand the property to the lender. That was averted, and StepStone became the owner in 2023 after a loan modification, according to The Real Deal, which updated its story on November 16 to say CIM Group is no longer involved with the property. CIM Group and QSuper did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Morningstar Credit data cited by The Real Deal show the loan went back to special servicing this past June because of a balloon payment or maturity default. Before the loan's interest rate cap is counted, the building's debt service coverage ratio was 0.15, meaning its income covered only a small share of its debt payments.

Amazon is the main tenant. It added 259,000 square feet last year and now occupies about 560,000 square feet, most of the building, in space run by WeWork. That lease expires in 2029, and Morningstar said "significant capital" might be needed then. The suit came a day after Morningstar DBRS flagged the expiration. The reports do not identify the exact plaintiff entity, the defendants, the index number or the amount claimed.
