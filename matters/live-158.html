Eagle Nest Investments, LLC v. Spencer (Pavilion Park Below-Market Land Sale & Breach of Fiduciary Duty Trial)

REIT & Securities Pending
Date
September 16, 2026
Added to tracker
September 19, 2026
Court or forum
Iowa District Court for Polk County
State
Iowa
Amount / scale
Alleged combined shortfall of ~$8.7M across two parcels (126.52-acre commercial parcel sold for $11.4M vs. an alleged $18.34M fair market value; 25.52-acre residential parcel sold for $1.6M vs. an alleged $1.99M fair market value)
Parties
Plaintiff: Eagle Nest Investments LLCPlaintiff: Gratias Properties LLCDefendant: TJK Investments LCDefendant: Wendover Reserve LLCDefendant: Landmark Brokerage Inc.

Minority owners of Pavilion Park LC, a West Des Moines-area development entity, allege that manager William Spencer and his affiliated entities TJK Investments LC and Landmark Brokerage Inc. arranged to sell two Pavilion Park parcels to another Spencer-controlled entity, Wendover Reserve LLC, at prices well below fair market value and over the minority owners' objection. The bench trial concluded in early September 2026 in Iowa District Court for Polk County, with post-trial briefs due October 9, 2026.

Why it matters

Illustrates the fiduciary-duty exposure a managing member of a closely held real estate LLC faces when selling jointly owned land to an entity that member also controls, and shows minority owners using a breach-of-duty and civil-conspiracy theory, rather than a simple valuation dispute, to challenge an insider transaction after the fact.

Original source ↗

A closely watched Iowa bench trial over the sale of roughly 150 acres of prime West Des Moines-area development land wrapped up its evidentiary phase in early September 2026, with post-trial briefs due October 9 in Eagle Nest Investments, LLC, et al. v. Spencer, et al., pending in the Iowa District Court for Polk County. The case, brought by minority owners of Pavilion Park LC against the entity's own manager and his affiliated companies, centers on a pattern increasingly familiar to commercial real estate lawyers nationally: a controlling member selling jointly held land to an entity he also controls, at what the minority owners allege was a steep discount to fair market value.
