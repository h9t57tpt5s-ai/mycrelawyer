Portage Township of St. Joseph County, Indiana v. City of South Bend (Eminent Domain in Redevelopment Areas Requires Council Authorization)

Indiana's Court of Appeals reversed a ruling for South Bend in a condemnation case, holding only a city's legislative body, not its mayor, can authorize eminent domain in a redevelopment project area.

On October 29, 2026, the Court of Appeals of Indiana held that Indiana Code section 36-7-14-20 gives a unit's legislative body exclusive authority to use eminent domain in a redevelopment project area. The City of South Bend, at its mayor's direction, had filed a condemnation complaint in October 2024 over property then owned by Argos 316, LLC without authorization from the South Bend Common Council. The court reversed the Marshall Circuit Court's order overruling the objection raised by Portage Township, which completed its purchase of the property shortly after the suit was filed, and 350 Columbia Street, LLC, which held easements on it.

Why it matters

The ruling makes a legislative-body resolution a prerequisite for condemnation inside an Indiana redevelopment project area, even though other local officials generally hold broad eminent-domain power. Chief Judge Tavitas concurred in the result but questioned the majority's finding that the statute is ambiguous.

Full write-up

In October 2024, the City of South Bend, acting at the direction of its mayor, filed a complaint to condemn real property owned by Argos 316, LLC in a redevelopment project area established by the South Bend Redevelopment Commission. Portage Township of St. Joseph County had already contracted to buy the property and completed the purchase shortly after the City filed. The Township and 350 Columbia Street, LLC, which held easements on the property, intervened and objected that the action was not authorized by the South Bend Common Council, as Indiana Code section 36-7-14-20(a) contemplates. The Marshall Circuit Court, Judge Janette E. Surrisi presiding, overruled the objection, concluding that Council authorization is one way, not the only way, for the City to condemn property in a redevelopment project area.

The Court of Appeals reversed in an opinion by Judge Nancy H. Vaidik, joined by Judge May. The City argued that the statute only sets a procedure for when the legislative body chooses to act and leaves other officials' broad condemnation power under Indiana Code section 36-1-4-5(a) intact; the objectors argued it confines the power to the legislative body. Finding both readings reasonable on the text alone, the court looked to the surrounding chapter, which sets out how a redevelopment commission is created, how a project area is designated with plan commission and legislative body approval, and how the commission must first try to negotiate purchases. Read as a whole, the redevelopment scheme "makes clear that the power lies with the unit's legislative body," the court wrote.

Because the Common Council never authorized the action, the court held the trial court erred in overruling the objection, and it did not reach the objectors' other arguments. Chief Judge Tavitas concurred in the result, noting that neither party had argued the statute was ambiguous.
