Four Mile Capital LLC v. Reynolds South Tower LLC (River's Edge at Manchester Construction-Defect Fraud Suit)

Construction Defect Pending

Parties
Plaintiff: Four Mile CapitalDefendant: Reynolds South Tower LLCDefendant: Purcell Construction Corp.

Four Mile Capital, which bought the 212-unit River's Edge at Manchester apartment building in Richmond for nearly $50M in 2021, sued developer Reynolds South Tower LLC and principals Guy Blundon and Mark Purcell in Richmond Circuit Court in May 2025, alleging fraud, breach of contract, negligence, and business conspiracy over water leaks a 2023 investigation attributed to a building envelope not built to its design, including missing waterproof sheathing Four Mile says it was told had been installed. At an August 7 hearing, the defense moved to dismiss the fraud counts, arguing Four Mile was not misled; Chief Judge Jacqueline S. McClenney said she expects to rule within a few weeks.

Why it matters

Tests how far a seller's negotiated post-closing repair commitment for a known defect can survive scrutiny once an independent investigation reveals the underlying construction problem was materially different, and larger, than represented at the time of sale. Buyers who accept a repair arrangement in lieu of a price reduction for a known issue should treat the case as a guide to preserving a later fraud claim if the seller's account of the defect's cause proves false.

Original source ↗

A $40 million dispute between the developer of a Richmond riverfront apartment tower and the investment firm that bought it is now before the Richmond Circuit Court, testing how far a seller's post-closing repair promises can shield it from a fraud claim once the underlying defects turn out to run deeper than disclosed. Four Mile Capital, the Denver-based investment firm that paid nearly $50 million in 2021 for River's Edge at Manchester, a 212-unit riverfront apartment building at 505 Porter Street in Richmond, Virginia, is asking the court to let a fraud claim against the building's developer proceed to trial. The developer, Reynolds South Tower LLC, an entity associated with Guy Blundon of CMB Development and Mark Purcell of Purcell Construction Corp., is fighting to have that fraud count dismissed, arguing at an August 7 hearing that Four Mile was never misled about the condition of the building it bought.
