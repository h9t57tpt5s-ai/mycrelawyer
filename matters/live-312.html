Shadowbox Studios and affiliated real estate company Henrico 183 sued DeKalb County, Georgia in DeKalb County Superior Court on Aug. 5, 2026, asking a judge to declare the county's data center moratorium invalid and let them build a data center on a nearly 200-acre site at 3600 International Park Drive, Decaturish reported. The suit says the companies spent more than $30 million on the project after county planning officials told them a data center would be allowed, and that the moratorium has cut the property's value by $200 million. It also seeks "just compensation" and attorneys' fees; the county has said the planning letters were not legally binding.

Why it matters

The case tests whether written assurances from planning staff that a use is allowed can support claims for relief once a moratorium blocks the project. It also puts at issue the procedure the county used to adopt and extend its data center moratorium.

Full write-up

Shadowbox Studios and an affiliated real estate company, Henrico 183, sued DeKalb County in DeKalb County Superior Court on Aug. 5, 2026, Decaturish reported in a story published by WABE. They ask a judge to declare the county's data center moratorium invalid, allow them to build a data center on a nearly 200-acre property at 3600 International Park Drive in south DeKalb, and award "just compensation" for the alleged violation of their property rights, plus attorneys' fees. The suit says the companies have spent more than $30 million developing the site for a data center and that the moratorium has cut its value by $200 million. A county spokesperson said the county does not comment on pending litigation.

According to the complaint as described by Decaturish, Henrico bought the land in 2021 to expand the studio, and the county issued a land disturbance permit for a film studio in 2022. After demand for studio space fell, the companies asked planning officials whether the site's light-industrial zoning allowed a data center. In emails and letters between June 2023 and February 2025, officials said a data center would be treated as a communication utility or a warehouse and allowed. On July 8, 2025, the Board of Commissioners voted 4-3 for a temporary moratorium; planning officials then acknowledged sending the zoning letters but said they were not legally binding. The suit alleges county staff agreed to a July 17, 2025 meeting about revising the permit while knowing the freeze was coming, and that the county "deliberately misled" the companies.

The county has extended the moratorium several times, most recently on July 7, 2026, through March 30, 2027, weeks after commissioners rejected planning staff's proposed data center regulations. The suit says that rejection shows the moratorium is a pretext to ban data centers indefinitely. County officials denied the companies' March 2026 request to change the studio permit to a data center permit.

On Sept. 9, 2026, the county's Zoning Board of Appeals voted to affirm that denial, citing the pending lawsuit, Atlanta News First reported; one board member said the dispute "seems to be a judicial matter." Counsel for Shadowbox told the board the moratorium was passed and extended without notice or an opportunity to be heard, and said the studio had spent $37 million preparing the land.
