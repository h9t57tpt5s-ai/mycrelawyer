Sierra Club v. Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources: Settlement Leaves Vantage's $15 Billion Port Washington Data Center Permits Intact and Has Vantage Fund a Statewide Data Center Impact Study

The Sierra Club settled its suit over permits for Vantage's $15 billion Port Washington data center campus; the permits stand and Vantage will fund a statewide data center impact study.

The Sierra Club, the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources and Vantage Data Centers settled on October 16, 2026 the Sierra Club's suit over the DNR's decision to permit Vantage's $15 billion Lighthouse data center campus in Port Washington without an environmental impact statement, WPR reported. The permits remain valid and no further environmental review of them is required. In return, the DNR will complete a statewide analysis of AI data center build-out and its effects by January 31, 2028, paid for by Vantage, with the DNR keeping sole control of the study.

Why it matters

The settlement leaves the permits for one of Wisconsin's largest data center projects in place while moving the environmental debate to a statewide study. That study's findings could shape how regulators and opponents approach the next large campus that seeks permits, though the deal does not pause permitting while it is under way.

Full write-up

Denver-based Vantage Data Centers is developing the roughly $15 billion Lighthouse campus in Port Washington, Wisconsin, with Oracle and OpenAI, according to WPR. The campus would include four data centers on roughly 670 acres. Its first phase would draw about 1.3 gigawatts of power, with potential growth to 3.5 gigawatts.

In July 2026, an environmental law nonprofit sued the DNR in Ozaukee County Circuit Court on behalf of the Sierra Club, challenging the agency's decision to issue permits for the project without an environmental impact statement. WPR reported that the groups had obtained emails showing the agency first told Vantage officials that an impact statement would be required, and that company officials were "reviewing (and panicking) internally" about it.

The settlement, reached October 16 and signed by the DNR, the Sierra Club and Vantage, ends the case without an impact statement for Lighthouse. The parties agreed that the permits remain valid and that no further environmental review of those approvals is required. The Sierra Club will withdraw its petition for a contested case hearing, and the DNR will provide records the groups had requested.

By January 31, 2028, the DNR must analyze the direct, indirect and cumulative effects of planned AI data center build-out and related infrastructure on agriculture, air and water quality, climate, energy, the economy, cultural resources and other areas. The agency will take public comment, hold one or more public hearings and summarize effects on energy bills. Vantage will pay for the analysis, the DNR will keep sole control of it, and the agency keeps full authority to decide whether future projects comply with state law. The deal does not halt permitting while the review runs.

A Vantage spokesperson said the company "supports a thoughtful, fact-based discussion about responsible data center development." Counsel for the Sierra Club said the statewide study would provide "a holistic, comprehensive look" at environmental impacts. WPR reported that data centers have become an issue in the governor's race.
