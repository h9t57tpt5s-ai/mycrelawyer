State of Idaho, Idaho Transportation Board v. Triple Crown Development, LLC (Business-Damages Condemnation Appeal)

The Idaho Supreme Court affirmed summary judgment against business-damages claims brought by Triple Crown Development, LLC and affiliated Thueson-controlled entities after the state condemned Triple Crown's land in Canyon County for a highway interchange. The Court held that Idaho Code section 7-711(2)'s business-damages remedy belongs only to the fee owner of the condemned parcel, not commonly controlled businesses on adjoining land, and separately rejected the state's request for attorney fees.

Why it matters

Clarifies that Idaho's statutory business-damages remedy tracks legal title to the condemned parcel rather than economic interdependence among commonly controlled entities, and confirms that a never-permitted, never-operated business plan cannot support a business-damages award even for the actual fee owner.

Original source ↗

The Idaho Supreme Court has affirmed a ruling that sharply limits which businesses can recover "business damages" when the state condemns land for a highway project, rejecting an attempt by a family of commonly controlled companies to share in compensation for a taking that struck only one of their parcels. In a decision issued in Docket No. 52872, the Court sided with the State of Idaho, Idaho Transportation Board against Triple Crown Development, LLC and a group of intervenor entities controlled by developer Lance Thueson, holding that Idaho Code section 7-711(2)'s business-damages provision belongs to the fee owner of the condemned property alone.
