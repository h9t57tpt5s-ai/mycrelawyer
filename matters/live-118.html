The longtime operator of the Original Saugus Café in Santa Clarita and his company, The Original Saugus Café LLC, sued North Valley Construction Co. The 16-count complaint alleges damages exceeding $1 million.

Why it matters

The case tests a commercial tenant's claims over a lockout carried out without a court process, together with a fight over who owns a long-running restaurant's name. The allegations have not been tested in court.

Full write-up

The longtime operator of the Original Saugus Café, a Santa Clarita restaurant whose history KHTS traces to the late 1800s, sued North Valley Construction Co. LLC and two individuals tied to the landlord family's trust on January 14, 2026 in Los Angeles County Superior Court's Chatsworth Courthouse, KHTS reported. The plaintiffs are the operator, individually and doing business as the café, and The Original Saugus Café LLC.

The operator says he bought and began running the restaurant in 1998 and for nearly three decades operated under a month-to-month oral lease with the landlord family. He alleges that after the longtime landlord died in August 2025 he was told he had to sign a formal rental agreement to stay, and signed it under duress to avoid an immediate lockout, and that the lease wrongly stated the landlord supplied equipment and fixtures he says he owned. He also alleges the defendants tried in late August 2025 to file trademark applications for "The Original Saugus Café," "Saugus Café" and similar names without his consent, and that he was never formally told the property had been placed under North Valley Construction Co. LLC.

On January 5, 2026, the operator says, he arrived to find the locks changed and a notice on the door saying the café would be "reopening under new management soon," and was kept from retrieving equipment, supplies, alcohol inventory and other property. The 16-count complaint includes breach of contract, improper self-help eviction under California Civil Code § 789.3, improper removal of tenant property, state and federal trademark infringement and unfair competition, conversion, interference and fraud-in-the-inducement claims. It alleges damages exceeding $1 million and seeks compensatory and punitive damages, attorney's fees and injunctive relief. KHTS noted the allegations have not been tested in court and reported no response from the defendants; a case management conference was set for June 23, 2026.
