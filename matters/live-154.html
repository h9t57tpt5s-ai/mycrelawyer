Why it matters

Members of a closely held real estate LLC are protected only as far as its consent provisions are honored; here the plaintiffs say a unanimous-consent requirement was bypassed. Families and co-investors holding property through private LLCs should check who can amend or dissolve the entity and what notice members are owed.

Full write-up

Simon Property Group co-founder Herb Simon has sued the family of his late nephew, David Simon, over the restructuring of a private real estate company, according to The Real Deal, citing Inside Indiana Business. Herb Simon and several affiliated entities filed suit against 17 defendants in Indiana state court in late July 2026. The case concerns SFG Co., formed in 1995 to hold property interests that stayed outside Simon Property Group when the REIT went public.

The complaint alleges David Simon used his control of SFG Manager LLC to dissolve SFG Co. and, less than two weeks before he died of pancreatic cancer in March 2026, transfer its assets into a new entity, SFG HoldCo. The new operating agreement allegedly removed long-standing distribution preferences for some equity holders, gave management more discretion over asset sales and waived fiduciary duties owed to members. Herb Simon and other preferred equity holders say they learned of the transaction only after SFG Co. had been dissolved, when they received papers stating they had been automatically admitted to HoldCo and were deemed to accept an operating agreement they had never seen or signed.

The plaintiffs argue the restructuring violated SFG Co.'s operating agreement, which they say required unanimous consent to change preferred distribution rights or to take steps that would make it impossible for the company to keep operating. The suit says talks between Herb and David over a broader liquidation had broken down, with David seeking the right to buy family members' interests at steep discounts and Herb insisting on fair-market valuations, and it alleges David then achieved 'by fiat what he could not get through negotiations.' The plaintiffs ask the court to unwind the restructuring, restore the prior management structure and award compensatory and punitive damages.

Eli Simon, David's son, who succeeded his father as CEO of Simon Property Group and heads SFG Manager, dismissed the suit as a 'meritless complaint' and said he remains focused on leading the Indianapolis-based company and delivering long-term value for shareholders. The Real Deal noted the family has litigated before: after Mel Simon died in 2009, his children fought his widow, Bren Simon, over control of his estate.
