Advenir Azora Development, LLC v. City of Hendersonville (Zoning Denial Suit — Partial Dismissal)

A Henderson County judge kept Advenir Azora's arbitrary-and-capricious claim alive but dismissed two others in its suit over Hendersonville's denial of a 180-unit, 21-acre rental rezoning.

Special Superior Court Judge Troy J. Stafford dismissed two of three counts in Miami-based developer Advenir Azora Development LLC's suit over the Hendersonville City Council's January 2026 denial of its rezoning request for a 180-unit rental community on Haywood Road, the Hendersonville Lightning reported. The dismissed counts alleged that the denial was not authorized by statute and that the city's conditional zoning provisions are unconstitutional. The claim that the denial was arbitrary and capricious remains, and no hearing date has been set.

Why it matters

The city keeps its conditional zoning process intact for now, while the developer's challenge to the denial itself goes forward. Whether the council's January 2026 decision stands now turns on the remaining arbitrary-and-capricious claim.

Full write-up

The Hendersonville City Council voted unanimously on January 7, 2026 to deny Advenir Azora Development LLC's request to rezone a 21-acre site on Haywood Road east of Blythe Street for a 180-unit development, after the planning board had unanimously recommended denial, the Hendersonville Lightning reported. The Miami-based developer proposed a rental community of duplexes, triplexes and quadplexes, renamed LEO of Hendersonville, and had asked to change the land from R-15 zoning to a planned residential conditional zoning district.

The developer sued in March 2026. According to the Lightning, the complaint alleges the denial was arbitrary and capricious and rested on pretextual reasons rather than competent, substantial evidence, and that city ordinances limiting most "Missing Middle" housing to a conditional rezoning path are invalid under state statutes and the North Carolina Constitution. It seeks declaratory and injunctive relief, court approval of the project and rezoning, and attorney's fees and costs. City Manager John Connet said the city "intends to vigorously defend" the council's land-use authority.

After a three-and-a-half-hour hearing on the city's motion to dismiss, Judge Stafford issued an order on September 1, 2026 dismissing two of the three counts: one alleging the denial was ultra vires, or not authorized by statute, and one alleging the city's conditional zoning provisions are unconstitutional, the Lightning reported. The claim that the denial was arbitrary and capricious survives. No hearing date has been set on it, and the judge gave the city's attorneys 20 days to respond to the developer's claims.

The city said its motion was "granted in significant part," and City Attorney Angela Beeker called the ruling a positive step while acknowledging the developer's right to appeal. Counsel for the developer has argued that the project met all city code standards and that no substantial evidence supported the denial.
