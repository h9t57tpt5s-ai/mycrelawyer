SCLS Realty, LLC v. Town of Johnston (Eminent Domain Suit and Contempt Motion)

After a July 28 ruling against Johnston, RI's condemnation of a 31-acre site planned for 252 affordable units, the landowners sought contempt; on Aug. 11 the judge found the town had not violated the injunction.

One week after U.S. District Judge Melissa R. DuBose ruled on July 28, 2026 against Johnston, Rhode Island's attempt to take a 31-acre George Waterman Road property, the landowners asked her to hold town officials in contempt over two July 31 Town Council resolutions, the Rhode Island Current reported. DuBose ordered the town to show cause, but on August 11 she found the town's actions did not violate the April 2025 preliminary injunction and canceled the hearing, according to the court docket. The case continues in discovery.

Why it matters

The court found that putting a charter amendment on the ballot and creating a building authority did not breach the injunction against the condemnation. The landowners' remaining claims are still pending.

Full write-up

U.S. District Judge Melissa R. DuBose on July 28, 2026 granted the landowners partial summary judgment on one count of their suit against the Town of Johnston, a ruling the Rhode Island Current described as finding the town's attempted seizure of a 31-acre property for a proposed apartment complex unlawful. The owners' companies planned a 252-unit affordable housing complex on the site on George Waterman Road.

The dispute dates to December 2024, when Mayor Joseph Polisena Jr. issued a public letter opposing the project and warned the town would 'fight back' using 'all the power of government' available, the Current reported. Polisena said he wanted the land for a new public safety complex and town hall. On March 10, 2025, after an appraisal valued the property at $775,000, the Town Council voted 5-0 to begin condemning the site, and the owners sued the same night, arguing the taking was pretextual. DuBose issued a temporary restraining order on March 19, 2025, followed by a preliminary injunction.

On August 4, 2026, the owners moved for contempt, arguing that two resolutions passed at a July 31 special council meeting, one putting a charter amendment on the November ballot to expand the town's condemnation authority and one creating a Municipal Public Building Authority, violated the injunction. DuBose ordered the town to show cause and to produce the meeting's agenda, minutes and resolutions; a town spokesperson told the Current that 'motions cannot be ignored.' On August 11, after reviewing the town's response, DuBose found the town's actions on and around July 31 did not violate the April 2, 2025 injunction and canceled the show-cause hearing, according to the docket. On August 28 she denied the town's request for a separate final judgment on the count it lost, and the case is in discovery.
