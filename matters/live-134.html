The condo association at the 239-unit Palm Beach Marriott Singer Island is seeking punitive damages and a freeze on fees charged by hotel owner Urgo Hotels; its lawyer says fees charged since 2013 could top $45 million. Trial is set for early 2027.

The condominium association for the 239-unit Palm Beach Marriott Singer Island in Riviera Beach, Florida, sued the hotel's owner, Urgo Hotels, in 2015, alleging unit owners were charged duplicative fees for the upkeep of shared facilities. In August 2026 the association asked the court for punitive damages and a temporary stay of the fees ahead of a trial set for early 2027. Its attorney, Stevan Pardo, told The Real Deal the fees charged since 2013 could exceed $45 million. Urgo has argued in court filings that a 2013 settlement of an earlier suit should have ended the case.

Why it matters

In a hotel-condominium the hotel owner usually controls the shared facilities and sets what unit owners pay for them, so disputes turn on whether the fees track real costs. Hotel owners and associations both benefit from itemized accounting of formula-based charges such as per-occupied-day fees, because disputes over those charges can run for years.

Full write-up

Unit owners at the Palm Beach Marriott Singer Island, a 239-unit hotel-condominium at 3800 North Ocean Drive in Riviera Beach, Florida, have spent more than a decade in court with the hotel's owner, Urgo Hotels, over fees they call unfair and duplicative, The Real Deal reported. The condo association sued Urgo in 2015, claiming owners were billed for maintaining shared hotel facilities with no assurance the charges were fair. In August 2026, association attorneys Stevan Pardo and Greg Weiss asked the court for punitive damages and a temporary stay of the fees while the case awaits trial early next year.

Maryland-based Urgo, led by CEO Donald Urgo, bought the building in 2009 for $7.1 million from developer WCI Communities and rebranded it under the Marriott flag. The association first sued Urgo in 2010 over shared-facility maintenance charges, and that case settled in 2013, according to court documents. Owners later said the overcharging continued and asked Urgo for its accounting records; after Urgo produced an audit, they accused it of using an arbitrary formula, not its actual expenses, to set shared-facility costs.

One disputed charge is an administrative fee of about $45 for each day a unit is occupied, which court records say covers the cost of 'integrating' hotel guests into the building. Owners also pay a quarterly assessment for shared facilities, and the suit claims both charges are excessive and make owners pay twice for the same upkeep. Pardo told The Real Deal the fees collected since 2013 could total more than $45 million.

Urgo's lawyers have argued in court filings that the 2010 case, which was settled, barred this lawsuit from going forward. The company also says some owners no longer back the suit and that the association's board put off elections to keep opponents from gaining control. Ronald Gache, a lawyer for Urgo and its entity UH-SI LLC, declined to comment to The Real Deal. Association president Marina Shtutman told the publication the board would prefer to avoid long, costly litigation but sees the courts as the only way to hold Urgo accountable.
