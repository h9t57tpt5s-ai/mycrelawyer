Why it matters

Shows how fast a CMBS loan on a lightly leased Manhattan office building can move from missed payments to a foreclosure complaint that asks for a receiver at the outset. When most of a building's space has gone dark, the lender's case for taking control of the property is easier to make early, and a consent order on a receiver can come within weeks of filing.

Full write-up

The lender on a $37.5 million commercial mortgage-backed securities loan has sued in federal court to foreclose on 10 East 34th Street, a 10-story, 55,000-square-foot office building between Fifth and Madison avenues, across the street from the Empire State Building. The complaint, filed August 14, 2026, in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York (No. 1:26-cv-06952), names the borrower, 10East34th, LLC, and asks the court both to foreclose and to appoint a receiver to run the property.

According to The Real Deal, Caerus Group bought the building for $51.7 million in 2016, and it soon changed hands again. The current owner took out a 10-year, $37.5 million mortgage from Morgan Stanley in 2018, according to court documents cited by The Real Deal, and the loan was packaged into the BANK 2018-BNK13 CMBS trust. Caerus is not the owner or a party to the case.

The complaint alleges that the owner stopped making monthly payments on April 1, 2026. Servicer Trimont sent a letter on May 6 asking for more than $500,000 to cover the missed April and May payments; June and July passed without payments, and the lender's lawyers sent another letter on June 30. The lender alleges that occupancy has fallen sharply over the past two years and that the lead tenant stopped paying rent for several months. The ground-floor retail tenant, a virtual reality venue, has closed, and the two remaining tenants occupy less than 30 percent of the building, The Real Deal reported.

The case is before Judge Ronnie Abrams. In October the parties submitted a proposed stipulation and consent order on the appointment of a receiver, and the court asked them to propose the amount of the receiver's undertaking. After an October 30 status conference, the court ordered a joint status letter by December 1, 2026.
