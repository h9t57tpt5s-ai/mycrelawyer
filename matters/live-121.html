ATC Indoor DAS LLC v. MM CCM 48M Leasing, LLC and MM CCM 48M, LLC (Collin Creek Mall Lease Dispute)

A Dallas appeals court held the buyer of Collin Creek Mall could not invoke impracticability to excuse ending ATC Indoor DAS's antenna lease, which it had assumed and performed, and rendered judgment for ATC.

The Fifth Court of Appeals in Dallas reversed a summary judgment for the owners of the closed Collin Creek Mall in Plano and rendered judgment for wireless-infrastructure tenant ATC Indoor DAS LLC on its breach-of-contract claim, according to the Texas Civil Justice League's summary of the January 29, 2026 opinion. MM CCM 48M, LLC bought the mall in 2018, assumed ATC's host lease and performed under it for several months before a letter from MM CCM 48M Leasing, LLC ended the lease, said the mall had closed and gave ATC 30 days to remove its equipment. The court held the buyer was quasi-estopped from claiming impracticability and that the defense failed anyway because shifting market conditions do not excuse performance; it remanded ATC's attorney's fees claim and its claims against MM CCM 48M Leasing.

Why it matters

The decision confirms that a buyer who knowingly acquires a declining property, assumes its leases and performs under them cannot later argue that the property's decline made those leases impracticable. Buyers of retail properties with specialty tenants, such as antenna, rooftop or signage leases, should treat assumed leases as binding even if they plan to close or redevelop the property.

Full write-up

ATC Indoor DAS LLC installs and maintains distributed antenna systems that improve wireless coverage inside large buildings. Through a predecessor, it entered a host lease with Collin Creek Mall, L.P., the owner of Collin Creek Mall in Plano, Texas. The lease let ATC license space to AT&T and Verizon, which paid ATC for better in-mall coverage, and ATC paid the landlord 25% of that revenue, according to the Texas Civil Justice League's summary of the case. Both carrier licenses ended automatically if ATC stopped being the tenant.

By 2018 the mall was in serious financial trouble. MM CCM 48M, LLC bought it, assumed the lease and agreed to perform its covenants, including quiet enjoyment. A few months later, MM CCM 48M Leasing, LLC sent ATC a letter, as successor to the original landlord, ending the lease, saying the mall had closed and telling ATC to remove its equipment within 30 days. ATC removed the equipment, lost its carrier rent and sent the landlord its final 25% share, which was cashed. ATC sued for breach of contract and attorney's fees. The owner raised impossibility or impracticability, arguing that when the lease was signed it was not foreseeable the mall would lose its customers, and MM CCM 48M Leasing argued it was not a landlord under the lease. The 471st District Court of Collin County ruled for the owners, signing judgment on June 24, 2024.

In an opinion by Justice Emily Miskel, the Fifth Court of Appeals reversed. The court's docket shows that a December 18, 2025 opinion was withdrawn and replaced by the January 29, 2026 memorandum opinion. The court held ATC proved as a matter of law that the owner was quasi-estopped from asserting impracticability, because it bought the mall knowing its condition, expressly agreed to perform the lease and performed for months. Separately, it held the defense failed as a matter of law because shifting market conditions, including a mall's loss of customers, do not make performance impracticable under Texas law. The court rendered judgment for ATC on breach of contract, found a fact issue on whether MM CCM 48M Leasing is a proper party, and remanded ATC's fee claim and its claims against MM CCM 48M Leasing. The mandate issued April 15, 2026.

For buyers of retail properties, the ruling is a reminder that specialty leases such as antenna, rooftop and signage agreements can survive a mall's closing, and that assuming and performing such a lease makes it hard to argue later that the property's decline excuses performance.
