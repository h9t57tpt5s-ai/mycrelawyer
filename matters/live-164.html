NSI, A.1 LLC v. City of Springfield (Developer Sues to Block Springfield's Data Center Moratorium)

NSI, A.1 LLC sued Springfield, Missouri to block its 120-day data center moratorium, saying the city twice confirmed in writing that the site's PD 251 zoning allowed the planned use.

Developer NSI, A.1 LLC sued the City of Springfield in Greene County Circuit Court on September 17, 2026, seeking to block the 120-day data center moratorium the City Council adopted June 29, the Springfield Business Journal reported. The six-count petition argues the moratorium is zoning legislation in substance that did not follow Missouri's planning and zoning law, was not validly enacted under the city charter's rules for emergency and one-reading bills, and should not apply to a project the developer says the city confirmed in writing was allowed under the site's PD 251 zoning. A separate count alleges the city violated the Sunshine Law in handling a public records request. SBJ reported in November that the judge denied NSI's request for a preliminary injunction.

Why it matters

The case tests whether a city can pause a use through a moratorium after its staff has issued written zoning verifications for a specific project, and whether that moratorium had to follow the procedures that govern zoning changes. Developers who spend on design and predevelopment in reliance on a city's written zoning confirmation will watch how the court treats that reliance.

Full write-up

Developer NSI, A.1 LLC filed a 31-page, six-count petition against the City of Springfield, Missouri in Greene County Circuit Court on September 17, 2026, according to the Springfield Business Journal. The suit targets the 120-day moratorium on data center development applications that the City Council adopted on June 29. Citing its past coverage, SBJ reported that the developer's preapplication meeting on the project prompted the council's freeze.

According to SBJ, the developer bought a 2.8-acre parcel at 2900 S. National Ave. with two vacant commercial buildings, planning a headquarters there for Lumon Solutions LLC, an affiliated company. The developer told the paper the office would start with 10 to 15 employees and that a basement would hold a small edge data center drawing 5 to 10 megawatts. In a statement to SBJ, the developer said the city twice issued written zoning verification that the headquarters could include a basement data-processing center, and that the city changed its position after the company relied on those determinations.

The petition asks the court to find that the moratorium is "zoning legislation in substance" that does not comply with Missouri law on municipal planning and zoning, and that it "was not validly enacted" under the city charter's provisions on emergency and one-reading bills. It alleges NSI incurred planning, design, professional and project-development expense in reliance on PD 251's text and city staff communications. NSI asks Judge Nathan Taylor to enjoin the city from applying or enforcing the moratorium, while saying any injunction should not approve the project, guarantee utility service or decide the final interpretation of PD 251. A separate count alleges the city violated Missouri's Sunshine Law in handling NSI's public records request and seeks production of records, a civil penalty, attorney's fees and costs.

SBJ said the city did not immediately respond to requests for comment and that city officials typically do not comment on pending litigation. In a November 10 follow-up, SBJ reported that the judge denied NSI's request for a preliminary injunction against the moratorium, which the paper said was set to expire November 16 and could be extended to February 24.
