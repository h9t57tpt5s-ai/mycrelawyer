CommunityAmerica Credit Union filed suit in Cook County Circuit Court on November 3, 2026 to foreclose the $220 million loan on 35 West Wacker Drive, the 50-story Leo Burnett Building, claiming $236 million is due from borrower Liberty 35 Wacker LLC, an entity of Shaya Prager's Opal Holdings. The complaint cites unpaid property taxes, missed loan payments since July, and a default triggered when a bankruptcy case against Prager, who guaranteed the loan, was not dismissed within 90 days. The credit union says it will separately ask the court to appoint a receiver. Prager and Opal did not immediately respond to The Real Deal's requests for comment.

Why it matters

A well-occupied tower can still end up in foreclosure when the sponsor's wider finances trigger loan defaults, here through unpaid taxes and a clause tied to a bankruptcy case against the guarantor. Lenders and co-investors should read guaranty and bankruptcy-default provisions closely, because a pending case against a guarantor can both trigger a default and delay collection from that guarantor.

Full write-up

CommunityAmerica Credit Union, a Kansas-based lender, sued in Cook County Circuit Court on November 3, 2026 to foreclose the $220 million loan on 35 West Wacker Drive, the 50-story, roughly 1.1 million-square-foot Loop office tower known as the Leo Burnett Building, The Real Deal reported. The suit claims $236 million is due from the borrower, Liberty 35 Wacker LLC, an entity of Shaya Prager's Opal Holdings. CommunityAmerica acquired the loan in its 2025 merger with the lender that originated it, Unify Financial Credit Union, and says it will separately seek a court-appointed receiver for the tower.

The complaint lists three defaults. It says the first installment of the tower's 2025 property taxes went unpaid in April, after which the credit union sent a default notice; no loan payment has been made since July, and the second tax installment also went unpaid. CommunityAmerica says it has advanced $13.5 million to pay the taxes, added that to the loan balance, and that interest is running at about $64,700 a day. The third default is the bankruptcy case against Prager: under the loan agreement, a bankruptcy case against him had to be dismissed within 90 days, and the July 27 deadline passed with the case still pending. In June, the credit union had offered the loan for sale as part of a $409 million package of nine loans, and Commercial Mortgage Alert described it at the time as performing and the tower as more than 90 percent occupied, according to The Real Deal.

Opal and its partner Prana Equities bought the tower for $415 million in 2022. According to the report, Prana kept the land and borrowed $217 million against it, while Opal took the building under a 99-year ground lease and borrowed $220 million from Unify at a 4.1 percent fixed rate. Prager is a personal guarantor of the loan but is not named in the suit, since the pending bankruptcy protects him for the time being; the suit instead names a second Prager entity that also guaranteed the loan, Liberty 35 Wacker Member LLC.

Creditors filed in April to put Prager and his wife into involuntary Chapter 7 bankruptcy. Both are contesting the petitions, and a trial on their motion to dismiss is set for February 10, The Real Deal reported. The publication said lenders have obtained more than $122 million in personal judgments against Prager, including $36.2 million in litigation over Normandale Lake Office Park in Minnesota and $15 million in a Wells Fargo foreclosure in Florida, and that GreenState Credit Union took over the leasehold at the Corporate 500 campus in Deerfield through a sheriff's sale in February. Prager, his attorneys, Opal Holdings, CommunityAmerica and its attorneys did not immediately respond to requests for comment.
