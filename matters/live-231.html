A federal judge dismissed DC Blox unit DCB Nashville's claims that Metro's moratorium targeted its data center site by the Nashville Zoo, letting it refile three; it filed an amended complaint.

DCB Nashville, LLC, a subsidiary of DC Blox, sued the Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County in federal court on August 11, 2026, seeking to strike down Metro's data center moratorium as applied to its planned data center next to the Nashville Zoo, Axios Nashville reported. The company alleges the moratorium targeted its project. On October 6, 2026, U.S. District Judge Eli J. Richardson dismissed all nine counts in one form or another and denied a preliminary injunction as moot, while allowing the company 21 days to refile three constitutional counts. It filed an amended complaint on October 27.

Why it matters

The case tests whether a data center developer can challenge a municipal moratorium in federal court as a targeted act against one project. The court's ruling turned on the pleading of property interests and comparators and left the state-law vested-rights claims out of federal court for now.

Full write-up

DCB Nashville, LLC, a subsidiary of DC BLOX Parent LLC, sued Metro Nashville in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Tennessee on August 11, 2026, seeking to strike down the city's moratorium on data centers, Axios Nashville reported. According to the court's later opinion, the company closed on the purchase of about 23.49 acres at 648 and 650 Grassmere Park Drive, next to the Nashville Zoo, for about $23 million on July 16, 2026. The complaint says Asurion previously ran office and data center functions on the property under Metro permits.

Axios reported that after the zoo raised concerns about the project's effect on its animals and more than 500,000 people signed a petition opposing it, the Metro Council imposed a moratorium until December 1 and created new rules for data centers. The lawsuit calls the moratorium "a targeted attack against DC Blox," alleges that Metro kept processing another pending data center application while putting the company's demolition, grading and building permits on hold, and argues the project is vested because a data center was allowed there when it applied. Axios reported that Metro officials have signaled they disagree that the property is vested. The company had offered to subdivide the property so the zoo could acquire the remainder.

On October 6, Judge Eli J. Richardson granted Metro's motion to dismiss the class-of-one equal protection count, finding no similarly situated comparator plausibly alleged. He also dismissed the substantive due process, procedural due process and takings counts, which depend on a protected property interest, without prejudice to refiling within 21 days. He declined jurisdiction over five state-law declaratory judgment counts, dismissing them without prejudice, and denied the preliminary injunction motion as moot. The docket shows DCB Nashville filed an amended complaint and a motion for reconsideration on October 27, and the court ruled on that motion on October 29.
