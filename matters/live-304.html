Straus Group v. Antheus Capital (Lake Meadows Investor Buyout Dispute)

Straus Group affiliates sued Antheus Capital, claiming it is blocking a put option to buy out their $30.5 million stake in Chicago's 1,869-unit Lake Meadows complex, Bisnow reported.

Two companies affiliated with The Straus Group, which invested $30.5 million in the entity that owns Chicago's 1,869-unit Lake Meadows apartment complex, sued in Cook County Circuit Court on Oct. 6, 2026, claiming owner Antheus Capital is blocking their exit, Bisnow and Crain's Chicago Business reported. They allege a put option was triggered because emptying the buildings for renovation is no longer part of the business plan; a lawyer for the Antheus affiliate responded that neither trigger had occurred. The plaintiffs seek a court-ordered valuation and damages equal to the fair market value of their stake, which a document filed with the suit puts at $70 million as of June.

Why it matters

The case turns on how the put option's trigger language applies when the owner keeps renovating with residents in place rather than emptying buildings. The answer decides whether the investors can force a buyout or sale before the agreement's 10-year date.

Full write-up

Two companies affiliated with The Straus Group, a Fort Lee, New Jersey-based private investment office, sued in Cook County Circuit Court on Oct. 6, 2026, claiming Antheus Capital is blocking their exit from the Lake Meadows apartment complex in Bronzeville, Bisnow reported. Crain's Chicago Business, which also reported the suit, said it alleges financial misconduct by Antheus, a major South Side landlord. Lake Meadows Holdings LLC, an affiliate of Englewood, New Jersey-based Antheus, bought the 1,869-unit complex at 3233 S. King Drive for $161 million in 2023, and the Straus affiliates invested $30.5 million in it, according to the lawsuit.

The Straus affiliates say their investment carried a put option giving them a one-time right to require another Antheus subsidiary to buy their interests at fair market value or to market the property for sale. According to the suit, the option becomes exercisable at the earliest of the 10-year anniversary of the agreement, completion of the property's renovation, or the date renovations are no longer part of the company's plans. They allege that Antheus co-founder Eli Unger presented investors with two business plans, one renovating with residents in place and one emptying buildings for major work, and that "the emptying of the Lake Meadows buildings has, for some time, no longer been part of the company's business plan," which they say triggers the option.

The plaintiffs say they tried to exercise the option in September and, 10 days later, received a letter from a lawyer for the Antheus affiliate saying neither trigger had occurred because the 10-year anniversary had not passed and renovations were ongoing. The suit says Antheus has spent about $10 million on upgrades since the purchase without relocating any residents. The plaintiffs ask the court to compel a valuation process and award damages equal to the fair market value of their investment, which a document filed with the suit puts at $70 million as of June. Neither Antheus nor Straus could immediately be reached for comment, Bisnow reported.
