A Cole County judge let Preserve Montgomery County's suit over an Amazon data center proceed, finding the group has standing, but dismissed a grant-monitoring claim against Missouri's economic development agency.

Cole County Circuit Judge Cotton Walker ruled on Sept. 18, 2026 that the advocacy group Preserve Montgomery County has standing to pursue most of its lawsuit against Montgomery County, Missouri over an Amazon data center, the Columbia Missourian reported in a story published by KBIA. The group, which sued in February to halt the project, alleges the county and state failed to post proper public notice, did not adequately disclose the data centers' water and electricity use, and did not fully inform residents of the tax-abatement terms. The judge dismissed a claim that the Missouri Department of Economic Development failed to properly monitor an American Rescue Plan Act grant agreement, removing the agency from the case.

Why it matters

The ruling lets a citizen group press public-notice and disclosure claims over a county's approvals and tax abatement for a large data center project, though it does not decide the merits. The county and state have argued there is no factual evidence supporting the claims.

Full write-up

Cole County Circuit Judge Cotton Walker has ruled that Preserve Montgomery County, an advocacy group opposed to data centers in Montgomery County, Missouri, can continue its lawsuit over the county's handling of an Amazon data center project, the Columbia Missourian reported in a story published by KBIA through the Missouri News Network. The ruling came Friday, Sept. 18, 2026; the judge had taken the motion to dismiss under advisement after hearing arguments in August. According to the report, Google and Amazon are building data centers covering a combined 1,900 acres on opposite sides of Interstate 70 east of New Florence.

The group sued Montgomery County and the Missouri Department of Economic Development in February in an effort to halt the Amazon data center. It argues the county and state did not post proper public notice of the project, did not adequately provide information about the data centers' water and electricity use, and did not fully inform residents of the terms of the tax abatement. A separate claim alleged the department failed to properly monitor and administer a grant agreement involving American Rescue Plan Act funds.

Attorneys for the county and the department moved to dismiss in March, arguing there is no factual evidence to support the claims. Judge Walker denied the motion as to most of the suit, finding the group has standing to assert its claims, but dismissed the ARPA grant-monitoring claim and removed the department as a party. The ruling does not decide the merits, and no further hearings had been scheduled, according to the report.
