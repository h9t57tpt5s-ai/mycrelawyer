Preserve Montgomery County v. Montgomery County (Judge Lets Sunshine Law Challenge to Amazon Data Center Incentives Proceed)

Zoning & Land Use Ruling Issued
Date
September 18, 2026
Added to tracker
September 21, 2026
Court or forum
Circuit Court of Cole County, Missouri
State
Missouri
Presiding judge
Cotton Walker
Property type
Data Center
Amount / scale
Not a monetary claim; advocacy group seeks to halt a data center project over alleged Sunshine Law and public-notice violations tied to county-extended incentives and an ARPA-funded grant agreement
Parties
Plaintiff: Preserve Montgomery CountyDefendant: Montgomery County, MissouriDefendant (dismissed): Missouri Department of Economic Development

Cole County Circuit Judge Cotton Walker ruled September 18, 2026 that advocacy group Preserve Montgomery County has standing to continue its challenge to a Montgomery County, Missouri data center project tied to Amazon, denying most of the county's motion to dismiss while dismissing a related claim against the Missouri Department of Economic Development. The suit, filed in February 2026, alleges the county violated Missouri's Sunshine Law and failed to give legally required public notice before extending approvals and incentives to the project.

Why it matters

Confirms that a citizen group can have standing to enforce sunshine-law and public-notice obligations against a county extending incentives to a large data center project, adding real transparency-compliance risk to how Missouri local governments structure data center incentive packages. It is the third distinct Missouri data center matter to reach a court in September 2026, alongside separate developer-driven disputes in Greene and Benton counties.
