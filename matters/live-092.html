Rialto Capital Advisors v. WC MRP Calumet Center, LLC ($45.6M Calumet City Retail Foreclosure)

Special servicer Rialto Capital has sued to foreclose on a Calumet City, Illinois shopping center held by an entity backed by Nate Paul, seeking $45.6 million after a 2025 reinstatement deal broke down, The Real Deal reported.

Rialto Capital, acting as special servicer for a CMBS trust, filed a $45.6 million foreclosure suit in Cook County against WC MRP Calumet Center, LLC, an entity backed by Austin developer Nate Paul's World Class Holdings. The property is a shopping center of more than 159,000 square feet at 410-500 River Oaks West Road in Calumet City, Illinois, and it is one of six retail centers securing a $38.3 million loan, The Real Deal reported. The lawsuit alleges the borrower paid the first $500,000 quarterly installment under a 2025 reinstatement agreement but did not make the December payment or those due in 2026.

Why it matters

A reinstatement agreement protects a borrower only while the payments keep coming; here the suit alleges one missed installment led to a default notice and then acceleration of the debt. Owners whose property is one of several securing a single cross-collateralized loan should watch the whole pool, because the lender can enforce against each property in turn.

Full write-up

Rialto Capital, a Miami-based special servicer, has filed suit in Cook County to foreclose on the Calumet Center shopping center in Calumet City, Illinois, seeking $45.6 million, The Real Deal reported. The defendant is WC MRP Calumet Center, LLC, an entity backed by Austin developer Nate Paul. Rialto acts for the financial institutions that bought the pool of securitized loans that includes this one. Representatives of Paul's firm, World Class Holdings, and the lender's attorneys did not immediately respond to The Real Deal's requests for comment.

According to Cook County records cited by The Real Deal, the Paul-backed entity bought the retail center, more than 159,000 square feet at 410-500 River Oaks West Road, for $7.7 million in 2014. It was later folded into a $38.3 million loan that German American Capital Corporation made in 2015 against six World Class-owned retail centers in Illinois, Iowa, Missouri and Oklahoma. The loan was then pooled with other commercial mortgages and sold to investors.

The suit says an earlier alleged default led the lender to sue in 2022, and that case was dismissed after the borrower came back into compliance through a reinstatement agreement with Rialto signed about a year before this filing. That agreement called for $500,000 quarterly payments beginning in September 2025. According to the complaint, the borrower paid the first installment but not the December payment or the 2026 payments, Rialto sent a notice of default in January 2026, and it accelerated the debt in March. The lawsuit also says the lender has already foreclosed on the portfolio's Kansas City-area property in April 2026 with a $4.6 million credit bid and on its Oklahoma City-area property in July with a $7 million credit bid.

Public loan data cited by The Real Deal shows the portfolio's combined appraised value fell from $56.6 million when the loan was made to $46.3 million as of December, and Fitch said in May that the loan makes up nearly 30 percent of the CMBS trust. The suit comes after Paul's federal criminal case. The Real Deal reported that federal authorities raided his offices in 2019, which led to charges that he submitted false financial statements to secure $172 million of loans, and that in April 2025, after he pleaded guilty to making false statements to financial institutions, a federal judge sentenced him to four months of home confinement, five years of supervised release and a $1 million fine.
