Full write-up

The Dayton's Project, New York developer 601W's $350 million redevelopment of the former Dayton's department store on Nicollet Mall into 1.2 million square feet of office and retail space, is headed toward foreclosure after a judge's recent ruling approving it, Axios Twin Cities reported on April 27, 2026. 601W owes lender Fortress Investment Group about $220 million in unpaid principal, interest and fees, while the city assesses the building at about $27 million.

Axios reported in October 2024 that a Hennepin County judge placed the property in receivership on September 23, 2024, removing 601W from management and operations, according to court documents first reported by Twin Cities Business. Fortress Credit Corp. alleged in its complaint that 601W and its partner, Hightower Initiatives, had missed mortgage payments and owed $177 million in principal, interest and advances, and said it had put up nearly $4 million to avoid the "termination of various utilities and essential services." An attorney for 601W declined to comment at the time.

The project launched in 2017 with plans that included an Andrew Zimmern-curated food hall. The building once reached 28% occupancy but is losing its largest tenant, accounting firm EY, and the food hall has been dropped, Axios reported. At a January 2026 hearing, a Fortress attorney said that if it forecloses, the lender will get "the privilege of owning an empty building that is going to require significant operating costs." Court filings indicate building out the remaining office space would cost about $110 per square foot.

Fortress declined to comment on its plans, and 601W's attorney did not respond to Axios, so it was not clear whether 601W would appeal. No auction date had been set as of the April report.
