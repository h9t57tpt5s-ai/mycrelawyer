Why it matters

The case tests whether an informal inducement a city offered to attract an office tenant can be enforced years later, after a change in administration and a competing city project on the same site. For owners whose buildings rely on one anchor tenant, it shows how the loss of an amenity such as parking can become a lease-retention risk.

Full write-up

A commercial developer's lawsuit against the City of Fresno over downtown parking was set for a jury trial beginning October 12, 2026 before Fresno County Superior Court Judge D. Tyler Tharpe, GV Wire reported on October 2. The developer owns the Grand 1401 building, whose anchor tenant is the construction firm Tutor Perini.

He says the city, under then-Mayor Ashley Swearengin, promised him 100 free parking spaces in perpetuity to help bring Tutor Perini downtown. The city disputes that such an agreement existed, though it honored the arrangement for six years, according to GV Wire. In 2018 the city told him he would have to pay about $6,400 a month for the spaces. Construction of a new parking garage, which Mayor Jerry Dyer calls key to downtown revitalization, has cut off access to the spaces at least for now, and his request to halt the garage work during the case was unsuccessful.

The developer says Tutor Perini will leave if the lot closes and that losing the tenant could cost him more than $7 million; in a January filing he said a full closure would cut his net operating income by more than $543,000 a year. The suit also named Councilmember Miguel Arias, alleging he obstructed the project and sought a campaign donation. Arias denies soliciting a donation, and in May 2022 Judge Tharpe granted Arias's anti-SLAPP motion and dismissed him from the case for lack of evidence, GV Wire reported. Neither city officials nor the developer's lawyers responded to GV Wire's requests for comment.
