Kaanapali Land's KLMC subsidiary filed to foreclose a lien on Newport Hospital Corporation's 14.9-acre West Maui site over an unpaid sewer-line receivable. NHC, which claims KLMC caused delays and wants the agreement voided, moved to stay or dismiss.

Kaanapali Land, LLC disclosed in its Form 10-Q filed November 12, 2026 that its subsidiary, Kaanapali Land Management Corp. (KLMC), filed a complaint in Maui on September 29, 2026 to foreclose a lien on a 14.9-acre West Maui site it sold to Newport Hospital Corporation (NHC) in 2014. KLMC says NHC failed to repay sewer-line costs owed under an Infrastructure Improvement Agreement; NHC moved to stay or dismiss on November 5, 2026. NHC has a pending arbitration claiming KLMC caused delays and cost increases and seeking to void the agreement.

Why it matters

The foreclosure and NHC's arbitration test the same 2014 infrastructure agreement from opposite directions: KLMC seeks to collect under it, while NHC seeks to have it declared void or reformed. Kaanapali Land has fully reserved the receivable and says there is no assurance it will prevail or collect.

Full write-up

According to Kaanapali Land's 10-Q, KLMC sold NHC a parcel of approximately 14.9 acres in West Maui in September 2014 under a property and option purchase agreement. An Infrastructure Improvement Agreement, later amended, commits KLMC to fund up to about $0.6 million for off-site road, sewer and electrical improvements. If KLMC builds those improvements, NHC must repay the costs, with interest, less KLMC's committed amount, and that obligation is secured by a first-priority lien on the site. The site is intended for a critical access hospital, skilled nursing, assisted living and independent living facilities, and Kaanapali Land retains approval rights over its uses and design.

In 2023 KLMC contracted to install a sewer line and paid about $1.1 million, recorded as a receivable from NHC less KLMC's $0.2 million sewer commitment, accruing interest at 6.5 percent. Kaanapali Land has reserved the entire receivable and accrued interest, $1.094 million as of September 30, 2026. The filing says that after NHC failed and refused to pay, KLMC sent a notice of default and demand on September 4, 2026, and when NHC did not cure, filed its foreclosure complaint in Maui on September 29, 2026. NHC moved to stay or dismiss the foreclosure on November 5, 2026.

NHC has its own claims. On August 5, 2024 it served KLMC with a demand for arbitration alleging that KLMC wrongfully caused significant delays, increased costs and related damages in NHC's planning and construction of infrastructure required under the agreement. NHC seeks a declaration that the agreement is void or, alternatively, its reformation, plus damages. KLMC answered and counterclaimed in October 2024. The parties agreed to defer the arbitration while they evaluate an alternative, and it has been rescheduled for June 21-25, 2027 if they cannot agree. Kaanapali Land says KLMC will defend vigorously but that there is no assurance the arbitration will not result in material liability.
