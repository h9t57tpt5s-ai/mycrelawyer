Crocker Park, L.L.C. v. City of Westlake, Ohio (Right of First Refusal Waived for One Sale, Kept for Future Offers)

Right of first refusal over Market Square, which a third-party buyer later purchased for $2.6 million
Parties
Plaintiff-Appellee (Declarant): Crocker Park, L.L.C.Plaintiff-Appellee (filed the lien): CP Commercial Delaware, L.L.C.Third-Party Defendant-Appellee: Crocker Park Management, L.L.C.Defendant-Appellant: City of Westlake, Ohio

On reconsideration, an Ohio appeals court held Crocker Park waived its right of first refusal on Westlake's $2.6 million Market Square sale by adding conditions, but kept the right for future offers.

On October 22, 2026, Ohio's Eighth District Court of Appeals, on reconsideration, vacated its July 30, 2026 decision and issued a new one in Crocker Park's dispute with the City of Westlake over Market Square, a city-owned parcel inside the Crocker Park development. The court affirmed summary judgment against the city's slander-of-title and abuse-of-process claims. It held that Crocker Park waived its right of first refusal as to the city's sale to E&C Sports Group, L.L.C., because its $2.6 million matching offer added conditions and so was a counteroffer, but that the right continues for future offers. The July decision had held the right waived without that limit.

Why it matters

Under a price-only right of first refusal, the holder can exercise the right by matching the third party's price, but adding non-price conditions makes the response a counteroffer and the right lapses for that offer. The reconsidered opinion also reads the agreement's wording as a continuing right, so a waiver as to one buyer does not end it for later offers.

Full write-up

Ohio's Eighth District Court of Appeals released a new decision on October 22, 2026 in Crocker Park, L.L.C. v. Westlake, 2026-Ohio-4138, after granting reconsideration and vacating its July 30, 2026 decision in the same appeal. Presiding Judge Eileen T. Gallagher wrote both opinions, with Judges Emanuella D. Groves and Mary J. Boyle concurring. The judgment is the same, affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded, but the new opinion narrows the ruling against Crocker Park on its right of first refusal.

According to the opinion, Crocker Park is a mixed-use development on Crocker Road in Westlake with retail stores, restaurants, offices, apartments and green space, governed by a Declaration of Covenants, Conditions, Restrictions and Easements recorded in 2003 that names Crocker Park, L.L.C. as declarant. Under a 2014 development agreement, Crocker Park conveyed about two acres to the city at no cost, and the city built Market Square, which opened in 2015. The city took the land subject to the Declaration, and the agreement gave Crocker Park a right of first refusal to buy the property "at the same price offered by such third party." Crocker Park Management, L.L.C. ran Market Square under an operation and maintenance agreement until the city ended it in December 2023.

Disputes followed over the city's share of common expenses, a $22,367.37 lien that CP Commercial Delaware, L.L.C. placed on Market Square, and the city's planned sale to E&C Sports Group, L.L.C. The city set deadlines for Crocker Park to exercise its right, and Crocker Park sued in October 2024. In November 2024 it offered $2.6 million, E&C's price, on conditions that the property be exempt from property tax and that the price be eligible for reimbursement from the "Crocker Park Phase 3 waterfall." The city rejected the offer. In January 2025 Crocker Park agreed to forbear, and E&C bought Market Square for $2.6 million. The trial court ruled for Crocker Park on the city's counterclaims and declared that Crocker Park had not waived its right of first refusal on any offers.

The appeals court affirmed the rulings against the city's slander-of-title and abuse-of-process claims, finding no evidence of malice or an improper purpose and a legitimate basis for the lien. On the right of first refusal, it said whether Crocker Park acted within a reasonable time was a fact question, but held that its conditional offer was a counteroffer, so it waived the right as to E&C's offer. The July decision had stopped there and sustained the city's assignment of error in full. The reconsidered opinion adds that the agreement "expressly confers the right of first refusal to CP 'any time' following an offer from 'any unrelated third party,'" so Crocker Park did not waive it as to future offers.
