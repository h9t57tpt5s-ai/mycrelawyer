NSI6 LLC v. Benton County (Missouri Court Blocks County Data Center Moratorium for Now)

A Missouri judge granted developer NSI6 LLC a TRO against Benton County's six-month data center moratorium on Sept. 8 and extended it Sept. 21; the county can't enforce it pending a final ruling.

A Benton County, Missouri judge granted a temporary restraining order on Sept. 8, 2026 blocking the county's six-month data center moratorium, adopted Aug. 10, from applying to a project by developer NSI6 LLC, and extended it after a Sept. 21 hearing on a preliminary injunction, KY3 reported. The county argued the moratorium applied because the developer had only committed to buy the land and had not started work; the purchase of the roughly 12-acre site closed Sept. 9. As of Nov. 9, the county could not enforce the moratorium until a final judgment, KY3 reported.

Why it matters

The dispute turns on whether a moratorium adopted after a developer committed to buy land, but before closing, can reach that project. The court's interim orders have so far kept the moratorium from applying while the case proceeds, but no final ruling has been issued.

Full write-up

A developer and its company, NSI6 LLC, asked a Benton County, Missouri judge for a temporary restraining order after county commissioners adopted a six-month moratorium on data centers on Aug. 10, 2026, KY3 reported. The moratorium allowed projects already under construction to continue. At a Sept. 2 hearing attended by about 100 people, the county argued the moratorium applied because the developer did not yet own the land, had not started the project and could still withdraw. The developer's attorney said the developer chose Benton County because it has no planning and zoning and understood there would be no restrictions when he began trying to buy the land; he had committed to the purchase on July 23, with closing set for Sept. 9, and testified that withdrawing would cost $100,000.

The judge granted the restraining order on Sept. 8, and the purchase of the roughly 12-acre property on old Highway 65 closed Sept. 9, KY3 reported. At a Sept. 21 hearing on whether to grant a preliminary injunction, the county's presiding commissioner testified that commissioners had met the developer in June about a possible data center at a different site and had no further contact, and the county argued the moratorium should stand because NSI6 LLC did not become a legal entity until Aug. 13, three days after the moratorium. The judge extended the restraining order and urged caution on construction until a final decision. The developer plans two buildings of about 20,000 square feet each.

The same company is also suing the City of Springfield over that city's data center moratorium, KY3 reported. In a Nov. 9 story on the Springfield case, KY3 reported that Benton County commissioners cannot enforce their moratorium until a final judgment is entered, and that no court date had been set in the Benton County case.
