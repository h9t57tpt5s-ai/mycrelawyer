City of Chicago Dept. of Public Health v. City of Chicago Dept. of Administrative Hearings (Southeast Side Recycling Permit Denial Reinstated)

An Illinois appeals court reinstated Chicago's 2022 denial of a large recycling facility permit for RMG affiliate General III on the Southeast Side, reversing a hearing officer who had vacated it.

On November 6, 2026, the Appellate Court of Illinois, First District, affirmed a Cook County circuit court order that reversed an administrative law judge and reinstated the Chicago Department of Public Health's February 2022 denial of a large recycling facility permit for General III, LLC. General III, 80% owned by Reserve Management Group entities, sought to operate the facility at RMG's site at 116th Street and Burley Avenue on the city's Southeast Side. The court held the department was authorized to rely on a health impact assessment and on the compliance history of affiliated entities, and rejected General III's claim of a vested right to the permit.

Why it matters

The decision holds that Chicago's public health commissioner may use a health impact assessment, a tool not named in the city code, under the department's general public-health and study powers when deciding an industrial permit. It also holds that a zoning approval and spending on construction before a permit is granted did not give the applicant a vested right to the permit.

Full write-up

For decades General Iron Industries ran a metal scrap recycling business in Chicago's Lincoln Park neighborhood. By July 2018 it had agreed to sell assets to Reserve Management Group, and in a September 2019 term sheet with the city, General Iron and RMG agreed to end the Lincoln Park operations, with RMG stating it intended to transition to a new operation at the Burley site, a 175-acre property where RMG affiliates already ran four recycling facilities. The city agreed to reasonably cooperate on permitting while reserving its authority to enforce its ordinances to protect public health. General III obtained a special use approval from the Zoning Board of Appeals in April 2019, an Illinois EPA construction permit in June 2020 and a city air pollution control permit in September 2020, and built the facility from September 2020 to March 2021 at a cost it puts at about $80 million.

General III applied for the city's large recycling facility permit in November 2020. After HUD opened an investigation of a civil rights complaint about the move and U.S. EPA Administrator Michael S. Regan wrote in May 2021 suggesting the city first study cumulative health effects, the department suspended its review and, with U.S. EPA assistance, prepared a health impact assessment that recommended denial. On February 18, 2022, the department denied the permit, relying mainly on that assessment and also on compliance issues at affiliated facilities on the site. After a January 2023 hearing, an administrative law judge vacated the denial on June 1, 2023, finding the assessment beyond the commissioner's authority and the compliance-history rule inconsistent with the code. In August 2024 the circuit court, Judge Allen P. Walker, reversed and reinstated the denial.

In an opinion by Justice Pucinski, joined by Justices Hyman and Evans, the First District affirmed. It treated the administrative law judge's decision as the final decision under review but held that the health department, not the hearings department, is owed deference in interpreting the code. It held that the commissioner's code powers to protect public health, conduct studies and cooperate with federal agencies authorized use of the assessment, that the zoning board's approval did not limit the health department's separate review, and that the delegation of authority was constitutional. It also held that the department's rule allowing review of an applicant's compliance history does not conflict with the code provision on prior permit revocations and is not limited to adjudicated violations.

The court rejected General III's argument that its spending in reliance on earlier approvals gave it a vested right to the permit, noting that the doctrine has been applied to zoning classifications and that the record showed no pattern of official assurances that this permit would issue. Southeast Side community groups filed an amicus brief supporting the department; business and manufacturing groups filed one supporting General III.
