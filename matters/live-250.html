White Sulphur Springs Holdings LLC, a company set up by TRT Holdings, the parent of Omni Hotels, sued Greenbrier Hotel Corporation, related Greenbrier entities and members of the Justice family in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of West Virginia on April 9, 2026, seeking a receiver for The Greenbrier resort after acquiring the resort's loan debt previously held by Carter Bank & Trust. The Justice family countersued in Greenbrier County Circuit Court, alleging TRT and Carter Bank conspired to take the resort, which TRT called false. On August 14, 2026, after the family closed a joint venture with Kennedy Lewis Investment Management, the parties stipulated to dismiss the federal case with prejudice, and the state case was also dismissed, The Intermountain reported.

Why it matters

The case shows how a buyer of a hotel's defaulted debt can use a receivership motion to press for repayment, and how a borrower can respond with a state-court suit and a refinancing. Here the dispute ended with new capital and a change in majority ownership rather than a court-appointed receiver, and the casino license review shows how gaming regulators become part of a fight over a licensed resort.

Full write-up

White Sulphur Springs Holdings LLC (WSSH), a company set up by Texas-based TRT Holdings, parent of Omni Hotels & Resorts, filed suit on April 9, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of West Virginia against Greenbrier Hotel Corporation, other Greenbrier entities and members of the Justice family, the federal docket shows. WSSH had acquired the resort's loan debt previously held by Carter Bank & Trust; West Virginia Watch put that debt at about $300 million, and The Intermountain later reported that court documents showed more than $387 million owed. WSSH moved for appointment of a receiver and later for a preliminary injunction before Chief Judge Frank W. Volk.

The Justice family filed its own suit in Greenbrier County Circuit Court days later and on April 16 moved to stay the federal case. An amended state complaint, as described by West Virginia Watch, alleged that TRT and Carter Bank violated antitrust laws and conspired to sabotage refinancing efforts and force a default so TRT could take over The Greenbrier and remove a competitor to Omni's Homestead resort in Virginia. TRT said the allegations were false and an attempt to deflect from the family's failure to pay its debts, and that WSSH bought the debt in full compliance with all applicable laws and agreements. The West Virginia State Tax Division moved to intervene in the federal case in May.

In May the Justice family announced a $500 million non-binding financing proposal from New York-based Kennedy Lewis Investment Management to pay off major creditors and fund capital improvements, according to The Intermountain. The federal court pushed back its hearing schedule several times over the summer, and the docket shows the borrowers responding to an order to show cause and filing repeated status reports. On August 13, lawyers for the Justice family told Judge Volk the Greenbrier Casino Club would have to close and its 90 employees be laid off pending a license for the new owner; the acting Lottery Commission director then said the casino could keep operating while the commission reviewed the change.

On August 14, 2026, the Justice family and Kennedy Lewis completed a joint venture in which Kennedy Lewis holds a 51% controlling interest and the family 49%, The Intermountain reported. As a result, WSSH and the defendants filed a stipulation dismissing the federal case with prejudice, and the family's state-court case was also dismissed. The final payoff amount was not disclosed. WVTF reported that the resort also received Lottery Commission approval to keep operating the casino.
