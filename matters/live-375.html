Torchlight Loan Services, special servicer for the CMBS bondholders who took the 610-key JW Marriott Chicago through foreclosure, sued Prime Group in Cook County Circuit Court on October 30, 2026. The complaint says a Prime electrical subcontractor cut power to the hotel's phone and internet equipment on October 27, a sold-out day, and that a 2014 easement agreement requires the hotel's consent for work that disrupts shared systems. Torchlight wants the conversion work stopped until Prime has that consent. Prime's Mike Reschke said he was surprised by the suit because the two sides had been meeting regularly about construction, The Real Deal reported.

Why it matters

Office-to-apartment conversions in buildings shared with hotels depend on easement and consent terms as much as on construction budgets. A lender that takes over a hotel in such a building can use those terms to stop work that disrupts operations, so developers should line up consents and plan shutdowns of shared systems before demolition begins.

Full write-up

Torchlight Loan Services, the special servicer for bondholders who own the JW Marriott Chicago at 208 South LaSalle Street, sued Mike Reschke's Prime Group in Cook County Circuit Court on October 30, 2026, The Real Deal reported. The suit seeks to stop Prime's conversion of office floors above the 610-key hotel into apartments until Prime obtains the hotel's consent. According to the complaint, at about 9:15 a.m. on October 27 a Prime electrical subcontractor cut power to a 14th-floor room that houses the hotel's internet and phone equipment. The hotel says it lost phone service for more than four hours on a sold-out day, with 230 rooms checking out and 230 checking in, and struggled to check in guests and encode key cards.

The hotel argues that the building's 2014 easement agreement requires its consent for changes that raise its costs or disrupt shared systems, and it says Prime has withheld an engineering study since mid-2025. The hotel pays 54 percent of the building's shared costs, and about 70 of its higher-priced rooms and its executive lounge sit directly below the construction, according to the complaint. Prime plans about 167 apartments on floors 13 through 16 of the 22-story landmark building, plus roughly 70 rooms on floor 17 to be added to The LaSalle Chicago, the hotel Reschke built on the top five floors. The project is part of the city's LaSalle Street Reimagined program. Prime paid about $23 million in 2023 to buy the office floors out of receivership.

The hotel came under lender control after its loan failed. Natixis originated a $203.5 million senior loan in 2017, and the loan moved to special servicing in April 2020. After two forbearance agreements with Rialto Capital, the original special servicer, the borrower defaulted, and Wells Fargo, as trustee, won the hotel with a $251 million bid at a July 2022 foreclosure auction. Torchlight became special servicer in September 2025 and expects to resolve the loan by April, The Real Deal reported. Public loan data cited by the publication show the hotel was appraised at $205 million in May.

"We are shocked over the need for a lawsuit because we were having regular meetings over construction logistics," Reschke said, according to The Real Deal. A hearing is set for January 4. The publication noted a similar dispute a block away at 105 West Adams Street, where special servicer CWCapital controls the Club Quarters hotel below a planned office-to-apartment conversion.
