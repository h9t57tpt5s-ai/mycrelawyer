Zecorra Enterprises, LLC v. Redford Township (Commercial Property Tax Valuation Appeal)

Zoning & Land Use Ruling Issued

$525,000 true cash value determination affirmed
Parties
Petitioner-Appellant: Zecorra Enterprises, LLCRespondent-Appellee: Redford Township

A Michigan appeals court affirmed a $525,000 true cash value for a Redford Township former bowling alley being converted for a Dollar General, rejecting the owner's bid for a larger renovation deduction.

On October 14, 2026, the Michigan Court of Appeals affirmed the Michigan Tax Tribunal's determination that a Redford Township commercial property, a former bowling alley being converted for a Dollar General store, had a true cash value of $525,000 for the 2023 tax year. The panel rejected owner Zecorra Enterprises, LLC's argument that the tribunal should have deducted its full renovation costs of about $797,783 instead of adopting the township appraiser's $525,000 deduction, and held the tribunal was not required to accept the property's $200,000 sale price as its value.

Why it matters

The decision shows Michigan courts deferring to the Tax Tribunal's choice between competing appraisal adjustments when the chosen one is supported by the record, and treating a renovation deduction as a market calculation rather than a pass-through of actual construction costs. It also shows that a purchase price is not presumed to be true cash value when the tribunal finds it did not reflect the market.

Full write-up

The building on the property operated as a bowling alley until 2019. Zecorra signed a purchase agreement in June 2022, contracted with Mann Construction in September 2022 to convert the building for a proposed Dollar General store, and closed in October 2022 for a total acquisition cost of $450,000: a $200,000 contract sales price plus $250,000 in other consideration, which the owner's appraiser attributed to personal property, fixtures and furniture. The property had been listed at $525,000 in June 2021. In December 2022 Zecorra leased it to Dolgencorp, LLC (Dollar General), agreeing to complete construction by April 1, 2023, and Dollar General took possession in May 2023.

Zecorra challenged the township's 2023 assessment, which turned on value as of December 31, 2022. Its appraiser valued the property at $180,000 after a renovation allowance of about $57,000. The township's appraiser valued it at $570,000, calling the $200,000 price a significant outlier and deducting $525,000 from a stabilized income value for renovation costs and lost rent over a four-month holding period. After a three-day hearing, the tribunal on May 27, 2025, gave the owner's deduction no weight, found the owner's photographs of the property's condition inconsistent and incomplete, and made its own determination: $529,000 by sales comparison and $521,000 by income capitalization after adopting the township's deduction, reconciled to $525,000.

Judges Borrello, Bazzi and Lievense affirmed in an unpublished per curiam opinion. They held the tribunal's adoption of the township's deduction was supported by substantial evidence, because that figure was a market-based calculation of what an investor would pay on tax day rather than a measure of actual construction costs, and evidence showed some work had been done in 2022. They also held the tribunal properly valued the property for its undisputed highest and best use, a repurposed commercial use, rather than as a vacant bowling alley, and was not required to accept the sale price, noting the $525,000 value matched the property's 2021 listing price.
