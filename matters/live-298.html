Zecorra Enterprises, LLC v. Redford Township (Commercial Property Tax Valuation Appeal)

Petitioner-Appellant: Zecorra Enterprises, LLCRespondent-Appellee: Redford Township

A Michigan appeals court affirmed a $525,000 true cash value for a Redford Township former bowling alley converted for a Dollar General, rejecting the owner's lower appraisal for the 2023 tax year.

The Michigan Court of Appeals affirmed the Michigan Tax Tribunal's determination that a Redford Township commercial property, a former bowling alley converted for a Dollar General store, had a true cash value of $525,000 for the 2023 tax year. The panel rejected owner Zecorra Enterprises, LLC's argument that the tribunal should have relied on its own appraiser's lower valuation and renovation deduction rather than the township's.

Why it matters

The decision is a reminder to commercial owners pursuing property tax appeals that a below-market purchase price and a party's own renovation-cost ledger will not control a true-cash-value determination if the tribunal finds the sale price unrepresentative of the market and the owner's supporting evidence incomplete or poorly documented. It also confirms that Michigan courts give substantial deference to a tax tribunal's choice between competing appraisal methodologies and deduction amounts when both are grounded in record evidence.
