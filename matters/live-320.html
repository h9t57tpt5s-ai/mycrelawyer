A Michigan appeals court affirmed a $411,131.50 judgment on an arbitration award for landlord Padilla Investments after tire-shop tenants left a Detroit building over a sprinkler requirement.

The Michigan Court of Appeals on October 21, 2026 affirmed a $411,131.50 judgment confirming an arbitration award for landlord Padilla Investments, LLC against tenants Trucks Rx, Inc. and Jana Trucking Line, Inc. and the two owners who personally guaranteed their lease. The tenants stopped paying rent and left a Detroit building, formerly a hardware store, after city correction orders; the chief problem was that the building lacked an automatic sprinkler system needed to store tires. The arbitrator found the as-is lease made the tenants responsible for complying with the law to run their business, and the unpublished per curiam opinion held he neither exceeded his authority nor made an error of law.

Why it matters

Michigan courts reviewing an arbitration award look only for an error of law evident on the face of the award, and the panel found none in the arbitrator's reading of an as-is lease with a tenant compliance clause. The opinion also rejected impossibility and mutual-mistake defenses, relying on Lenawee County Board of Health v. Messerly, where an as-is clause put the risk of a property's condition on the buyer.

Full write-up

The Michigan Court of Appeals on October 21, 2026 affirmed a Wayne Circuit Court judgment of $411,131.50 for Padilla Investments, LLC, entered on an arbitration award in its lease dispute with Trucks Rx, Inc., Jana Trucking Line, Inc. and their owners. Trucks Rx and its owner appealed; the unpublished per curiam opinion was issued by Judges Patel, M. J. Kelly and Garrett.

According to the opinion, Padilla Investments owns a Detroit property previously used as a hardware store. In February 2022 the two companies signed a three-year lease to use it to sell new vehicle tires, and their owners personally guaranteed it. The tenants agreed to comply with all laws and ordinances at their own expense and accepted the property "as is." The city issued correction orders to the landlord in April and June 2022, the June order requiring the use to stop because no permit had been issued. The tenants stopped paying rent in July 2022. In August the city cited the building for operating illegally as a major auto repair and semi-truck storage and dispatch site when its only legal use was retail. The tenants left in September 2022, and two months later the landlord obtained a certificate of compliance for retail or wholesale use. The chief problem was the lack of an automatic sprinkler system required to store tires, which the appellants estimated would cost more than $60,000 and take four to six months to install. The landlord's managing member offered to go with the Trucks Rx owner to a meeting with city officials, but he did not attend.

The landlord sued in October 2022, the tenants counterclaimed, and the parties agreed to arbitrate. The arbitrator found the tenants breached the lease by stopping rent and vacating, that the unambiguous lease required them to do what was needed to operate lawfully, and that the landlord was not required to do any work. He awarded $411,131.50 plus interest and held the two owners liable as guarantors. The trial court confirmed the award.

The Court of Appeals held the arbitrator did not exceed his authority or make an error of law evident on the face of the award. It rejected the appellants' impossibility and impracticability argument, noting the tenants were in the better position to know what their business required; rejected their mutual-mistake argument because the need for a sprinkler system did not go to the whole substance of the agreement; and held that, as in Lenawee County Board of Health v. Messerly, the as-is clause meant the tenants took the risk of the property's condition. The court addressed the arguments even though most had not been preserved.
