Silver Point Finance, Vista Bank and NexPoint Real Estate Finance forced Lurin Capital founder Jon Venetos into involuntary Chapter 7, citing $33.7 million in personal guaranty judgments from his multifamily syndication collapse.

Silver Point Finance, LLC, Vista Bank, and NexPoint Real Estate Finance, Inc. filed an involuntary Chapter 7 bankruptcy petition against Jon Venetos, founder of Dallas-based multifamily syndicator Lurin Capital, on September 17, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas. The petitioning creditors cite a combined roughly $33.7 million in judgments already entered against Venetos personally on guaranties tied to Lurin's defaulted apartment portfolio, a step observers have called one of the rarest moves in bankruptcy.

Why it matters

The filing shows how quickly a sponsor's personal guaranty exposure can outrun a corporate Chapter 11 once multiple lenders reduce separate guaranties to judgment, and illustrates that creditors facing a shrinking, overlapping pool of a guarantor's personal assets may find a coordinated involuntary petition more effective than racing each other to collect individually.

The unwinding of Lurin Capital, the Dallas-based multifamily syndicator that once controlled nearly 10,000 Class-C apartment units across five Sun Belt states, has reached an unusual legal milestone: three of its lenders have asked a federal bankruptcy court to force its founder, Jon Venetos, into Chapter 7 bankruptcy against his will. Silver Point Finance, LLC, Vista Bank, and NexPoint Real Estate Finance, Inc. filed the involuntary petition on September 17, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, citing a combined roughly $33.7 million in judgments already entered against Venetos personally. Industry observers have called involuntary filings against an individual one of the rarest moves in bankruptcy, a reflection of how unusual it is for creditors to take this step rather than simply chasing a judgment debtor's assets individually.
