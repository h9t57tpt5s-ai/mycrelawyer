Eagan Capital v. City of Eagan (Minnesota Data Center Moratorium Challenge)

Data center owner Eagan Capital sued Eagan, Minnesota for $50,000, arguing state utility regulators, not the city, control electricity demand, so its one-year data center moratorium is invalid, MPR News reported.

Data center owner Eagan Capital sued the City of Eagan, Minnesota in a complaint filed June 15, 2026, arguing the city lacked authority to adopt its February 2026 one-year moratorium on data centers using more than 20 megawatts of electricity within 500 feet of homes, MPR News reported. The complaint says the Minnesota Public Utilities Commission has exclusive authority over electricity demand in the state and seeks $50,000. The city says it will vigorously defend the moratorium and that it denied the company an interim use permit after unpermitted trailer-mounted chillers were installed and operated at the site.

Why it matters

The suit tests whether a Minnesota city can pause data center development based on electricity use, or whether that is a matter for state utility regulators. Eagan was the first Minnesota city to impose a hard pause on data centers, according to MPR News.

Full write-up

Eagan Capital, a data center owner, has sued the City of Eagan over the one-year moratorium the city adopted in February 2026 on development projects that would use more than 20 megawatts of electricity within 500 feet of homes, MPR News reported on July 7, 2026. The complaint, filed June 15, argues the city has no authority to regulate electricity and quotes the Minnesota Public Utilities Commission's "exclusive authority to regulate electricity demand in Minnesota." The company seeks $50,000 in damages. According to MPR News, Eagan was the first Minnesota city to impose a hard pause on data centers, and the moratorium does not appear to affect the city's two existing data centers.

The city told MPR News that it denied Eagan Capital an interim use permit after staff found temporary trailer-mounted chiller units had been installed and operated without required permits, and that the City Council denied the permit in May because the proposed use was not lawful under the property's current zoning. The city said it is "confident in its position" and will vigorously defend the moratorium.
