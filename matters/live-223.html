Greater DC Diaper Bank seeks more than $775,000 from its former warehouse landlord and manager over years of roof leaks; the landlord counterclaimed and the case moved to federal court in Maryland.

The Greater DC Diaper Bank is seeking more than $775,000 plus interest from WWDC Industrial Park, LLC and property manager Donohoe Real Estate Services, Inc., alleging they failed for years to fix a leaking roof at its warehouse on Monard Drive in Silver Spring, Maryland, WJLA/7News reported. The suit, first filed in D.C. Superior Court, was removed to federal court in Washington on August 17, 2026 and transferred to the District of Maryland in September. WWDC has counterclaimed against the Diaper Bank, and both defendants filed motions to dismiss before the transfer.

Why it matters

The case turns on a landlord's handling of a documented, recurring roof leak under a commercial warehouse lease, with the tenant claiming losses beyond repair costs. The landlord's counterclaim puts the tenant's own lease performance at issue.

Full write-up

The Greater DC Diaper Bank, a nonprofit, sued its warehouse landlord, WWDC Industrial Park, LLC, and the property manager, Donohoe Real Estate Services, Inc., alleging that a leaking roof at its warehouse on Monard Drive in Silver Spring went unrepaired for years, WJLA/7News reported on September 4, 2026. According to the complaint as described by 7News, the leaks worsened from 2024, staff covered products with tarps and moved inventory during rain and snow, and the nonprofit notified the defendants more than a dozen times, but repairs were delayed or were temporary fixes that failed. A Montgomery County health inspector reportedly found the warehouse unsuitable for receiving and storing items such as baby food and formula, and the Diaper Bank stopped operating there in February 2025.

The nonprofit says conditions forced it to close its Baby Pantry program, whose distributed value fell from about $930,000 in 2024 to $313,000 in 2025. It also alleges that at a January 15 meeting the parties agreed to terms that included more than $47,000 in rent abatement and termination of the lease if it vacated, and that after it left the defendants backed away and demanded more rent. The complaint asserts breach of contract, breach of the implied covenant of quiet enjoyment, constructive eviction and promissory estoppel and seeks more than $775,000 plus pre-judgment interest. 7News said it had not heard back from WWDC or Donohoe.

Court records show the case was filed in D.C. Superior Court (No. 2026-CAB-004696) and removed by WWDC to the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia on August 17, 2026. On August 24, WWDC filed a counterclaim against the Diaper Bank and a motion to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction or to transfer, and Donohoe moved to dismiss. On September 14, Judge Timothy J. Kelly granted a joint motion to transfer the case to the District of Maryland, where it was docketed on September 28 (No. 8:26-cv-03821) and assigned to Judge Deborah K. Chasanow.
