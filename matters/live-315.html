Full write-up

The auction of the Hungerford Building at North Goodman and East Main streets in Rochester is on hold after the attorney for the building's owner and affiliated LLCs appealed the foreclosure decision, the Rochester Business Journal reported on October 15, 2026. The appeal, entered in state Supreme Court in Monroe County in early October, contends Justice Daniel J. Doyle erred in granting summary judgment because he relied on a referee's report the defendants say was flawed. The RBJ reported it was not yet known when the Fourth Department would take up the appeal.

Elizon DB Transfer Agent began the foreclosure in September 2025, alleging The Hungerford Building LLC, its owner and affiliated entities defaulted on an original mortgage of $8.2 million after the parties had twice agreed to extend the maturity date, the RBJ reported. The owner personally guaranteed the loan in April 2022. The court-appointed referee calculated $10,490,036.37 owed. In a decision dated September 2, Doyle denied the defendants' motion challenging those calculations, finding 'no gaps in the evidentiary record,' and signed the foreclosure and sale order. The referee then scheduled the auction for October 29 at the Hall of Justice.

The appeal argues the referee's report 'suffered evidentiary gaps' and relied on testimony from a witness without personal knowledge. Separately, the owner told The Real Deal he intends to pay off the $10.4 million balance with a construction loan before any bids are taken, the RBJ reported. The property at 1115 East Main St. and 420 N. Goodman St., once home to the J. Hungerford Smith Co. syrup maker, had been a hub for artists and creatives.
