Norhaven Partners, LLC v. Bartow County (Georgia Court of Appeals Affirms County's Industrial Conditional Use Permit Over Developer's Zoning Challenge)

The Georgia Court of Appeals affirmed Bartow County's grant of a conditional use permit letting Rice Commercial Investments expand an industrial waste-processing operation next to developer Norhaven Partners' planned 97-unit townhome community.

The Court of Appeals of Georgia affirmed a trial court's rejection of developer Norhaven Partners, LLC's challenge to Bartow County's grant of a conditional use permit allowing neighboring landowner Rice Commercial Investments, Inc. to expand an industrial mulch-processing and recovered-materials operation adjacent to Norhaven's planned 97-unit townhome development. The court held the county's quasi-judicial decision was supported by the record and that Norhaven's procedural objections to the trial court's review did not warrant reversal.

Why it matters

The decision underscores the deference Georgia courts give to a local government's conditional use and zoning decisions in judicial review proceedings, reviewing only whether the record supports the local agency's final decision rather than re-litigating the policy merits. Developers should treat a neighboring parcel's separate zoning rights and pending conditional use applications as a live diligence item even after their own project's site plan is fully approved, since a county's later accommodation of an adjacent industrial expansion is very difficult to undo through litigation.
