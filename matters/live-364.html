Norhaven Partners, LLC v. Bartow County (Georgia Court of Appeals Affirms County's Industrial Conditional Use Permit Over Developer's Zoning Challenge)

Georgia's Court of Appeals upheld Bartow County's permit for a waste-processing expansion beside Norhaven's planned 97-unit townhome site, holding a 1,000-foot buffer covers only built dwellings.

On November 2, 2026, the Court of Appeals of Georgia affirmed the denial of Norhaven Partners, LLC and Peeples Owner LLC's petition for review of the Bartow County Commissioner's grant of a conditional use permit to Rice Commercial Investments, Inc. and its owner. The permit lets Rice expand its heavy-industrial site next to Norhaven's planned 97-unit townhome community to add a recovered-materials processing and solid-waste handling facility for construction and demolition materials. The court held that the county's 1,000-foot buffer between waste handling areas and residential dwellings covers only dwellings actually built at the time of the application, and none had been built.

Why it matters

The ruling reads the buffer rule to protect existing buildings, not approved but unbuilt residential lots, so a developer's approved site plan alone did not trigger it. The court also found that the trial court erred in confining its review to the hearing transcript, but held the error harmless.

Full write-up

In January 2023, Bartow County approved conceptual site plans from Norhaven Partners, LLC for a 97-unit townhome community, the Overlook on Pettit, on residentially zoned land, and by October 2023 it had fully approved the development plans and a land disturbance permit. Rice Commercial Investments, Inc. and its owner own adjacent property to the east, zoned heavy industrial and used for mulch processing, wood shredding and rock crushing. On July 2, 2024, Rice applied for a conditional use permit to add a recovered-materials processing and solid-waste handling facility for construction and demolition materials. No townhomes had been built, but 46 of the 97 planned units would be within 1,000 feet of the proposed waste handling area. Section 9.5.6 of the county zoning ordinance bars a waste handling area within 1,000 feet of any residential dwelling "so used at the time of the application."

The Bartow County Planning Commission recommended approval with conditions in August 2024. After a September 4, 2024 hearing at which Norhaven's representatives and members of the public opposed the application, the Bartow County Commissioner approved it, with a condition that a 1,000-foot separation from residential homes be maintained. Norhaven and Peeples Owner LLC petitioned for review on October 3, 2024, arguing the approval violated section 9.5.6 because the ordinance reads "used" to include "intended, arranged, or designed to be used." The trial court remanded for written findings; the commissioner found the waste handling area was at least 1,000 feet from any residential dwelling in use at the time of the application, and on January 5, 2026 the trial court denied the petition.

Presiding Judge Sara L. Doyle wrote for the court, with Judge Davis concurring and Judge Whitaker concurring in the judgment only. The court agreed with Norhaven that the trial court wrongly limited its review to the hearing transcript, but found no harm: read with the ordinance's definitions of "dwelling" and "structure," a residential dwelling means a building or structure, not an undeveloped lot planned for homes. The remand was proper, the commissioner followed its instructions, and undisputed testimony, including from Norhaven's own representative, showed no dwellings had been built. Rice's motion to dismiss the appeal was denied as moot.
