Wilmington Trust v. SPNA-Linked Borrowers (137-Unit Chicago Condo Foreclosure)

Wilmington Trust sued in Cook County to foreclose on 137 condo units in seven Chicago towers held by entities tied to Strategic Properties of North America, seeking nearly $51 million on two credit lines.

Wilmington Trust National Association, as trustee for the Redwood Trust subsidiary that holds the debt, sued in Cook County Circuit Court to foreclose on 137 condominium units in seven Chicago buildings and their rents. The Real Deal, which obtained the filing, reported that the suit seeks nearly $51 million in principal and accrued interest drawn on two revolving credit lines totaling $85 million, originated by CoreVest and extended in November 2021 and January 2023. The borrowers are two entities for which Yitzy Klor, head of Strategic Properties of North America (SPNA), is the registered agent, and Klor personally backs the debt. Klor did not immediately respond to The Real Deal's request for comment.

Why it matters

Lenders that finance a sponsor's piecemeal purchase of condo units are betting on a future building-wide buyout that unit owners can block. When those buyouts stall, the collateral is scattered units in occupied buildings rather than a stabilized rental property, and that is a different credit. Condo boards weighing a bulk offer, and lenders backing these strategies, should plan for the vote failing.

Full write-up

Wilmington Trust National Association, acting as trustee for a Redwood Trust Inc. subsidiary that holds the loans, has sued in the Circuit Court of Cook County to foreclose on 137 condominium units spread across seven Chicago buildings, along with the rents those units produce. According to documents The Real Deal obtained from the court, the lender seeks nearly $51 million in principal and accrued interest, plus late charges, attorneys' fees and other costs that are not included in that figure. The loans were originated by CoreVest, Redwood's San Francisco-based lending arm.

The debt consists of two revolving credit lines, extended in November 2021 and January 2023, that together total $85 million. The borrowers are two entities for which Yitzy Klor, head of Strategic Properties of North America (SPNA), is the registered agent under state records, and Klor personally backs the debt. The complaint alleges Klor drew at least $51 million that remains unpaid. It dates the first default to Nov. 10, 2023, and describes a forbearance agreement reached in March 2024 and later extended to January 2026, followed by a loan modification in May 2026, before the lender filed suit.

SPNA's model is the condo deconversion: buying units one at a time, then offering to buy the rest in bulk so the building can be run as a rental. Chicago lets a buyer force out holdouts once owners holding a set share of the building agree; The Real Deal noted the city raised that threshold from 75 percent to 85 percent of a building's value in 2019. The largest block in the suit, 47 units, is at 200 North Dearborn Street, a 309-unit high-rise. SPNA offered $95 million for that building in 2022, and two more offers made this year through an entity called YK Investments were rejected, The Real Deal reported. The other units are at 1850 North Clark Street, 70 West Huron Street, 33 West Delaware Place, 211 East Ohio Street, 3660 North Lake Shore Drive and 440 North Wabash Avenue.

The Real Deal also reported that SPNA's Skokie office, the borrowers' registered address, is the subject of a separate $340,000 foreclosure suit filed earlier this year, and that attorneys for Byline Bank have brought litigation involving that building. Klor did not immediately respond to a request for comment, the publication said.
