Full write-up

The parents of a 17-year-old high school senior who was shot and killed in the Mall of Louisiana food court on April 23, 2026 filed a wrongful-death and negligence suit on Oct. 7 in East Baton Rouge Parish's 19th Judicial District Court, Acadiana News First reported. The suit names Mall of Louisiana LLC, Brookfield Properties Retail Inc. and Marksman Security LLC, described as the mall's owner, its operator/manager and its security contractor, along with the person accused of firing the gun. Four people who were sitting with the teen joined as plaintiffs: two who suffered gunshot wounds and two who allege severe emotional distress.

According to the suit, a confrontation broke out in the food court and multiple shots were fired. The accused shooter, who was 17 at the time, has been indicted as an adult on one count of second-degree murder, six counts of attempted second-degree murder and one count of illegal use of a weapon during a crime of violence, and has pleaded not guilty.

The plaintiffs allege the defendants failed to take reasonable steps to protect patrons despite a history of violence at the property, citing a 2013 food court disturbance that led to the mall's evacuation and closure and a 2022 shooting near the mall that killed two people. They allege failures in security staffing, monitoring of the food court, response and de-escalation, threat assessment, surveillance and enforcement of the mall's firearms ban. "There were zero security officers in the food court on April 23, 2026," the suit alleges.

The family seeks damages for the teen's pain and suffering before death and for their own losses, including grief, loss of companionship and funeral expenses; the other plaintiffs seek damages for physical injuries or emotional trauma. The suit does not state a dollar amount but says damages exceed the threshold for a jury trial. The article did not include a response from the defendants.
