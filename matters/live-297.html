New York's Second Department upheld denial of CG & J Realty's motion to vacate a lien bond on its Queens property, holding a NYSCEF filing did not satisfy Lien Law §59's service requirement.

On October 14, 2026, the Appellate Division, Second Department, affirmed a Queens County order denying CG & J Realty, LLC's motion to vacate a Lien Law §19 bond and dismiss CFS Enterprises, Inc.'s cause of action to foreclose a mechanic's lien on CG & J Realty's Queens property. The court held that CG & J Realty only filed its order to show cause on NYSCEF and did not serve it on the lienor personally or at its last known place of residence, as Lien Law §59 requires. It also denied CFS Enterprises' request for appellate attorneys' fees, finding CFS had not shown the appeal was frivolous.

Why it matters

The decision applies the rule that mechanic's liens exist only by statute, so New York courts require strict compliance with Lien Law procedure and have no discretion to excuse a shortfall. A party seeking to vacate a lien or discharge bond under Lien Law §59 cannot rely on electronic filing alone to give the lienor notice.

Full write-up

On October 14, 2026, the Appellate Division, Second Department, affirmed an order of the Supreme Court, Queens County (Justice Cassandra A. Johnson), that denied CG & J Realty, LLC's motion to vacate a lien bond and to dismiss CFS Enterprises, Inc.'s cause of action to foreclose a mechanic's lien against it. The panel was Justices Francesca E. Connolly, Valerie Brathwaite Nelson, Barry E. Warhit and James P. McCormack.

According to the decision, CFS Enterprises filed a notice of mechanic's lien on November 7, 2022 against real property in Queens owned by CG & J Realty, and sued to foreclose the lien in February 2023. In April 2023, CG & J Realty posted a bond under Lien Law §19 to discharge the lien. In July 2023, Century Construction Group Corp. filed a bond under Lien Law §37 and moved to discharge all liens and claims against the property. In an October 10, 2023 order, the trial court discharged all liens and claims against the property and said the Section 37 bond would supersede all prior bonds.

CG & J Realty then moved, by order to show cause, under Lien Law §59 to vacate and cancel its Section 19 bond and to dismiss the foreclosure claim against it. The trial court denied the motion on October 24, 2024. On appeal, the Second Department noted that mechanic's liens are purely statutory and that strict compliance is required. Section 59 requires that notice be served on the lienor personally or left at the lienor's last known place of residence with a person of suitable age. The court found that CG & J Realty had only filed the order to show cause on NYSCEF, so its motion was properly denied.

The court declined CFS Enterprises' request for the attorneys' fees and expenses of the appeal under New York's frivolous-conduct rule, finding CFS had not shown that CG & J Realty's appeal was frivolous. The order was affirmed with costs.
