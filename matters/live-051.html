A federal judge approved and directed the $70 million receivership sale of Centre Square, Philadelphia's largest office complex, to affiliates of Dean Adler and PMC Property Group, Bisnow reported.

On Aug. 17, 2026, U.S. District Judge Nitza I. Quiñones Alejandro approved and directed the $70 million sale of Centre Square, the 2.2-million-square-foot two-tower office complex at 1500 Market St., to affiliates of Dean Adler and PMC Property Group ahead of an Oct. 16 deadline, Bisnow reported, citing the Philadelphia Business Journal. The buyers had tried to get out of the deal despite a $5 million nonrefundable deposit, and rival bidder Universal Group Co. has appealed. The sale comes in a CMBS foreclosure that Wells Fargo, as trustee, filed in January 2023.

Why it matters

The court approved the receiver's sale even though the winning buyer had tried to exit and a rival bidder said it had offered more. Universal Group's pending appeal to the Third Circuit means the order is still under challenge.

Full write-up

U.S. District Judge Nitza I. Quiñones Alejandro of the Eastern District of Pennsylvania on Aug. 17, 2026 granted receiver CBRE's motion to approve the private sale of Centre Square, Philadelphia's largest office property, to affiliates of Dean Adler and PMC Property Group for $70 million, ahead of an Oct. 16 deadline for the transaction, Bisnow reported, citing the Philadelphia Business Journal. According to the PBJ, she wrote that there was "no just reason for delay" and that the order was final immediately for purposes of appeal.

The two-tower, 2.2-million-square-foot complex at 1500 Market St. has been in foreclosure since 2023. Wells Fargo Bank, as trustee for a CMBS trust, filed the foreclosure complaint against owner NG 1500 Market St. LLC on Jan. 12, 2023, and the court appointed CBRE as receiver on April 14, 2023, according to the court docket. Nightingale Properties and InterVest Capital Partners bought the property in 2017 and refinanced it two years later with a $390 million loan from JPMorgan Chase before stopping payments, Bisnow reported. Occupancy was 36% when CBRE listed the property for sale as a potential conversion in August 2025. The $70 million price is 21% of what Nightingale and InterVest paid.

Adler and PMC went under contract with CBRE in March 2026 with plans for a partial mixed-use redevelopment including apartments and a hotel. Later that month, CSC Coliving and Universal Group Co. challenged the sale, saying they had submitted higher bids; CSC Coliving later withdrew its challenge. Adler and PMC then sought to get out of the purchase despite a $5 million nonrefundable deposit, with Adler telling The Philadelphia Inquirer in July that the buyers needed to do more homework.

The court denied Universal Group's motion to intervene on July 27 and its motion for reconsideration on Aug. 7, according to the docket. Universal filed a notice of appeal to the Third Circuit on Aug. 26.
