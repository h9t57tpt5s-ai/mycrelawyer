CRG Acquisition, LLC v. Planning Board of Holliston: Massachusetts Appeals Court Rules 800,000-Square-Foot Warehouse Is Not a Permitted-as-of-Right Use

On October 14, 2026, the Massachusetts Appeals Court reversed a Land Court judgment that had let CRG Acquisition, LLC build an approximately 800,000-square-foot warehouse and distribution facility in Holliston without a special permit. The panel held that, even without deferring to the planning board's interpretation, the proposed facility's scale takes it out of the zoning bylaw's as-of-right 'wholesale office or showroom' warehouse category and into the bylaw's general-industrial-use category, which requires a special permit the planning board had already denied in 2021.

Why it matters

The ruling warns logistics and warehouse developers that a town's use table listing 'warehouse' as a permitted use does not guarantee as-of-right status for a large-scale distribution facility; courts will look past the label to the project's actual size and intensity, including loading docks and trailer parking, to decide which use category really governs. It also shows that winning a favorable declaratory-judgment ruling at the trial level does not foreclose reversal on appeal, especially where the underlying discretionary special-permit denial already stands on its own, independent grounds.

The Massachusetts Appeals Court reversed a Land Court judgment on October 14, 2026 that had declared CRG Acquisition, LLC's proposed 800,000-square-foot warehouse and distribution facility in Holliston a use permitted as of right, meaning no special permit was required. The panel's ruling effectively reinstates the position of the town and its planning board, which had already denied CRG a special permit for the project in 2021 after eleven public hearings. Because that discretionary denial was never disturbed, the Appeals Court's decision leaves the project without a zoning path forward in its current form.
