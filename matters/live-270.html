5 Beekman Property Owner, LLC v. Henick-Lane, Inc.: Plaintiffs Ordered to Return $850,000 Insurer Paid on a Settlement That Never Closed

New York's First Department affirmed an $850,000 judgment, plus interest, ordering 5 Beekman's owner and Broadway Construction Group to return money an HVAC contractor's insurer paid on a settlement that was never finalized.

On October 6, 2026, New York's Appellate Division, First Department unanimously affirmed an $850,000 judgment, plus prejudgment interest, against 5 Beekman Property Owner, LLC and Broadway Construction Group, LLC. One of HVAC contractor Henick-Lane, Inc.'s insurers had paid them $850,000, apparently believing their defective-HVAC suit had settled, when no full settlement agreement was in place. The court also upheld a civil contempt finding against the plaintiffs for failing to return the money.

Why it matters

The court held that an insurer's payment made in the mistaken belief that a settlement existed is not a voluntary payment the recipient may keep, even though the check was labeled a settlement payment. It also confirmed that contempt was available to enforce the return order because no money judgment had yet been entered when the contempt order issued.

Full write-up

The underlying case, filed in New York County Supreme Court under Index No. 657290/19, concerns an HVAC system that Henick-Lane, Inc. installed. According to the decision, the plaintiffs, 5 Beekman Property Owner, LLC and Broadway Construction Group, LLC, allege the system leaked, damaging the owner's property and forcing them to spend money containing the damage. They sued for breach of contract, and Henick-Lane counterclaimed for nonpayment and to foreclose a mechanic's lien. Those claims were not part of this appeal.

During settlement negotiations, one of Henick-Lane's insurers, apparently believing the parties had reached a settlement, paid $850,000 to Broadway Construction Group even though a full settlement agreement was not in place. The check said it was a "Settlement Payment for" Henick-Lane. Justice Andrew Borrok ordered the plaintiffs to return the money with interest, later held them in civil contempt for not doing so, and on December 11, 2025 entered judgment against them for $850,000 plus prejudgment interest. The plaintiffs appealed the judgment and the contempt order.

A five-justice panel affirmed both. It held that the judgment brought up the earlier return order for review because the dispute over the payment was separate from the HVAC, nonpayment and lien claims still in the case. It held that Henick-Lane had standing, an issue the plaintiffs raised for the first time on appeal, because its insurer paid on its behalf out of a policy Henick-Lane paid for. It rejected the plaintiffs' reliance on the voluntary payment doctrine: the insurer had reserved its position by labeling the check, and because there was no settlement, the payment rested on a mistake.

The court also upheld the contempt finding. Although contempt may not be used to enforce a money judgment, no judgment existed when the contempt order issued. The panel rejected Broadway Construction Group's argument that it could not be held in contempt because the return order did not name it, noting that the plaintiffs' corporate representative stated that both plaintiffs received and deposited the check.
