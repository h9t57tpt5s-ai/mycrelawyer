Travelers and St. Paul Fire and Marine seek a ruling that pollution exclusions bar coverage for cleaning up a former dry cleaner's PCE contamination at an Indianapolis shopping center.

Travelers Property Casualty Company of America and St. Paul Fire and Marine Insurance Company sued Washington Shoppes LP and The Broadbent Company, Inc. in Marion Superior Court on Nov. 26, 2025, seeking a declaration that they owe no defense or indemnity for cleanup costs at an Indianapolis shopping center, The Indiana Lawyer reported. The insurers point to pollution exclusions and argue coverage may also be barred if the center knew of the contamination before buying the site or before the policies were issued.

Why it matters

The case shows insurers seeking an early coverage ruling on a state environmental claim over a former dry-cleaning tenant's contamination at a shopping center. Its outcome turns on how the policies' pollution exclusions and prior-knowledge terms apply to contamination that predates the current owner.

Full write-up

Travelers Property Casualty Company of America and St. Paul Fire and Marine Insurance Company, which issued excess policies under Travelers, filed a declaratory judgment action in Marion Superior Court on Nov. 26, 2025 against Washington Shoppes LP and The Broadbent Company, Inc. (No. 49D01-2511-CE-056363), The Indiana Lawyer reported. The insurers ask the court to declare that they owe no defense or indemnity coverage for cleanup at the shopping center at 10021 E. Washington St. in Indianapolis. Washington Shoppes has owned the site since 1998; The Indiana Lawyer describes Broadbent as owning the retail area that Washington Shoppes operates.

Sunrise Dry Cleaners operated at or around the address from 1987 to 1996. A 2023 site investigation requested by the shopping center found tetrachloroethylene (PCE) and its degradation products in the soil and groundwater. The Indiana Department of Environmental Management later identified the property owner as potentially responsible for the contamination, and the center sought coverage for defense and cleanup costs.

The insurers say each of their primary and excess policies excludes coverage for pollutants such as tetrachloroethylene. They also argue that, even if Washington Shoppes shows it qualifies as an insured, coverage may be barred if it knew of actual or potential contamination before acquiring the site or before the policies were issued. Attorneys for both sides did not respond to The Indiana Lawyer's requests for comment.
