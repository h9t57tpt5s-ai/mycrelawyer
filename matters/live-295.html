GSD Contracting, LLC v. All American Builders & Debris Removal, Inc.: Florida Appeals Court Affirms $71,582.98 Retainage After Mold Dispute on Hotel Project

State
Florida
Presiding judge
Spencer D. Levine

Parties
Appellant: GSD Contracting, LLCAppellee: All American Builders & Debris Removal, Inc.

A Florida appeals court held GSD Contracting wrongly withheld a $71,582.98 retainage from a hotel subcontractor it terminated after a mold dispute, but reversed a $45,300 award for unperformed work.

On October 14, 2026, Florida's Fourth District Court of Appeal affirmed a Broward County judgment that GSD Contracting, LLC breached its subcontract with All American Builders & Debris Removal, Inc. by not paying a $71,582.98 retainage on a five-story hotel project. The court reversed $45,300 the trial court had also awarded for work All American never performed, because All American had not sought that amount and the subcontract barred recovery for unperformed work. It remanded for entry of a judgment consistent with the opinion.

Why it matters

The decision shows a Florida court enforcing a subcontract's retainage clause as written: once the holdback period after final completion ran, the retainage was payable, and without a termination-for-cause clause or similar provision the contractor had no contractual basis to keep it over an alleged default. It also shows a trial court cannot award damages the plaintiff did not seek and the contract bars.

Full write-up

All American was the framing, drywall, insulation and acoustical ceiling tile subcontractor on a five-story hotel project. After the original general contractor was terminated, GSD was hired as the replacement general contractor. The subcontract let GSD terminate for convenience, in which case All American could recover payment for work performed through termination plus demobilization costs, but nothing for work not performed. A separate clause required ten percent of each partial payment to be held back until ninety days after final completion of the project.

When GSD took over, All American had completed about 93.9% of its work and was owed a $71,582.98 retainage; the rest of its work was waiting on other trades. The project then developed significant mold from water intrusion, and All American refused to finish until the mold was properly remediated for its workers' safety. GSD said it had done enough by removing some drywall and doing limited mold testing; All American said that was insufficient because GSD never hired an industrial hygienist or prepared a formal remediation plan. GSD terminated All American and never paid the retainage.

All American sued in Broward County circuit court for the retainage, and its owner testified at trial that it was not seeking the $45,300 for unperformed work. The trial court, Judge John B. Bowman, found GSD breached by not paying for work performed and by not properly remediating the mold, and awarded $116,882.98: the retainage plus $45,300.

In an opinion by Judge Spencer D. Levine, joined by Judges Klingensmith and Lott, the Fourth District held the subcontract's plain language supported the retainage award: the project was completed in 2023, the ninety-day period had long expired, and the subcontract had no termination-for-cause clause or other provision letting GSD keep the retainage after a termination. The court noted its analysis "could be different" if the contract had a for-cause provision. Having affirmed on that ground, it did not decide whether GSD also breached by failing to remediate the mold. It reversed the $45,300 because the complaint sought only the retainage, All American conceded the point, and the subcontract barred payment for work not performed. The opinion is not final until any timely rehearing motion is resolved.
