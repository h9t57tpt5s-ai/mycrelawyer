Kuna Neighbors' Appeal of Gemstone Technology Park Data Center Approval (Idaho Supreme Court)

A Kuna farmer and two Toledo dairy companies appealed to the Idaho Supreme Court after a judge rejected their claim that a council member's conflict tainted approval of a 620-acre data center park.

A neighboring farmer and two dairy companies, Toledo Dairy I LLC and Toledo Land Holdings LLC, petitioned in August 2025 for judicial review of the City of Kuna's approval of Diode Ventures' 620-acre Gemstone Technology Park data center project, BoiseDev reported. They argued council member Greg McPherson should have recused himself because the farmer employed him. District Judge James Cawthon denied the petition in May 2026, finding no "immediate or direct" economic impact on the farmer, and the petitioners appealed to the Idaho Supreme Court in June 2026.

Why it matters

The appeal asks how direct a neighboring landowner's economic stake must be before a council member he employs has to step aside. A ruling for the petitioners could reopen an approval the council granted by a single vote.

Full write-up

Kansas-based Diode Ventures proposed Gemstone Technology Park, a 620-acre data center project in Kuna, Idaho. The Kuna City Council rezoned the farmland from agricultural to industrial on a 3-2 vote after hours of public comment, with Mayor Joe Stear breaking a tie in favor of the project, Idaho News 6 reported. The project is separate from a data center Meta Platforms is building in Kuna, BoiseDev noted.

In August 2025, a neighboring farmer and two dairy companies, Toledo Dairy I LLC and Toledo Land Holdings LLC, petitioned for judicial review of the city's approval, according to BoiseDev. They argued that council member Greg McPherson, who voted on the project, should have recused himself because he was employed by the farmer and so had an economic conflict of interest.

District Judge James Cawthon denied the petition in May 2026. He wrote that the evidence the farmer presented at the planning and zoning commission and council hearings "did not establish an immediate or direct economic impact," and that his concerns about traffic, possible rezoning of his own land and higher power rates were speculative or would be felt by the whole community. Without a direct conflict on the farmer's part, the judge found, McPherson was not required to recuse. The farmer and the Toledo entities appealed to the Idaho Supreme Court in June 2026.
