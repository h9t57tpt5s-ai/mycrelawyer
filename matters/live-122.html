The Fourteenth Court of Appeals revived Holiday Inn Club Vacations' water-intrusion defect suit against CBRE, T&G and ACI over a Galveston resort, reversing a take-nothing summary judgment on limitations and other grounds.

On February 4, 2025, the Fourteenth Court of Appeals in Houston reversed a take-nothing summary judgment against Holiday Inn Club Vacations Inc. in its construction-defect suit over the Holiday Inn Club Vacations Galveston Seaside Resort. The court held that CBRE, Inc., T&G Corporation f/k/a T&G Constructors and ACI Architectural Concepts, Inc. had not conclusively established their limitations defenses, that Holiday Inn raised fact issues on its standing and capacity to sue CBRE as the original owner's assignee, and that the economic loss rule did not conclusively bar its negligence claim. Rehearing was denied, and the Supreme Court of Texas denied review in October 2025.

Why it matters

The opinion holds that evidence an owner knew of some leaks during construction does not by itself start the limitations clock on later, broader defect claims unless the defendant ties those early leaks to the damage being sued over. It also treats an anti-assignment clause covering "the agreement" as no bar to a successor owner pursuing an assigned claim for breach.

Full write-up

The case concerns the Holiday Inn Club Vacations Galveston Seaside Resort. According to the court's opinion, the original owner, Silverleaf Resorts, Inc., contracted with CBRE to oversee and manage construction, with T&G as general contractor and with ACI for construction administration and oversight. Construction began in April 2014, Holiday Inn says it took control when it acquired Silverleaf in transactions ending in August 2016, and the City of Galveston issued a certificate of occupancy on October 13, 2016. During construction, a testing firm found water penetration at many tested windows and parts of the curtain wall; after repairs the windows passed retesting in May 2016, though the tester warned that some windows might leak later.

Holiday Inn says it noticed water intrusion in fall 2019 and spring 2020, and an April 2020 consultant report attributed leaks to defective work on the pool terrace, parking garage, roof, exterior cladding and stucco, elevated walkways and windows. Holiday Inn sued CBRE in state court on October 8, 2020 and sued T&G and ACI in federal court the next day. The federal court dismissed that case for lack of diversity jurisdiction on May 5, 2023, and Holiday Inn refiled against T&G and ACI in state court on May 9, 2023. The 405th District Court of Galveston County granted summary judgment for all three defendants and signed a take-nothing judgment on December 4, 2023.

In a memorandum opinion by Justice Kevin Jewell, the appeals court reversed. On limitations, it held that the discovery rule can apply to construction-defect claims, that Holiday Inn's pleading was not a judicial admission that it knew of the defects it sued over, and that CBRE had not shown that the leaks found during construction, or leaks reported in 2017 and 2018, were related to the damage in the 2020 report. T&G and ACI, which relied on CBRE's arguments, likewise failed to negate the discovery rule, and Holiday Inn raised fact issues on tolling for the period its claims were in federal court. The court also found fact issues on Holiday Inn's standing and capacity to sue CBRE as Silverleaf's assignee, and on the economic loss rule, citing expert evidence of moisture damage to structural components and mold that went beyond the subject of the CBRE contract.

The court denied rehearing on March 20, 2025. The Supreme Court of Texas denied review on October 24, 2025, and the mandate issued on December 5, 2025, returning the case to the trial court for further proceedings.
