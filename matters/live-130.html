Town of Nahant v. Northeastern University (East Point Eminent Domain Reversal)

The Massachusetts SJC reversed a Superior Court ruling that Nahant's easement taking on Northeastern University's East Point land was in bad faith, and ordered entry of an order establishing the town's right to take.

On September 3, 2026, the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court reversed a Superior Court judgment that had dismissed the Town of Nahant's petition to take conservation and access easements over about 12 acres of Northeastern University's land at East Point, and it vacated an award of more than $1 million in fees and costs to the university. Writing for the court, Justice Elizabeth Dewar held that Northeastern had no reasonable expectation of proving the taking was a bad-faith pretext to block its planned Marine Science Center expansion. The case was remanded for entry of an order establishing the town's right to take.

Why it matters

The decision holds that a town's documented conservation and open-space purpose can support a taking even when the town pursued it in response to a specific development and the taking will block that project. As the SJC reads its 1987 Pheasant Ridge decision, the bad-faith rule reaches extreme cases where the stated purpose is not genuinely intended, not every taking driven partly by opposition to a project.

Full write-up

The Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court ruled on September 3, 2026 that the Town of Nahant may proceed with taking conservation and access easements over roughly 12 acres of Northeastern University's property on East Point, a 28.7-acre peninsula where the university runs its Marine Science Center. The unanimous opinion by Justice Elizabeth Dewar reversed a Superior Court judgment for Northeastern; the case reached the SJC on direct appellate review.

According to the opinion, the federal government took East Point by eminent domain in 1941 for a military installation and later transferred 20.4 acres to Northeastern; the town bought the remaining 8.3 acres in 1975 and keeps a public park there. In 2018 Northeastern presented plans to expand its campus with a new 55,000-square-foot building. After local opposition, town meeting voters in May 2021 authorized the board of selectmen to take easement interests in the Northeastern parcel and appropriated $1.5 million toward the cost. The easements would bar new buildings in the easement areas, which would prevent the planned building, and would give the public access to Canoe Beach. The town filed its petition in Superior Court in September 2021. Northeastern argued the taking was in bad faith because its real aim was to block the project, and on cross motions for summary judgment a Superior Court judge agreed. In May 2025 he entered judgment dismissing the petition and awarding Northeastern more than $1 million in attorney's fees, expert fees and costs.

The SJC held that conservation, open space and public access are proper public purposes, consistent with the town's earlier actions showing interest in the area, including placing East Point in a natural resource zoning district in 1991 and listing the Northeastern parcel in town open space plans. The extreme circumstances of Pheasant Ridge, where a town's stated purposes were a pretext it never intended to carry out, were not present. The court acknowledged it was undisputed that the town acted in response to Northeastern's announcement and that the taking would block the project, but said preventing development is the very purpose of a conservation easement and that a town's eminent domain vote is a legislative act entitled to a presumption of validity. It also rejected arguments that private funding of the taking, including a $3 million gift from the Nahant Preservation Trust, made it invalid, and that the Dover Amendment's limits on zoning educational uses barred it.

The court remanded for entry of an order establishing the town's right to take and for further proceedings, noting that the town may take the easements only upon paying just compensation. Town Administrator Alison Nieto told CommonWealth Beacon the town was 'pleased' with the decision. A Northeastern spokesperson told The Boston Globe the university was 'deeply disappointed' given what it called evidence of improper motive, and urged town officials to pursue a collaborative path forward.
