National Hairston Clan v. Stokes County: Community Groups File Second Suit to Void 1,850-Acre Project Delta Data Center Rezoning

Four community groups and neighboring residents sued Stokes County, N.C., to void its second rezoning of about 1,850 acres on the Dan River for the Project Delta data center campus, eleven days before a vote on a 15-year development agreement.

On November 12, 2026, four community organizations and a group of residents filed a new suit in Stokes County Superior Court challenging the Board of Commissioners' 3-2 vote on September 14 to rezone about 1,850 acres along the Dan River near Walnut Cove for a hyperscale data center campus known as Project Delta. The plaintiffs say the approval rested on a rushed analysis that appears to have been AI-generated and was never independently checked, and that it conflicts with the county's long-range plan. They ask the court to void the rezoning and bar the county from relying on it. An earlier rezoning of the same land was voided by consent judgment in August after the same lawyers sued over notice defects.

Why it matters

The suit shows that curing a notice defect and re-voting does not end the litigation risk on a large rezoning; the second round attacks the quality of the county's analysis itself. It also puts a pending 15-year development agreement in play, which the plaintiffs say would exempt the site from any future data center moratorium and commit the county to help defend the developer. For data center developers, landowners and capital sources, the case is a reminder that entitlement on a speculative, no-named-user site remains contestable long after the vote.

Full write-up

A second lawsuit over the Project Delta data center site was filed in Stokes County Superior Court on November 12, 2026, according to the case page kept by one of the two legal organizations representing the plaintiffs, which lists the complaint under file number 26CV0000976-840. The plaintiffs are four organizations, the National Hairston Clan, CleanAIRE NC, the Dan River Basin Association and 7 Directions of Service, joined by sixteen county residents, some of whom own land next to the site. The defendant is Stokes County, sued over the action of its Board of Commissioners.

The target is the board's 3-2 vote on September 14, 2026, to rezone about 1,850 acres of farmland and forest along the Dan River near Walnut Cove so that a data center campus can be built there. The plaintiffs' lawyers describe the proposal as speculative and as one of the largest AI data center campuses in the country. Public radio coverage identifies the applicant as Engineered Land Solutions. The plaintiffs say the commissioners approved the request over the county Planning Board's recommendation and heavy public opposition.

This is the second time the same land has been rezoned and the second time it has drawn a suit. Commissioners first approved a rezoning in January 2026. The same legal organizations sued on March 12, 2026, alleging the county skipped required procedural steps, and the county walked the decision back in April. In August the parties filed a joint motion for a consent judgment and the court voided the January rezoning. The developer then submitted a new application, which produced the September vote now under challenge.

The new complaint, as described by the plaintiffs' counsel and by local reporting, shifts from notice defects to the substance of the county's decision-making. The plaintiffs allege the board relied on summaries that were inaccurate and incomplete, that the supporting analysis appears to have been generated by AI and not independently verified, and that the rezoning is inconsistent with the county's long-term development plan. They also say the approval came without a confirmed water source, without limits on on-site power generation and without a complete survey of graves and archaeological sites on the land, which they say includes Saura village sites and a cemetery of about 65 graves. The county did not respond to a public radio request for comment.

Timing matters. The plaintiffs' counsel says the commissioners are scheduled to vote on November 23 on a 15-year development agreement for Project Delta. According to the lawsuit as described by counsel, that agreement would lock in the rezoning's shortcomings, exempt the property from any future data center moratorium and commit the county to assist in defending the developer against challenges from residents. The plaintiffs ask the court to declare the rezoning void and to block the county from relying on or implementing it. No hearing date or assigned judge has been reported, and the allegations have not been tested.

For developers and investors pursuing large rezonings, the case offers practical points. A re-vote after a procedural defect invites a second suit aimed at the adequacy of the staff analysis and consistency findings, so the record supporting the second approval needs to be stronger than the first, with verifiable sources for each conclusion. Any use of automated tools in preparing staff reports or applicant studies should be disclosed and checked, because opponents are now pleading it. Open infrastructure questions such as water supply and on-site generation are better resolved, or made express conditions, before the vote. And a development agreement signed while a challenge to the underlying rezoning is pending gives only as much protection as the rezoning itself; lenders and purchasers should underwrite the entitlement as contested until the suit is resolved.
