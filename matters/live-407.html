National Hairston Clan v. Stokes County: Community Groups File Second Suit to Void 1,850-Acre Project Delta Data Center Rezoning

Four community groups and 16 residents sued Stokes County, N.C., to void its second rezoning of about 1,850 acres on the Dan River for the Project Delta data center, 11 days before a development agreement vote.

On November 12, 2026, four community organizations and 16 residents, represented by two public-interest legal groups, sued Stokes County in Superior Court over the Board of Commissioners' 3-2 vote on September 14 to rezone about 1,850 acres along the Dan River near Walnut Cove for a data center campus known as Project Delta. According to their lawyers' announcement, the suit alleges the approval rested on a rushed analysis that appears to have been generated by AI without independent verification, and asks the court to void the rezoning and bar the county from relying on it. The county voided an earlier rezoning of the same land in April after many of the same plaintiffs sued over the process in March, and a court formally voided it in August on the parties' joint motion.

Why it matters

The suit shows that re-voting after a flawed process does not end the litigation risk on a large rezoning; the second challenge targets the quality of the record behind the decision. It also bears on a pending 15-year development agreement that, according to the plaintiffs, would exempt the site from any future data center moratorium. Developers, landowners and lenders should treat the entitlement as contested while the suit is pending.

Full write-up

A second lawsuit over the Project Delta data center site was filed in Stokes County Superior Court on November 12, 2026, according to an announcement by the plaintiffs' lawyers; the complaint posted on their case page carries file number 26CV0000976-840. The plaintiffs are four organizations, the National Hairston Clan, CleanAIRE NC, the Dan River Basin Association and 7 Directions of Service, joined by 16 county residents, some of whom own land next to the site. They challenge the Stokes County Board of Commissioners' 3-2 vote on September 14, 2026, to rezone about 1,850 acres of farmland and forest along the Dan River near Walnut Cove for a data center campus, which the plaintiffs' lawyers call speculative and one of the largest AI data center campuses in the country. Public radio station WFDD identifies the developer as Engineered Land Solutions.

This is the second rezoning of the same land and the second suit. Commissioners first approved a rezoning in January 2026, by the same 3-2 margin, WFDD reported. The same lawyers sued on March 12, 2026, challenging the decision's process, and in April the county voided that rezoning. In August the parties filed a joint motion for a consent judgment and the court formally voided the January rezoning. Engineered Land Solutions then reapplied, and the plaintiffs say the commissioners approved the new request against the county Planning Board's recommendation and heavy public opposition.

The new complaint, as described by the plaintiffs' counsel, shifts from process to the substance of the county's decision. The plaintiffs allege the supporting analysis was rushed and deficient and appears to have been generated by AI without independent verification. They say the approval came during a drought, without a confirmed water source, without limits on on-site power generation and without a complete survey of graves and archaeological sites on the land, which they say include Saura village sites and a mapped cemetery of about 65 graves. For its part, the developer revised its proposal several times after January, in part to add stricter standards on noise, energy generation and water use, which its CEO said responded to community concerns, WFDD reported in September.

Timing matters. The plaintiffs' counsel says the commissioners are set to vote on November 23 on a 15-year development agreement for Project Delta. According to the lawsuit as described by counsel, that agreement would lock in the rezoning's shortcomings, exempt the property from any future data center moratorium and commit the county to help defend the developer's interests against challenges from residents. The plaintiffs ask the court to declare the rezoning void and to block the county from relying on or implementing it. No hearing date or assigned judge has been reported, and the allegations have not been tested. A second approval after a procedural defect is now being tested on the strength of its record, and a development agreement signed while the rezoning is challenged protects only as much as the rezoning itself.
