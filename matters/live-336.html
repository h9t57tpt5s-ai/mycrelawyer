Illinois Appeals Court: Landlord Proved Conversion of Its Billboard Structures, But Gets Only Nominal Damages

Commercial Lease Disputes Ruling Issued

October 26, 2026
Added to tracker
October 27, 2026
Court or forum
Appellate Court of Illinois, Fourth District
State
Illinois
Presiding judge
Peter C. Cavanagh
Amount / scale
$19,907.97 past-due rent judgment affirmed; nominal damages for conversion (fair market value of the removed billboard structures not proven)
Parties
Plaintiff-Appellee/Cross-Appellant (Landlord): Michelmann Steel Construction CompanyDefendant-Appellant/Cross-Appellee (Tenant/Assignee): Link Media Midwest, LLC

An Illinois appeals court found Link Media Midwest converted billboard structures on Michelmann's Quincy lot but allowed only nominal damages, and upheld a $19,907.97 rent judgment.

In a nonprecedential order filed October 26, 2026, the Illinois Appellate Court, Fourth District, held that Link Media Midwest, LLC converted billboard structures it removed in April 2019 from a Quincy lot owned by Michelmann Steel Construction Company, reversing the Adams County circuit court on that count. Because Michelmann offered no evidence of the structures' fair market value, the court directed an award of nominal damages only. It otherwise affirmed, including the $19,907.97 judgment against Link Media for rent from May 2018 through April 2024.

Why it matters

The order applies the Illinois rule that, unless a lease allows removal after termination, a tenant must remove its fixtures while it remains in possession as a tenant or they become the landlord's property. It also shows that proving a conversion does not produce a damages award without evidence of fair market value, and that a landlord's election to treat a holdover occupant as a tenant carries the old lease's renewal terms forward.

Full write-up

Michelmann Steel Construction Company, an Illinois corporation, owns a lot on 24th Street in Quincy, Illinois, that it has leased for billboard advertising since a 1956 agreement with Quincy Outdoor Advertising Co. Leases from 1968 on used a preprinted form with a one-year original term and automatic year-to-year renewals for up to five years unless a party gave 30 days' written notice. The lease at issue, signed May 1, 1987 with Outdoor Graphics, Inc., leased "the existing structures" on the lot for $400 a year with 10% annual increases, and said structures the tenant placed there would remain its property, removable up to a reasonable time after the lease ended.

Link Media Midwest, LLC, which admitted it was Outdoor Graphics' assignee, had the billboard structures dismantled and removed on April 19, 2019. Michelmann sued in Adams County circuit court for conversion, tortious interference with prospective business relationships and breach of contract for unpaid rent. After a bench trial on documentary evidence only, Judge Scott Douglas Larson ruled on December 18, 2024 that Michelmann had not proved it owned the billboards, but awarded it $19,907.97 in past-due rent. Link Media appealed the rent award and Michelmann cross-appealed.

Justice Peter C. Cavanagh delivered the judgment, with Justices DeArmond and Grischow concurring. Reviewing the record de novo, the court held that the structures, fixtures that had stood on Michelmann's land for over half a century, were presumed to be Michelmann's, that Link Media did not rebut the presumption, and that removing them was a conversion. Michelmann's damages evidence went to the $68,931.17 cost of building replacement billboards and a claimed 10% loss in value from a required setback. It cited no authority for that measure, and with no proof of fair market value it was entitled only to nominal damages. Its tortious-interference claim was forfeited for lack of a reasoned argument.

On rent, the court held that Michelmann had elected to treat Link Media as a holdover tenant, creating a new tenancy on the 1987 lease's terms, including its automatic renewals, and that no written termination notice was proven. Link Media argued its liability ended when it removed the structures in April 2019; the court found it owed rent through April 2024 and held its request for equitable relief forfeited for lack of supporting authority. The case was remanded with directions to enter judgment for Michelmann on conversion and award nominal damages.
