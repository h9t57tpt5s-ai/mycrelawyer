State ex rel. Sommers Development Group, L.L.C. v. Cuyahoga County Board of Elections (Zoning Referendum Challenge)

Zoning & Land Use Ruling Issued
Date
October 19, 2026
Court or forum
Supreme Court of Ohio
State
Ohio
Property type
Land/Development
Amount / scale
Not a monetary claim; dispute over whether a zoning referendum stays on the ballot, with no dollar figure stated in the opinion
Parties
Relator: Sommers Development Group, LLCRespondent: Cuyahoga County Board of Elections

Ohio's Supreme Court refused to keep a citizen referendum off the Mayfield Heights ballot that could reverse a council rezoning for Sommers Development Group's planned townhome community.

The Ohio Supreme Court denied writs of prohibition and mandamus sought by developer Sommers Development Group, LLC to keep a citizen-initiated referendum off the November 3, 2026 Mayfield Heights ballot. The referendum would let voters reverse a May 2026 city council vote that rezoned a Rockwell Automation-owned parcel for Sommers' planned townhome community. The court rejected a laches defense raised against Sommers but held Sommers failed to show the Cuyahoga County Board of Elections abused its discretion or clearly disregarded the law in certifying the petition and denying Sommers' protest.

Why it matters

The decision shows that a developer's approved rezoning is not necessarily final in a chartered municipality that reserves initiative and referendum rights to its voters, and that Ohio courts give an election board wide latitude on the procedural questions a developer is most likely to raise to block a referendum. Developers relying on a council vote as the end of the entitlement process should separately account for ballot-box risk and charter-level referendum procedures before committing capital to a project.

A decision issued October 19, 2026 by the Supreme Court of Ohio is a reminder that in a chartered municipality with initiative and referendum rights, the city council's vote can be only the opening round. In State ex rel. Sommers Development Group, L.L.C. v. Cuyahoga County Board of Elections, the court unanimously denied a developer's request for writs of prohibition and mandamus that would have kept a citizen-driven referendum off the November 3, 2026 ballot, clearing the way for voters in Mayfield Heights to decide whether to reverse a zoning change the city council had already approved for a planned townhome community.
