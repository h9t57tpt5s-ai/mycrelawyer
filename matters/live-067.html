Sudhoff v. Deiso Moss (Ritz-Carlton Houston Broker Exclusivity Dispute)

Commercial Lease Disputes Pending
Date
August 7, 2026
Added to tracker
September 3, 2026
Court or forum
Harris County District Court, Texas
State
Texas
Amount / scale
$1M+ sought

A former Douglas Elliman Texas CEO's $1M+ pay claim against a Deiso Moss affiliate over the $290 million Ritz-Carlton Residences, Houston went to arbitration after his bid to halt construction failed.

Jacob Sudhoff, former CEO of Douglas Elliman Texas, sued an entity affiliated with developer Deiso Moss and principals Andrew Deiso and Taylor Moss in Harris County District Court, alleging he was never fully compensated for work he says helped secure the $290 million Ritz-Carlton Residences, Houston project's headline $30 million penthouse buyer and a major equity investor. The court denied Sudhoff's bid for a temporary restraining order and injunction to pause construction on the 45-story tower, instead sending the dispute to arbitration. Deiso Moss says Sudhoff was engaged only to raise equity, not sell units, and that his engagement ended before the sales launch.

Why it matters

Illustrates how an arbitration clause in a broker or consultant's engagement agreement can keep a compensation dispute from threatening an active construction timeline, and underscores the importance of precisely scoping exclusivity obligations between capital-raising and sales mandates on ground-up luxury projects.
