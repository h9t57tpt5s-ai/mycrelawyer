Computershare Trust Co. v. Showboat Properties LLC (CMBS Foreclosure on Atlantic City's Showboat Hotel)

Computershare, for CMBS investors, seeks to foreclose on Atlantic City's 475-room Showboat Hotel, arcade and garage, alleging no payments since April 2025 on a $48.5 million loan; Showboat wants a rent receiver.

Computershare Trust Co., representing holders of commercial mortgage-backed securities, filed a foreclosure complaint in June 2026 over a $48.5 million loan on Atlantic City's Showboat hotel, the Press of Atlantic City and The Philadelphia Inquirer reported. The lender alleges developer Bart Blatstein's Showboat entities have made no monthly payments since April 2025 and breached other loan terms. Showboat's attorneys asked the court to appoint a rent receiver instead of ordering foreclosure, and Blatstein told the Press there were no financial problems.

Why it matters

Beyond missed payments, the lender pleads unfinished renovations, contractor liens, a tax lien and unpaid insurance as separate defaults. Showboat's alternative, a rent receiver who would collect income and resume debt payments, would keep the hotel operating while the case proceeds.

Full write-up

The lender behind a $48.5 million loan on Atlantic City's Showboat hotel has filed a foreclosure suit alleging the owner defaulted on payments and violated multiple terms of its financing, the Press of Atlantic City reported on August 7, 2026. Computershare Trust Co., which represents investors holding commercial mortgage-backed securities backed by the loan, filed the complaint in New Jersey Chancery Court in June, The Philadelphia Inquirer reported. Barclays Capital Real Estate made the loan on October 31, 2022, according to Casino.org, and Blatstein personally guaranteed it, according to the Inquirer.

The action covers the 475-room Showboat Hotel, the Lucky Snake Arcade and the 1,950-space parking garage; the property's separate residential tower and the Island Waterpark are not part of it, the Inquirer reported. The lender alleges $13.6 million in missed payments since April 2025, unfinished room renovations and mold remediation, unpaid contractor liens, a $401,768.99 state tax lien and a suspension of Showboat's business registration. Casino.org reported that the lender says it advanced $969,695 in insurance premiums after Showboat failed to pay for coverage. The lender seeks to extinguish Blatstein's interest, have a receiver run the property and send it to sheriff's sale.

Showboat's attorneys did not dispute the missed payments, according to Casino.org, but said the debt is 'being addressed' and asked the court to appoint a rent receiver to manage the property and resume debt payments instead of ordering foreclosure. Showboat's court reply, as described by the Inquirer, said the liens, taxes and registration issues were resolved or being addressed, that mold was limited to a valet garage and a back-of-house room, and that a receivership would be highly disruptive. Blatstein told the Press of Atlantic City there were 'none whatsoever' financial problems. A judge was scheduled to rule on November 6, 2026 on the lender's motion for summary judgment; the outcome had not been reported in the sources reviewed.
