Grand at Doral I Condominium Association, Inc. v. Prime Properties at Doral, LLC et al. (Developer-Affiliate Association Control Suit)

Plaintiff: Grand at Doral I Condominium AssociationDefendant: Prime Properties at DoralDefendant: Premier Properties at Doral

The owners' association for the 80-unit Grand at Doral I condominium sued entities affiliated with the building's original developer, the Pablo Jose Valdes Florida Irrevocable Trust, alleging the trust's affiliates, Prime Properties at Doral and Premier Properties at Doral, hold 41 of the building's 80 units and a package of developer-only rights -- including exemption from leasing restrictions and capital-contribution requirements -- years after construction completed in 2022. The declaratory judgment complaint asks the court to determine whether Prime and Premier are validly exempt from the building's leasing restrictions and how parking spaces tied to an adjacent developer-controlled lot should be assigned.

Why it matters

Illustrates a recurring Florida condo-conversion risk in which a developer conveys a majority of units to affiliated entities that retain outsized voting power and disparate rights after the statutory turnover point, and where the declaration's own amendment mechanics can make those provisions effectively unamendable without the affiliates' own consent. Developers and buyers of post-conversion condominium interests should scrutinize affiliate unit-holdings and exemption clauses, since Florida courts have shown increasing willingness to entertain owner challenges to governance structures that preserve developer control indefinitely.

Original source ↗

Four years after Grand at Doral I welcomed its first residents, the 80-unit condominium's owners' association has gone to court to settle a question that has quietly shaped life in the building since it opened: who actually controls it. The association filed a declaratory judgment action in Miami-Dade County Circuit Court against entities affiliated with the building's original developer, the Pablo Jose Valdes Florida Irrevocable Trust, alleging that the developer never fully relinquished control of the property even though construction wrapped in 2022. According to the complaint, the trust transferred 41 of the building's 80 units -- a bare majority -- to two affiliated companies, Prime Properties at Doral and Premier Properties at Doral, both managed by Lucilla Suero and Niurka Fonte-Esquivel in their capacity as co-trustees of the Valdes trust.
