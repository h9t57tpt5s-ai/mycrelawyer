Hetley Associates v. Lemoyne Borough Zoning Hearing Board: Pennsylvania Commonwealth Court Rejects Strip-Center Owner's Use Variance for Overflow Parking on Adjacent Residential Lots

On October 9, 2026, the Commonwealth Court of Pennsylvania affirmed the denial of a use variance sought by Hetley Associates, owner of the Hetley Plaza strip shopping center in Lemoyne Borough. The owner wanted to demolish three houses it owns in the borough's Urban Residential District and replace them with parking for the center, which has fewer spaces than its commercial zoning requires. The court held that the claimed hardship belonged to the separate commercial parcel and to the owner, not to the residential lots that needed the variance. That failure alone defeated the request.

Why it matters

Retail owners often try to fix nonconforming parking by buying neighboring residential parcels. This ruling confirms that in Pennsylvania a use variance must rest on a hardship unique to the parcel being rezoned, not on the business needs of an adjoining commercial site under the same ownership. Owners and buyers of parking-deficient centers should not count on a variance to add capacity on residential land next door. The opinion is non-precedential, and its footnote expressly declines to decide whether consolidating the lots through a reverse subdivision would change the analysis.

Hetley Associates owns Hetley Plaza, a strip shopping center in Lemoyne Borough, Cumberland County, zoned Commercial General. It also owns three nearby residential lots in the borough's Urban Residential District, one of them a rented house. The center operates as a lawful nonconforming use with fewer parking spaces than the ordinance now requires. The owner argued that changing conditions around the plaza had made the shortage worse, and that a vacant space once occupied by a restaurant was hard to re-lease without more parking.
