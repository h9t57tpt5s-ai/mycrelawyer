Hetley Associates v. Lemoyne Borough Zoning Hearing Board: Pennsylvania Commonwealth Court Rejects Strip-Center Owner's Use Variance for Overflow Parking on Adjacent Residential Lots

Pennsylvania's Commonwealth Court upheld denial of a use variance letting the Hetley Plaza strip-center owner replace three houses on residential lots with overflow parking, finding no hardship tied to those lots.

On October 9, 2026, the Commonwealth Court of Pennsylvania affirmed the denial of a use variance sought by Hetley Associates, owner of the Hetley Plaza strip shopping center in Lemoyne Borough. The owner wanted to demolish three houses it owns in the borough's Urban Residential District and replace them with parking for the center, which has fewer spaces than its commercial zoning requires. The court held that the claimed hardship belonged to the separate commercial parcel and to the owner, not to the residential lots that needed the variance. That failure alone defeated the request.

Why it matters

Following its Bawa Muhaiyaddeen decision, the court held that a use variance must rest on hardship to the parcel for which relief is sought, not on the needs of a separate commercial parcel under the same ownership. The opinion is unreported, and a footnote leaves open whether consolidating the lots through a reverse subdivision would change the analysis.

Full write-up

Hetley Associates owns Hetley Plaza, a strip shopping center in Lemoyne Borough, Cumberland County, zoned Commercial General. It also owns three residential lots around the corner in the borough's Urban Residential District; one of the houses is rented. According to the opinion, the plaza is a prior nonconforming use with fewer parking spaces than the district requires. Its businesses mostly serve few customers, but one space, formerly occupied by a restaurant, is vacant. The owner argued that changing conditions around the plaza had worsened its parking problem.

In January 2025 the owner asked the borough Zoning Hearing Board for a use variance to demolish the three houses and build a parking lot. After a hearing, the Board denied the request on March 27, 2025, finding among other things that the owner's decision to rent to a restaurant had aggravated the parking problem. The Court of Common Pleas of Cumberland County affirmed on September 4, 2025, after argument on August 11. It found that the residential lots could continue to be used as rentals, which are in demand, and that the claimed hardship concerned the plaza, not the lots that were the subject of the request.

On appeal the owner challenged the Board's findings on neighborhood character, self-created hardship, practicality and minimum variance. In a memorandum opinion by Judge Christine Fizzano Cannon filed October 9, 2026, the Commonwealth Court affirmed on a single ground. Applying the borough ordinance, Section 910.2(a) of the Municipalities Planning Code, and its decision in Bawa Muhaiyaddeen Fellowship v. Philadelphia Zoning Board of Adjustment, it held that the owner was claiming a hardship to its separate commercial plaza, while the residential lots remain usable and valuable for residential purposes. The owner cited no authority allowing a variance on one property based on hardship to another, so the court did not reach the other factors.

In footnotes, the court noted that the plaza and the residential lots remain separate parcels despite common ownership, that nothing showed an attempt to consolidate them, and that it expressed no opinion on a reverse subdivision. It also rejected the owner's argument that the minimum-variance requirement does not apply to use variances. The opinion is designated as not reported.
