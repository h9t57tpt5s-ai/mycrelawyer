A federal judge let a class action proceed against three Lightstone Value Plus REITs, alleging 2022 proxies seeking to scrap liquidation deadlines hid the chairman's $59.8M conflicting stake.

U.S. District Judge Michael A. Shipp ruled on August 5, 2026 that investors adequately alleged that 2022 proxy statements and solicitor calls for three Lightstone Value Plus REITs omitted a material conflict held by chairman and CEO David Lichtenstein, AltsWire reported. Plaintiffs allege his subordinated participation interests, potentially worth more than $59.8 million, would have been worthless had REIT II and REIT III liquidated on their original schedules, and that the proxies disclosed the interests but not that fact. All four counts, including breach of fiduciary duty claims against the directors and the advisory entities, will proceed.

Why it matters

Signals that courts will scrutinize bundled, omnibus REIT charter-amendment proxies — especially those pairing liquidation-timeline changes with fiduciary-duty waivers, quorum reductions, or indemnification expansions — for whether insiders' financial stakes in the outcome were meaningfully disclosed, not just nominally mentioned. Non-traded REIT sponsors, external advisers, and independent directors involved in comparable extension votes should expect increased plaintiff interest and revisit both proxy drafting practices and D&O coverage.

Full write-up

A federal judge in New Jersey has allowed investors to pursue a putative class action alleging that the directors and advisers of three non-traded Lightstone Value Plus REITs did not disclose their chairman's conflict of interest before shareholders voted to remove the funds' liquidation deadlines, AltsWire reported. U.S. District Judge Michael A. Shipp ruled on August 5, 2026 that the plaintiffs adequately alleged that the proxy statements and related solicitor calls omitted a material conflict involving David Lichtenstein, the REITs' chairman and chief executive, who through affiliated entities owns the funds' external advisers.

According to the complaint, Lichtenstein held subordinated participation interests through three special-purpose entities, potentially worth more than $59.8 million, that would have had no value if REIT II and REIT III had liquidated by the deadlines in their original charters, September 2024 and March 2025. The plaintiffs allege the 2022 proxies disclosed that the interests existed but not that they depended on extending the REITs' lives, which the court found could have significantly changed how a reasonable shareholder viewed the vote. The court also found that phone solicitors made misleading statements between a December 2022 meeting, where the amendments failed, and a January 2023 reconvened meeting, where they passed; investors allege solicitors called a "yes" vote "the most likely path to liquidity."

The charter amendments, presented as a single item, also eliminated board fiduciary duties to shareholders, cut quorum requirements to as little as 33% of votes entitled to be cast, restricted shareholder access to investor lists and expanded indemnification for officers and directors, according to AltsWire. All four counts survive, including breach of fiduciary duty claims against the director defendants and the advisory entities. Lightstone did not respond to AltsWire's request for comment. The case, filed in November 2024, is now in initial scheduling before Magistrate Judge Tonianne J. Bongiovanni.
