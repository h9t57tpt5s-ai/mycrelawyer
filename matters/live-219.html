N17 Development sued Menlo Park, alleging the city violated state housing laws in reviewing its 665-unit Willow Park project; the city says it followed state law. A writ hearing is set for Dec. 14.

N17 Development filed a complaint and petition for writ of mandate against the City of Menlo Park on October 2, 2026, alleging the city violated state housing laws by not processing its 665-unit Willow Park project at 80 Willow Road under AB 2011 and by denying it Builder's Remedy protections, according to the city and KQED. The suit followed a July 29, 2026 notice from the California Attorney General's office that the city had violated AB 2011 and the Housing Accountability Act. Menlo Park disputes the Attorney General's analysis and says it processed the application under state law.

Why it matters

KQED reported that under AB 712 a court ruling against the city must come with fines of $10,000 per housing unit, at least $6.65 million here, and The Registry's headline puts up to $33 million in state penalties in play. The case also tests the city's position that a project with a hotel does not qualify for the state's streamlined housing approvals.

Full write-up

N17 Development sued the City of Menlo Park on October 2, 2026, filing a complaint and petition for writ of mandate over the city's review of Willow Park, a proposed redevelopment of the former Sunset Magazine campus at 80 Willow Road, according to the city's project page. KQED reported the case is in San Mateo County Superior Court. The 6.7-acre project would include about 665 homes, about 100 of them below market rate according to KQED, plus about 332,000 square feet of office space, about 17,000 square feet of retail and a 130-room hotel, in towers ranging from 301 to 458 feet.

N17 filed a preliminary application in December 2023 under the Builder's Remedy. The city's timeline shows it found the application incomplete twice in 2024, deemed it complete in November 2024, and then repeatedly found it inconsistent with development standards and ineligible for streamlined processing under AB 2011. The lawsuit says the project is 'by right' and that 'the city is not permitted to say no to the project,' KQED reported. N17 also seeks a refund of roughly $300,000 in legal fees the city had it pay for the review.

On July 29, 2026, the Attorney General's office sent the city a notice under AB 712 stating that it had violated AB 2011 and the Housing Accountability Act, and the suit invokes the penalties described in that notice. After a September 29 closed session, the city council said it disagreed with the Attorney General's analysis, and on October 1 the city told the Attorney General it had processed the application under state law. Mayor Betsy Nash told KQED, 'We are not rejecting the project. We are continuing our review in the standard way.' The city's position is that the hotel space keeps the project from qualifying as a housing project under the recent laws, KQED reported.

The Registry, which described the city as refusing to approve the project, reported that up to $33 million in state penalties are in play; KQED reported that a loss would require fines of at least $6.65 million under AB 712. According to the city, briefing runs through December 8, a hearing on the writ petition is set for December 14, 2026, and a decision is expected in mid-to-late January 2027.
