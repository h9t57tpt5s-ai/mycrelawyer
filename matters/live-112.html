State of Mississippi v. RW Development, LLC (Biloxi Casino Pier Tidelands Lease Authority)

Mississippi's Supreme Court held Biloxi had statutory authority to lease tidelands at Veterans Avenue to RW Development for a public/private pier without a lease from the Secretary of State.

The Mississippi Supreme Court on March 2, 2023 affirmed a Harrison County Chancery Court ruling that the City of Biloxi and Harrison County could lease public trust tidelands at the foot of Veterans Avenue to RW Development, LLC for a joint public/private pier without first obtaining a tidelands lease from the Secretary of State. Writing for the en banc court, Justice Coleman held that state ports and harbors statutes gave the city that authority. WLOX later reported that the lease helped RW win Mississippi Gaming Commission approval in December 2023 for a casino site at Veterans Avenue and Highway 90.

Why it matters

The ruling holds that specific statutory grants to Gulf Coast municipalities can authorize leases of public trust tidelands without a separate lease from the Secretary of State. For RW Development, the upheld lease became part of its successful casino site application.

Full write-up

The dispute concerns public trust tidelands at the foot of Veterans Avenue in Biloxi, where the Biloxi Port Commission had built a pier decades earlier without a tidelands lease from the Secretary of State; Hurricane Katrina destroyed that pier in 2005. On July 2, 2019, the City of Biloxi authorized an option agreement with RW Development, LLC to build and lease a new public municipal pier. Under the agreement, RW could use the pier as part of its adjacent commercial developments, but no gaming was allowed on the pier or on vessels docked there.

On July 11, 2019, the State, through then-Secretary of State Delbert Hosemann as trustee of the public tidelands, sued the city and RW, seeking a declaration that a party must obtain a tidelands lease from the Secretary of State before building on tidelands, along with injunctive relief. Harrison County intervened as a defendant, and the case later proceeded in the name of Secretary of State Michael Watson. In April 2021 the county and city approved a joint lease to RW. In October 2021 the Harrison County Chancery Court, with Judge Jennifer T. Schloegel presiding, granted summary judgment to RW, finding the city and county had authority under the state's ports and harbors statutes and the Seawall Act to lease the property for public use.

The Mississippi Supreme Court affirmed on March 2, 2023 (No. 2021-CA-01134-SCT). Writing for the en banc court, Justice Coleman held that Mississippi Code Sections 59-15-1 and 59-7-405 gave the city authority to acquire the site by reclamation and to build and lease the pier, and that the general statutes the State relied on must yield to those specific grants. The court noted that the county had rebuilt the sand beach with federal help after a 1947 hurricane and that the port commission had maintained a pier at the site for decades.

WLOX reported in December 2023 that the Mississippi Gaming Commission had approved RW's site application for a casino at Veterans Avenue and Highway 90 after four tries in 16 years, along with a plan to rebuild the pier and make it handicapped accessible. RW's lawyer said the lease upheld by the courts helped win approval, while an attorney for opponents argued the lease limits the premises to non-gaming use. Officials said construction of the $3 million pier would begin before work on the casino site.
