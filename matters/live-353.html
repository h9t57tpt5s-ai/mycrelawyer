Lampton Securities Partnership, LLP v. Louisville-Jefferson County Metro Government (Reverse Condemnation Ripeness Ruling)

Kentucky's Court of Appeals affirmed dismissal of Lampton Securities' reverse condemnation suit over an Ohio River floodplain tract, holding the claim wasn't ripe because Lampton never sought a variance from the floodplain board.

Lampton Securities Partnership, LLP sued Louisville-Jefferson County Metro Government and the Louisville and Jefferson County Metropolitan Sewer District for reverse (inverse) condemnation, alleging that 1997 floodplain ordinances stripped all economically beneficial use from a roughly two-acre Ohio River tract it bought for $800,000 in 1998. On October 30, 2026, the Kentucky Court of Appeals affirmed summary judgment for the government defendants, holding that Lampton's claim was not ripe because it never applied to the Metro Sewer District's Floodplain Board for a variance -- relief the ordinance made available and that the Board had granted to other landowners.

Why it matters

The decision reinforces that a regulatory-takings or inverse-condemnation claim generally cannot proceed until the landowner has exhausted the administrative relief the regulation itself makes available, such as a variance application. Commercial owners holding land restricted by a floodplain, zoning, or similar regulation enacted after acquisition should pursue and document every available administrative avenue before filing a takings suit, since skipping that step risks dismissal on ripeness grounds without the court ever reaching the merits.

The Kentucky Court of Appeals on October 30, 2026 affirmed summary judgment against Lampton Securities Partnership, LLP in its reverse condemnation suit against Louisville-Jefferson County Metro Government and the Louisville and Jefferson County Metropolitan Sewer District, holding that the claim was not ripe for judicial review. The panel, in an opinion authored by Judge Lisa Payne Jones and joined by Judges Combs and Easton, found that Lampton never sought the variance the local floodplain board was authorized to grant, so no court could yet determine whether the challenged regulation actually destroyed the value of Lampton's property.
