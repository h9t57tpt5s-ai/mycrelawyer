A Milwaukee County judge approved the sale of Southridge Mall from a Simon affiliate to a receiver-linked buyer assuming the $101.9 million loan and paying $7 million in cash, ending a CMBS foreclosure.

On October 6, 2026, Milwaukee County Circuit Judge William Pocan entered an order approving the sale of Southridge Mall in Greendale, Wisconsin, ending a foreclosure that began in December 2020, the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel reported. The mall, excluding its separately owned department store anchors, is being sold by a Simon Property Group affiliate to Southridge Mall Member LLC, a newly formed buyer tied to court-appointed receiver Spinoso Management Group. The buyer will assume the mall's $101.9 million loan and pay $7 million in cash, according to court documents cited by the paper.

Why it matters

The case shows how long a contested regional-mall foreclosure can take: almost six years from complaint to an approved sale, even after the borrower agreed to a foreclosure judgment in 2021. The structure is also notable. A receiver's affiliate buys the asset by assuming the securitized debt instead of the lender taking title or holding an open auction, which gives CMBS holders and special servicers a model for exiting long-stalled retail loans.

Full write-up

Milwaukee County Circuit Judge William Pocan entered an order on October 6, 2026 approving the sale of Southridge Mall in Greendale, a Milwaukee suburb, according to Milwaukee Journal Sentinel reporting republished by The Daily Reporter. The sale, planned since 2021, does not include the department store anchor parcels, which are owned separately. Under the approved terms, an affiliate of Simon Property Group Inc. is selling the mall to Southridge Mall Member LLC, a newly formed entity. According to court documents cited in the Journal Sentinel's reporting, the buyer will assume the mall's $101.9 million loan and also pay $7 million in cash. The buyer is tied to Syracuse-based Spinoso Management Group LLC, which a judge appointed in 2020 as receiver to run the property. A joint venture of affiliate Spinoso Real Estate Group LLC and Denver-based Wynkoop Financial is taking over repayment of the loan as part of the deal.

The debt goes back to 2013, when Barclays Bank PLC made a $125 million loan to mall owner Southridge LP, a Simon affiliate. Morningstar Credit data cited by Connect CRE places the loan in two CMBS deals, JPMBB 2013-C14 and JPMBB 2013-C12. The mall's vacancy rose as former anchors, including Kohl's and Boston Store, went dark, and a foreclosure suit was filed in December 2020. About a year later, the Simon affiliate and a JPMorgan Chase affiliate that had taken over the Barclays loan agreed to a foreclosure judgment and sale. Various legal complications then delayed the sale for years. Reported loan figures differ slightly: the Journal Sentinel cites court documents for the $101.9 million being assumed, while the Morningstar data cited by Connect CRE lists the loan at $100.9 million; this entry uses the court-document figure.

In a court filing supporting the sale, the JPMorgan Chase affiliate that brought the foreclosure pointed to Spinoso's relationship with the Village of Greendale and its understanding of the local market. Village Manager Mike Hawes said mall operations and management would continue as before in the near term. The separately owned former Boston Store property at 5300 S. 76th St. was bought in May for $3.3 million by BLVD Southridge LLC, an affiliate of Barrett Lo Visionary Development, which plans about 740 apartments and 20,000 square feet of retail there and expects to begin demolition in October.
