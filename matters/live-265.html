The owner of the Wellsworth Hotel in Southbridge, Massachusetts filed Chapter 11 the day before lender X-Caliber's foreclosure sale and now proposes a $5 million sale to an affiliate.

Southbridge Associates LLC, which owns the Wellsworth Hotel and an adjoining office building at 14 Mechanic Street in Southbridge, Massachusetts, filed a Chapter 11 petition on October 6, 2026, the day before lender X-Caliber Rural Capital, LLC's scheduled October 7 foreclosure sale. X-Caliber, which lent the owner $13.25 million in November 2022, has moved for relief from the stay or dismissal, alleging a bad-faith filing; the debtor says it filed to stop the foreclosure and restructure. On November 2 the debtor moved to sell the property for $5 million to an affiliate, Lundgren Equity Partners, LLC, with a hearing set for November 19.

Why it matters

The case shows a hotel owner using a Chapter 11 filing to stop a scheduled foreclosure and then proposing a court-supervised sale to an affiliate, with higher offers invited, while the lender contests the filing. The court's November 19 hearings on the sale, the lender's motion and cash collateral will decide whether the property moves through the bankruptcy sale process or back toward the lender.

Full write-up

Southbridge Associates LLC filed a voluntary Chapter 11 petition in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Massachusetts on October 6, 2026 (Case No. 26-41208). The petition lists its principal assets at 14 Mechanic Street in Southbridge and estimates liabilities of $10 million to $50 million. According to the debtor's cash collateral motion, the property, part of the Southbridge Innovation Center, includes the Wellsworth Hotel, formerly the Southbridge Hotel & Conference Center, an upscale independent hotel with approximately 203 rooms, and a 72,399-square-foot office building that is partly leased. An affiliate, Southbridge Associates II LLC, filed its own Chapter 11 case on September 21, 2026 (Case No. 26-41128). Both cases are before Judge Elizabeth D. Katz.

The debtor's motion says it borrowed about $13.25 million from X-Caliber Rural Capital, LLC in November 2022, secured by a first mortgage on the property, and filed for bankruptcy to stop a foreclosure X-Caliber had scheduled for October 7, to keep operating and to restructure its obligations. It says that by restructuring its secured debt, marketing the hotel and leasing vacant office space it can operate with positive cash flow. The court has authorized use of cash collateral on an interim basis through November 19 and ordered the debtor to file reconciled and projected budgets.

On October 19, X-Caliber moved for relief from the automatic stay or, alternatively, dismissal of the case. It says it is owed about $14.48 million and that there is no equity in the property, and it alleges the petition was filed in bad faith by the debtor's principal, whose entities recently acquired control of the debtor and who, it says, has conflicting interests in an affiliated cogeneration plant that supplies the hotel. X-Caliber says it rejected the principal's $5 million offer for the property before the filing, and it cites a September 2026 appraisal of $5.8 million with the plant's current power purchase agreement in place and $11.8 million without it. On October 29 the court continued the hearing on the motion to November 19 and set a November 2 deadline for the debtor to file a sale motion.

On November 2 the debtor moved to sell the property free and clear of liens for $5 million to Lundgren Equity Partners, LLC or its nominee, which the debtor discloses is an affiliate, with liens attaching to the proceeds. The notice invites higher offers of at least $5.25 million by noon on November 18, with a hearing on November 19. The debtor's motion says there is no indication of fraud or collusion in the proposed sale.
