German bank Helaba (Landesbank Hessen-Thüringen Girozentrale), which made a $50 million mortgage on 29 West 34th Street in 2018, sued Jeff Sutton's 29 West 34 Owner LLC to foreclose in September 2025 after sending default notices over unpaid real estate taxes. Why it matters

A foreclosure can end without an auction when the loan changes hands and the new holder reaches terms with the borrower. Borrowers and receivers should watch for mortgage assignments during a case, because a new lender can change its direction quickly.

Full write-up

The foreclosure case over Jeff Sutton's retail property at 29 West 34th Street in Herald Square has been settled, The Real Deal reported. On August 5, 2026, lawyers for 29 West 34 Owner LLC, the current lender and court-appointed receiver Ian Lagowitz signed a stipulation ending the case. Under its terms the receiver will be discharged, control of the property returns to Sutton's entity immediately, and the suit will be dismissed without prejudice once Judge Francis Kahn III signs the order.

Helaba, the German bank formally named Landesbank Hessen-Thüringen Girozentrale, originated the $50 million mortgage in 2018. It sent notices of default over unpaid real estate taxes in early 2025 and filed for foreclosure that September. Sutton, who allegedly signed a limited guarantee on the loan, was named as a defendant, and the court appointed Lagowitz as receiver. Lagowitz separately sued the owner in January 2026 to make Sutton personally cover $12.2 million of unpaid property taxes; The Real Deal reported that suit is expected to be dropped now that the receivership is ending.

The settlement came soon after Helaba left the case. Court records show the bank transferred the loan on July 17, 2026 to an entity called 29 W. 34th Street Holdings LLC, which then moved it to 29 W. 34th Street Lender LLC. According to The Real Deal, financial documents list SL Green's chief legal officer, Andrew S. Levine, as an executive vice president of the Holdings entity. Sutton, whose net worth Forbes estimates at $2.7 billion, bought the property in a joint venture with SL Green in 2006 and bought out SL Green's stake a few years later. Sutton and the lawyers involved did not respond to The Real Deal's requests for comment.
