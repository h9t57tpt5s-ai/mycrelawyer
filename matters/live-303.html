Ex parte City of Fairhope Planning Commission and City of Fairhope (Developer's Negligence Claim Over Denied Apartment Applications Barred by Immunity)

Alabama's Supreme Court held Fairhope and its planning commission immune from a developer's negligence claim over denied apartment applications; its arbitrary-and-capricious and due-process claims remain pending.

The Alabama Supreme Court granted a writ of mandamus on October 16, 2026, directing a Baldwin County circuit court to enter judgment for the City of Fairhope and its Planning Commission on developer 68V BTR Holdings, LLC's negligence claim over the denial of two apartment-complex applications, holding that both the volunteer Commission members and the city employees who advised them are immune from suit. The ruling leaves 68V's separate arbitrary-and-capricious and substantive-due-process claims against the City and Commission pending in the trial court.

Why it matters

The decision confirms that a negligence claim against a city or its planning commission over a development denial runs into both municipal immunity and, for volunteer board members, the Volunteer Service Act's additional layer of protection, even where a developer alleges the denial was made in bad faith. Developers who believe a planning body acted with an improper motive are generally better served pursuing a direct mandamus action or administrative appeal of the denial itself rather than a tort claim against the decisionmakers.
