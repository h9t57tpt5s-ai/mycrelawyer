In re F-Star Socorro, L.P. (Five Star Development): Court Conditionally Approves Disclosure Statement, Sending Commercial Property Debtors' $180M Exit Plan to a Creditor Vote

A Texas bankruptcy judge let Five Star Development's Commercial Property Debtors seek votes on a plan that would roll $180 million of bankruptcy loans into exit loans and pay allowed unsecured claims in full.

On November 13, 2026, the Houston bankruptcy court handling the F-Star Socorro cases conditionally approved the disclosure statement for the Commercial Property Debtors' Chapter 11 plan, so voting can begin, according to the ElevenFlo case summary. The plan, filed that day, would turn a $120 million senior mortgage DIP facility and a $60 million mezzanine DIP facility into exit loans without any cash changing hands. Ballots and objections are due December 7, and a combined hearing on final disclosure approval and confirmation is set for December 16, 2026.

Why it matters

The plan offers one route out of a lender dispute: replace the original lender's financing with new bankruptcy loans, then carry those loans forward as exit debt rather than refinance at emergence. Whether it is confirmed, and how the separate guaranty dispute tied to the lender's credit bid is resolved, will shape the outcome for creditors.

Full write-up

F-Star Socorro, L.P. and affiliated companies filed Chapter 11 petitions in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas on November 4, 2025, according to the ElevenFlo case summary and the court docket. The group, which developed a Ritz-Carlton project in Arizona and also owned industrial and commercial property around El Paso, filed amid a liquidity crisis and a dispute with lender Madison Realty Capital. The debtors allege Madison stopped funding construction and manufactured defaults on the Arizona project; Madison had scheduled a foreclosure on the Arizona collateral.

The Texas side of the business, which the docket calls the Commercial Property Debtors, is now on its own track. On September 25, 2026, the court authorized the sale of four El Paso County properties to Madison through a $90 million credit bid, subject to closing conditions, and separately approved a $180 million replacement financing facility from Farallon along with the use of cash collateral.

On November 13 the court conditionally approved the disclosure statement for the Commercial Property Debtors' plan, which lets voting begin and leaves final approval for a combined hearing with confirmation. Under the plan, the $120 million senior mortgage DIP facility and the $60 million mezzanine DIP facility would become exit loans dollar for dollar, with no cash payment. Allowed general unsecured claims would be paid in full from a claims reserve, without postpetition interest; insider and affiliate claims would receive senior interests in a plan trust, and parent equity would receive junior interests. Funding the reserve and meeting the exit-financing conditions are among the conditions for the plan to take effect.

Ballots and confirmation objections are due December 7, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. Central Time, and the combined hearing is set for December 16. A dispute over the clawback guaranty in the September sale order is set for hearing on November 19. Conditional approval is a procedural step, so objections to the disclosure statement and the plan remain open for the combined hearing.
