OKO Group affiliate 700 Edgewater Development sued four builder's-risk insurers in Miami federal court for $22.4 million it says remains unpaid on a $55.7 million claim over foundation settling at Missoni Baia; the insurers have paid $33.9 million.

On April 3, 2026, 700 Edgewater Development, LLC, an affiliate of Vlad Doronin's OKO Group, sued Zurich American Insurance, Allianz Global Risks US Insurance, ACE American Insurance and National Fire & Marine Insurance in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida. The complaint says uneven foundation settling during construction of the 249-unit Missoni Baia tower in 2021 led to delays and costs that brought its builder's-risk claim to $55.7 million, of which the insurers paid $33.9 million. The case, assigned to Judge Roy Kalman Altman, was stayed on May 28, 2026, according to the docket.

Why it matters

Builder's-risk claims on large towers can include delay costs such as postponed sale closings, financing expense and soft costs, and carriers may pay part of a claim while contesting the rest. Developers should expect a coverage fight over an unpaid balance to run alongside any defect suits over the same building.

Full write-up

An affiliate of Miami developer OKO Group, led by billionaire Vlad Doronin, has sued four insurers for $22.4 million it says they still owe on a builder's-risk claim for the Missoni Baia condo tower in Edgewater, The Real Deal reported. The complaint, filed April 3, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida by 700 Edgewater Development, LLC, names Zurich American Insurance Company, Allianz Global Risks US Insurance Company, ACE American Insurance Company and National Fire & Marine Insurance Company. Attorneys for the OKO affiliate and spokespeople for the insurers did not respond to The Real Deal's requests for comment.

The suit alleges the 60-story tower's foundation was damaged by uneven settling during construction in 2021. According to the complaint, fixing it set back the tower's temporary certificate of occupancy at least 16 months, delaying sale closings and causing losses it lists as business interruption, lost rental value, soft costs and financing expenses. The affiliate began submitting claims in 2023; it says the total reached $55.7 million, the insurers paid $33.9 million, and they have refused to pay the remaining $22.4 million despite more than a year's notice. OKO and Cain International completed the 249-unit tower at 777 Northeast 26th Terrace, the first residential project branded by Missoni, in 2024.

The coverage suit follows a January 2026 suit by the tower's condominium association in Miami-Dade Circuit Court against OKO's affiliate and nearly two dozen contractors and design firms. That complaint alleges cracks in slabs, foundations and columns, water intrusion, and problems with fire alarms, sprinklers, plumbing and elevators, and says OKO did not fix defects its own consultants flagged or disclose deviations from the plans. The association also says the building has not obtained a final certificate of occupancy because of unresolved construction and code issues. The federal docket shows the coverage case was stayed on May 28, 2026; a second complaint the same plaintiff filed on April 7 was closed in May.
