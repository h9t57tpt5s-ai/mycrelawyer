SITC Inc. v. Sarasota County (Live Local Act Zoning-Preemption Dispute)

Sitc Inc. sued Sarasota County and its commissioners over a 540-unit Live Local Act project, one of four suits after the county excluded two zoning districts from the law, Business Observer reported.

Sitc Inc. sued Sarasota County and its five commissioners on May 20, 2026, after the commission voted unanimously on April 7 that Florida's Live Local Act does not apply to the county's Open Use and Residential Estate One zoning districts, halting Sitc's 540-unit apartment application, the Business Observer reported. It is one of four suits by developers whose Live Local projects together proposed more than 4,000 apartments; the county has moved to dismiss all four. A non-jury trial in the Sitc case is set for Aug. 23, 2027.

Why it matters

The suits test whether a county can exclude zoning districts from the Live Local Act by interpretation, a course the county attorney warned in an April 3 memo would be an "uphill battle." Two of the suits also name commissioners personally, which led the commission to cap the legal fees it will reimburse.

Full write-up

Sarasota County has been sued four times in recent months by developers alleging it violated Florida's Live Local Act, the Business Observer reported on Sept. 2, 2026. The act lets developers bypass ordinary development review in areas zoned commercial, industrial and mixed-use if 40% of units are reserved for households earning at or below 120% of area median income. The suits generally allege commissioners broke state law when they voted unanimously on April 7 that the act does not apply to the county's Open Use and Residential Estate One zoning districts. County Attorney Joshua Moye had warned in an April 3 memo that excluding those districts would be an "uphill battle" that could expose the county to litigation and fees, according to court documents cited by the Observer.

Sitc Inc., a company controlled by the president of Lakewood Ranch-based Halfacre Construction, sued the county and its five commissioners, in their official and personal capacities, on May 20. Sitc owns land off Lorraine Road east of Interstate 75, zoned open use estate, and on Dec. 4, 2025 applied for a 540-unit apartment project there. The suit alleges the county's five-step process for Live Local projects violates a state law barring moratoriums and burdensome or restrictive regulations in hurricane-affected areas before Oct. 1, 2027, and that commissioners acted with "animus and malice toward developers." A motions hearing was set for Sept. 3, and a non-jury trial is set for Aug. 23, 2027.

The other suits were brought by New Pope Holdco III LLC over a planned 2,250-unit project at 421 Tatum Road, which also names commissioners personally; by D.R. Horton Inc. and the owners of a 50.85-acre Raymond Road property near the Celery Fields, over a planned 864-unit project; and by Yeshua's Love Biblical Fellowship of Sarasota Inc. and Curco Land Holdings LLC over a 352-unit project at 8893 Fruitville Road. In the New Pope case, Judge Hunter W. Carroll heard the county's motion to dismiss on Aug. 25, and a non-jury trial is set for March 29, 2027. On Aug. 25 the commission also voted to keep negotiating a proposed settlement with Yeshua's Love and Curco without approving it.

At the same Aug. 25 meeting, the county attorney reported that motions to dismiss had been filed in all four cases, and commissioners capped reimbursable legal fees at $100,000 per commissioner before they must seek further funding for their defense.
