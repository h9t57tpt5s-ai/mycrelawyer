Why it matters

The ruling puts Phoenix's special-permit requirement for data centers to a test under Proposition 207, which requires compensation when a new land-use law lowers property values unless the rule falls within an exemption such as protecting public health. The city will have to make that showing with evidence rather than win dismissal outright.

Full write-up

A Maricopa County Superior Court judge has ruled that the City of Phoenix must prove its data-center zoning ordinance is necessary to guard public health or face a lawsuit seeking more than $54 million, KJZZ reported on Oct. 28, 2026. The ruling, issued earlier in October, narrows but does not end the suit brought by West Buckeye Road L.P. (WBR), a Texas developer, and sets a full evidentiary hearing for Nov. 17.

According to KJZZ, WBR says it bought industrial land in Phoenix in 2021 and spent millions preparing it for a data center. In 2025, Phoenix adopted a zoning ordinance requiring special permits to build data centers within city limits. WBR says the rules impair its right to use the property as intended and would force it to buy state-of-the-art cooling equipment and soundproofing panels, resulting in "millions in unexpected costs." It seeks more than $54 million in lost property value under Proposition 207, the Private Property Rights Protection Act approved by Arizona voters in 2006, which requires governments to compensate owners when a new land-use law reduces property values but exempts rules meant to protect public health, such as fire and building codes or waste management.

The judge rejected WBR's second claim, ruling that nothing in the city ordinance allows a developer to sue Phoenix for compensation, but kept the Proposition 207 claim alive. Full dismissal depends on whether Phoenix can show at the Nov. 17 hearing that the ordinance is necessary to guard public health. Phoenix Mayor Kate Gallego, in a post on X, hailed the decision as a partial victory for the city. KJZZ did not report a statement from WBR.
