Hillwood's $20 billion Joliet data center plans are on hold after it offered ComEd a $1 letter of credit against a $587 million guarantee demand and ComEd scrapped the deal; Hillwood's suit is pending in federal court in Chicago.

PowerHouse Hillwood's plan for a $20 billion, roughly 800-acre data center campus in Joliet is on hold, The Real Deal reported on November 2, 2026, citing Crain's. Commonwealth Edison sought a financial guarantee that Crain's put at $587 million. Hillwood offered a $1 letter of credit, arguing that the full amount is not due until power reaches the site around 2029, and ComEd scrapped the deal and the related electricity agreement. Hillwood's suit against ComEd is pending in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois, where the judge declined to reinstate the retail electricity agreement because that question belongs to the Illinois Commerce Commission.

Why it matters

The case shows that the credit-support terms in a utility power agreement, often treated as boilerplate, can stall a multibillion-dollar data center before construction starts. Developers, lenders and equity partners on large data center sites should pin down when, and how much, security is owed before relying on a power commitment.

Full write-up

PowerHouse Hillwood's plans for a $20 billion data center campus on about 800 acres in Joliet, Illinois, are on hold and may be at risk because of a dispute with Commonwealth Edison over a financial guarantee, The Real Deal reported on November 2, 2026, citing Crain's. According to the report, Hillwood says a customer is ready to use the campus, though it has not yet bought the land.

ComEd began requiring new data center developments in 2025 to post financial guarantees covering the power they will need, the report said; for Hillwood's project, Crain's put that amount at $587 million. Hillwood, the Texas-based firm associated with Ross Perot Jr., offered a $1 letter of credit instead, arguing that the full amount is not due until power is actually flowing to the site, sometime in 2029 after the campus is completed. ComEd rejected that reading and scrapped the deal and the related electricity agreement.

PowerHouse Hillwood Holding LLC sued ComEd in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois on July 26, 2026 (No. 1:26-cv-08860), where the case is assigned to Judge Sara L. Ellis. The docket shows the court entered an order on October 26, 2026 on Hillwood's motion for a preliminary injunction and ComEd's motion to dismiss. According to The Real Deal, the judge denied Hillwood's request to reinstate the retail electricity agreement, saying that question falls under the Illinois Commerce Commission's jurisdiction, but also described the agreement as poorly written, which the publication said suggests Hillwood may have a strong argument on the main dispute. Federal regulators were also asked to step in, and the report said Federal Energy Regulatory Commission members David Rosner and David LaCerte said the $1 letter of credit offers no real protection and called the offer insulting.

ComEd's Max Leichtman told Crain's that six other developers signed the same type of agreement without calling it ambiguous, and that they "all posted the full amount." The case is unresolved, and the project's timing is uncertain while it continues.
