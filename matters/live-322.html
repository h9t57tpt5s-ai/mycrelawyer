Joseph F. Farivar Investment Group, Inc. v. HPC Vineburn, LLC (Groundwater Contamination Judgment Reversed)

A California appeals court reversed a $12,245,108.42 groundwater contamination judgment against the current and former owners of a Los Angeles industrial property over a 1,4-dioxane plume.

California's Second Appellate District on October 22, 2026 reversed a $12,245,108.42 judgment against HPC Vineburn, LLC, the current owner of an industrial property at 1919 Vineburn Avenue in Los Angeles, and former owner Meruelo Maddux Properties-1919 Vineburn Street, LLC. The trial court had granted summary adjudication to neighboring owner Joseph F. Farivar Investment Group, Inc. and its tenant, Bordan Shoe Company, Inc., on negligence, continuing nuisance, continuing trespass and declaratory relief claims over 1,4-dioxane contamination that migrated from the Vineburn property, where a manufacturer operated before 1989. In an unpublished opinion, the court held the plaintiffs did not prove breach of a duty of care as a matter of law, used a damages measure continuing torts do not allow, and should not have obtained partial summary adjudication of declaratory relief.

Why it matters

The opinion holds that damages for a continuing nuisance or continuing trespass are limited to injury suffered before the suit was filed, so 20-year projections of cleanup costs and lost use could not support summary adjudication of those claims. It also holds that a consultant's report showing contamination migrated from a neighboring site does not by itself establish that the current or former owner breached its duty of care.

Full write-up

California's Second Appellate District, Division One, on October 22, 2026 reversed in full a Los Angeles County Superior Court judgment of $12,245,108.42 against HPC Vineburn, LLC and Meruelo Maddux Properties-1919 Vineburn Street, LLC, the current and prior owners of 1919 Vineburn Avenue in Los Angeles. Justice Bendix wrote the unpublished opinion in Joseph F. Farivar Investment Group, Inc. v. HPC Vineburn, LLC, joined by Presiding Justice Rothschild and Justice Weingart. The plaintiffs are Joseph F. Farivar Investment Group, Inc., which owns the adjacent downgradient property at 4335 Valley Boulevard, and its tenant, Bordan Shoe Company, Inc.

According to the opinion, before 1989 the Nardon Manufacturing Company, a metal fabricator, operated at the Vineburn property and discharged waste including 1,4-dioxane. The plaintiffs do not allege that the defendants themselves put the chemicals into the groundwater; they alleged Meruelo Maddux owned the site from about 2005 to 2013 and HPC Vineburn since 2013. In 2018 the Los Angeles Regional Water Quality Control Board directed HPC Vineburn to assess possible sources, and a May 7, 2020 report by its consultant, Hazard Management Consulting, concluded the Vineburn property had contributed to the 1,4-dioxane groundwater problem at the Farivar property. HPC Vineburn then proposed a cleanup and said it had money set aside for it. A 2023 board order called for further investigation.

Relying heavily on that report, the plaintiffs won summary adjudication in September 2024. Their experts put the present value of a 20-year cleanup of the Farivar property at $11,779,000 and loss of use at $192,887. HPC Vineburn's expert estimated cleanup of the combined contamination at $5 million and said some of it originated on the Farivar property. After the plaintiffs dismissed their remaining claims, the court entered judgment on November 7, 2024 for $12,245,108.42, including prejudgment interest, and declared the defendants responsible for the contamination. Meruelo Maddux had not opposed the motion.

The Court of Appeal held that a landowner owes a duty to act reasonably toward adjacent property, but that there were triable issues on whether the defendants breached it, and the statutes the plaintiffs cited did not create a presumption of negligence. Damages for continuing nuisance and continuing trespass are limited to injury suffered before the suit, and the plaintiffs sought prospective, 20-year damages. The court also held the trial court erred by partially adjudicating the declaratory relief claim. It reversed the judgment, which also reverses the prejudgment interest award, and said it expressed no opinion on the merits at trial.
