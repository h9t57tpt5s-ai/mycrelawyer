A California appeals court reversed a $12,245,108.42 groundwater contamination judgment against the current and former owners of a Los Angeles industrial property over a 1,4-dioxane plume.

California's Second Appellate District reversed a $12,245,108.42 judgment on October 22, 2026 against the current and former owners of an industrial property at 1919 Vineburn Avenue in Los Angeles, holding that a downgradient neighbor's reliance on an environmental consultant's contamination report did not establish liability as a matter of law. The trial court had granted summary adjudication to plaintiffs Joseph F. Farivar Investment Group and tenant Bordan Shoe Company on negligence, continuing nuisance, continuing trespass, and declaratory relief claims over a 1,4-dioxane plume that migrated from the former Nardon Manufacturing Company facility onto their property.

Why it matters

The reversal shows that even an adverse environmental consultant's report commissioned by the defendant itself does not automatically establish the duty-of-care and damages elements needed for tort liability, meaning owners of contaminated or formerly industrial sites facing neighbor claims retain real defenses even where causation facts seem largely undisputed. It also reaffirms that successor owners of contaminated industrial parcels can face continuing nuisance and trespass exposure for legacy pollution for decades, regardless of who currently holds title.
