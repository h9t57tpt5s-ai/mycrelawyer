The Fifth Circuit held a Louisiana surplus lines property policy's arbitration clause unenforceable against its nine domestic insurers, and sent back whether their court case should pause while two foreign insurers arbitrate.

On September 3, 2026, the Fifth Circuit affirmed a ruling that the arbitration clause in a surplus lines commercial property policy covering Transportation Consultants, Inc.'s Louisiana property cannot be enforced against the nine domestic insurers on the policy, while the two foreign insurers must still arbitrate. Following Town of Vinton v. Indian Harbor Insurance Co. and Crescent City Surgical Operating Co. v. Interstate Fire & Casualty Co., the panel also rejected the insurers' equitable estoppel argument. It vacated the order lifting the stay of litigation against the domestic insurers and remanded for the district court to decide whether that litigation should pause during the arbitration.

Why it matters

The ruling confirms that, in the Fifth Circuit, a Louisiana commercial property policy shared by domestic and foreign insurers can split one coverage dispute into arbitration with the foreign insurers and court litigation with the domestic ones. Whether the court case must wait for the arbitration is left to a fact-specific stay analysis in the district court.

Full write-up

On September 3, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit decided what it called 'one of many insurance disputes following Hurricane Ida.' Transportation Consultants, Inc., which does business as TCI Trucking, owns property in Louisiana insured under a surplus lines commercial property policy subscribed by nine domestic insurers and two foreign insurers. The policy's arbitration clause covers 'all matters in difference' between the insured and the insurers in relation to the insurance, and a Contract Allocation Endorsement says the policy is to be construed as a separate contract between the insured and each insurer.

After a coverage dispute, Transportation Consultants sued all eleven insurers in Louisiana state court. The insurers removed the case to the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Louisiana under the Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards and obtained an order compelling arbitration with all of them and staying the case. After the Louisiana Supreme Court held in Police Jury of Calcasieu Parish v. Indian Harbor Insurance Co. that a Louisiana statute, La. Rev. Stat. 22:868, prohibits arbitration clauses in insurance contracts and that equitable estoppel cannot be used to get around it, the district court granted reconsideration as to the domestic insurers. It reversed the part of its order compelling arbitration with them and lifted the stay for them, while leaving arbitration with the foreign insurers in place.

In an opinion by Judge Stephen A. Higginson, for a panel with Judges Carl E. Stewart and James C. Ho, the Fifth Circuit adopted the reasoning of its decision in Town of Vinton and its unpublished Crescent City decision. Because the endorsement makes each insurer's contract separate, only the contracts with the foreign insurers fall under the Convention; Louisiana law governs the domestic insurers' contracts and bars enforcement of their arbitration clauses, and the insurers cannot use equitable estoppel to avoid that law. The court affirmed the order compelling the foreign insurers to arbitrate and the denial of arbitration as to the domestic insurers.

The insurers also asked the court to pause the litigation against the domestic insurers until the arbitration ends, as the Crescent City panel had ordered in that case. Because the district court lifted the stay before Town of Vinton and Crescent City were decided, and the stay factors had not been briefed, the Fifth Circuit vacated that order and remanded for the district court to consider a stay in the first instance. The district court docket on CourtListener shows the case was reopened on October 15, 2026, with motion briefing under way in November.
