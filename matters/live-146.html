Blue Sky Towers IV sued Waldoboro, Maine and its town boards after a variance denial for a 194-foot tower about five miles from Route 1, alleging an effective prohibition of wireless service.

Blue Sky Towers IV LLC sued the Town of Waldoboro, Maine, its Board of Appeals and its Planning Board in the U.S. District Court for the District of Maine on September 11, 2026, Wireless Estimator reported. The suit follows the Board of Appeals' unanimous August 20 denial of a variance for a 194-foot tower at 2734 Friendship Road, about five miles from Route 1, where a town rule confines wireless facilities to within 300 yards of that road. Blue Sky alleges the denial effectively prohibits personal wireless service in violation of the Telecommunications Act and lacked substantial evidence.

Why it matters

The case tests whether a town rule limiting wireless facilities to one road corridor can stand when a carrier claims a coverage gap elsewhere in town. The town attorney's view that the rule is a location requirement the appeals board could not vary leaves the federal question to the court.

Full write-up

Blue Sky Towers IV LLC, suing on its own behalf and for the benefit of Cellco Partnership d/b/a Verizon Wireless, filed suit on September 11, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the District of Maine against the Town of Waldoboro, its Board of Appeals and its Planning Board, Wireless Estimator reported on September 15. The company wants to build a 194-foot self-support tower at 2734 Friendship Road in South Waldoboro, with Verizon antennas at 190 feet, to fill what it says are coverage gaps along Route 220, Finntown Road, Back Cove Road and nearby areas. The case (No. 2:26-cv-00440) is assigned to Judge Stacey D. Neumann.

Waldoboro's Rural Zoning District allows wireless facilities with Planning Board site-plan approval, but a separate provision limits them to within 300 yards of Route 1; the site is about five miles away. Blue Sky sought a dimensional variance. Town attorney Amy K. Tchao advised that the provision is a location requirement rather than a setback, that Maine law left the Board of Appeals without jurisdiction to vary it, and that the federal effective-prohibition question belonged to the courts. The board unanimously denied the variance on August 20, 2026. The complaint also alleges that the Planning Board chairman spoke against the tower at a July 9 hearing and later recused himself at Blue Sky's request.

The complaint claims the denial effectively prohibits personal wireless services under Section 332(c)(7) of the Telecommunications Act and was not supported by substantial evidence, and adds a state-law claim that the board erred by not considering the effective-prohibition issue. Blue Sky seeks expedited review, a declaration that the 300-yard rule is invalid as applied, an order requiring site-plan approval within 30 days after it resumes its application, and costs and attorneys' fees. Wireless Estimator reported no response from the town. A separate federal docket shows that Blue Sky Towers III LLC sued the Town of Nobleboro's Appeals Board in February 2026; that case closed in May 2026.
