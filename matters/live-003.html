The CMBS trustee for Benchmark 2026-B43 sued to foreclose a $34.4M loan secured by a Methuen, MA parcel and a Michigan shopping center after the borrowers missed their very first payment and two guarantors filed Chapter 11. The complaint alleges the borrowers let their own affiliated tenants under 99-year leases stop paying rent rather than enforce those leases — conduct the trustee calls 'affiliate atrophy' — and seeks mortgage foreclosure, UCC foreclosure on personal property, and appointment of a receiver to collect rents and step into the affiliate leases and bankruptcies.

Why it matters

Signals that special servicers are willing to argue a borrower's failure to enforce intercompany leases against its own affiliates is itself a form of loan mismanagement, looking past special-purpose-entity structures to related-party cash flows. Sponsors relying on affiliated master-lease structures, and guarantors whose personal bankruptcy filings can independently trigger cross-default, should take note as CMBS distress enforcement accelerates.
