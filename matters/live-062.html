Blackstone-affiliated landlord LivCor will pay $7 million and stop using rivals' non-public data to set rents, settling DOJ and nine-state RealPage antitrust claims. It admitted no wrongdoing.

U.S. District Judge William L. Osteen Jr. of the Middle District of North Carolina entered a final judgment between the Justice Department and LivCor, LLC on May 19, 2026, and a consent judgment between LivCor and nine states on June 26, 2026. Both resolve claims that the Blackstone-affiliated landlord used RealPage software and rivals' data to set apartment rents. North Carolina Attorney General Jeff Jackson announced the $7 million state settlement on June 18, ABC11 reported; LivCor agreed to stop using other landlords' non-public data to set rents and admitted no wrongdoing. Why it matters

The LivCor resolution pairs a payment with limits on how the landlord sets rents, following earlier settlements by Greystar and Cortland that North Carolina's attorney general cited. It narrows the case to RealPage and the landlords that have not fully resolved the claims.

Full write-up

A federal judge in North Carolina has entered settlements between LivCor, LLC, a multifamily landlord affiliated with Blackstone, and both the U.S. Justice Department and nine states over claims that LivCor used RealPage's revenue-management software and competitors' data to set apartment rents. Judge William L. Osteen Jr. of the Middle District of North Carolina entered the federal final judgment on May 19, 2026, and the states' final consent judgment and dismissal with prejudice on June 26, 2026, according to the court docket.

LivCor was added to the government's antitrust case against RealPage in an amended complaint filed January 7, 2025, which named several large landlords. The Justice Department filed its proposed settlement with LivCor on December 23, 2025, and in April 2026 moved for its entry, certifying compliance with the Antitrust Procedures and Penalties Act. LivCor and the states filed a joint motion for their consent judgment on June 18, 2026.

Attorney General Jeff Jackson announced the state settlement that day, ABC11 reported. His office said the settlement amount was $7 million and that LivCor controls about 3,500 apartments in North Carolina. LivCor will stop using non-public data from other landlords, through RealPage's software or by other means, to set rents. The attorneys general of California, Colorado, Connecticut, Illinois, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Oregon and Tennessee joined North Carolina. LivCor admitted no wrongdoing and did not respond to ABC11's request for comment.

Jackson said Greystar and Cortland, the state's two largest landlords, had already settled. On September 30, 2026, Judge Osteen denied motions to dismiss filed by RealPage, Camden Property Trust, Pinnacle Property Management Services and Willow Bridge Property Company, and denied LivCor's and Greystar's motions as moot. Those four defendants answered the amended complaint on November 11, 2026. The Justice Department has separately filed proposed settlements with Willow Bridge and Pinnacle.
