Wilmington Trust, for CMBS bondholders, sued to foreclose a $25.4 million loan on the land under Chicago's Hotel Lincoln after it matured unpaid; the hotel's leasehold is not targeted.

Wilmington Trust, representing bondholders in a commercial mortgage pool, sued Nakash Lincoln LLC and Lincoln 1816 LLC in Cook County on Aug. 14, 2026, alleging they failed to repay a $25.4 million loan on the land at 1816 North Clark Street when it matured earlier this year. The suit seeks the owner's fee interest and landlord rights in the land under Hotel Lincoln, and leaves the hotel's ground lease and leasehold, held by a separate Nakash-led entity, in place, The Real Deal reported. The hotel building has its own $35 million loan, which is not part of this case.

Why it matters

Splitting a hotel into a land parcel and a building leasehold, each with its own loan, means a default on one need not reach the other: here the lender is going after the land and the rent it earns, not the hotel. Lenders and buyers looking at split-ownership hotels should review each layer's debt and the ground lease separately.

Full write-up

Wilmington Trust, acting for bondholders in a pool of commercial mortgage debt, filed a foreclosure suit in Cook County court on Friday, Aug. 14, 2026 against Nakash Lincoln LLC and Lincoln 1816 LLC, entities backed by the Nakash family, founders of the Jordache denim brand, The Real Deal reported. The lender alleges the $25.4 million loan on 1816 North Clark Street, the site of Hotel Lincoln across from Lincoln Park, matured earlier this year without being repaid. It also claims $842,580 in accrued interest and default fees.

The loan is on the land, not the hotel building. The suit seeks the fee simple interest and the landlord's rights at 1816 North Clark Street, and leaves intact the operating ground lease and the leasehold held by a separate Nakash-led entity that runs the 184-room hotel. The building has its own $35 million loan from Delaware Life Insurance Company, which The Real Deal said does not appear to have been securitized. The Nakash family bought the land and the building together for $73 million in 2016 from Neil Bluhm's Walton Street Capital.

According to Morningstar loan data cited by The Real Deal, the land loan was originated by LStar Capital Finance in 2016 against a $30 million valuation. Ground-lease rent from the hotel entity covered debt service in 2025, but the appraisal was cut by nearly $6.4 million in May. After the maturity default the loan moved to special servicer LNR Partners. The borrower hired workout firm Iron Hound to negotiate an extension, but servicer updates show LNR filed suit while it was still waiting for a written proposal.

Wilmington's attorney and representatives of LNR and the Nakash family did not immediately respond to The Real Deal's requests for comment. The publication also reported that Wells Fargo filed a separate foreclosure complaint in New York over the Nakash-owned retail space at 633 Third Avenue days earlier, and that the family has defaulted on a loan on the Ocean Drive Breakwater Hotel in Miami Beach.
