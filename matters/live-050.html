Cushman & Wakefield replaced SL Green as manager of Worldwide Plaza under receiver Hilco Global as a $940 million CMBS foreclosure proceeds, Bisnow reported.

A judge approved moving management of Worldwide Plaza, the 49-story office tower at 825 Eighth Ave., away from SL Green by July 1, and Cushman & Wakefield has replaced it under court-appointed receiver Hilco Global, Bisnow reported, citing court filings. Hilco was appointed in March in a foreclosure suit over the $940 million senior mortgage, now pursued by Wilmington Trust for CMBS bondholders after Goldman Sachs and Deutsche Bank were removed as plaintiffs. Separately, Extell Development is pursuing a UCC foreclosure on the property's mezzanine debt, which owners SL Green and RXR are contesting on appeal.

Why it matters

The case shows lenders at different levels of the capital stack pursuing separate remedies against one building: a receivership and judicial foreclosure by the senior lender and a UCC foreclosure by a mezzanine holder. The receiver has already replaced the owners' property manager before any sale.

Full write-up

Cushman & Wakefield has replaced SL Green Realty as property manager of Worldwide Plaza, the 49-story Midtown Manhattan office tower at 825 Eighth Ave., according to court filings reported by Bisnow on Aug. 10, 2026. A judge approved transferring management away from SL Green by July 1. Hilco Global was appointed temporary receiver in March, and as part of the transition the receiver set up new bank accounts, obtained other insurance coverage and hired a new tax consultant and leasing brokers.

Goldman Sachs, Deutsche Bank and a trustee for CMBS bondholders, together responsible for the $940 million senior mortgage, sued in New York State Supreme Court in Manhattan to foreclose, Commercial Property Executive reported in February 2026, citing Bisnow. Goldman Sachs and Deutsche Bank have since been removed as plaintiffs, leaving Wilmington Trust representing the bondholders. According to the lawsuit as described by CPE, the 2017 loan was split into a $705 million CMBS loan and a $235 million companion loan, and beginning in 2025 the borrowers repeatedly missed or only partly made interest payments, including a payment of more than $2.9 million due Dec. 8, and did not pay $6.5 million of a $21.6 million tax bill in January. An SL Green spokesperson told Bisnow at the time that the suit was a procedural step and that all parties were working toward a resolution.

Law firm Cravath, Swaine & Moore left its 617,000 square feet at the tower in 2024, and an appraisal the next year cut the property's valuation by $1.4 billion, Bisnow reported. SL Green's earnings statement put occupancy at 61% as of June 30, while the receiver's June report put it at 51%. The receiver also reported monthly net operating income of negative $484,000, said WNET was preparing to leave when its 95,000-square-foot lease expired, and said default and eviction proceedings were under way against three retail tenants. On SL Green's July 23 earnings call, Chairman and CEO Marc Holliday said the asset needed to be recapitalized after Cravath's move-out.

Extell Development acquired the property's $190 million senior mezzanine loan in October 2025, about a year after the owners defaulted on that debt, and scheduled its own UCC foreclosure auction. SL Green and RXR sued to block it, but Justice Andrea Masley denied their request for a preliminary injunction; they appealed, and that case is ongoing. A $70 million junior mezzanine loan is also in monetary default, according to an SEC filing by New York REIT Liquidating LLC, which holds a 49.9% interest in the property; the SL Green-RXR venture owns 50.1%. Bondholders stand to lose up to $488 million, Bloomberg reported, according to Bisnow. Representatives for SL Green, RXR and Cushman did not immediately respond to Bisnow's requests for comment.
