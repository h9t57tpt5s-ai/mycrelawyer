Developer Brian O'Neill sued Upper Merion's supervisors on Sept. 16, 2026 to reverse their Aug. 13 denial of five data center plans near King of Prussia, alleging a bad-faith reversal, the Inquirer reported.

Developer Brian O'Neill filed land-use appeals on September 16, 2026 in Montgomery County Court of Common Pleas against the Upper Merion Township Board of Supervisors, seeking to reverse its unanimous August 13 denial of five data center plans totaling about 4.6 million square feet, The Philadelphia Inquirer reported. The suits claim the board acted in bad faith after officials had said data centers qualify as permitted warehouses. Township officials declined to comment pending review. In October, PhillyVoice reported, the developers withdrew related zoning hearing board applications while pursuing the court challenges.

Why it matters

The appeals ask a court to decide whether the township could reject data center plans after its zoning officer had called the use permitted. The developer is also asking the court to take fact-finding away from the board by appointing an independent referee.

Full write-up

Developer Brian O'Neill, with MLP Ventures, proposed five data centers on five of his properties in Upper Merion Township, between West Conshohocken and Bridgeport, totaling about 4.6 million square feet, The Philadelphia Inquirer reported. At an August 13, 2026 meeting, the Board of Supervisors unanimously rejected all five plans, saying they lacked specificity, including on fire safety, and did not meet zoning requirements. The day before, O'Neill had sued over the township's refusal to extend the review to September 30; Judge Garrett D. Page paused township proceedings, but the pause was vacated the next day, hours before the vote.

On Wednesday, September 16, 2026, O'Neill filed land-use appeals in Montgomery County Court of Common Pleas. His team says township officials had said data centers qualify as warehouses permitted under the zoning code, including in a zoning officer's determination letter, and reversed course after residents objected. The suits claim the board acted in bad faith and denied the plans over "minor technical items," and ask a judge to reverse the denial, grant preliminary approval and appoint an independent third-party referee to hold an evidentiary hearing. O'Neill's attorney said the township "did a 180" under public pressure. Township Manager Anthony Hamaday said officials had not yet received the documents and declined to comment pending review.

Residents have organized against the plans, and a petition had about 18,000 signatures, the Inquirer reported. On October 19, 2026, the developers withdrew seven applications before the township's zoning hearing board, PhillyVoice reported; it said their September suits also argue the project should be judged under the zoning law in place before a March 2026 ordinance tightened rules for such projects. The township has said the developers did not provide enough information on community and environmental impacts and did not meet zoning requirements.
