MLP Ventures v. Upper Merion Township (Renaissance Park Data Center Denial Appeal)

MLP Ventures, developer Brian O'Neill's real estate company, sued Upper Merion Township, its Planning Commission, and its Board of Supervisors in Montgomery County Court of Common Pleas on September 16, 2026, seeking to reverse the township's denial of five proposed data centers totaling more than 4.6 million square feet near King of Prussia. The suit follows an earlier round of litigation in which a judge briefly enjoined the township from acting on the applications, an order vacated hours before the supervisors unanimously denied the project on August 13, 2026.

Why it matters

Illustrates the growing litigation exposure municipalities face as AI-driven data center proposals collide with zoning codes not written for hyperscale campuses, and shows a rejected developer using both emergency injunctive relief and a full land-use appeal, including a request for an independent referee, to contest a politically charged denial.

Original source ↗

A Pennsylvania developer has taken its fight over a massive data center campus back to court, suing Upper Merion Township, its Planning Commission, and its Board of Supervisors for a second time in five weeks after the township formally rejected the project. The case is an unusually sharp example of the litigation risk now attaching to data center development nationally, as AI-driven demand for power-hungry computing facilities collides with municipal zoning codes never written with hyperscale campuses in mind. The developer, MLP Ventures, led by longtime regional real estate executive Brian O'Neill, is seeking to build what it has called the Renaissance Park and Innovation Data Center Campus, five separate data center buildings totaling more than 4.6 million square feet, spread across O'Neill-owned parcels in a stretch of Upper Merion Township between West Conshohocken and Bridgeport, near King of Prussia in Montgomery County. The project would rank among the larger data center proposals to reach a Philadelphia-area suburb, and it has drawn organized local opposition, including a resident petition against the plans that gathered more than 18,000 signatures.
