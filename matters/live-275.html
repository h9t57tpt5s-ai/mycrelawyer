City of San Antonio v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security: City Sues Under NEPA to Halt Conversion of 640,000-SF East Side Warehouse Into ICE Detention Center

San Antonio sued DHS and ICE under NEPA to halt conversion of a nearly 640,000-square-foot East Side warehouse, bought for $66.1 million, into a detention center for up to 1,500 people.

The City of San Antonio sued the U.S. Department of Homeland Security and U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement in federal court in San Antonio on October 13, 2026 (Case No. 5:26-cv-06542). The city wants to stop federal plans to turn a nearly 640,000-square-foot industrial warehouse on the city's East Side into a detention and processing center for up to 1,500 people. The city argues that the agencies have not done the environmental review the National Environmental Policy Act requires. It asks the court to bar further development until that review is complete.

Why it matters

The case tests whether a city can use NEPA and the Administrative Procedure Act to delay a federal agency's purchase and conversion of a large private industrial building that it had no zoning power to block. For industrial owners and brokers, it shows that a sale to a federal user can lead to litigation over what happens on the site after closing. Neighboring owners and lenders also get an early read on how courts handle impact studies of water, wastewater, power and emergency services when federal reuse changes a site's load on local infrastructure.

The property is the Oakmont 410 industrial warehouse at 542 SE Loop 410 Access Road on San Antonio's East Side. Texas Public Radio reports that the building is about 640,000 square feet and that ICE bought it early in 2026 for $66.1 million. In August, ICE hired a federal services contractor to renovate the building into a processing and detention facility. Texas Public Radio puts that contract at $17.3 million. Local reporting says the facility would hold up to 1,500 people and that ICE expects to open it this winter.
