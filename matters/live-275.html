City of San Antonio v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security: City Sues Under NEPA to Halt Conversion of 640,000-SF East Side Warehouse Into ICE Detention Center

San Antonio sued DHS and ICE under NEPA to halt conversion of a nearly 640,000-square-foot East Side warehouse, bought for $66.1 million, into a detention center for up to 1,500 people.

The City of San Antonio sued the U.S. Department of Homeland Security and U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement in federal court in San Antonio on Oct. 13, 2026 (No. 5:26-cv-06542), seeking to halt conversion of a nearly 640,000-square-foot East Side warehouse into an immigration detention and processing facility for up to 1,500 people, the San Antonio Report reported. The city argues the agencies have not done the environmental review the National Environmental Policy Act requires and asks the court to bar development until they do. On Oct. 22, Judge Jason K. Pulliam held the city's request for injunctive relief in abeyance and ordered the parties to file an update by Nov. 30, 2026.

Why it matters

The case tests whether a city can use NEPA review to slow a federal agency's conversion of a private industrial building it has bought. For industrial owners and lenders, it shows that a sale to a federal user can lead to litigation over the site's reuse after closing.

Full write-up

The property is the Oakmont 410 industrial warehouse at 542 SE Loop 410 Access Road on San Antonio's East Side. The San Antonio Report, in a story republished by Texas Public Radio, reported that ICE acquired the nearly 640,000-square-foot building in January 2026 and plans to convert it into a detention and processing facility that could hold up to 1,500 people. The San Antonio Current put the purchase price at $66.1 million and reported in late August that ICE had picked GardaWorld Federal Services to renovate the warehouse; the San Antonio Report put that contract at $17.3 million.

The city filed its complaint on Tuesday, Oct. 13, 2026, in the Western District of Texas. The CourtListener docket lists it as a review of agency action under NEPA, with an environmental-matters nature of suit. The defendants are DHS, ICE, and the DHS Secretary and an ICE official in their official capacities. City Attorney Andy Segovia said the required review covers not only air and water quality but also effects on the surrounding community, and that he expects a preliminary environmental assessment to find significant impacts, which could trigger a fuller study taking months. The complaint asks the court to restrain and enjoin further work on the conversion.

City officials said they had sent five letters to DHS and ICE since February seeking details on environmental reviews, permits and public input. Federal officials answered once, in June, promising coordination, community feedback and engineering and design reports, which the city says it never received. The week before the suit, the City Council directed city departments and local utilities to assess the facility's effects on water, wastewater, electricity and emergency services. DHS and ICE did not respond to the San Antonio Report's request for comment. Council members Marc Whyte and Misty Spears opposed the suit; Whyte called it "political theater" and estimated it could cost the city $500,000 or more.

On Oct. 16, the court ordered the city to file any request for a restraining order or preliminary injunction separately from its complaint, as local rules require. On Oct. 22, Judge Pulliam granted the city's unopposed motion to vacate that order, held the request for injunctive relief in abeyance and directed the parties to file an advisory by Nov. 30, 2026. Government counsel appeared for the defendants on Oct. 21.
