Mississippi's en banc Court of Appeals affirmed 6-4 Hinds County's permits for a 5,800-acre solar farm and battery storage project, holding the Board's order and incorporated application met the ordinance.

On November 10, 2026, the Mississippi Court of Appeals, sitting en banc, voted 6-4 to affirm the Hinds County Board of Supervisors' approval of conditional use permits for Soul City Storage, LLC and Soul City Energy, LLC to build a solar farm and battery storage facility on about 5,800 acres of undeveloped land stretching from Bolton to north of the Natchez Trace. A resident who opposed the project argued the Board had not issued the written decision certifying compliance that the county zoning ordinance requires. The majority held that the Board's order and minutes, which incorporated Soul City's application, site plan and supplemental materials, satisfied that requirement and that substantial evidence supported the permits. Four judges dissented.

Why it matters

The majority opinion indicates that, under this ordinance, a Mississippi county board can meet a written-certification requirement by incorporating a detailed application into its order, and that conditions an applicant volunteers can count as part of what the board approved. The 6-4 split, with the dissent reading the ordinance to require a specific written certification, shows the question was closely contested.

Full write-up

On November 10, 2026, the Mississippi Court of Appeals, sitting en banc, affirmed a Hinds County Circuit Court judgment upholding the Hinds County Board of Supervisors' approval of two conditional use permits for Soul City Storage LLC and Soul City Energy LLC (together, Soul City). Soul City plans a solar farm and battery storage facility on about 5,800 acres of undeveloped land stretching from Bolton to an area north of the Natchez Trace. Judge Lassitter St. Pé wrote for a six-judge majority; Judge Westbrooks dissented, joined by three other judges.

According to the opinion, Soul City applied to the Hinds County Planning Commission in April 2024. After a community meeting in Raymond attended by more than 100 people, it voluntarily revised its applications, including relocating construction traffic, offering a road use agreement, managing trees and vegetation, and using agricultural fencing and wildlife corridors. At a May 2024 hearing, the Planning Commission voted unanimously to deny both applications after hearing opposition, including from a resident who spoke as a designated spokesperson for opponents. On Soul City's appeal, the Board of Supervisors held a hearing in June 2024, at which the mayor of Raymond asked for a one-year moratorium on solar projects and the county's permit and zoning director said Soul City had followed the zoning guidelines. The Board approved both applications 3-2.

The resident appealed to the Hinds County Circuit Court, arguing the Board had not issued the written findings the ordinance requires and that substantial evidence did not support the permits. The circuit court affirmed in April 2025. The Court of Appeals majority held that the ordinance does not prescribe a particular form for the Board's written decision, and that by incorporating Soul City's application, site plan and supplemental materials into its order and minutes, the Board certified the project's compliance. It also found substantial evidence supported the permits, treated Soul City's voluntary conditions as part of what the Board approved, and rejected a judicial estoppel argument.

The dissent would have held that the ordinance's mandatory language required a written certification specific to the permit criteria, which the minutes did not provide. It pointed to the project's size, its expected 30-year life, the Planning Commission's unanimous denial and the close Board vote.
