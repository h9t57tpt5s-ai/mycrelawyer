Seven Baltimore residents sued the city and an MCB Real Estate affiliate to void the 2023 bills behind the $900 million Harborplace redevelopment, alleging violations of the City Charter and Maryland Constitution.

Seven Baltimore residents sued the Mayor and City Council of Baltimore, the City Council, the Board of Estimates and MCB HP Baltimore LLC in the Circuit Court for Baltimore City on September 21, 2026, seeking to block MCB Real Estate's $900 million plan to demolish and replace the Harborplace pavilions, Baltimore Fishbowl reported. The 31-page complaint asks the court to declare void three 2023 City Council bills that changed zoning and removed height limits for the project, along with the city's March 31, 2023 lease with MCB. It alleges the bills are unconstitutional "special laws" and that the city granted MCB rights in public parkland without following the City Charter's franchise procedures. The city said it would reserve comment for court, and MCB had not responded to a request for comment.

Why it matters

The suit attacks the legal process behind the approvals rather than the design, targeting the enabling bills, the lease and the Charter's rules for granting rights in public parkland. If the court agreed, the project's enabling legislation would be void regardless of its political support.

Full write-up

Seven Baltimore residents have sued to block MCB Real Estate's $900 million plan to demolish and replace the Harborplace pavilions at Pratt and Light streets, Baltimore Fishbowl reported. The suit was filed Monday, September 21, 2026, in the Circuit Court for Baltimore City against the Mayor and City Council of Baltimore, the City Council, the Board of Estimates and MCB HP Baltimore LLC, the MCB affiliate. Six of the plaintiffs live in the city, and most belong to a group called the Inner Harbor Coalition.

MCB unveiled the project in October 2023: two apartment towers with 900 or more residences, plus offices, shops, restaurants and public space. MCB owns the pavilions, and the city owns the land beneath them. The complaint alleges city officials pursued a rushed, behind-the-scenes plan to hand public land and parkland to a favored developer, and says the three bills that advanced the project were drafted by MCB's attorney and introduced at MCB's request. It also cites a three-year rent abatement in the March 2023 lease and a property tax reduction approved by the Baltimore Development Corporation.

The complaint claims that Bills 23-0444, 23-0446 and 23-0448 are "special laws" passed for MCB's benefit in violation of the Maryland Constitution, and that the city granted MCB a franchise and rights in Harborplace and Inner Harbor Park "by implication," without the ordinance, Board of Estimates review and time limits the City Charter requires. It says a Charter amendment ratified by voters authorized the project on 1.3 acres previously dedicated in perpetuity as public land, and it alleges City Code and Zoning Code violations. The plaintiffs seek declarations that the bills and the lease are void, a writ of mandamus, and injunctions against demolition, construction and permits until the city complies.

The Mayor's Office declined to comment; a representative said the city would "reserve comment for the appropriate judicial forum." MCB had not responded to a request for comment. According to the complaint, MCB has applied for permits for a first phase that includes demolishing the pavilions, with a proposed start date of October 15, 2026. Tenants told Baltimore Fishbowl they had been notified the pavilions would stay open until the beginning of 2027.
