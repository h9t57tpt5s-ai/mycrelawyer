The owners of a 98,000-square-foot industrial building at 2000 Folsom Street in San Francisco's Mission District sued robotics startup Physical Intelligence in San Francisco Superior Court, alleging it would not leave after its lease ended in September, The San Francisco Standard reported on October 6, 2026. The landlords seek possession and holdover damages of more than $13,000 a day, and say a new tenant wants to move in; The Standard reported that sources confirmed that tenant is Anthropic. Physical Intelligence had not responded to the complaint, and neither company nor the landlords' attorney responded to The Standard's requests for comment.

Why it matters

The case shows how a lease amendment that drops a month-to-month option and sets a fixed end date can leave a tenant that stays on exposed to daily holdover damages once a replacement tenant is lined up. It also reflects the competition The Standard describes among AI and robotics companies for San Francisco industrial space suited to building and testing robots.

Full write-up

The owners of a 98,000-square-foot industrial building at 2000 Folsom Street in San Francisco's Mission District sued tenant Physical Intelligence in San Francisco Superior Court, The San Francisco Standard reported on October 6, 2026, describing the suit as filed the previous week. The landlords allege the robotics startup has stayed on since its lease ran out in September, and they want the space back plus holdover damages of more than $13,000 a day. The building, a former food manufacturing site, had been vacant until the recent AI robotics boom, according to The Standard.

According to the lease attached to the complaint, as described by The Standard, Physical Intelligence signed a lease in January for about 29,000 square feet on the ground floor at $60,000 a month, running from February to May with an option to continue month to month. The space was used to develop and test robots and for related office work. In May, the landlord sent a termination notice setting a June move-out date. Instead of leaving, Physical Intelligence agreed in June to an amendment that raised the rent to $160,000 a month for a larger footprint through September and eliminated the month-to-month option. In that amendment the company said it intended to relocate and wanted to time its exit to the delivery of new space; The Standard previously reported that it has an agreement to lease 60,000 square feet at 808 Brannan St.

The complaint also alleges Physical Intelligence expanded into areas outside the lease without written consent and made improvements without permission. The Standard reported that the landlord agreed to waive that violation but is seeking the company's immediate departure. The landlords say a new tenant wants to move in as early as the week of the article, and The Standard reported that sources confirmed the tenant is Anthropic.

Physical Intelligence had not responded to the complaint, according to court records cited by The Standard. Anthropic, Physical Intelligence and the landlords' attorney did not respond to The Standard's requests for comment. The allegations have not been tested in court.
