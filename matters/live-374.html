Ritz-Carlton Development Co. Loses Appeal Over Eagle County Valuation of Bachelor Gulch Fractional Units

Colorado's Court of Appeals upheld Eagle County's doubled valuation of 54 fractionally owned units at the Ritz-Carlton Bachelor Gulch, rejecting Ritz-Carlton Development Co.'s appeal.

The Colorado Court of Appeals on November 5, 2026 upheld the Eagle County Assessor's valuation of 54 fractionally owned condominium units attached to the Ritz-Carlton Bachelor Gulch Hotel and Spa in Beaver Creek, Bloomberg Tax reported. Ritz-Carlton Development Co., a division of Marriott International, had argued the assessor was wrong to double the units' previous valuation. In an unpublished opinion, the court sided with the county and rejected RCDC's argument based on a two-step analysis.

Why it matters

The result leaves in place a county valuation of fractional resort units that doubled their prior assessed value. Because the opinion is unpublished, it is not binding precedent in other Colorado cases.

Full write-up

Ritz-Carlton Development Co. (RCDC), a division of Marriott International Inc., asked the Colorado Court of Appeals to hold that the Eagle County Assessor erred in doubling the previous valuation of 54 condominium units attached to the Ritz-Carlton Bachelor Gulch Hotel and Spa in Beaver Creek, Bloomberg Tax reported. The units are divided into 648 ownership interests, each entitling its owner to 21 days of use a year.

On Thursday, November 5, 2026, the court sided with the county in an unpublished opinion, rejecting RCDC's argument based on a two-step analysis, according to Bloomberg Tax. The portion of the article available without a subscription does not give the dollar values involved, the tax year, or the rest of the court's reasoning.
