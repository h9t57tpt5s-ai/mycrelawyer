A Houston appeals court affirmed summary judgment for a shopping-center landlord on a restaurant's repair, sublease, fraud and DTPA claims, along with $23,580 in trial and $15,000 in appellate fees.

On October 16, 2026, Texas's First Court of Appeals in Houston affirmed summary judgment for 2020 Harwin 59 Shopping Centers, Ltd., Harwin Exchange Center, Inc. and Harwin Exchange's president on claims by a restaurant operator and its owner over repairs and a refused sublease. The court held the lease assigned electrical, plumbing and HVAC upkeep to the tenant, that there was no evidence of the written notice the lease required for roof repairs, and that an oral promise to consent to a sublease was barred by the statute of frauds. It also held the DTPA did not apply because base rent of $8,700 a month for 60 months totaled $522,000, above the statute's $500,000 cutoff, and upheld fee awards of $23,580 for the trial court and $15,000 for appeal.

Why it matters

The opinion applies the lease's maintenance split and written-notice requirement as written, leaving the tenant without a breach claim for systems the lease assigned to it. It also shows a commercial lease's total base rent can take a tenant outside the DTPA once it passes $500,000.

Full write-up

A panel of Texas's First Court of Appeals in Houston, in an October 16, 2026 memorandum opinion by Justice Clint Morgan, affirmed a Harris County judgment against a restaurant operator and its owner, who had sued 2020 Harwin 59 Shopping Centers, Ltd., Harwin Exchange Center, Inc. and Harwin Exchange's president for breach of contract, fraud, Deceptive Trade Practices Act violations and quantum meruit over repairs and a refused sublease.

According to the opinion, the five-year lease for a restaurant space on Richmond Avenue in Houston was signed on October 1, 2019, on a Texas Association of Realtors form, with Harwin Exchange as landlord. It made the landlord responsible for roof repairs after prompt written notice from the tenant, and the tenant responsible for grounds, parking, plumbing, drainage and HVAC, including an HVAC service contract. The tenant accepted the space "as-is," and subletting required the landlord's written consent. The appellants alleged they were not told the lease had been assigned to 2020 Harwin in January 2021, that they spent hundreds of thousands of dollars on repairs and improvements the landlord would not reimburse, and that Harwin Exchange's president promised to approve a sublease and a sale of the business. They signed an estoppel certificate stating the landlord was not in default as a condition of seeking that consent, but consent was refused, and a materialmen's lien was later placed on the property.

The court upheld each ground for summary judgment. The lease assigned electrical, plumbing and air-conditioning repairs to the tenant, and the appellants did not address those terms. There was no evidence of a written request for roof repairs, the lease imposed no duty to reimburse, and the as-is clause defeated any claim that the roof had to be fixed before move-in. The proposed sublease ran longer than a year, so an oral agreement to consent to it was unenforceable under the statute of frauds. Quantum meruit was unavailable because the lease covered the same repairs. The DTPA claim failed because base rent of $8,700 a month over the 60-month term totaled $522,000, above the statute's $500,000 exemption for nonresidential transactions, and the appellants did not challenge the dismissal of their fraud claim for failure to replead after special exceptions.

The court also affirmed the fee awards of $23,580 for the trial court and $15,000 for appeal. The appellees had requested $31,574.87 for the trial court; the court said the lower award indicated the billing records adequately separated recoverable fees, and the appellants had not preserved their complaint that a jury should have decided fees.
