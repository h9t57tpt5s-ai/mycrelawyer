The Port of Tacoma sued PQ LLC in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Washington on February 20, 2026, seeking to recover cleanup costs under Washington's Model Toxics Control Act and a declaratory judgment. Why it matters

The case is a state-law cost-recovery claim by a public landowner against the successor to a site's former operator after an interim cost-sharing agreement ended. It shows how a buyer of a former industrial site can pursue the prior operator's successor for cleanup costs years after acquiring the land.

Full write-up

The Port of Tacoma filed suit on February 20, 2026 against PQ LLC, a Pennsylvania chemical company, in federal court in Tacoma, according to the complaint on CourtListener. The Port seeks to recover remedial action costs under Washington's Model Toxics Control Act and a declaratory judgment over two Port-owned parcels at 1114 and 1202 Taylor Way on the Tacoma Tideflats, which it calls the PQ Corporation Facility.

The complaint alleges that PQ's predecessors, the Philadelphia Quartz Company of California and later PQ Corporation, owned the parcels from 1941 to 2008 and made sodium silicate there, and that releases of sodium silicate, fuel oil and other materials raised soil pH and left metals, petroleum hydrocarbons and carcinogenic PAHs. The Port bought the property on April 1, 2008, found sodium silicate deposits in 2009 and enrolled the site in the state Department of Ecology's Voluntary Cleanup Program in 2012. According to the complaint, Ecology rated the site a 'High' risk in a March 2025 assessment.

The Port and PQ's predecessor signed an interim cost-sharing agreement on March 17, 2016. The Port alleges that in 2025 PQ refused to reimburse costs for a several-month period, and that the Port ended the agreement on April 15, 2025 and continued the cleanup on its own. It says it spent $7.3 million on an interim cleanup in the summer and early fall of 2025 and more than $8 million in all, and expects future work to cost several million dollars more.

PQ filed an answer and counterclaim on March 26, 2026; the docket entries available on CourtListener do not describe the counterclaim's contents. The court set trial and pretrial dates in June, and the parties filed a joint status report on November 16, 2026. The Port's allegations have not been decided.
