Lot 2 SBO, LLC v. Twitter, Inc. n/k/a X Corp.: Colorado Appeals Court Reverses $8.2M Boulder Office Rent Judgment Over Tenant-Improvement Rent Credit

The Colorado Court of Appeals reversed an $8.2 million rent judgment against X Corp. (formerly Twitter) over its Boulder office lease, holding the tenant-improvement rent credit applied automatically.

On October 22, 2026, a unanimous Colorado Court of Appeals panel reversed the Boulder County judgment that had held Twitter, now X Corp., liable to landlord Lot 2 SBO, LLC for about $8.2 million in unpaid rent and expenses on a 64,557-square-foot office building in Boulder. The panel held that the lease's tenant-improvement rent credit took effect automatically once the build-out reached the lease threshold and the tenant gave written notice, whether or not the landlord had yet received lien-clearing draw documents. It did not enter judgment for X. It sent the case back to the trial court with directions.

Why it matters

The ruling shows how a lease's tenant-improvement allowance mechanics can decide whether a tenant who holds back rent is in default or is simply using a credit it earned. Landlords who make TI credits depend on lien waivers or draw packages need to say clearly that the paperwork is a condition of the credit, not only of the cash payout. Tenants get a reminder that applying a disputed credit against rent can lead to eviction and an eight-figure trial judgment before an appeals court sides with them.

Twitter signed a 10-year lease with Colorado landlord Lot 2 SBO, LLC in 2020 for a 64,557-square-foot building in Boulder. The building, the Railyards at S'PARK office building at 3401 Bluff St., was still being built at the time. Courthouse News reports that the lease set monthly base rent at $199,866.88 once the improvements were finished. To get the company to sign while the shell was under construction, the landlord offered a tenant-improvement allowance of $90 per square foot. Twitter spent heavily turning the shell into a flagship office and then never moved in.
