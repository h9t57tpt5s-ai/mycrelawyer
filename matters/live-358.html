Why it matters

Suing on the property and the guaranty at the same time gives a lender a second source of recovery that does not depend on the sale price, and pushes the guarantor to negotiate early. Sponsors who signed personal guaranties should expect a default to reach them directly, not just the borrowing entity.

Full write-up

Old Second National Bank, a lender based in Aurora, Illinois, filed suit in Cook County Circuit Court on October 26, 2026 to foreclose on and sell a retail building at 400 N. State Street in Chicago's River North neighborhood. The complaint says the borrower, Great Street LLC, defaulted on a $9 million loan and owed about $9 million as of October 21, with more interest still accruing. The bank also seeks up to $2.9 million from Chicago investor Marc Bushala under his personal guaranty of the debt, and it names MAB Capital Management and WFB Gift as defendants.

The Real Deal, which reported the suit on October 29, said Great Street LLC is registered to a Chicago address connected to Bushala's firm, Spirits Investment Partners. According to the publication, the City of Chicago recently approved a $3 million permit for interior and exterior work at the four-story building, with plans to turn its ground and second floors into a restaurant. LoopNet listing information cited by The Real Deal shows the building's tenants include Dough Bros pizza and a manufacturing firm, Ghost LLC.

Bushala told The Real Deal that the owner wants to keep the building. In an email, he said the owner is "in advanced discussions on the recapitalization of the property," including a restructuring of the current loan, and that he expects a positive resolution soon. He did not describe the terms of the recapitalization.

The complaint pairs a foreclosure claim against the property with a separate claim on the guaranty. That structure lets the lender pursue the guarantor without waiting for a foreclosure sale, and it raises the stakes for any restructuring talks. Until the loan is restructured or the case is resolved, the pending foreclosure may also affect the timing of the planned restaurant conversion.
