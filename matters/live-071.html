Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. v. 181 West Madison Property LLC ($240M Loan Foreclosure on Chicago Loop Office Tower)

Parties
Plaintiff: Wells Fargo Bank, N.A.Defendant: 181 West Madison Property LLC

Wells Fargo Bank, N.A., as trustee for the J.P. Morgan Chase Commercial Mortgage Securities Trust 2020-LOOP and acting through special servicer Situs Holdings, LLC, filed a foreclosure suit against the borrower entity behind 181 West Madison Street, a 50-story Chicago Loop office tower, over a $240 million loan, coupling the complaint with a motion to appoint Hilco Real Estate's Matthew Mason as receiver. The borrower is wholly owned by Bermuda-based SinOceanic I Limited, which took ownership in 2023 out of prior landlord HNA Group's bankruptcy; the default follows a downsized lease renewal by anchor tenant Northern Trust and reports of unpaid utility bills and vendors.

Why it matters

Illustrates how quickly a single anchor tenant's downsized renewal can push a stabilized-looking office asset into default, and how special servicers are now bundling receivership requests directly into foreclosure complaints to secure operational control of distressed towers well before any final judgment.

Original source ↗CourtListener docket ↗

Wells Fargo Bank, N.A., as trustee for the CMBS trust holding a $240 million commercial mortgage loan secured by 181 West Madison Street, a 50-story office tower in the heart of the Chicago Loop, and acting through its special servicer, Situs Holdings, LLC, filed a foreclosure suit on March 27, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois, Eastern Division, against the borrower entity that owns the building. The trustee followed the foreclosure complaint with an April 3, 2026 motion asking the court to appoint Matthew Mason of Hilco Real Estate as receiver — a request, increasingly common in CRE special-servicing practice, to install independent control over the property's operations and cash flow while the foreclosure proceeds rather than waiting for a final judgment.
