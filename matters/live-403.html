Prologis v. City of Trenton, Ohio: Developer Files Federal Suit Over Voter-Approved Data Center Ban

Prologis sued Trenton, Ohio, in federal court nine days after voters capped data centers at 25 megawatts, arguing the cap cannot reach its permitted, nearly 1 million square foot project.

Prologis filed a federal lawsuit against the City of Trenton, Ohio, on November 12, 2026, challenging a charter amendment that voters approved on November 3, WVXU reported. The amendment prohibits data centers larger than 25 megawatts inside city limits. Prologis asks the court to declare the ban unconstitutional and to bar the city from using it to stop a nearly 1 million square foot data center at Kennel and Woodsdale roads, saying the city issued zoning certificates and building permits months before the vote.

Why it matters

This is a direct test of whether a ballot-box land use restriction can be applied to a project that already holds permits and is under construction. Developers and lenders on entitled sites in other towns face the same risk as local data center opposition shifts from council votes to charter amendments and referenda. The outcome will bear on how much protection issued permits and money spent give against a later change in local law.

Full write-up

Prologis, the San Francisco-based industrial real estate company, has sued the City of Trenton, Ohio, in federal court over a voter-approved limit on data centers. According to WVXU, the complaint was filed on Thursday, November 12, 2026, nine days after the election. The report does not give the case number or name the federal district, and city officials could not be reached for comment.

The dispute concerns a data center of nearly 1 million square feet that Prologis is developing at Kennel and Woodsdale roads in Trenton, a Butler County city north of Cincinnati. According to the complaint, the city encouraged and authorized the project and issued zoning certificates and building permits months before the vote. Prologis says it has spent more than $42 million on the project and describes it as a "vested, permitted, and substantially constructed project."

On November 3, 2026, Trenton voters approved Issue 17, a charter amendment that prohibits data centers larger than 25 megawatts within city limits, with about 80 percent of the vote according to unofficial Butler County Board of Elections results. After the vote, attorneys for a group of Trenton taxpayers asked the city to revoke the project's zoning certificate, arguing that the facility is no longer a permitted use. The measure reached voters after a court fight: on September 1, the Ohio Supreme Court ordered the city to certify the residents' charter-amendment petition, FOX19 reported.

Prologis argues that the ban is unconstitutional and that the law "does not permit the City to change the governing rules after the fact to revoke what it already authorized." It asks for a declaration that the ban is unconstitutional and an order preventing it from stopping the project. The reporting does not identify the specific constitutional provisions pleaded or mention a damages demand, and no response from the city, judge or hearing date has been reported.
