Sinmier, LLC v. Everest Indemnity Insurance Co. (Sixth Circuit Affirms Lender's Insurance Claims Died With Its Own Settlement)

The Sixth Circuit held lender Sinmier's deed-in-lieu settlement with defaulted borrower Vintro extinguished its mortgagee insurable interest, dooming its suit against the resort's insurers.

The Sixth Circuit affirmed summary judgment against commercial real estate lender Sinmier, LLC in its suit against the insurance broker and insurers of its defaulted borrower, Vintro Hotels and Resorts Ohio LLC, over storm and fire damage at the Maui Sands Resort and Waterpark in Sandusky, Ohio. The court held that Sinmier's own settlement agreement with Vintro, under which Vintro conveyed the resort by deed in lieu of foreclosure as full payment of its loan obligations, had already extinguished Sinmier's insurable mortgagee interest in the property, leaving it with no cognizable claim against the insurers.

Why it matters

The ruling shows that a lender resolving a defaulted loan through a deed in lieu of foreclosure or similar settlement can unintentionally extinguish its own standing to pursue a related insurance-coverage claim arising from the same collateral, if the settlement documents characterize the deed as full satisfaction of the debt. Commercial lenders negotiating workouts on distressed, storm- or casualty-damaged properties should coordinate the timing and drafting of borrower settlements with any pending insurance claims, and confirm they are named as mortgagee (not merely an additional insured) on the property coverage itself.
