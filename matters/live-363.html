Sinmier, LLC v. Everest Indemnity Insurance Co. (Sixth Circuit Affirms Lender's Insurance Claims Died With Its Own Settlement)

The Sixth Circuit held lender Sinmier's deed-in-lieu settlement with borrower Vintro extinguished its insurable mortgagee interest, defeating its claims against the resort's insurance broker and insurers.

On November 2, 2026, the Sixth Circuit affirmed summary judgment against commercial real estate lender Sinmier, LLC in its suit against the insurance broker, insurer and underwriter of its former borrower, Vintro Hotels and Resorts Ohio LLC, over the Maui Sands Resort and Waterpark in Sandusky, Ohio. The court held that Sinmier's October 2019 settlement, under which Vintro's deed in lieu of foreclosure was "considered payment in full" of its loan obligations, extinguished Sinmier's insurable interest as mortgagee, and every claim depended on that interest.

Why it matters

Applying Ohio law, the court treated a mortgagee's insurable interest as lasting only as long as the debt: once a settlement fully releases the debt, claims premised on the mortgagee interest fall away. The court also held that Sinmier forfeited several arguments by not raising them in the district court.

Full write-up

In 2018, Sinmier, LLC loaned Vintro Hotels and Resorts seven million dollars to buy the Maui Sands Resort and Waterpark on Lake Erie in Sandusky, Ohio, recording a mortgage on September 7, 2018. Vintro, which was required to insure the property, worked through broker Bankers Insurance (now Bearing Insurance Group, LLC). Policies from Berkley and Everest Indemnity Insurance Company were cancelled when the closing was delayed, and Everest's reissued policy, effective October 19, 2018, listed Sinmier only as an additional insured under the general liability coverage, not as a mortgagee.

That winter, a frozen pipe burst and flooded the resort, two rooftop HVAC units froze and caught fire, and other wind and cold losses followed. Vintro filed a claim with Everest on January 26, 2019; Everest advanced $350,000 and settled for an additional $775,000 in June 2019. After Vintro defaulted, Sinmier sued it in March 2019 for breach of contract, fraud and intentional misrepresentation and sought foreclosure. In an October 2019 settlement, Vintro conveyed the resort to Sinmier by a deed in lieu of foreclosure, "considered payment in full" of Vintro's obligations under the note or mortgage, and Vintro and its owner agreed to pay Sinmier $627,375 within nine months, secured by a consent judgment.

Sinmier then sued Bankers, Everest and EverSports & Entertainment Insurance, Inc. in the Northern District of Ohio, as a third-party beneficiary and, against Bankers, also for negligence, misrepresentation and fraud. Judge James R. Knepp II granted summary judgment to all defendants. Writing for a panel with Judges Gibbons and Davis, Judge Jeffrey S. Sutton held that a mortgagee's insurable interest extends only to the amount of the debt and ends when the debt does, citing Ohio cases going back to the 1840s and decisions from other states. By fully releasing Vintro's mortgage debt, Sinmier ended that interest, and all of its claims depended on it.

The panel rejected Sinmier's argument that the deed's no-merger language conflicted with the settlement, finding it forfeited and, in any event, consistent with the release. It held that the settlement's reservation of how insurance proceeds would be divided did not preserve the debt, that the $627,375 obligation was secured by the consent judgment rather than the mortgage, and that the policy clause protecting a mortgagee that has started foreclosure did not apply because the deed ended the action. Sinmier's claims that Bankers negligently failed to tell it about a cancellation or to secure mortgagee coverage were forfeited, and the second also failed on the merits.
