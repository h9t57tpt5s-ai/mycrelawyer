South Union Township v. Namdar Realty Group & Uniontown Mall Realty (Court-Supervised Mall Safety Compliance)

South Union Township's suit led a Fayette County judge to order Namdar Realty to fix blocked exits, vermin and crumbling roads at Uniontown Mall; she set a Sept. 30 site visit, the Herald-Standard reported.

South Union Township, Pennsylvania sued Uniontown Mall owners Namdar Realty Group and Uniontown Mall Realty in November 2025 over conditions it called dangerous and unlawful, including blocked, locked or obstructed emergency exits, vermin and deteriorating roads and parking areas. Fayette County Judge Linda Cordaro ordered Namdar on May 22, 2026 to make repairs, and in early July found "moderate compliance, although not complete compliance," the Herald-Standard reported. She scheduled a Sept. 30 site visit and an Oct. 15 hearing if work remained, and said she would order the mall closed if Namdar still had not finished.

Why it matters

The township used a lawsuit and a court order, not citations alone, to push repairs at the mall, and the judge has kept the case open to check the work. Her warning that she could order the mall closed raises the stakes if the emergency-exit repairs are not finished.

Full write-up

South Union Township sued Namdar Realty Group and Uniontown Mall Realty in November 2025 over conditions at the 689,585-square-foot Uniontown Mall, the Observer-Reporter reported. Township inspectors said they found multiple emergency exit doors that were locked, obstructed or so damaged they needed "excessive force to open," rodents in a storage area, and deteriorating driveways and parking areas with potholes. More than 100 citations had been issued against Namdar since August 2025, according to that report; Namdar did not respond to the paper's request for comment.

On May 22, 2026, Fayette County Judge Linda Cordaro ordered Namdar to obtain a permit and repair the emergency exits, with the work inspected by the township's building code office; to eliminate the vermin through a licensed exterminator; to repair the parking lot, roadways and potholes on Restaurant Row; and to address electrical, stormwater, HVAC, roof-leak and flooring problems, the Herald-Standard reported. Counsel for Namdar referred questions to the company, which did not respond by press time.

At a compliance hearing in early July, Cordaro said the owners appeared to have taken the order seriously, reaching "moderate compliance, although not complete compliance," the Herald-Standard reported on July 2, 2026. The township's attorney said the emergency exits remained the biggest outstanding issue: Namdar had a building permit but had not started those repairs, and stormwater and Restaurant Row road work were also incomplete, while HVAC had been restored in all but one section. Cordaro scheduled a Sept. 30 site visit with representatives of both sides and a second hearing on Oct. 15 if the work was not done, and said she would order the mall closed if Namdar still had not finished. A company spokesman did not respond to a request for comment.

Namdar bought the mall from PREIT in 2015 for $23 million, according to the Herald-Standard. We have not found a report on the outcome of the Sept. 30 visit or the Oct. 15 hearing.
