Kane Creek Development Watch v. Lieutenant Governor of Utah (Echo Canyon Preliminary Municipality Challenge)

Plaintiff: Friends of the Abajos d/b/a Kane Creek Development WatchPlaintiff: Living RiversDefendant: Utah Lieutenant Governor (official capacity)Defendant: Kane Creek Preservation & Development, LLCDefendant: Echo Canyon preliminary municipality

Kane Creek Development Watch and Living Rivers sued the Utah Lieutenant Governor and the developer behind Echo Canyon on February 10 in Utah's Seventh District Court, seeking to void the state's 2024 'preliminary municipality' law and the certification it conferred on Echo Canyon, a proposed 176-acre resort development near Moab. The suit alleges SB258 unconstitutionally hands zoning and land-use authority to a developer-controlled governing board without an election or public vote.

Why it matters

Utah's preliminary municipality statute is among the most aggressive state-law vehicles yet for letting a developer exercise quasi-governmental zoning authority over its own project, and a ruling on its constitutionality will inform how other states calibrate similar developer-self-governance proposals. Developers and lenders relying on first-of-their-kind statutory entitlement structures should treat certification as provisional, not settled, until the litigation resolves.

Original source ↗

A pair of Utah nonprofits, Kane Creek Development Watch and Living Rivers, filed suit on February 10, 2026 in Utah's Seventh District Court in Grand County against the Lieutenant Governor of Utah, who administers the certification process at issue, and against the developer behind Echo Canyon, a proposed 176-acre mixed-use resort development in the Kane Creek canyon corridor southwest of Moab along the Colorado River. The complaint asks the court to invalidate both Senate Bill 258, the 2024 statute that created Utah's 'preliminary municipality' designation, and the certification the law conferred on Echo Canyon as the state's first project to use it.
