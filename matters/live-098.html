Waukesha State Bank v. 225 East Michigan Street LLC ($6.57M Mackie Building Foreclosure)

Waukesha State Bank's foreclosure suit over Milwaukee's historic Mackie Building was dismissed by stipulation on Sept. 14 after the owner and lender settled; the developer says the loan was reinstated.

Waukesha State Bank sued 225 East Michigan Street LLC, an affiliate of J. Jeffers & Co., in Milwaukee County Circuit Court on August 3, 2026, alleging it defaulted on a $6.57 million loan on the historic Mackie Building and owed $6.2 million, including $5.9 million in principal. Developer Joshua Jeffers attributed the default to lower percentage rent from anchor tenant The Grain Exchange. The Milwaukee Journal Sentinel reported that Judge William Pocan dismissed the suit by stipulation on September 14 and that a companion money-judgment suit in Waukesha County was dismissed the next day; Jeffers said the loan had been reinstated.

Why it matters

The case shows how a percentage-rent lease can leave a landlord short of debt service when an anchor tenant's revenue stays below pre-pandemic levels. It also shows a foreclosure filing ending in a negotiated resolution: both suits were dropped by agreement within about six weeks.

Full write-up

Waukesha State Bank filed a foreclosure suit on August 3, 2026 in Milwaukee County Circuit Court against 225 East Michigan Street LLC, the owner of the Mackie Building at 225 E. Michigan St. and an affiliate of developer J. Jeffers & Co., according to Milwaukee Journal Sentinel reporting republished by The Daily Reporter. The bank said the LLC defaulted on a five-year, $6.57 million loan due in June 2027 and owed $6.2 million, including $5.9 million in principal plus interest and fees. The bank also sued Jeffers & Co. and its CEO, Joshua Jeffers, in Waukesha County Circuit Court seeking a money judgment.

Jeffers told the Journal Sentinel that the building's 25 apartments and smaller commercial tenants were performing well, but that The Grain Exchange, an event venue run by Bartolotta Restaurant Group, had not returned to its pre-pandemic level of business. The venue pays rent based on a percentage of its revenue, and Jeffers said the lower rent made it hard to cover the mortgage. Bartolotta's lease was winding down, with a renewal still under discussion. Jeffers' group bought the building in 2014 and completed a $14 million renovation in 2016 that turned three underused office floors into apartments.

Both suits were dismissed by stipulation: the foreclosure through a September 14 order from Milwaukee County Circuit Judge William Pocan, and the money-judgment case through a September 15 order from Waukesha County Circuit Judge Zachary Wittchow, the Journal Sentinel reported. "The matter is resolved," Jeffers said. "The loan has been reinstated." Waukesha State Bank did not immediately respond to the paper's request for comment, and Bartolotta said it was pleased the matter was resolved quickly.
