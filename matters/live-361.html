Lenders backed by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are foreclosing on apartment properties tied to Lakewood, New Jersey-based investor Chaim Bialostozky in Illinois, Connecticut and Oklahoma, with nearly $44 million of mortgage debt in court, The Real Deal reported on September 22, 2026. In the Connecticut, Chicago and Tulsa cases, the lenders allege he used back-to-back purchases through an intermediary to obtain loans larger than his real costs. Fannie Mae's newest suit, a $6.2 million Cook County foreclosure against Yellow Portfolio Holdings LLC, does not allege fraud. Court records showed no appearance or response by Bialostozky in any of the foreclosure cases, according to the publication.

Why it matters

The lenders are using orders from one state to seek fast receiverships in another, treating one borrower's defaults as a single pattern rather than as separate workouts. For multifamily owners with agency debt, a default or an unanswered complaint on one property can quickly bring scrutiny and receivership requests across every property under common control.

Full write-up

Lenders backed by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac have taken apartment properties tied to Chaim Bialostozky, a real estate investor based in Lakewood, New Jersey, to foreclosure court in Illinois, Connecticut and Oklahoma. The Real Deal reported on September 22, 2026 that nearly $44 million of mortgage debt on property he owns or recently owned is in foreclosure proceedings, most of it secured by Chicago apartment buildings. According to the publication, at least $25.5 million of that debt is Bialostozky's own exposure; the other roughly $18 million relates to a South Side Chicago portfolio he sold to an LLC managed by Israel Bialostozky.

The fraud claims follow the same pattern in each state, the report said. The lenders allege that Bialostozky bought properties through back-to-back sales with an intermediary, hid the first, lower-priced purchase made days or even hours earlier, and gave lenders paperwork showing only the higher second price. They say this led to mortgages large enough to cover his full acquisition cost, so he could buy with no down payment and take cash out of the deals. Walker & Dunlop, acting for Freddie Mac, has made those allegations over portfolios on Chicago's Far West Side and in two suits it filed on July 1 in Waterbury, Connecticut, against Courtyard Apt Holdings LLC and Tower View Apt LLC. In emergency receivership motions filed in Connecticut in late August, Walker & Dunlop attached receivership orders from Chicago and Oklahoma and argued that Bialostozky was carrying out a "common plan and unlawful fraudulent scheme" that justified appointing a receiver without a hearing.

Fannie Mae has sued separately. Its newest case, filed in Cook County Circuit Court the week before the report, seeks to foreclose a $6.2 million loan to Yellow Portfolio Holdings LLC secured by 90 apartments at 8200 South Maryland, 7845 South Avalon and 7901 South Drexel avenues. That complaint does not allege fraud. It says the borrower stopped making loan payments in April and let the buildings build up serious code violations and unpaid municipal utility judgments. The Real Deal noted that the ownership history of those buildings resembles the deals Walker & Dunlop has challenged: Bialostozky bought them weeks after the seller had acquired them in June 2021.

Bialostozky did not return The Real Deal's requests for comment, and Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and Walker & Dunlop also did not respond. The publication reported that he and his entities had not appeared or answered in the Connecticut cases since being served in early July, and that court records showed no appearance or response from him in any of the foreclosure proceedings. The fraud claims are the lenders' allegations.
