Metropolitan Milwaukee Association of Commerce v. City of Port Washington: Judge Dismisses Business and CRE Groups' Challenge to Voter-Approval Rule for Large TIF Districts

An Ozaukee County judge dismissed business and commercial real estate groups' challenge to Port Washington's voter-approved rule requiring a referendum for tax incremental districts over $10 million.

On October 19, 2026, Ozaukee County Circuit Judge Adam Gerol dismissed a suit by the Metropolitan Milwaukee Association of Commerce and other business, contractor and commercial real estate groups against the City of Port Washington, the Wisconsin Law Journal reported. The suit challenged a voter-approved ordinance requiring a referendum before the city creates a new tax incremental district above $10 million in project cost or base value. In an oral ruling, the judge found the groups had not shown actual or sufficiently imminent injury, and he also concluded the ordinance is consistent with state law and not too vague to apply.

Why it matters

The ordinance arose from opposition to the $458 million in local incentives for Vantage Data Centers' $15 billion campus and was described as the first U.S. ballot initiative aimed at restricting future data center development. While it stands, large TIF packages in Port Washington need voter approval as well as council action. MMAC has said further review in higher courts is warranted.

Full write-up

Port Washington's ordinance was proposed in fall 2025 by opponents of the $15 billion AI data center campus being developed there by Vantage Data Centers, Oracle and OpenAI, as the project's $458 million in local tax incentives moved through approval, according to the Wisconsin Law Journal. The citizen group Great Lakes Neighbors United gathered the signatures needed to put the measure on the ballot, and voters approved it on April 7, 2026 with about 66 percent support, WUWM reported. It requires a referendum for future tax incremental districts with project costs or base value above $10 million and does not affect the TID already approved for the Vantage project.

Before the vote, the Metropolitan Milwaukee Association of Commerce, Associated General Contractors of Greater Milwaukee, Building Advantage, the Commercial Association of Realtors Wisconsin, the Commercial Real Estate Development Association of Wisconsin, the Wisconsin Realtors Association and Sid's Sealants LLC sued the city in Ozaukee County Circuit Court on January 29, 2026. They argued the ordinance would slow development, conflict with state laws on how TIF incentives are approved, and was too vague. City officials said they largely agreed with the groups but had to advance the ordinance under Wisconsin's direct legislation law, which dates to 1910. Great Lakes Neighbors United intervened to defend it, and in February Judge Gerol denied a preliminary injunction and let the vote proceed, calling it "protected speech."

In an oral ruling on October 19 that lasted over an hour, Gerol found that the groups had not identified a specific development, financing plan or proposed TID that the referendum requirement would harm, so their claims rested on speculation. He also addressed the merits, concluding that a referendum supplements rather than conflicts with the state's TID procedure and that the ordinance, though awkwardly drafted, is understandable. He relied in part on the Wisconsin Supreme Court's 2003 decision in Mount Horeb Community Alert v. Village Board of Mount Horeb.

In a statement to the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, an MMAC spokesperson said the group was disappointed and confident that "further examination in higher courts is warranted." Great Lakes Neighbors United called the ruling "a win for every voter in Port Washington." On September 16, the same judge dismissed the group's separate suit challenging the Vantage campus's tax incentives.
