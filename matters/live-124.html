California Department of Toxic Substances Control Settlement Over Former Halford's Cleaners Site (Modesto Groundwater Contamination Superfund Site)

A federal judge approved DTSC's $2 million consent decree with the owners of the Modesto property that housed Halford's Cleaners, resolving the state's cleanup-cost claims over PCE contamination.

On July 7, 2026, Senior U.S. District Judge John A. Mendez approved a consent decree under which the owners of the property at 941 McHenry Avenue in Modesto, where Halford's Cleaners operated, will pay the California Department of Toxic Substances Control and the Toxic Substances Control Account $2 million toward past and future cleanup costs at the Modesto Groundwater Contamination Superfund site. DTSC's complaint, filed February 26, 2026 together with the proposed decree, sought cost recovery under CERCLA from the owners, who had leased the property to dry-cleaning operators. The owners did not admit liability. DTSC announced the settlement on July 15, 2026.

Why it matters

The case shows California's toxic-substances agency recovering cleanup costs under CERCLA from the landlords of a long-running dry-cleaning tenant as owners of the facility, years after the same owners settled with the EPA. For owners and buyers of property leased to dry cleaners, the site's history shows how solvent contamination can produce government claims over decades.

Full write-up

According to DTSC, Halford's Cleaners operated a dry-cleaning business at 941 McHenry Avenue in Modesto from about 1948 to 2025 and allegedly discharged significant volumes of hazardous substances, mainly tetrachloroethylene (PCE), into the soil, contaminating groundwater and nearby sewer lines. The site, now called the Modesto Groundwater Contamination site, was placed on the U.S. EPA's National Priorities List in March 1989 after a city water supply well was contaminated with PCE from the site. DTSC said it took over operating the groundwater extraction and treatment system in 2012 and the monitoring well network in 2024.

On February 26, 2026, DTSC and the Toxic Substances Control Account sued the property's owners in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of California, seeking recovery of response costs under CERCLA section 107(a) and declaratory relief. The complaint alleges the owners bought the property in 1974 and leased it to dry-cleaning operators from 1974 to 2025. It also states that the EPA sued the owners and a former operator in 2007, and that the owners resolved the EPA's claims in a 2011 consent decree to which DTSC was not a party.

Under the consent decree, signed by Judge Mendez on July 7, 2026, the owners will pay a total of $2 million in installments. The decree states that the settling defendants do not admit liability. The court's order made the decree its final judgment and closed the case. DTSC announced the settlement on July 15, 2026.
