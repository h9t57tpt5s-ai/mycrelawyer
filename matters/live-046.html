A Texas judge's Aug. 17 TRO barred Westlake from voting on an agreement for the Circle T Data Center after a neighboring HOA alleged defective notice; the order lapsed in September.

Judge Kimberly Fitzpatrick of Tarrant County's 342nd District Court signed a temporary restraining order on Aug. 17, 2026 barring the Town of Westlake, Texas from voting on or implementing Resolution 26-25, a proposed development agreement for the 1.1-million-square-foot Circle T Data Center, in a suit by the Lakes at Marshall Ridge Homeowners Association and two residents alleging the town failed to give legally required notice of earlier zoning action, The Dallas Express reported. The judge struck broader proposed restrictions on permits and site work. The order expired Sept. 25 without extension, Fox 4 reported, and the City of Keller, which had sought its own injunction, signed a settlement with developer entity Circle T Owner LP on Nov. 5.

Why it matters

The case shows how a neighbor's notice challenge can delay a town vote on a large data center agreement even where the town says the use is already allowed by existing zoning. Here the temporary order was narrow and short-lived, and the neighboring city's dispute ended in a negotiated settlement over noise, screening and emergency-response funding.

Full write-up

On Monday, Aug. 17, 2026, Judge Kimberly Fitzpatrick of the 342nd District Court in Tarrant County signed a temporary restraining order barring the Town of Westlake from "holding a vote on, approving, adopting, or taking any action to implement Resolution 26-25 concerning the Circle T Data Center," including at a special meeting set for that day, The Dallas Express reported. The order took effect once the plaintiffs, the Lakes at Marshall Ridge Homeowners Association Inc. and two residents, posted a $5,000 bond. They allege Westlake failed to provide legally required public notice in connection with earlier zoning action affecting the project, and the order notes their contention that approving the resolution would authorize immediate grading, site work and construction. The judge struck four broader proposed provisions that would have barred approval of a development agreement, permits and site work, and the order did not resolve the merits.

The project, by Virginia-based developer PowerHouse, consists of four buildings totaling about 1.1 million square feet on an 87.9-acre site along Denton Highway, Newsweek reported. Westlake has said the property has carried commercial and industrial zoning since 1998, that a data center is an allowed use, and that the project would expand its tax base while meeting local noise, water and landscaping rules, according to The Dallas Express. Newsweek, citing CBS News, reported that the plaintiffs also contend the site plan omits a setback and landscaped buffer the town's zoning ordinance requires between the data center and single-family homes. The judge heard arguments on Aug. 26 and left the order in place pending a decision on extending it.

The restraining order expired on Sept. 25 after the judge declined to extend it, saying she needed more time to review documents delivered that morning, and set a further hearing for Oct. 23, Fox 4 reported. The neighboring City of Keller, which had sought its own temporary injunction, signed a $1.876 million settlement with Circle T Owner LP on Nov. 5, The Dallas Express reported. It provides $1.5 million for a noise mitigation fund, $300,000 for fire protection and $76,000 toward Keller's legal and consultant costs, along with nighttime and daytime noise limits, quarterly noise monitoring, a landscaped berm and emissions controls on backup generators; the payments depend on a separate Keller-Westlake settlement and dismissal of Keller's claims against the town. The status of the homeowners association's own claims was not reported.
