The complaint alleges violations of Section 10(b) of the Exchange Act and SEC Rule 10b-5, plus control-person claims under Section 20(a), and says Arbor's 2026 statements about resolving its nonperforming loans and foreclosed properties were misleading as those assets grew to about $1.25 billion. Why it matters

The suit is built on specific, dated projections a mortgage REIT gave investors about working out problem loans, and on later results that diverged from them. It will test whether that gap supports a securities-fraud claim; the allegations have not been tested in court.

Full write-up

A shareholder sued Arbor Realty Trust, Inc. (NYSE: ABR) and its chief executive and chief financial officers on Nov. 9, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of New York, InvestmentNews reported. The complaint asserts claims under Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act and SEC Rule 10b-5, and control-person claims under Section 20(a) against the two executives.

According to InvestmentNews, the complaint points to a March 11, 2026 investor presentation in which Arbor said it "has clear visibility to resolution on non-performing assets," projected taking back $50 million to $125 million of nonperforming loans as real-estate-owned (REO) property from the second through fourth quarters, and targeted about $300 million of REO by year-end. The complaint also cites first-quarter earnings remarks, a May 8 quarterly report, a July 31 earnings release, and an Aug. 3 presentation describing a "defined path" to resolve nearly all existing delinquencies. It alleges the company lacked a reasonable basis for its projections while loans it had described as "ring-fenced" kept producing new delinquencies.

The complaint relies on Arbor's third-quarter 2026 figures: nonperforming loans rose to 32 with an unpaid principal balance of $647.6 million, from 19 loans and $428.8 million at June 30, and management reported about $750 million in delinquencies and about $500 million in REO, roughly $1.25 billion in total and about $200 million more than the prior quarter. InvestmentNews reported no response from the company and noted that the allegations have not been tested and no court has ruled on them.
