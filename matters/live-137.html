Former New York developer Josh Schuster was sentenced on August 11, 2026 to four years in federal prison for securities fraud in what The Real Deal called a $13 million Ponzi scheme; the court also entered a $13.8 million forfeiture money judgment.

U.S. District Judge Valerie E. Caproni of the Southern District of New York sentenced developer Joshua Schuster on August 11, 2026 to four years in prison and three years of supervised release. Schuster pleaded guilty on February 27, 2026 to one count of securities fraud. The judgment requires forfeiture of $13,830,665, and the court entered a separate restitution order on October 7, 2026. His surrender date was later moved to November 17, 2026.

Why it matters

Sponsors who raise money from investors for development deals face criminal exposure, not just civil claims, when they misuse it. Investors should note that forfeiture and restitution are separate; here the court set the forfeiture amount at sentencing and decided restitution about two months later.

Full write-up

Joshua Schuster, a former New York real estate developer, was sentenced on August 11, 2026 to four years in federal prison and three years of supervised release, The Real Deal reported. U.S. District Judge Valerie E. Caproni of the Southern District of New York imposed the sentence after Schuster pleaded guilty to one count of securities fraud, which carries a maximum of 20 years. The Real Deal described the scheme as a $13 million Ponzi scheme. The court's judgment orders Schuster to forfeit $13,830,665 under a consent preliminary order of forfeiture and money judgment signed the same day.

The court docket shows a two-count indictment filed on May 6, 2025 and unsealed the next day, when Schuster was arrested in the Southern District of Florida. He first pleaded not guilty, then on February 27, 2026 pleaded guilty to Count Two; the remaining count was dismissed at sentencing on the government's motion. A later defense filing describes the offense conduct as tied to his former work in real estate.

At the sentencing hearing, Schuster told the judge that drugs and alcohol had sent his life out of control, that he had been sober for four years and that he was no longer the man who lied to and stole from his investors, according to The Real Deal. Judge Caproni questioned whether the change was real, noting that after moving to Boca Raton, Florida, he rented an expensive house and bought a Ford Bronco while his wife drives a Mercedes-Benz. 'I don't think you've given up the desire to look like you're successful,' she said. She denied his request for home confinement, saying the sentence should deter white-collar crime; the federal guidelines range was 4.25 to 5.25 years.

The judgment deferred the restitution decision, and the court entered an order of restitution on October 7, 2026, followed by an amended judgment on October 13. Schuster had been due to surrender to the Bureau of Prisons on October 20, 2026; on October 5 the court moved the date to November 17, 2026 and said it would not be extended again.
