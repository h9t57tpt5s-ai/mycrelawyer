A Botetourt County, Virginia resident filed suit in Botetourt County Circuit Court on November 2, 2026, asking the court to invalidate the county's June 2025 economic development performance agreement with Helio Capital LLC, the company behind Google's data center project at the Botetourt Center at Greenfield, Cardinal News reported. The suit alleges the Board of Supervisors discussed the agreement in a closed meeting on June 24, 2025 that its published agenda did not disclose, then approved it 5-0 without discussion after adding it as a walk-on item, in violation of Virginia's Freedom of Information Act. The county said it would not comment on pending litigation.

Why it matters

The case tests whether an open-meetings defect in how a locality approved an incentive agreement can unwind the approvals that followed, including expedited permits, for a data center campus already under construction. Developers negotiating performance agreements with local governments have reason to watch how the court treats the closed-session record.

Full write-up

A Botetourt County resident has asked the Botetourt County Circuit Court to invalidate the county's economic development performance agreement for Google's data center campus in Troutville, Cardinal News reported. The suit, filed November 2, 2026, names the Board of Supervisors, the county, its economic development authority and Helio Capital LLC, the limited liability company behind the project, as respondents. All were parties to the June 2025 agreement, which set in motion Google's $14 million purchase of 312 acres at the Botetourt Center at Greenfield.

According to the suit, the board's published agenda for June 24, 2025 did not disclose that it would discuss the agreement in a closed meeting. After returning to open session, the board voted 5-0, without discussion, to approve the agreement and a resolution adopting it, both added to the agenda during the meeting as walk-on items. The suit alleges the board never did "reasonably identify the substance" of what it was approving and calls the process "subterfuge."

The resident asks the court to "rescind all actions" taken under the agreement, including the issuance of expedited permits and Google's eligibility for the county's third-party inspection program. Through a spokesman, the county said it would not comment on the specific allegations because the matter involves pending litigation. County Administrator Gary Larrowe has said Google will pay the same property tax rate as any other landowner, and the published agreement commits Google to invest at least $1 billion in each data center and $4 million over five years in community projects. The resident's attorney said the law calls for a hearing within seven working days.
