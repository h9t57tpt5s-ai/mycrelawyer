Trustees Main/270, LLC v. ApplianceSmart, Inc.: Sixth Circuit Holds Shopping-Center Landlord Could Reject Replacement Tenants Tied to Its Defaulting Tenant

The Sixth Circuit affirmed a Columbus shopping-center landlord's judgment against big-box tenant ApplianceSmart and guarantor JanOne, holding it could reject replacement tenants tied to the defaulting tenant.

A Sixth Circuit panel affirmed a judgment for Trustees Main/270, LLC, owner of a Columbus, Ohio shopping center, against its former big-box tenant ApplianceSmart, Inc. and the lease guarantor JanOne, Inc. The defendants argued the landlord had failed to mitigate its damages by turning down two replacement tenants that the guarantor and an affiliated company had proposed. The court held that Ohio law does not make a commercial landlord deal with replacement tenants that carry an undue risk of another default, and that the landlord's multi-year search, which ended in an AutoZone lease, was a reasonable effort to mitigate.

Why it matters

The panel applied the Ohio Supreme Court's Frenchtown Square decision and Restatement section 350 to hold that a landlord's duty to mitigate does not require it to accept replacement tenants that carry an undue risk of another default, including affiliates of the defaulting tenant and its guarantor. It also held that conditions such as prepaying arrears and a nonrefundable deposit were a reasonable response to that risk.

Full write-up

ApplianceSmart, Inc. ran a store in the big-box space of a Columbus, Ohio shopping center owned by Trustees Main/270, LLC from 2008 until 2022. The lease began with a predecessor, Appliance Recycling Centers of America, which became the guarantor in 2017 when ApplianceSmart took over as tenant and the term was extended to June 30, 2025. That guarantor was reincorporated as JanOne, Inc. and remained guarantor after JanOne sold ApplianceSmart to Live Ventures, Inc. in 2019.

According to the Sixth Circuit's opinion, the tenant defaulted three times. A 2019 rent default ended in a settlement of a state-court suit. A second default during ApplianceSmart's 2021 bankruptcy was resolved with a lease modification under which Live Ventures agreed to pay more than $185,000 in back rent and the landlord gained a right to terminate on 30 days' notice. After a third payment default in February 2022, the landlord used a lease clause letting it retake possession and change the locks without ending the lease, leaving the tenant liable for rent and reletting costs, and sued ApplianceSmart and JanOne in the Southern District of Ohio in April 2022.

The defendants did not dispute the breach; their defense was mitigation. JanOne proposed an affiliated recycling business that would assume the lease and keep trading as ApplianceSmart, and Live Ventures offered one of its portfolio companies, a flooring retailer. The landlord asked the first candidate to prepay all arrears and post a nonrefundable six-month deposit, which JanOne declined, and sent the second a standard questionnaire that went unanswered. The landlord's leasing agent kept marketing the space, and AutoZone signed a lease in September 2024; because of zoning and permitting contingencies, it did not begin paying rent until after the ApplianceSmart term would have ended.

District Judge Algenon L. Marbley awarded $155,267.15 in undisputed rent on summary judgment and, after a bench trial on mitigation, entered final judgment on October 3, 2025 awarding $1,064,674.64 more in rent and charges and $126,637.52 in broker fees, plus interest. In an October 7, 2026 opinion by Judge John B. Nalbandian, not recommended for publication, the Sixth Circuit affirmed, holding that Ohio law does not require a landlord to mitigate by dealing with "demonstrably unreliable parties." It rejected the defendants' analogy to employment-discrimination cases and found their argument about a rejected purchase offer likely forfeited. The panel first raised the complaint's failure to plead the parties' citizenship and accepted post-argument letter briefs as amendments confirming diversity jurisdiction.
