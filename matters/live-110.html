Thompson Thrift Construction, Inc. v. Modus Studio, PLLC (Fayetteville Student Housing Defect Suit)

After a $13,529,726 award against contractor Thompson Thrift over a Fayetteville student housing project, an Arkansas appeals court revived its claims against architect Modus Studio despite the statute of repose.

After owner West Center Partners, LLC won a $13,529,726 arbitration award against general contractor Thompson Thrift Construction over defects in a Fayetteville student housing project, Thompson Thrift sought to recover from the project's architect, Modus Studio, PLLC, and several subcontractors. A Washington County circuit court dismissed Thompson Thrift's refiled claims against Modus as barred by Arkansas's five-year construction statute of repose. On April 2, 2025, the Arkansas Court of Appeals reversed, holding in a case of first impression that the one-year savings statute lets a party that timely filed such claims refile them within a year of a nonsuit even after the repose period has run.

Why it matters

The decision establishes in Arkansas that the construction statute of repose does not cut off the savings statute for a claim first filed within the five-year window, dismissed without prejudice and refiled within a year. That gives a general contractor held liable for defects more room to pursue claims against the designers and subcontractors who did the work, and makes repose deadlines less absolute once a qualifying dismissal and timely refiling are involved.

Full write-up

West Center Partners, LLC sued Thompson Thrift Construction, Inc. in Washington County Circuit Court in July 2019, alleging Thompson Thrift was responsible as prime contractor for construction defects in a Fayetteville student housing project West Center owned, according to the Arkansas Court of Appeals' opinion. The parties agree the project was substantially complete on August 14, 2014. On August 14, 2019, Thompson Thrift and its surety filed a third-party complaint against eight third-party defendants, including the project's architect, Modus Studio, PLLC, asserting negligence, contribution, breach of contract, breach of warranty and indemnity to the extent Thompson Thrift was liable to West Center.

West Center then nonsuited to pursue arbitration, and on December 2, 2020 the circuit court dismissed Thompson Thrift's third-party complaint without prejudice as moot. In May 2022, after Thompson Thrift had subcontractors joined in the arbitration, the circuit court entered a declaratory judgment that West Center and Thompson Thrift could not arbitrate with four of them. West Center went on to win a $13,529,726 arbitration award against Thompson Thrift alone, which Thompson Thrift paid.

Thompson Thrift refiled its claims 364 days after the 2020 dismissal, within the one-year window of Arkansas's savings statute, Ark. Code Ann. § 16-56-126, but more than seven years after substantial completion, outside the five-year statute of repose in § 16-56-112. The circuit court ruled the savings statute does not extend the repose periods. Only Modus was dismissed on that basis, because Thompson Thrift's contractual-indemnity claims against the other defendants are not subject to § 112, and the court certified the Modus ruling for immediate appeal under Rule 54(b) to avoid trying the case twice.

In an opinion by Judge Brandon J. Harrison, joined by Judges Tucker and Thyer, the Court of Appeals reversed. The court held that § 112 sets a time to commence an action, that Thompson Thrift commenced its action in time, and that the savings statute therefore let it "commence a new action" within a year of the nonsuit. If the legislature had meant to exclude such claims from the savings statute, the court said, it would have said so more clearly.
