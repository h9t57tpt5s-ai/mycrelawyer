Schell Road, LLC v. Vanderford: Michigan Appeals Court Rejects Gravel Mine Mandamus Bid and Upholds Township's Amended Mining Ordinance

A Michigan appeals court held Schell Road's gravel mine application was incomplete, so no hearing could be compelled, and found Grant Township did not amend its mining ordinance in bad faith.

On October 13, 2026, the Michigan Court of Appeals affirmed a Grand Traverse Circuit Court ruling against Schell Road, LLC in its suit over a special land use application to operate a gravel mine on 80 acres it bought in Grant Township in 2022. The unpublished per curiam opinion held that the application was incomplete under the township's pre-amendment zoning ordinance, so the company had no clear legal right to the public hearing it sought through mandamus. The panel also held that the township did not amend its mining ordinance in bad faith while the case was pending and that the amended ordinance is consistent with the state statute on mining, MCL 125.3205.

Why it matters

The decision shows that a Michigan court can decide on summary disposition whether a special land use application was complete, and that an incomplete application defeats a mandamus claim to force a hearing. It also shows that a township may amend its zoning ordinance while an applicant's suit is pending when the record supports a general reason for the change, here updating mining rules unchanged since 1998 after a 2011 change in state law.

Full write-up

Schell Road, LLC bought 80 acres on Schell Road in Grant Township in 2022, planning to develop and operate a gravel mine. It submitted a special use permit application on September 20, 2022, with site plans from a civil engineer; it was the first mining application the township had received since 2003. Township officials, advised by counsel, questioned whether the ordinance's mining rules, unchanged since 1998, accounted for a 2011 amendment to state law. The company submitted supplemental information in December 2022 and January 2023 to address deficiencies, and officials told it they intended to delay consideration until the ordinance was amended.

After an exchange of letters in which the company's counsel accused the township of delaying review to defeat the application, Schell Road filed a complaint on February 17, 2023, seeking a writ of mandamus to compel a public hearing. Following a planning commission recommendation, the township adopted amendments to the ordinance on March 23, 2023, effective March 30. Schell Road amended its complaint to allege the amendments were enacted in bad faith to stop its mine and exceeded the township's authority under MCL 125.3205, and added due process, injunctive and declaratory claims. The trial court granted the township summary disposition, finding the application incomplete, finding no bad faith, and holding the amended ordinance complied with the statute.

A panel of Judges Korobkin, Rick and Maldonado affirmed on all three issues. On mandamus, it held the trial court could decide completeness on the documentary record at summary disposition, and agreed the application was incomplete: among other gaps, the reclamation plan lacked required information on phasing and timing, and the record did not show the plan had been submitted to the county extension office and soil and water conservation district as the ordinance required. On bad faith, it agreed the record did not support the claim: the township had not received a mining application since 2003, acted on counsel's advice, told the company in December 2022 that amendments were under way, and adopted amendments that apply to any mining application. The court said the application was "a catalyst" for the amendments but that this did not establish bad faith.

On validity, the court rejected the company's arguments that MCL 125.3205 sets only a standard for courts on appeal and that the amended ordinance's requirements on market need and on property-value effects along the haul route go beyond the statute, finding those requirements track the statute's need and "very serious consequences" factors. The opinion is unpublished.
