Schell Road, LLC v. Vanderford: Michigan Appeals Court Lets Township Amend Mining Ordinance Mid-Litigation to Defeat Gravel Mine Permit Bid

The Michigan Court of Appeals affirmed a Grand Traverse Circuit Court ruling rejecting Schell Road, LLC's mandamus action against Grant Township over its special land use application to operate a gravel mine on 80 acres it purchased in 2022. The panel held that Schell Road's application was incomplete under the township's pre-amendment zoning ordinance, so the company never had the clear legal right to a public hearing that mandamus requires, and let stand the trial court's finding that the township did not act in bad faith when it amended its mining ordinance for the first time since 1998 while the case was pending.

Why it matters

The ruling shows commercial developers how much latitude a township retains to revise its own zoning ordinance in the middle of a pending entitlement dispute, and how high the bar is for a mandamus claim when the underlying application's completeness is contested. Developers pursuing extractive or other special land uses should treat application completeness, not just filing priority, as the decisive fact a court will resolve against them on summary disposition if the record is thin.

The Michigan Court of Appeals on October 13, 2026 affirmed a trial court ruling that handed Grant Township, a local government in Grand Traverse County, a decisive win over a commercial landowner that had sought to force a public hearing on its special land use application. The unpublished, per curiam decision in Schell Road, LLC v. Vanderford rejected developer Schell Road, LLC's bid for a writ of mandamus against the township's zoning administrator and planning commission, and upheld the township's decision to amend its mining ordinance while the litigation was pending.
