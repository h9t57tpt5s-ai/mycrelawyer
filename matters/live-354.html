D.C. Attorney General Brian Schwalb announced on September 14, 2026 that JBG Associates, L.L.C. (JBG Smith) and Mid-America Apartments, L.P. (MAA) will pay a combined $9.3 million under separate consent judgments resolving the District's antitrust claims against them in District of Columbia v. RealPage, Inc., No. 2023-CAB-006762 in D.C. Superior Court. The District alleged that JBG Smith used RealPage's revenue-management software to set rents at multiple District properties and MAA at one. JBG Smith will pay $8.1 million and MAA $1.2 million; both accepted limits on such software, and neither admitted liability.

Why it matters

The consent judgments show the District resolving claims against a landlord whose alleged software use was limited to one property, with the payment differing by the scale of alleged use. The orders pair the payments with multi-year conduct limits, 10 years for JBG and eight for MAA, and a monitor the District can appoint if it suspects noncompliance.

Full write-up

The District of Columbia's Office of the Attorney General announced on September 14, 2026 that JBG Associates, L.L.C. (JBG Smith) and Mid-America Apartments, L.P. (MAA) had agreed to separate consent judgments resolving the District's antitrust claims against them. JBG Smith will pay $8.1 million and MAA $1.2 million, a combined $9.3 million that the OAG said covers civil penalties, money for affected residents and legal fees. The case, District of Columbia v. RealPage, Inc., No. 2023-CAB-006762, is before Judge Shana Frost Matini in D.C. Superior Court.

The District alleges that RealPage's revenue-management software uses non-public pricing data from owners and managers to recommend rents, and that JBG Smith, MAA and 12 other defendant landlords shared sensitive data and delegated rent-setting to RealPage. According to the OAG, JBG Smith used the software for multiple District properties and MAA for one; the OAG said JBG Smith owns more than 4,500 units in the District and MAA owns 269.

Under the consent orders, the companies may not use revenue-management software that relies on other companies' non-public data, promote such software to other owners, or share non-public information with other landlords; the restrictions run for 10 years for JBG and eight years for MAA. If the District believes either company is not complying, it may appoint an independent monitor at the company's expense. JBG's order states that it is not an admission of liability, and MAA's order states that MAA denies liability. The OAG had earlier announced settlements in the same case with W.C. Smith and, in June 2026, with Avenue5 Residential and Bell Partners for $1.4 million combined.

In their joint quarterly report for the period ended September 30, 2026, Mid-America Apartment Communities, Inc. and Mid-America Apartments, L.P. disclosed the consent judgment, said the payment was reflected in their accrual for loss contingencies, and said they do not believe the commitments will require material changes to current operations. They also reported that they continue to defend a separate suit filed in July 2025 by Kentucky's Attorney General against RealPage and several of that state's largest landlords.
