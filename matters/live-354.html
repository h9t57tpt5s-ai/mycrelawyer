JBG Smith agreed to pay $8.1 million and Mid-America Apartment Communities $1.2 million to settle the D.C. Attorney General's claims that both landlords used RealPage's software to coordinate apartment rents.

The District of Columbia's Attorney General announced on September 14, 2026 that JBG Smith (through JBG Associates, L.L.C.) and Mid-America Apartment Communities, Inc. (MAA) agreed to consent judgments resolving the District's claims that both landlords used RealPage's revenue-management software to coordinate apartment rents, part of the District's broader suit against RealPage and a group of its largest landlords, No. 2023-CAB-006762 in D.C. Superior Court. JBG Smith will pay $8.1 million, tied to its use of the software across multiple District properties, and MAA will pay $1.2 million, tied to a single District property, for a combined $9.3 million; neither company admitted wrongdoing, and both accepted multi-year prospective restrictions on using shared-data pricing software.

Why it matters

The settlements add two more names to a rapidly growing list of RealPage-related resolutions across the DOJ's case, the Tennessee multidistrict class litigation, and a string of state attorneys general suits, confirming that enforcement authorities will pursue antitrust liability even against a landlord whose software use was limited to a single property. REIT landlords and managers still facing RealPage exposure should expect a settlement path combining a payment scaled to the extent of their software use with years-long forward commitments on data-sharing and pricing-software practices.
