The State of Texas v. JRJ Pusok Holdings, LLC

The Texas Supreme Court held the State cannot invoke sovereign immunity to block JRJ Pusok Holdings' statutory right to repurchase surplus Tomball land TxDOT condemned for a highway project.

The Supreme Court of Texas held on June 12, 2026 that the State cannot invoke sovereign immunity to defeat a former landowner's claim under Chapter 21 of the Texas Property Code to repurchase condemned land no longer needed for public use, Texas Scorecard reported. The case involves a Mueschke Road property in Tomball that TxDOT acquired for a road project along the Grand Parkway after filing a condemnation suit in 2014; the owners agreed to sell before judgment, and TxDOT later said about 20,000 square feet was surplus after the road was rerouted. Justice Jane Bland wrote for a six-justice majority affirming the Fourteenth Court of Appeals and remanding to the trial court; Justice Young concurred, and Justice Hawkins dissented, joined by Justice Sullivan and, except as to one part, Chief Justice Blacklock.

Why it matters

The ruling limits a condemning authority's ability to keep land it acquired under a condemnation suit once the project no longer needs it: the repurchase right applies even when the suit settled before judgment, and it can reach only the surplus portion of a tract. Owners of land along road and utility corridors should track whether condemned parcels later become unnecessary for the stated use.

Full write-up

In 2013, the Texas Department of Transportation offered to buy the owners' property at 19502 Mueschke Road in Tomball for a new road along the Grand Parkway (State Highway 99), Texas Scorecard reported. After the owners pushed back, the State filed an eminent domain suit in 2014. That suit was dismissed when the owners agreed to sell at $1.05 per square foot. Years later, TxDOT said in an email that about 20,000 square feet of the property was surplus because Mueschke Road had been rerouted, but it denied a request to sell that land back.

JRJ Pusok Holdings, LLC, standing in for the former owners according to Texas Scorecard, sued the State and the director of TxDOT's Right of Way Division in Harris County Civil Court at Law No. 4 to enforce the repurchase right in Chapter 21 of the Texas Property Code. The State argued the repurchase statutes did not apply because it acquired the land by settlement rather than a final condemnation judgment, that any repurchase would have to cover the whole parcel, and that sovereign immunity barred the suit. The trial court granted the State's plea and dismissed the case. On December 28, 2023, the Fourteenth Court of Appeals in Houston reversed the dismissal, finding a valid waiver of immunity and holding that the State acquired the property through eminent domain because it compelled the transfer by filing a condemnation action.

The Supreme Court of Texas affirmed and remanded on June 12, 2026, holding, as quoted by Texas Scorecard, that the State has "no immunity from Chapter 21 claims to repurchase condemned property no longer necessary for public use." The opinion said repurchase claims derive from constitutional limits on the State's eminent domain power and that Chapter 21 permits repurchase of a portion of condemned property. Justice Bland wrote for the Court, joined by Justices Lehrmann, Devine, Busby, Huddle and Young; Justice Young also filed a concurrence, and Justice Hawkins dissented, joined by Justice Sullivan and, except as to Part III(A), Chief Justice Blacklock. The case returns to the trial court for proceedings on the merits.

For owners whose land lies along a planned or existing road or utility corridor, the decision means a later reroute or smaller footprint can give the former owner an enforceable right to buy back the surplus land, even where the original acquisition settled before judgment.
