NFI Apartment Management, L.L.C. v. Third Coast Insurance Company (Fifth Circuit Affirms Time-Barred Hail Coverage Claim)

The Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Third Coast Insurance against a San Antonio apartment owner's hail claim, holding the owner's 2024 suit came after the limitations period ran.

On October 20, 2026, the Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Third Coast Insurance Company against NFI Apartment Management, L.L.C. over a May 2020 hailstorm claim on a San Antonio apartment complex. Third Coast's July 21, 2020 letter said it could not pay because the covered loss, estimated at $37,058.29, did not exceed the policy's $359,856.60 deductible, and it closed the claim on July 28, 2021, after a reinspection estimate of $61,839.72. The court held NFI's claims accrued no later than the file closing, so its July 2024 suit was untimely under the policy's two-years-and-one-day limit and the two-year statutory periods.

Why it matters

The decision holds that under Texas law an insurer's below-deductible determination is a denial of coverage that starts the limitations period, even though the insurer acknowledges some covered damage. It also holds that later reinspections and correspondence did not restart the clock or bar the insurer's limitations defense where the insurer stood by its decision and reserved its rights.

Full write-up

NFI Apartment Management's policy with Third Coast Insurance Company, issued March 15, 2020, covered several NFI properties, including an apartment complex in San Antonio, and barred suits not brought within two years and one day after the cause of action accrued. After a May 2020 hailstorm, NFI submitted a claim. On July 21, 2020, Third Coast wrote that it was unable to pay because the covered loss, estimated at $37,058.29, did not exceed the $359,856.60 deductible, and invited any new information. NFI submitted an engineer's report, the parties jointly reinspected in March 2021, and on July 28, 2021, Third Coast said it would close the file because its consultant's revised estimate, $61,839.72, was still well below the deductible.

In February 2022 NFI's counsel sent a pre-suit notice demanding that Third Coast reverse its denial and pay about $1 million. Third Coast reopened the claim only to respond, and its counsel wrote that the insurer stood by its measure of the loss, offered to discuss a reinspection, and reserved its rights. Discussions did not resolve the dispute. NFI sued in Texas state court on July 23, 2024, for breach of contract and violations of the Texas Insurance Code and the Deceptive Trade Practices Act. Third Coast removed the case to the Western District of Texas, which granted it summary judgment on limitations and denied as moot NFI's motion to compel discovery of Third Coast's internal claim records.

A panel of Chief Judge Elrod and Judges Richman and Ramirez affirmed in an unpublished per curiam opinion. Relying on Texas precedent that a denial needs no "magic words," it held the July 2020 letter unambiguously denied the claim and that a below-deductible determination is still a denial of coverage. Even if that letter were ambiguous, the court said, the claims accrued no later than July 28, 2021, when Third Coast closed the file. Third Coast's later reinspection and correspondence did not withdraw the denial, so there was no waiver or estoppel. The court also rejected NFI's challenge on the discovery motion, noting NFI did not argue that the withheld material, logged as privileged, was discoverable.
