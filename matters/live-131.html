Jovia Financial Federal Credit Union v. S Indiana Avenue LLC (South Loop Office Foreclosure & Personal Guaranty Suit)

Jovia Financial Federal Credit Union filed a $17.1 million foreclosure on a 129,200-square-foot South Loop office building after the State of Illinois ended its primary tenant's lease, and is also pursuing the owner's principal under a personal guaranty.

On September 4, 2026, Jovia Financial Federal Credit Union filed a $17.1 million foreclosure suit in Cook County Circuit Court against S Indiana Avenue LLC, an entity controlled by Lakewood, New Jersey investor Reuven Y. Rivlin, over a 10-story, 129,200-square-foot office building at 1911-1921 South Indiana Avenue. The credit union says the borrower defaulted on a $17.5 million mortgage after a three-month forbearance agreement expired in June. The building's primary tenant, a state agency that leased space for the Department of Children and Family Services, stopped paying rent after December 2025, and a state spokesperson told The Real Deal the lease was terminated for cause.

Why it matters

A single-tenant office loan can fail quickly when the tenant can terminate over the building's condition; here the state cited code violations and structural maintenance problems. Because the lender is also suing under an unlimited personal guaranty, its recovery is not limited to what the building sells for.

Full write-up

Jovia Financial Federal Credit Union, based in Westbury, New York, has sued to foreclose on a 10-story office building in Chicago's South Loop, The Real Deal reported. The September 4, 2026 complaint, filed in Cook County Circuit Court, names S Indiana Avenue LLC, an entity controlled by Lakewood, New Jersey investor Reuven Y. Rivlin, and seeks $17.1 million. Jovia alleges the borrower defaulted on a $17.5 million mortgage on the 129,200-square-foot building, built in 1996 at 1911-1921 South Indiana Avenue, after a three-month forbearance agreement expired in June. Neither Rivlin nor Jovia's attorneys responded to The Real Deal's requests for comment.

The complaint also seeks to hold Rivlin personally liable under an unlimited guaranty he signed in 2022 as part of the loan, which would let the credit union pursue him for the full $17.1 million balance whatever the foreclosure produces. Rivlin's entity bought the building on December 31, 2021 for $14 million and took the $17.5 million loan from Jovia on January 5, 2022. The Real Deal noted that court filings do not say how the loan proceeds above the purchase price were used.

The default traces to the Illinois Department of Central Management Services, which leased 129,126 square feet on behalf of the Department of Children and Family Services. According to the lender's court filings, the state stopped paying rent after December 2025. A CMS spokesperson told The Real Deal that 'this lease was terminated for cause per the terms of the lease.' City records show more than three dozen open code violations at the building since 2024 and several failed inspections, and the City of Chicago sued the landlord in August 2024 seeking more than $39,000 in fines and penalties, a case still pending in the circuit court's municipal division.

After the lease ended, the borrower filed a claim against CMS in the Illinois Court of Claims seeking unpaid 2026 rent and property tax escalation payments for 2024 and 2025. In January, the property tax appeal firm Schmidt Salzman & Moran filed a lien of nearly $52,000 against the property over unpaid legal fees, according to county records. The Real Deal also reported that three days before the foreclosure was filed, Rivlin's company ABS Management announced a nationwide office and retail acquisition strategy.
