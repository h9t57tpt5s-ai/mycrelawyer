Industrial Park Center LLC v. Great Northern Insurance Co. (Arizona Fortuity-Doctrine Coverage Ruling)

Plaintiff-Appellant: Industrial Park Center LLC (d/b/a Mainspring Capital Group)Defendant-Appellee: Great Northern Insurance Company

The Ninth Circuit vacated summary judgment for Great Northern Insurance on a $2.7 million claim for water and salt damage to an Arizona commercial building, applying the Arizona Supreme Court's new fortuity test.

Industrial Park Center LLC, doing business as Mainspring Capital Group, sued Great Northern Insurance Co., a Chubb unit, after the insurer denied a $2.7 million claim for years of water and salt damage to a commercial building leased to fish processor Star Fisheries, Business Insurance reported. Answering a question certified by the Ninth Circuit, the Arizona Supreme Court held on Sept. 1, 2026 that a loss is fortuitous unless the insured knew, when coverage attached, that it had occurred, was in progress or was certain to occur. On Oct. 16, 2026, the Ninth Circuit vacated the district court's summary judgment for Great Northern and its fee award and sent the case back.

Why it matters

Arizona now asks what the insured actually knew when coverage began, not whether a loss was reasonably foreseeable, the test the district court had used. The case returns to the district court, which must also decide Great Northern's wear-and-tear and settling exclusion defenses.

Full write-up

Industrial Park Center LLC, which does business as Mainspring Capital Group, sued Great Northern Insurance Co. after the insurer refused to pay for structural damage to Mainspring's commercial building in Arizona, Business Insurance reported. The tenant, Star Fisheries, had used water and salt in its operations for decades, gradually damaging the building's stairs, walls and foundation. Great Northern, a Chubb unit, denied the roughly $2.7 million claim, arguing the damage was too foreseeable to be insurable. The suit was filed in Maricopa County Superior Court and removed by Great Northern to federal court in July 2022, according to the federal docket.

In July 2024, U.S. District Judge Michael T. Liburdi granted summary judgment to Great Northern, applying the Ninth Circuit's reasonable-foreseeability test from Ingenco Holdings v. Ace American Insurance Co. and concluding the loss was "reasonably foreseeable and almost certain to occur," as the Ninth Circuit later summarized; the court also awarded Great Northern attorney's fees. Mainspring appealed, and in December 2025 the Ninth Circuit certified the question of what fortuity means under Arizona law to the Arizona Supreme Court.

In a Sept. 1, 2026 opinion by Justice Clint Bolick, the Arizona Supreme Court rejected the Ingenco test and held that a loss is non-fortuitous only when the insured knew, at the time coverage attached, that the loss-causing event had already occurred, was in progress, or was certain to occur. The court called this "a subjective standard." Business Insurance noted that the ruling did not decide who wins the case.

On Oct. 16, 2026, a Ninth Circuit panel of Judges Graber, Tallman and Bade, in an unpublished memorandum, vacated the summary judgment for Great Northern on the breach of contract and bad-faith claims, the denial of Mainspring's own summary judgment motion and the fee award, and remanded. The panel said the district court should decide in the first instance whether the wear-and-tear and settling exclusions, which Great Northern cited in denying the claim, bar coverage.
