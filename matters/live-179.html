Pinnacle North, LLC Veil-Piercing Appeal (Michigan Court of Appeals Affirms Personal Liability for Office Tenant's Lease Default)

In an unpublished opinion, Michigan's Court of Appeals affirmed rulings holding a defunct office tenant's sole member personally liable for its $52,548.24 lease judgment to Pinnacle North and voiding a $50,000 payment to him.

In an unpublished per curiam opinion issued March 24, 2026, the Michigan Court of Appeals affirmed Oakland Circuit Court rulings that landlord Pinnacle North, LLC could pierce the veil of its former tenant, Marketplace Home Mortgage, LLC, and hold the company's sole member and president personally liable for a $52,548.24 default judgment on its office lease, plus interest, costs and fees. The court also affirmed that a $50,000 check the insolvent company wrote to him in December 2019 was a voidable transfer, and upheld an award of about $92,000 in attorney fees under the lease.

Why it matters

The panel affirmed on a record showing that the tenant's assets were sold without the lease liability while its principal used company funds to pay down obligations for which he could be personally liable and to pay himself. Because the opinion is unpublished, it is not binding precedent in Michigan.

Full write-up

In April 2018, Marketplace Home Mortgage, LLC, a mortgage-loan company, signed a three-year lease for space in the Pinnacle North Office Center, owned by Pinnacle North, LLC. It defaulted on rent that summer. MHM sold its assets to New American Funding in a deal that closed December 31, 2018; the buyer assumed some liabilities but not the lease, and MHM was insolvent by then. In January 2019 MHM told the landlord it was no longer operating and had no revenue or employees. In December 2019 its sole member and president had MHM write him a $50,000 check. Pinnacle North later obtained a default judgment against MHM for $52,548.24 plus interest, costs and attorney fees.

Unable to collect, Pinnacle North sued the principal in Oakland Circuit Court to pierce MHM's veil and to avoid the $50,000 payment under Michigan's voidable-transfer statute, MCL 566.31 et seq. After a two-day bench trial, the court ruled for the landlord in 2023, holding him liable for the full default judgment plus costs, fees and interest, and later awarded about $92,000 in attorney fees covering both lawsuits.

A panel of Judges Patel, Swartzle and Mariani affirmed. It rejected the principal's late request to apply Minnesota law, where MHM was organized. On veil-piercing, the court pointed to evidence that he personally borrowed money to capitalize MHM, which MHM then repaid through a $193,000 payment in 2018; that MHM kept paying a $270,000 American Express debt ahead of other creditors because of his potential personal liability; and that he wrote himself the $50,000 check while MHM was insolvent. On the voidable-transfer claim, the court held MHM received no reasonably equivalent value for the payment, which the trial court found was a return of a prior capital contribution rather than a loan repayment. The court also upheld the fee award under the lease clause covering suits to collect rent.
