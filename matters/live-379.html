ZOM Holding Inc. and two affiliates filed Chapter 11 in Florida after a $308.6 million Arizona judgment for Gray Development Group. The debtors plan an asset sale and want ZOM's appeal of the judgment to continue.

ZOM Holding Inc., ZOM Associates and ZH Development I, affiliates of Winter Park, Florida-based developer ZOM Living, filed Chapter 11 petitions on October 30, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Middle District of Florida, where the cases are jointly administered. The filing follows a $308.6 million judgment, plus interest, entered in May by a Maricopa County court after a unanimous jury verdict for Gray Development Group, which alleged ZOM took its proprietary project information and then pursued the Arizona projects on its own. ZOM is appealing, and the debtors have asked the bankruptcy court to let that appeal continue while they run a sale of substantially all of their assets, with non-debtor affiliate ZOM Holding LP as the proposed stalking horse and lender.

Why it matters

The case shows how a joint venture that never closes can still produce damages measured by the profits of a whole project pipeline when the developer signed a non-circumvention agreement. It also shows a judgment debtor using Chapter 11 to keep its property-owning affiliate out of bankruptcy, keep its appeal alive and run a sale on its own timetable, which creditors and prospective partners should expect after a large verdict.

Full write-up

ZOM Holding Inc. and two affiliates, ZOM Associates and ZH Development I, filed Chapter 11 petitions on October 30, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Middle District of Florida, where the jointly administered cases are before Judge Grace E. Robson (lead case No. 6:26-bk-08509). The Real Deal reported that the debtors are affiliates of ZOM Living, a Winter Park, Florida-based multifamily and senior housing developer, but that ZOM Holding Inc. does not own any of ZOM Living's apartment properties. According to a declaration by chief restructuring officer Neil Luria, the debtors face an estimated $541 million in unsecured claims, including amounts owed to vendors and guarantees on properties in Miami, Texas, North Carolina and Virginia. The largest single liability is the Arizona judgment.

That judgment grew out of a 2019 proposal by Gray Development Group to develop several Arizona projects with ZOM. Gray and a ZOM subsidiary, ZT Development LP, signed a mutual confidentiality and non-circumvention agreement, and Gray says it shared its proprietary information under that agreement. According to the declaration, ZT Development terminated the purchase agreement a year later while the owners of the project sites went through bankruptcy, and another ZOM affiliate bought four of the properties out of bankruptcy for $82 million in 2020. Gray sued ZOM Holding in 2022 for breach of the non-circumvention agreement and bad faith. In May 2026, following a unanimous verdict and further proceedings after trial, a Maricopa County court entered a $308.6 million judgment plus interest, an amount representing the profits Gray estimated it would have earned on the projects.

ZOM disputes the judgment. In court filings it has stressed that the affiliate that signed the agreement with Gray, ZT Development LP, is a different entity from the affiliate that bought the sites, ZT Property Acquisition. On appeal, it argues that Gray failed to prove its damages and recovered profits on properties it never owned, according to the declaration.

The Real Deal reported that the bankruptcy was filed so ZOM could keep pursuing that appeal. A Chapter 11 case usually pauses other litigation, so the debtors filed a motion on the first day asking the court to modify the automatic stay and let the appeal go forward. They also plan a 12-week, court-supervised sale of substantially all of their assets, with ZOM Holding LP as the potential stalking horse bidder. In a statement, ZOM said ZOM Holding LP owns all of the ZOM companies' real estate, is not in bankruptcy and has agreed to provide debtor-in-possession financing. The declaration calls the filings the debtors' "last attempt to save their business and employees' jobs." ZOM's bankruptcy attorneys did not immediately respond to The Real Deal.
