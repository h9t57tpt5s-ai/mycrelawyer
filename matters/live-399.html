Ms. Audrey's Crafting Emporium, LLC v. Ghasem Properties, Inc. (Kentucky Appeals Court Upholds Retail Tenant's Liability, Reverses Future-Rent and 8% Interest Award)

Kentucky's Court of Appeals upheld a retail tenant's and its guarantor's liability to landlord Ghasem Properties but reversed the award of unaccrued future rent and 8% prejudgment interest, directing a new judgment at 6%.

In an unpublished opinion by Judge Lisa Payne Jones rendered November 13, 2026, the Court of Appeals of Kentucky affirmed a Jefferson Circuit Court summary judgment holding retail tenant Ms. Audrey's Crafting Emporium, LLC and its individual guarantor liable to landlord Ghasem Properties, Inc. for defaulting on a 62-month lease. It reversed the $131,855.91 award to the extent it covered rent and maintenance fees for the rest of the lease term, because the lease had no acceleration clause, and reversed the 8% prejudgment interest rate. The circuit court must enter a new judgment for rent and fees accrued as of the September 30, 2024 judgment, with 6% prejudgment interest.

Why it matters

The decision applies Kentucky's rule that, without an acceleration clause, a landlord may recover rent only as it comes due, and the court raised that issue itself although the tenant had not. It also holds that a party who ignores a summary judgment motion cannot use a post-judgment motion to raise objections it could have made earlier.

Full write-up

On February 8, 2023, Ms. Audrey's Crafting Emporium, LLC signed a 62-month lease for retail space with Ghasem Properties, Inc., and an individual guaranteed it up to $155,124.60. Rent and common-area maintenance totaled $2,585.41 a month starting in the third month. The tenant paid through December 2023 and then stopped. The landlord said it mailed a default notice on January 6, 2024, and a forcible-detainer action was dismissed in April 2024 after the tenant vacated.

The landlord sued in Jefferson Circuit Court on April 8, 2024. The tenant and guarantor admitted signing the lease, defaulting and not paying rent from January 2024 on, but denied responsibility for damages. The landlord moved for summary judgment on an affidavit from its president stating that rent for the rest of the term would have totaled $131,855.91 and that a listing agreement had not produced a new tenant. The defendants did not respond, and on September 30, 2024 the court signed the landlord's tendered judgment for $131,855.91 with 8% prejudgment interest from January 1, 2024, 6% post-judgment interest, and fees and costs. The court later denied the defendants' motion to vacate, saying their arguments should have been made before judgment, and entered a supplemental award of $8,299.30 in fees and costs.

The Court of Appeals, in an opinion joined by Judges Caldwell and Taylor, held that the defendants could not wait until after judgment to challenge the affidavit, and that their own admissions established liability. It found the interest issue properly raised, however, because the 8% rate first appeared in the landlord's tendered order. With no rate in the lease, the statutory rate for a judgment, including prejudgment interest, is 6% under KRS 360.040. On future rent, the court acknowledged the defendants never raised the point but addressed it to avoid applying the wrong law: under Kentucky precedent, without an acceleration clause, rent that has not yet accrued cannot be awarded in advance. Only nine months of rent had accrued at judgment, while the award covered the remaining 42 months, which the court called a windfall. The landlord may seek supplemental judgments for rent as it comes due if it cannot relet the space before the term ends in March 2028. The court affirmed the 6% post-judgment interest.
