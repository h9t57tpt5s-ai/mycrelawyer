Alder at Lake Park sued the Town of Lake Park, Florida in federal court, alleging the town attorney pushed it to sell its affordable housing site and then had 34 code violations issued; the town attorney denies it.

The Alder at Lake Park LLC, an entity linked to Daniel Goldstein of New York-based Bofsa Properties, sued the Town of Lake Park, Florida in the Southern District of Florida on Aug. 27, 2026 over its stalled Kelsey at Park Avenue mixed-use affordable housing project. The complaint alleges Town Attorney Tom Baird said "all problems would go away" if Alder sold its seven-parcel site to a developer he had lined up, and then directed 34 code violations after it refused. Alder asks the court to stop further code fines, require the town to decide its applications and award damages. Baird told The Real Deal he denies the allegations and called the suit retaliation for enforcing town codes.

Why it matters

The suit tests how far a developer can go in federal court when it says a small town used code enforcement to push it off a site. Developers working in small municipalities should keep a written record of every meeting with officials, especially any talk of selling to another buyer.

Full write-up

The Alder at Lake Park LLC, an entity linked to Daniel Goldstein of New York-based Bofsa Properties, filed a federal lawsuit against the Town of Lake Park on Aug. 27, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida, and amended its complaint the next day. Alder owns a seven-parcel site at Park Avenue and 10th Street in the Palm Beach County town, where it plans The Kelsey at Park Avenue, a mixed-use affordable housing project. The case is assigned to Judge Ed Artau.

According to the complaint as reported by The Real Deal, the town persuaded Alder to buy and include a run-down historic building at 918 Park Avenue in exchange for a larger project and faster approvals, then repeatedly rejected the project and fined Alder daily for the building's condition. The complaint says shifting requirements forced Alder to cut the project from 720 to 426 units. It alleges Baird told Alder's team he would "never let Alder build what it wants" and that "all problems would go away" if Alder sold to a developer he had lined up, and that after Alder refused, Baird had a code officer issue 34 violations. Alder seeks an order barring further code fines, an order requiring the town to rule on its applications, and damages.

In a statement to The Real Deal on behalf of himself and the town, Baird denied all the allegations against him, said the suit was meant to retaliate against him and town officials for doing their jobs, and said the town enforced its codes against Alder as it would against any owner that lets its properties decay. He said the owner admitted to code violations before the code magistrate, who found violations in six cases. Benjamin Diehl, a representative of Alder, said the team admitted no violations; Goldstein said no one disputes the current violations but called them "targeted" and "insignificant." "This is not about money," Goldstein told the publication. "We just want to get the project approved."

The court docket shows a motion to dismiss filed Oct. 9, 2026, an Oct. 27 order referring the case to mediation and setting a schedule, and a response in opposition filed Nov. 13.
