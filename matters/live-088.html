Alder at Lake Park, LLC v. Town of Lake Park (Affordable Housing Code-Enforcement Extortion Suit)

Plaintiff: The Alder At Lake Park LLCDefendant: Town of Lake Park

Alder at Lake Park LLC, an entity tied to Daniel Goldstein of Bofsa Properties, sued the Town of Lake Park, Florida in federal court in late August, alleging the town's attorney, Tom Baird, tried to coerce a sale of Alder's seven-parcel affordable housing assemblage by threatening that 'all problems would go away' if it sold to a developer he had lined up, then directed a code enforcement officer to issue 34 violations after Alder refused. The suit seeks to enjoin further code fines, compel the town to act on pending development applications for The Kelsey at Park Avenue, and recover damages; Baird has denied the allegations and called the suit retaliatory.

Why it matters

The case pairs a familiar developer grievance, hostile municipal code enforcement, with a far less common legal theory naming a sitting town attorney personally in an extortion and corruption scheme tied to a rival buyer. Developers assembling sites for affordable or mixed-income housing in small municipalities should treat it as a reminder of how concentrated a single official's discretion over permitting and code enforcement can become, and of the value of contemporaneously documenting every interaction that touches on alternative buyers or settlement overtures.

A New York-based developer has taken an unusually aggressive legal stance against a municipality's land-use apparatus, filing a federal complaint in late August 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida accusing the Town of Lake Park, Florida of corruption, blackmail, and extortion in connection with a stalled affordable housing project. The plaintiff, Alder at Lake Park LLC, an entity tied to Daniel Goldstein of New York-based Bofsa Properties, alleges that the town's own attorney, Tom Baird, tried to coerce it into selling its assembled development site to a developer of his choosing, and that when Alder refused, the town retaliated with a wave of code enforcement citations designed to grind the project to a halt.
