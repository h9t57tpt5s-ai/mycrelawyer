Bluetriton Brands, Inc. v. Washington Township Zoning Hearing Board and Weis Markets, Inc. (Pennsylvania Court Splits on Shopping Center Gas Station Fight)

Pennsylvania's Commonwealth Court held Weis Markets did not need a site plan for its proposed Bangor Plaza fuel station and remanded Bluetriton Brands' remaining challenges to the zoning board's special exception.

In a memorandum opinion by President Judge Renée Cohn Jubelirer filed November 10, 2026, the Commonwealth Court of Pennsylvania reversed a Northampton County Court of Common Pleas order that had overturned the Washington Township Zoning Hearing Board's approval of a Weis Gas-N-Go fuel station at Bangor Plaza. The court held the ordinance is ambiguous on whether a site plan was required and resolved that in Weis's favor, but also held Weis was bound by the zoning officer's unappealed ruling that a special exception was needed. Because common pleas never addressed objector Bluetriton Brands, Inc.'s other arguments, including its groundwater-contamination evidence, the court remanded for a new opinion within 60 days.

Why it matters

The decision shows that an applicant who does not appeal a zoning officer's ruling within 30 days is bound by it later, even when it recasts the issue as one of jurisdiction. It also applies the rule that ambiguous zoning language is read in the landowner's favor, while leaving the objector's environmental arguments to be decided on remand.

Full write-up

Weis Markets, Inc. operates a grocery store under a lease at Bangor Plaza, a 20.3-acre planned development in Washington Township's Commercial District in Northampton County, Pennsylvania. In an application dated June 17, 2022, Weis sought a special exception to add a Gas-N-Go fuel station with six fueling positions at three pumps under a canopy, replacing a previously approved bank use that was vacant. The township's zoning officer had denied a zoning permit for the station on the ground that a special exception was required, and Weis did not appeal that denial.

Bluetriton Brands, Inc., which withdraws groundwater from the Greenwaltz Spring as the primary source for its Deer Park brand and holds interests in 15 parcels in the township, including two that directly abut the site, objected. Its geologist testified that water flows from the plaza toward its spring and that the station's oil-water separator would discharge into a stormwater basin that forms part of Greenwalk Creek. Its petroleum-systems expert cited state Department of Environmental Protection records showing storage-tank violation notices at about half of Weis's Gas-N-Go locations in Pennsylvania. Weis's hydrologist testified that the aquifer is confined and not susceptible to contamination from surface spills or tank releases.

The zoning hearing board granted the special exception with conditions in February 2023. Common pleas remanded in January 2024, holding the board had to review and approve a site plan. On remand the board approved the site plan in May 2024, but in April 2025 a different common pleas judge reversed, holding the site plan did not meet the ordinance and that the board could not approve it based on future compliance. Weis appealed.

The Commonwealth Court rejected Weis's argument that no special exception was needed because a fuel station is permitted by right: because Weis had not appealed the zoning officer's determination within 30 days, that determination was binding. But the court agreed with Weis that the ordinance's provisions on site-plan review for a special-exception use that is also a land development are ambiguous, resolved that doubt in Weis's favor, and held no site plan was required at this time. Because common pleas had not addressed whether Weis met its burden for the special exception or whether the board adequately considered Bluetriton's evidence of harm, the court remanded for common pleas to decide those issues in a new opinion within 60 days.
