Wells Fargo foreclosed in September 2026 on 100 Summer St., a 32-story, 1.1-million-square-foot downtown Boston office tower owned by Rockpoint, which bought it for $806 million in October 2019 with a $470 million Wells Fargo mortgage, the Boston Globe and Bisnow reported. Why it matters

The $260 million result, Rockpoint's single bid at the auctioneer's opening price, is about a third of the 2019 purchase price. It follows other Boston office auctions, including One Lincoln St., which sold at auction last year for $400 million.

Full write-up

100 Summer St., a 32-story, 1.1-million-square-foot office tower at Summer and Devonshire streets near South Station, went to a foreclosure auction after its lender, Wells Fargo, foreclosed in September 2026, the Boston Globe and Bisnow reported. Owner Rockpoint, a Boston-based real estate private equity firm, bought the tower in October 2019 for $806 million with a $470 million Wells Fargo mortgage, according to Suffolk County deed records cited by the Globe. Banker & Tradesman first reported the auction, the Globe said.

Auctioneer Sullivan & Sullivan required bidders to hold a $10 million deposit in escrow the day before the sale. The tower was 72% leased, with tenants including Bloomberg and Bullhorn and at least $339 million in rent coming in, according to the auction house, the Globe reported. Rockpoint did not immediately respond to the Globe's request for comment.

The auction, first scheduled for Oct. 20, was rescheduled and held outside the building's entrance on the afternoon of Tuesday, Oct. 27, Bisnow reported. The auctioneer set the opening bid at $260 million, and Rockpoint was the sole bidder. Rockpoint did not respond to requests for comment at the auction.
