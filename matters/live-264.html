Microsoft v. JLL: Tenant Sues Project Manager for Defense and Insurance Coverage Over Forklift Injury During 122 Fifth Avenue Office Build-Out

Plaintiff: MicrosoftDefendant: JLL

Microsoft sued JLL in Manhattan state court, alleging the real estate services firm broke its promise to defend and indemnify Microsoft against lawsuits arising from the build-out of its office at 122 Fifth Avenue and to name Microsoft as an insured under JLL's $10 million liability policy. The suit stems from a worker's personal-injury case over a March 2023 forklift accident during the build-out, to which Microsoft was added as a defendant in 2025.

Why it matters

Construction-site injury suits in New York routinely sweep in owners and tenants under the Labor Law, so the real fight is often over who pays under indemnity and additional-insured provisions. This case shows a major office tenant turning on its own project manager over those terms, and JLL's defense that the injured worker was on a separate project retained by the building owner. Tenants, landlords and project managers should read it as a reminder to verify additional-insured endorsements are actually issued, not just promised, before work begins.

Microsoft filed suit against JLL on Friday, October 9, 2026, in New York Supreme Court, New York County, according to a Bisnow report published October 12. The dispute concerns 122 Fifth Avenue, a landmarked 11-story building in Manhattan's Flatiron area with a roughly 150,000-square-foot office block. The building's owner, The Bromley Cos., redeveloped the property for Microsoft, which moved in as anchor tenant in July 2023. JLL had served as project manager for properties Microsoft occupies since 2012, and the relationship also involved a 2013 facilities management outsourcing arrangement.
