Cimerring v. Arbor Realty Trust, Inc. ($175M Georgia Bridge-Loan Foreclosure Fraud Suit)

Former owners of a 474-unit College Park, Georgia apartment complex are suing Arbor Realty Trust for $175 million, alleging it held out a Fannie Mae refinancing before foreclosing; Arbor denies the claims and has moved to dismiss.

Brothers Yisroel and Hanoch Cimerring, who bought the 474-unit Chelsea Gardens Apartments in College Park, Georgia for nearly $45 million in 2022, sued Arbor Realty Trust in New York state court in late March 2026, seeking $175 million. They allege Arbor kept them on a high-rate bridge loan with promises of a Fannie Mae refinancing that never closed, ending in a foreclosure where an Arbor affiliate bought the property for $40 million. Arbor denies the allegations and in June asked the court to dismiss the case, saying the refinancing stalled because the owners would not make required repairs. Due diligence firm Velocity Consulting, also a defendant, has moved to dismiss as well.

Why it matters

For borrowers on bridge loans, a lender's statements about a future agency refinancing are not a commitment, especially when approval depends on inspections and repairs. Lenders should document refinancing conditions clearly, since a stalled takeout followed by a foreclosure sale to the lender's own affiliate invites this kind of suit.

Full write-up

Former owners of a 474-unit apartment complex in College Park, Georgia have sued Arbor Realty Trust, alleging the lender kept them on a high-interest bridge loan by promising a later refinancing and then foreclosed, The Real Deal reported. Brothers Yisroel and Hanoch Cimerring filed the suit in late March 2026 in New York state court and seek $175 million in damages. 'Arbor firmly denies all allegations,' a spokesperson told The Real Deal, adding that the owners mismanaged the property, that Arbor foreclosed after monetary defaults, and that it will seek a deficiency judgment for the balance owed.

According to the complaint, the brothers bought the complex, Chelsea Gardens Apartments at 2135 Godby Road, in 2022 for nearly $45 million with a $37.1 million bridge loan from an Arbor affiliate, paying more than $350,000 for a rate cap that held the rate at 5.75 percent for two years. They say they spent more than $4 million on renovations, raised occupancy from 50 to 90 percent and reached a $55 million appraisal. When they sought permanent financing in late 2023, they allege, Arbor ordered repeated inspections, demanded hundreds of thousands of dollars in immediate repairs, never approached Fannie Mae and delayed sending the loan to its loan committee. They also allege Velocity Consulting downgraded the property's rating below Fannie Mae's refinancing standard as part of the scheme. After the rate cap expired in March 2024 the interest rate nearly doubled, the brothers defaulted, and an Arbor affiliate bought the property at a foreclosure auction for $40 million, the suit says.

Arbor gives a different account in its June motion to dismiss. It argues the brothers sued to avoid a deficiency judgment in Georgia state court, where Arbor has asked the court to confirm the foreclosure sale. Arbor says it did contact Fannie Mae and obtained a pre-approval that depended on resolving the scope, budget and schedule of improvements, but it could not proceed because the owners refused to make repairs, including to fire-damaged units its engineer found on follow-up inspections in 2024. Arbor says it then modified the loan and released renovation funds to help the owners keep paying, but they defaulted anyway and its affiliate was the only bidder at the public auction. Velocity's lawyer, Neal Klausner of Davis + Gilbert, told The Real Deal the single claim against the firm 'is completely without merit' and that Velocity has moved to dismiss.
