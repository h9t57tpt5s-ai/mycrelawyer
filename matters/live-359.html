Texas Appeals Court: Dental Tenant's Square-Footage Fraud Claim Against Former Landlord Is Time-Barred

A Texas appeals court ruled a dental practice's fraud and overcharge claims against its former shopping-center landlord Brixmor were filed too late, affirming summary judgment.

The Thirteenth Court of Appeals affirmed summary judgment for Brixmor GA Apollo II TX LP against Veena Dental PA, which sued in 2024 alleging that its Northshore Plaza space in Portland, Texas, was leased as about 1,863 square feet but measured about 1,500. The court held all of Veena's claims barred by limitations: Veena had not pleaded the discovery rule for its contract and fraud claims, and its fraud and DTPA claims accrued in 2013 because its own 2013 construction plans showed the smaller size. The court did not reach Brixmor's argument that it was released when it sold the center in 2018.

Why it matters

The ruling shows that a tenant's own construction documents can establish when a square-footage claim should have been discovered, starting the limitations clock at lease signing. Because the court decided the case on limitations alone, it left open Brixmor's defense that the lease released it after the 2018 sale.

Full write-up

The Thirteenth Court of Appeals of Texas affirmed summary judgment on October 29, 2026 for Brixmor GA Apollo II TX LP in a suit by dental-practice tenant Veena Dental PA, holding that Veena's claims over the size of its leased space were barred by limitations. Justice Ysmael D. Fonseca wrote the memorandum opinion for a panel with Chief Justice Tijerina and Justice Cron.

According to the opinion, Veena leased space at 1548 Wildcat Drive in Portland, Texas, from Brixmor on March 23, 2013, intending to rebuild it as a dental office. The lease described the space as about 1,863 square feet, and the parties had negotiated the price per square foot. The lease was extended in September 2018, and in November 2018 Brixmor sold the Northshore Plaza shopping center to Northshore Plaza LP, which assumed the landlord's obligations under the lease. In 2023, Veena measured the space and found it was about 1,500 square feet; construction plans for its buildout, drawn March 13, 2013, showed a finish-out area of 1,432 square feet.

Veena sued on May 22, 2024 for breach of contract, fraud and Deceptive Trade Practices Act violations, alleging that Brixmor deliberately misrepresented the size to charge more rent. Brixmor denied liability, arguing that limitations had run, that it was the wrong defendant after the 2018 sale, and that Veena had already collected for overpaid rent from the new owner. The 343rd District Court of San Patricio County granted Brixmor summary judgment on September 30, 2025 and denied reconsideration in November 2025.

The appeals court held that the contract claim, which it said is better described as fraudulent inducement, accrued when Veena signed the lease, and that Veena waived the discovery rule for its contract and fraud claims by never pleading it. Even under the discovery-based accrual that applies to fraud and DTPA claims regardless of pleading, the court found the discrepancy was not inherently undiscoverable: Veena could have measured the space during its buildout, and its 2013 plans stated the true square footage. All claims were therefore untimely. Because limitations decided the appeal, the court did not address Brixmor's other grounds, including the lease's release of the landlord upon a transfer.
