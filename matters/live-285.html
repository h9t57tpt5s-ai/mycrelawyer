Concerned Citizens of Spartanburg County v. Spartanburg County: Judge Dismisses Zoning Challenge to Valara Holdings' $2.8B Data Center

A Spartanburg County judge dismissed neighbors' challenge to the 'minor' permit review of Valara Holdings' $2.8 billion data center for lack of standing and failure to appeal first; the group plans to appeal.

On October 6, 2026, Judge J. Derham Cole Jr. of the Seventh Judicial Circuit dismissed a suit by Concerned Citizens of Spartanburg County claiming the county should have reviewed Valara Holdings' $2.8 billion data center on South Pine Street, at the former Kohler plant, as a 'major' rather than a 'minor' land development. FOX Carolina reported that the judge found the group had not shown it was specially damaged and had not first appealed to the county Board of Zoning Appeals, and that he called a challenge to a second, still-pending permit premature. The ruling did not decide whether the project was correctly classified, and the group's lawyers say they will appeal; Valara has said it followed all state and local permitting requirements.

Why it matters

The ruling turned on procedure, not the merits: standing and the county's zoning-appeal process decided the case before the court reached the classification question. For developers relying on staff-level approvals it shows the weight of short administrative appeal windows, while the planned appeal and separate regulatory challenges keep this project's approvals contested.

Full write-up

Concerned Citizens of Spartanburg County, through its lawyers at an environmental-law nonprofit, sued in the Seventh Judicial Circuit on July 6, 2026 over how Spartanburg County was permitting a data center campus by Valara Holdings, which the Spartanburg Herald-Journal, in a story republished by South Carolina Lawyers Weekly, describes as a subsidiary of NorthMark Cloud and Compute. The project on South Pine Street, at the site of the former Kohler plant, is reported as a $2.8 billion investment. The group's lawyers, announcing the suit, described about 500,000 square feet of new buildings, a power yard, internal roads and a 450-megawatt gas-fired power plant.

The group argued that the county's Unified Land Management Ordinance required the project to be reviewed as a 'major' land development, which goes to the Planning Commission with public notice and comment, rather than as a 'minor' development reviewed by staff. It asked for a declaration that Valara must obtain a major development permit and for an injunction stopping the county from processing the minor application. FOX Carolina reported that the first permit, to redevelop the Kohler building, was approved in August 2025, and that a second application, for four new buildings, the power yard and cooling systems, is still pending. County officials have said the classification depends on the ordinance's specific language rather than a project's size alone, according to the Herald-Journal.

Judge Cole dismissed the case on October 6 without deciding whether the classification was correct. According to FOX Carolina, he found the group's concerns about noise, dust, traffic, health and transparency too general to show the special damage South Carolina law requires of neighbors, and he said the group should have appealed to the county Board of Zoning Appeals within 10 days before going to court. He called the challenge to the second permit premature because the county has not acted on it. The dismissal also ended the group's request to pause construction, which had been heard on September 17.

A senior attorney for the group told FOX Carolina the ruling conflicts with controlling South Carolina Supreme Court precedent and that residents could not have used the zoning-appeal route because they were told at the time that the project was not a data center; she said the group's lawyers expected to file a notice of appeal within days. The same lawyers are also pursuing a case, now before the South Carolina Court of Appeals, over whether Valara needed Public Service Commission approval, and has asked state regulators to reopen public comment on the project's air permit, issued September 18, over formaldehyde emissions from its planned gas turbines. Valara representatives have said the companies followed all state and local permitting requirements, and a county council member called the suit 'frivolous,' the Herald-Journal reported. FOX Carolina also reported that the county's one-year pause on new data center applications, adopted in June, does not reach Valara's project.
