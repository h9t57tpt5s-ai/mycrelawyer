The Eleventh Circuit upheld a $3,954,244.04 guaranty judgment on a $56.33 million hotel loan, holding the borrower's eve-of-foreclosure Chapter 11 filing was a full-recourse event.

The Eleventh Circuit on August 26, 2026 affirmed a Northern District of Georgia judgment holding the guarantor of a $56.33 million Access Point Financial loan on three Texas properties personally liable for $3,954,244.04, the balance left after foreclosure sales. The day before a scheduled foreclosure, the Houston borrower's membership interests were transferred without the lender's consent and the borrower, under new management, filed a Chapter 11 petition. The unpublished opinion held that the filing alone was a full-recourse event, rejecting the guarantor's argument that the new owner had no authority to file it.

Why it matters

The ruling applies the guaranty as written: where a borrower's voluntary bankruptcy petition triggers full recourse, an argument that new owners filed it without authority did not avoid liability. The opinion is unpublished and does not bind later Eleventh Circuit panels.

Full write-up

On August 26, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit affirmed a Northern District of Georgia summary judgment holding the guarantor of a $56.33 million loan from Access Point Financial, LLC personally liable for $3,954,244.04. In an unpublished per curiam opinion, Judges Newsom, Lagoa and Brasher held that a borrower's voluntary Chapter 11 petition, filed the day before a scheduled foreclosure sale, was a full-recourse event under the guaranty.

According to the opinion, Access Point lent $56,330,000 in October 2021 to three borrowers owning properties in Houston, Austin and Dallas, Texas; Bisnow reported in 2023 that the loan covered three former Crowne Plaza hotels and that the borrower was tied to Lockwood Development Partners. The guarantor, who managed the borrowers' parent entities, agreed to be personally liable for the full debt if, among other events, a borrower filed a voluntary bankruptcy petition or made a transfer the lender had not permitted. In September 2022 he replaced himself with DDI Equities, Inc. as the borrowers' manager without Access Point's consent. The Austin property was sold in 2022 for $30,739,480.22, which paid down the loan.

After the borrowers defaulted, Access Point scheduled foreclosure of the Houston and Dallas properties for September 5, 2023. The day before, the Houston borrower transferred all its membership interests to 3 Big MMM, LLC without Access Point's consent and, under new management, filed a voluntary Chapter 11 petition in the Southern District of Florida, which the bankruptcy court later dismissed as a skeletal petition. Access Point bought the Dallas property with a $10 million credit bid on September 5 and later the Houston property with a $7,846,000 credit bid, leaving about $3 million plus interest unpaid. It sued the guarantor on September 18, 2023, and the district court granted summary judgment on two grounds: the bankruptcy filing and the change of manager.

On appeal, the guarantor argued that 3 Big MMM had no authority to file the petition, so the borrower had not filed one within the guaranty's meaning. Applying Georgia law, which construes guaranties strongly against the guarantor, the panel held that the guaranty did not require an 'authorized' or 'successful' petition, and it was undisputed that the Houston borrower had filed one. Having affirmed on that ground, the court did not decide whether the manager change or the transfer to 3 Big MMM was also a full-recourse event.
