Foreclosure notices were filed over a roughly $59 million unpaid loan balance on Colorado Springs' two tallest buildings, the Wells Fargo Tower and FirstBank Building at Palmer Center.

El Paso County Public Trustee's Office records show a foreclosure notice filed against RPalmer LLC, Overland Palmer LLC and Palmer Center Owner LLC, Delaware companies controlled by real estate firms with principal offices in California and Canada, The Gazette reported on August 25, 2026. The owners have an unpaid principal balance of about $59 million on a $74 million loan now held by a trustee for Bank of America, and a foreclosure auction was scheduled for October 21. The filings cover the Wells Fargo Tower and FirstBank Building at Palmer Center, the city's two tallest buildings, and several business-suite properties; the adjacent Antlers hotel is not in foreclosure.

Why it matters

The filing puts the two most prominent office buildings in downtown Colorado Springs into Colorado's non-judicial public trustee foreclosure process. The Gazette reported that it was unclear what led to the default, while a longtime local developer it quoted tied the foreclosures to wider office-market conditions.

Full write-up

The Wells Fargo Tower at 90 S. Cascade Ave. and the FirstBank Building at 2 N. Cascade Ave., the two tallest buildings in Colorado Springs and part of the downtown Palmer Center, have fallen into foreclosure, The Gazette reported on August 25, 2026, citing El Paso County Public Trustee's Office records. A foreclosure notice was filed against RPalmer LLC, Overland Palmer LLC and Palmer Center Owner LLC, Delaware companies controlled by real estate firms with principal offices in California and Canada. A fourth entity, DPalmer LLC, was also noticed; it withdrew its authority to do business in Colorado in January 2021. Several business-suite properties reached through either building or the adjacent Antlers hotel are also in foreclosure, though The Gazette updated its story to clarify that the Antlers itself is not.

The owner entities bought the buildings in 2015, according to the county assessor. Records show an unpaid principal balance of about $59 million on a $74 million loan, which a trustee for Bank of America now holds, and a foreclosure auction was scheduled for October 21. The Gazette said it was unclear what caused the default and that it could not reach the attorney listed on the filing. The paper reported that tenants could likely keep operating as before.

The Gazette cited a Downtown Partnership report putting downtown office vacancy at 10.4% in the second quarter of 2026, against 12% citywide, with downtown Class A vacancy rising to 8.2% from 7.4%. A longtime Colorado Springs developer who is a FirstBank Building tenant called the foreclosures "a function of market circumstances."
