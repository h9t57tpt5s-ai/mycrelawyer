First Care Therapy, LLC v. Congress Ave Boynton, LLC (Commercial Lease Default Damages Reversed)

Commercial Lease Disputes Ruling Issued
Date
November 12, 2026
Court or forum
District Court of Appeal of Florida, Fourth District
State
Florida
Presiding judge
Alan O. Forst
Amount / scale
$85,782.80 damages award reversed and remanded for a properly noticed damages trial
Parties
Tenant/Appellant: First Care Therapy, LLCLandlord/Appellee: Congress Ave Boynton, LLC

A Florida appeals court affirmed a landlord's default judgment on liability against a commercial tenant and its guarantor but reversed the $85,782.80 damages award because no properly noticed damages trial was held.

Florida's Fourth District Court of Appeal affirmed a Palm Beach County judgment holding tenant First Care Therapy, LLC and an individual guarantor liable to landlord Congress Ave Boynton, LLC for breaching a commercial lease and guaranty in Boynton Beach. It reversed the $85,782.80 damages award because the damages were unliquidated and were fixed at a non-evidentiary summary judgment hearing rather than at a damages trial noticed under Florida Rule of Civil Procedure 1.440(d), and remanded for that trial.

Why it matters

Under the Fourth District's precedent, a default establishes liability but not unliquidated damages, and an affidavit of indebtedness does not make them liquidated. The court noted that Florida's district courts of appeal are divided on whether summary judgment can fix such damages, and the landlord did not ask it to revisit its rule.

Full write-up

On November 12, 2026, Florida's Fourth District Court of Appeal affirmed in part and reversed in part a final default summary judgment entered for landlord Congress Ave Boynton, LLC against tenant First Care Therapy, LLC and the individual who guaranteed its lease. Judge Alan O. Forst wrote the opinion, joined by Judges Shepherd and Shaw. The tenant had leased commercial premises in Boynton Beach from SNH Medical Office Properties Trust in September 2021; Congress Ave Boynton bought the property in May 2024 and took an assignment of the lease and guaranty.

The landlord sued on February 21, 2025, for nonpayment of rent without pleading a sum certain, attaching a lease ledger showing $34,337.91 in unpaid charges. Neither defendant responded, and the clerk entered defaults in March 2025. The landlord then moved for summary judgment on an affidavit of indebtedness from its managing agent totaling $85,782.80: $48,480 in rent, $4,222 in legal fees to draft and negotiate a new lease, a $10,557 reletting commission and $22,523 in tenant improvements for reletting. At a July 2025 hearing the guarantor appeared without a lawyer and no attorney appeared for the tenant. The court heard argument and entered judgment for the full amount requested, plus interest, and found the landlord entitled to attorney's fees.

The panel rejected the tenant's and guarantor's arguments that the judgment was void. The circuit court had subject-matter jurisdiction, and any objection to its authority in this particular case was waived. The landlord did not have to attach the written assignment, and by defaulting the defendants admitted the allegation that the lease and guaranty had been assigned to it. A company cannot appear in court without counsel, and the tenant's failure to retain one was not a due-process violation.

The damages award was another matter. Following its August 2026 decision in Affinity Health Partners, LLC v. Hayes Locums, LLC, the court held that a clerk's default supports only liquidated damages, and that these damages were unliquidated because the complaint pleaded no sum certain, the ledger showed less than half the award, and the final figure could be reached only by weighing the affidavit. The prejudgment interest and costs were likewise unliquidated. Because the July 2025 hearing was a non-evidentiary summary judgment proceeding rather than a damages trial noticed under Rule 1.440(d), the court reversed the award and remanded for such a trial, leaving the liability ruling in place. The opinion is not final until any timely motion for rehearing is resolved.
