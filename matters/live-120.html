City of San Antonio and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Texas, Inc. v. Southside Affordable Development, LLC

The Fourth Court of Appeals dismissed Southside Affordable Development's challenge to San Antonio's Toyota zoning agreement, finding no standing because the challenged ordinances predated its purchase.

The Fourth Court of Appeals in San Antonio on July 16, 2025 reversed the trial court and dismissed with prejudice Southside Affordable Development, LLC's claims against the City of San Antonio challenging the 2003 Project Starbright Agreement, which addresses zoning within a three-mile "Enhanced Zoning Area" around Toyota's South Side plant. Southside, which sued the city and Toyota in December 2023 after city officials said they would not support rezoning part of its 97-acre tract for multifamily housing, called the arrangement unconstitutional contract zoning and a taking, KSAT reported. The appeals court held Southside lacked standing because the challenged ordinances predated its 2017 purchase and it showed no assignment of the prior owner's claims.

Why it matters

A cautionary tale for any developer buying into an area covered by a long-standing development agreement between a city and a major industrial tenant: a purchaser who did not own the land when the underlying ordinances were adopted generally cannot revive a constitutional challenge to them absent an explicit assignment of claims from the prior owner, and informal statements of non-support from city staff do not themselves trigger a takings claim. Developers eyeing land inside negotiated 'enhanced zoning area' buffers near large industrial users should diligence the origin and assignability of any zoning challenge before relying on it as a path around a restrictive framework.

Full write-up

In 2003, the City of San Antonio and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Texas entered into the Project Starbright Agreement to bring Toyota's manufacturing plant to newly annexed land on the city's South Side. A companion City Agreement addressed zoning within a three-mile "Enhanced Zoning Area" around the plant. In January 2004 the city rezoned a 97-acre tract, then owned by Presto Tierra LLC, to an industrial classification that does not permit multifamily housing, and in April 2017 rezoned it again at Presto Tierra's request. The tract was conveyed on June 29, 2017 to FLG Preserve, LLC, later renamed Southside Affordable Development, LLC, according to the appeals court's opinion.

In 2022 and 2023, Southside sought to rezone about 34.5 acres of the tract for multifamily housing; in 2023 a council member and the City Attorney's office said the city would not support the project. Southside sued the city and Toyota on Dec. 4, 2023 in Bexar County's 285th Judicial District Court, alleging the agreement amounted to unconstitutional, illegal contract zoning and a taking of private property, and citing the Private Real Property Rights Preservation Act, KSAT reported. Southside said the land's value fell from $15 million to $3 million to $4 million. City Attorney Andy Segovia said the city would defend the suit, and Toyota declined to comment, according to KSAT.

The Fourth Court of Appeals reversed the trial court's May 8, 2024 denial of the city's plea to the jurisdiction and rendered judgment dismissing Southside's claims against the city with prejudice. Writing for the panel, Justice H. Todd McCray held that Southside lacked standing because all of the challenged ordinances were adopted before it acquired the property, and it identified no assignment of Presto Tierra's claims, nor did its deed contain assignment language. The court held Southside was not an "owner" under the property-rights statute for the earlier ordinances, and that statements by council members or city staff "are not substitutes for an official act" of the city. A separate appeal by Toyota was dismissed and treated as a mandamus petition, which the court denied as moot after granting the city's appeal.

In a footnote, the court said the ruling does not rule out standing for a future claim if the city formally denies a Southside rezoning request. The court of appeals issued its mandate on April 7, 2026, according to its docket.
