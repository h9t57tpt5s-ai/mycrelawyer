Ms. Audrey's Crafting Emporium, LLC v. Ghasem Properties, Inc. (Kentucky Appeals Court Upholds Retail Tenant's Liability, Reverses Future-Rent and 8% Interest Award)

Kentucky's Court of Appeals upheld a retail tenant's and its guarantor's liability to landlord Ghasem Properties but reversed the award of unaccrued future rent and 8% prejudgment interest, directing a new judgment at 6%.

In an unpublished opinion by Judge Lisa Payne Jones rendered November 13, 2026, the Court of Appeals of Kentucky affirmed a Jefferson Circuit Court summary judgment holding retail tenant Ms. Audrey's Crafting Emporium, LLC and its individual guarantor liable to landlord Ghasem Properties, Inc. for defaulting on a 62-month lease. It reversed the $131,855.91 award to the extent it covered rent and maintenance fees for the rest of the lease term, because the lease had no acceleration clause, and reversed the 8% prejudgment interest rate. The circuit court must enter a new judgment for rent and fees accrued as of the September 30, 2024 judgment, with 6% prejudgment interest.

$131,855.91 judgment reversed in part; new judgment limited to rent and fees accrued as of Sept. 30, 2024, with 6% prejudgment interest; guaranty capped at $155,124.60
Nov 13, 2026 Court of Appeals of Kentucky

Holmwood Capital Advisors, LLC v. HC Government Realty Holdings, L.P.: Delaware Chancery Rules Government-Lease REIT Owes Ousted Manager a 3x Termination Fee

After a three-day trial, the Court of Chancery held a REIT that owns federally leased buildings underpaid its terminated external manager by using a 1.5x fee multiplier instead of 3x, but awarded the REIT $517,463.35 for an unconsented CMBS transfer.

On November 13, 2026, the Delaware Court of Chancery issued a post-trial opinion in the fee dispute between HC Government Realty Trust, Inc., a REIT that owns properties leased to the federal government, and its former external manager, Holmwood Capital Advisors, LLC. The court held that the REIT wrongly reduced its stockholders' equity by booking an estimate of the termination fee as a liability, and that a 3.0x multiplier, not the 1.5x the REIT used, governs the fee. The REIT won on other points: it could pay fees in common stock, the manager must absorb offering costs above a 15% cap, and Holmwood Capital, LLC owes $517,463.35 for breaching a no-default representation tied to a CMBS loan. The parties were told to confer on a recalculated fee and a form of order.

Termination fee to be recalculated at a 3x multiplier ($1,645,453 was paid at 1.5x); $517,463.35 awarded to the REIT on its counterclaim
Nov 13, 2026 Delaware Court of Chancery

Harpers Ferry Residents v. West Virginia Department of Commerce Officials (Supreme Court Upholds Tourism District Law Exempting $138M Hill Top House Hotel From Local Zoning)

West Virginia's high court rejected residents' constitutional challenge to the 2020 law exempting large tourism projects in small towns from municipal zoning and building codes. The Hill Top House Hotel redevelopment is the only approved district.

On November 13, 2026, the Supreme Court of Appeals of West Virginia affirmed the dismissal of a suit by Harpers Ferry residents challenging the Tourism Development District Act, W. Va. Code § 5B-1-9. The 2020 statute lets state commerce officials designate districts in which qualifying tourism projects are exempt from municipal zoning, historic preservation, building permitting and other local rules. The only district approved so far covers SWaN Hill Top House, LLC's redevelopment of the Hill Top House Hotel, which the developer says will cost more than $138 million. The court held the Act is not an unconstitutional special law, does not violate equal protection, and does not infringe any right to local self-rule or to vote.

Hotel redevelopment the developer says will cost more than $138 million
Nov 13, 2026 Supreme Court of Appeals of West Virginia

Illinois Appellate Court Holds Commercial Landlords May Assign Claims for Tenants' Unpaid Rent

An Illinois appellate court held that a commercial landlord's claim for a tenant's already-accrued unpaid rent is a freely assignable asset, reviving a rent-collection suit against a Chicago tenant that had been dismissed for lack of standing.

The Appellate Court of Illinois, First District, held on November 9, 2026, that a commercial landlord may assign its claim for a tenant's already-accrued, unpaid rent to a third party, reversing a Cook County circuit court that had dismissed a rent-collection suit against a Chicago commercial tenant for lack of standing. The circuit court had relied on an 1872 Illinois Supreme Court decision and a 2018 First District opinion for the proposition that claims for back rent do not transfer when assigned separately from the property. The appellate panel found that reasoning inconsistent with the modern Illinois law of assignable claims and remanded the case for further proceedings on the merits of the unpaid-rent claim.

No dollar figure stated
Nov 9, 2026 Appellate Court of Illinois, First District

The Sixth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Lyon Township, Michigan, and its supervisor on a developer's civil-rights claims over the township's refusal to issue building permits before a county septic permit.

First Citizens Community Bank v. SA Living Fund LLC (Pennsylvania Court Upholds Confessed Judgment on $3.85 Million Loan)

Pennsylvania's Superior Court affirmed the denial of a borrower's and its guarantors' petition to strike or open a bank's confessed judgment on a $3.85 million loan secured by 827 Spring Garden Street in Philadelphia.

In a non-precedential memorandum by Judge Maria C. McLaughlin filed November 9, 2026, the Superior Court of Pennsylvania affirmed a Philadelphia County order denying SA Living Fund LLC's and its two individual guarantors' petition to strike or open a confessed judgment entered for First Citizens Community Bank on a $3.85 million loan secured by 827 Spring Garden Street. The court held that the trial court erred by looking beyond the record as it stood when judgment was entered, but that the error was harmless because the complaint adequately averred a default. A federal judge held that a Flint shopping-center lease's fire-casualty clause made landlord Hallwood Plaza LLC responsible for rebuilding H&R Block's burned-out store, and threw out the landlord's damages counterclaims.
