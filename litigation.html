The Tennessee Supreme Court upheld summary judgment for the seller of a Hilton-branded hotel near the Nashville airport, letting it keep $18,917,500 in earnest money as liquidated damages after the buyer failed to close.

Ms. Audrey's Crafting Emporium, LLC v. Ghasem Properties, Inc. (Kentucky Appeals Court Upholds Retail Tenant's Liability, Reverses Future-Rent and 8% Interest Award)

Kentucky's Court of Appeals upheld a retail tenant's and its guarantor's liability to landlord Ghasem Properties but reversed the award of unaccrued future rent and 8% prejudgment interest, directing a new judgment at 6%.

In an unpublished opinion by Judge Lisa Payne Jones rendered November 13, 2026, the Court of Appeals of Kentucky affirmed a Jefferson Circuit Court summary judgment holding retail tenant Ms. Audrey's Crafting Emporium, LLC and its individual guarantor liable to landlord Ghasem Properties, Inc. for defaulting on a 62-month lease. Holmwood Capital Advisors, LLC v. HC Government Realty Holdings, L.P.: Delaware Chancery Rules Government-Lease REIT Owes Ousted Manager a 3x Termination Fee

After a three-day trial, the Court of Chancery held a REIT that owns federally leased buildings underpaid its terminated external manager by using a 1.5x fee multiplier instead of 3x, but awarded the REIT $517,463.35 for an unconsented CMBS transfer.

On November 13, 2026, the Delaware Court of Chancery issued a post-trial opinion in the fee dispute between HC Government Realty Trust, Inc., a REIT that owns properties leased to the federal government, and its former external manager, Holmwood Capital Advisors, LLC. The court held that the REIT wrongly reduced its stockholders' equity by booking an estimate of the termination fee as a liability, and that a 3.0x multiplier, not the 1.5x the REIT used, governs the fee. The REIT won on other points: it could pay fees in common stock, the manager must absorb offering costs above a 15% cap, and Holmwood Capital, LLC owes $517,463.35 for breaching a no-default representation tied to a CMBS loan. The parties were told to confer on a recalculated fee and a form of order.

Termination fee to be recalculated at a 3x multiplier ($1,645,453 was paid at 1.5x); $517,463.35 awarded to the REIT on its counterclaim
Nov 13, 2026 Delaware Court of Chancery

Harpers Ferry Residents v. West Virginia Department of Commerce Officials (Supreme Court Upholds Tourism District Law Exempting $138M Hill Top House Hotel From Local Zoning)

West Virginia's high court rejected residents' constitutional challenge to the 2020 law exempting large tourism projects in small towns from municipal zoning and building codes. The Hill Top House Hotel redevelopment is the only approved district.

On November 13, 2026, the Supreme Court of Appeals of West Virginia affirmed the dismissal of a suit by Harpers Ferry residents challenging the Tourism Development District Act, W. Va. Code § 5B-1-9. The 2020 statute lets state commerce officials designate districts in which qualifying tourism projects are exempt from municipal zoning, historic preservation, building permitting and other local rules. The only district approved so far covers SWaN Hill Top House, LLC's redevelopment of the Hill Top House Hotel, which the developer says will cost more than $138 million. The court held the Act is not an unconstitutional special law, does not violate equal protection, and does not infringe any right to local self-rule or to vote.

Hotel redevelopment the developer says will cost more than $138 million

Illinois Appellate Court Holds Commercial Landlords May Assign Claims for Tenants' Unpaid Rent

An Illinois appellate court held that a landlord's claim for a tenant's past-due, unpaid rent can be assigned, reversing a Cook County dismissal for lack of standing in a suit against a Chicago commercial tenant.

The Appellate Court of Illinois, First District, held on November 9, 2026 that a landlord may assign its claim for past-due, unpaid rent, reversing a Cook County circuit court that had dismissed the assignees' suit against a Chicago commercial tenant for lack of standing. The circuit court had relied on an 1872 Illinois Supreme Court decision holding that rents in arrears are not assignable and on a 2018 First District opinion that quoted it. The panel found that rule inconsistent with the Supreme Court's modern view that assignability is the rule, and remanded for the circuit court to consider the tenant's other grounds for dismissal.

No dollar figure stated
Nov 9, 2026 Appellate Court of Illinois, First District
