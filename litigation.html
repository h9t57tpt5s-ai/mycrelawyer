Georgia's Court of Appeals revived premises-liability and punitive-damages claims against apartment owner SMRE Roswell SPE after an off-duty officer working as the complex's security wrongly raided an occupied unit using an outdated vacancy list.

The Georgia Court of Appeals on November 3, 2026 partially reversed summary judgment that had favored apartment owner SMRE Roswell SPE, LLC in a suit arising from a June 2022 incident in which an off-duty Roswell police officer, moonlighting as the Roswell Creek Apartments' part-time security guard, used an outdated vacancy list to mistakenly identify an occupied, rented unit as vacant, prompting fellow officers to force entry with weapons drawn and detain two residents. Sinmier, LLC v. Everest Indemnity Insurance Co. (Sixth Circuit Affirms Lender's Insurance Claims Died With Its Own Settlement)

The Sixth Circuit held lender Sinmier's deed-in-lieu settlement with defaulted borrower Vintro extinguished its mortgagee insurable interest, dooming its suit against the resort's insurers.

The Sixth Circuit affirmed summary judgment against commercial real estate lender Sinmier, LLC in its suit against the insurance broker and insurers of its defaulted borrower, Vintro Hotels and Resorts Ohio LLC, over storm and fire damage at the Maui Sands Resort and Waterpark in Sandusky, Ohio. The court held that Sinmier's own settlement agreement with Vintro, under which Vintro conveyed the resort by deed in lieu of foreclosure as full payment of its loan obligations, had already extinguished Sinmier's insurable mortgagee interest in the property, leaving it with no cognizable claim against the insurers.

$7 million original loan; Vintro's insurer settled the underlying property claim for $1,125,000; Sinmier separately recovered a $627,375 payment from Vintro's owner under the foreclosure settlement
Nov 2, 2026 U.S. Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit (N.D. Ohio)

Kentucky's Court of Appeals affirmed dismissal of Lampton Securities' reverse condemnation suit over an Ohio River floodplain tract, holding the claim wasn't ripe because Lampton never sought a variance from the floodplain board.

Lampton Securities Partnership, LLP sued Louisville-Jefferson County Metro Government and the Louisville and Jefferson County Metropolitan Sewer District for reverse (inverse) condemnation, alleging that 1997 floodplain ordinances stripped all economically beneficial use from a roughly two-acre Ohio River tract it bought for $800,000 in 1998. RJ's International Trading, LLC v. Crown Castle South, LLC (Florida Supreme Court: Easement Attorney's-Fee Clauses Don't Run With the Land)

Answering a certified question from the Eleventh Circuit, Florida's Supreme Court held an easement's prevailing-party attorney's-fee clause is a personal covenant that doesn't bind successors like Crown Castle.

The Florida Supreme Court, answering a certified question from the Eleventh Circuit, held on October 29, 2026 that a prevailing-party attorney's-fee provision in an easement agreement is a personal covenant that does not run with the land. Crown Castle South, LLC therefore cannot be forced to pay RJ's International Trading, LLC's attorney's fees under a fee clause that Crown Castle's predecessors-in-interest, not Crown Castle itself, agreed to decades earlier, even though Crown Castle was bound by -- and lost on -- the underlying easement and trespass claims.

$40,001 jury verdict ($1 for breach of easement, $40,000 for trespass); the attorney's-fee amount actually in dispute is not stated in the opinion
Oct 29, 2026 Supreme Court of Florida

Portage Township of St. Joseph County, Indiana v. City of South Bend (Eminent Domain in Redevelopment Areas Requires Council Authorization)

The Indiana Court of Appeals ruled on October 29, 2026 that a city's legislative body, not its mayor or other officials, holds exclusive authority to exercise eminent domain over property inside a statutorily designated redevelopment project area, reversing a trial court that had allowed the City of South Bend to pursue a condemnation action against property owned by Argos 316, LLC without a vote of the South Bend Common Council. Objectors Portage Township, which had purchased the property after the condemnation suit was filed, and 350 Columbia Street, LLC, which held easement rights over it, successfully argued the City's suit was unauthorized under Indiana Code section 36-7-14-20(a).

Not stated; dispute concerns authority to condemn, not a damages or compensation figure
