Bluetriton Brands, Inc. v. Washington Township Zoning Hearing Board and Weis Markets, Inc. (Pennsylvania Court Splits on Shopping Center Gas Station Fight)

Pennsylvania's Commonwealth Court reversed a trial court's site-plan ruling but remanded Bluetriton Brands' groundwater-contamination challenge to Weis Markets' proposed Bangor Plaza gas station, leaving the special exception unresolved.

The Commonwealth Court of Pennsylvania reversed a Northampton County trial court's holding that Weis Markets needed to submit a site plan before adding a fuel station to its Bangor Plaza supermarket, but remanded the case because the trial court never addressed all of objector Bluetriton Brands' arguments against the Zoning Hearing Board's grant of a special exception for the project. Bluetriton, which operates a spring-water extraction business on parcels adjoining and downstream of the site, presented hydrogeology and petroleum-storage expert testimony that the fuel station could contaminate the aquifer feeding its Deer Park brand's Greenwaltz Spring source.

The City of Colonial Heights, Virginia v. Alpene Land, LLC (Virginia Appeals Court Limits Retroactive Tax Penalties on Delinquent Commercial Land)

The Court of Appeals of Virginia held a city may collect default statutory interest and penalties on delinquent commercial real estate taxes even when its own ordinance is defective, but can't apply a newly passed penalty ordinance retroactively.

The Court of Appeals of Virginia held that Colonial Heights could collect delinquent real estate tax interest and penalties on commercial parcels owned by Alpene Land, LLC and Barker Investment Corporation under Virginia's statewide default penalty and interest rates, even though the circuit court had found the City's own local penalty ordinance ineffective. But the court agreed with the circuit court that a new penalty ordinance the City passed in August 2024, while the tax-sale litigation was pending, could not be applied retroactively to taxes already delinquent before it was enacted.

Over $65,000 in delinquent real estate taxes across nine tax years (2016-2024)
Nov 10, 2026 Court of Appeals of Virginia

Illinois Appellate Court Holds Commercial Landlords May Assign Claims for Tenants' Unpaid Rent

An Illinois appellate court held that a commercial landlord's claim for a tenant's already-accrued unpaid rent is a freely assignable asset, reviving a rent-collection suit against a Chicago tenant that had been dismissed for lack of standing.

The Appellate Court of Illinois, First District, held on November 9, 2026, that a commercial landlord may assign its claim for a tenant's already-accrued, unpaid rent to a third party, reversing a Cook County circuit court that had dismissed a rent-collection suit against a Chicago commercial tenant for lack of standing. The circuit court had relied on an 1872 Illinois Supreme Court decision and a 2018 First District opinion for the proposition that claims for back rent do not transfer when assigned separately from the property. The appellate panel found that reasoning inconsistent with the modern Illinois law of assignable claims and remanded the case for further proceedings on the merits of the unpaid-rent claim.

No dollar figure stated
Nov 9, 2026 Appellate Court of Illinois, First District

Columbus v. Ohio Wholesale Auto Sales, L.L.C. (Dismissed Foreclosure Does Not Extinguish Mortgage, Ohio Appeals Court Holds)

Ohio's Tenth District ruled a trial court wrongly held mortgagee Wiltshire Capital Partners' dismissed foreclosure case barred it, under res judicata, from any share of the City of Columbus's eminent domain payment on the same property.

The Tenth District Court of Appeals of Ohio reversed a Franklin County trial court that had barred mortgagee Wiltshire Capital Partners, LP from asserting any claim to proceeds in the City of Columbus's eminent domain case against Ohio Wholesale Auto Sales, LLC. The trial court had held that a 2018 dismissal of Wiltshire's separate foreclosure action on the same mortgage, entered for failure to prosecute, operated as res judicata and left Wiltshire with no interest to assert. The appellate court held a dismissal for failure to prosecute is not an adjudication on the merits and cannot support res judicata, and remanded the case for further proceedings.

$4,014 condemnation payment at issue; mortgage interest's share yet to be determined on remand

Sinmier, LLC v. Everest Indemnity Insurance Co. (Sixth Circuit Affirms Lender's Insurance Claims Died With Its Own Settlement)

The Sixth Circuit held lender Sinmier's deed-in-lieu settlement with defaulted borrower Vintro extinguished its mortgagee insurable interest, dooming its suit against the resort's insurers.

The Sixth Circuit affirmed summary judgment against commercial real estate lender Sinmier, LLC in its suit against the insurance broker and insurers of its defaulted borrower, Vintro Hotels and Resorts Ohio LLC, over storm and fire damage at the Maui Sands Resort and Waterpark in Sandusky, Ohio. The court held that Sinmier's own settlement agreement with Vintro, under which Vintro conveyed the resort by deed in lieu of foreclosure as full payment of its loan obligations, had already extinguished Sinmier's insurable mortgagee interest in the property, leaving it with no cognizable claim against the insurers.
